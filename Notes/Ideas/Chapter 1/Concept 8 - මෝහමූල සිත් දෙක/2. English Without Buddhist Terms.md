### Delusion and the Two Delusion-Rooted States of Consciousness

In this philosophical system, **delusion** is defined as an obscuring mental quality that darkens the mind when thinking about an object, preventing the correct comprehension of that object's true nature. While delusion is fundamentally present in all unwholesome mental states, it acts as the primary, dominant root in two specific states of consciousness. These two delusion-rooted states are distinguished from one another by two accompanying mental factors: **doubt (or perplexity)** and **restlessness (or agitation)**.

---

### Common Characteristics of the Two Delusion-Rooted States

The two delusion-rooted states of consciousness are:
1. **Consciousness accompanied by neutral feeling and conjoined with doubt.**
2. **Consciousness accompanied by neutral feeling and conjoined with restlessness.**

#### 1. The Cause of Affective Neutrality
When delusion is intensely dominant, it conceals the true nature of the object. Consequently, the mind lacks the capacity to apprehend a pleasant object as pleasant or an unpleasant object as unpleasant. Because there is no definitive certainty regarding the object, and because the mind wavers due to restlessness, it cannot relish or emotionally engage with the object. Therefore, neither joy (pleasure) nor displeasure (aversion) can arise; the experience is strictly confined to a **neutral feeling** (an indifferent, intermediate affect).

#### 2. The Unprompted (Spontaneous) Mode of Arising
Doubt and mental restlessness are phenomena that arise naturally and intrinsically within beings. They do not depend on external prompting, instigation, encouragement, or premeditation. Because they arise entirely without inducement, both delusion-rooted states are classified as **unprompted (spontaneous)**.

---

### The First State: Consciousness Conjoined with Doubt

When excessive delusion prevents the mind from arriving at a decisive judgment about an object, uncertainty, mistrust, and skepticism arise. This state of **doubt** functions as a mental hindrance that directly obstructs the performance of wholesome actions.

A critical distinction must be made regarding this concept: ordinary doubt that arises when one is simply unable to properly investigate an unfamiliar mundane matter is *not* this unwholesome hindrance. Rather, the unwholesome hindrance of doubt refers specifically to skepticism directed at eight fundamental bases:

1. **Doubt regarding the Enlightened Teacher:** Doubting whether an individual possessing the thirty-two physical marks of a great being could exist, or doubting the reality of supreme, all-encompassing knowledge.
2. **Doubt regarding the Teaching (the Truth):** Doubting the reality of the nine transcendent truths—namely, the four spiritual paths, the four fruitions of those paths, and the ultimate state of liberation.
3. **Doubt regarding the Realized Community:** Doubting the existence of the eight classes of noble individuals (four stationed on the paths and four in the fruitions), or doubting whether offering the four material requisites to them yields spiritual fruit.
4. **Doubt regarding Moral Conduct:** Doubting whether observing minor and major ethical precepts yields genuine spiritual benefit.
5. **Doubt regarding the Past:** Doubting previous lives—whether one existed in the past or whether beings lived prior to this life.
6. **Doubt regarding the Future:** Doubting future lives—whether there is rebirth or continuation of existence after death.
7. **Doubt regarding Both Past and Future:** Doubting both past and future existences simultaneously.
8. **Doubt regarding the Principle of Dependent Origination (Causality):** Doubting the causal process of reality—such as whether conditioned formations truly arise dependent upon ignorance, and doubting the universal validity of cause and effect.

#### The Consequences, Intensification, and Remedy of Doubt
When doubt is present, it turns the mind away from wholesome action and inclines it solely toward sensory indulgence. Even if a wholesome action is performed with a doubting mind, that action remains weak and lacks spiritual potency.

This doubt intensifies when ordinary individuals attempt to critically dissect profound subjects that lie beyond ordinary human intellect—matters accessible only to beings of supreme wisdom—or when they listen to and read the vain criticisms of ordinary people regarding the afterlife. The remedy for this state is to understand the limitation of one's own intellect in contrast to supreme wisdom, thereby cultivating confidence and conviction.

---

### The Second State: Consciousness Conjoined with Restlessness

**Restlessness** is the unsteady, turbulent, and uncalm nature of the mind. In the cognitive process, when a thought arises focused on an object, the mental faculty of **one-pointedness** holds the mind upon that object until the moment of the thought's dissolution. If this focused stability is maintained without agitation, it leads to meditative concentration.

However, when restlessness arises due to delusion, the power of one-pointedness weakens and wavers. This destabilizes all other associated mental factors, preventing the proper apprehension of the object and leading the mind astray. Because calmness is necessary for wholesome action, restlessness blocks virtue and directs the mind toward the unwholesome. While restlessness is present in all unwholesome mental states, it becomes prominent and dominant when delusion is the primary root.

#### Practical Occurrences and Karmic Potency
In human experience, this restless consciousness arises frequently during sudden emergencies, when attempting to perform multiple tasks at the same time, or when acting with hasty, frantic speed.

Delusion-rooted states do not produce results as severe as states rooted in greed or aversion. Because the restless state of consciousness is exceptionally weak in its karmic potency, it lacks the power on its own to generate rebirth in realms of extreme suffering upon death (a capacity that the other eleven unwholesome states possess).

---

### Classification of the Twelve Unwholesome States of Consciousness

Within this framework, the twelve unwholesome states of consciousness are classified as follows:

*   **By Underlying Root:** 8 rooted in greed/attachment, 2 rooted in aversion/ill-will, and 2 rooted in delusion.
*   **By Affective Feeling:** 4 accompanied by joy (pleasure), 6 accompanied by neutral feeling (4 rooted in greed + 2 rooted in delusion), and 2 accompanied by displeasure (mental pain/aversion).
*   **By Mode of Arising:** 7 unprompted/spontaneous states (4 rooted in greed + 1 rooted in aversion + 2 rooted in delusion), and 5 prompted/induced states (4 rooted in greed + 1 rooted in aversion).
