### 1. The Nature of Elevated Fine-Material Consciousness
In ordinary life, the untrained mind is uncollected, agitated, and incessantly swept along by various sensory objects, arising and perishing with extreme rapidity, thereby generating great harm and suffering. However, when an individual recognizes the perils of sensual attachment and seeks release by cultivating meditation to steady the mind, an elevated state of consciousness arises that is far superior, stronger, calmer, and purer than ordinary sensory consciousness. Because these states of mind prevail predominantly in the refined, fine-material spheres of existence, they are termed **fine-material consciousness** or **consciousness of meditative absorption**.

This category of consciousness is systematically divided into fifteen states: five wholesome states, five resultant states, and five functional states. All fifteen are mundane (operating within the conditioned world), accompanied by reflective knowledge, intrinsically refined, and prompted by deliberate cultivation. Regarding their affective quality, the twelve states comprising the first four levels of absorption across the wholesome, resultant, and functional categories are accompanied by joyful mental pleasure, whereas the three states of the fifth level are accompanied by neutral equanimity.

### 2. The Definition of Absorption and the Four Modes of Overcoming Mental Impurities
The term *absorption* is understood through two core meanings:
1. **Direct, Close Contemplation of the Object:** Just as a person wishing to thoroughly inspect an object brings it close, excludes distractions, and closely observes its form and color for a long duration, in meditation one brings consciousness near to an object (such as an earth disk) and fixes it stably and firmly with mindfulness and clear comprehension, holding the object as if embracing it.
2. **The Burning of Opposing Forces:** It is termed absorption because it burns up and suppresses the mental hindrances that obstruct moral wholesomeness, meditative concentration, and the realization of the noble paths and fruits.

The abandonment or overcoming of mental impurities occurs through four distinct processes:
* **Temporary Overcoming:** The momentary suppression of impurities by an ordinary wholesome thought, comparable to lighting a lamp in a dark night where the darkness is dispelled only as long as the lamp burns, returning immediately once the flame is extinguished.
* **Overcoming by Sustained Suppression:** The long-duration suppression of impurities achieved through meditative absorption. This is comparable to parting the thick waterweeds covering a pond with a vessel or a stick; as long as the absorption is maintained, the impurities remain pushed aside and cannot manifest. If the absorption is lost, the impurities cover the mind again, just as the waterweeds return.
* **Overcoming by Root Eradication:** The permanent, irreversible destruction of impurities by the transcendent path consciousness, comparable to a tree being struck by lightning and destroyed completely along with its roots so that it never grows again.
* **Overcoming by Total Stilling:** The complete pacification of the eradicated impurities through the transcendent fruition consciousness, comparable to pouring several pots of water over the hot embers of a burnt pile of rubbish to cool its residual heat entirely.

Fine-material absorptive consciousness suppresses mental hindrances specifically through the second mechanism: **sustained suppression**.

### 3. The Five Hindrances and Their Counteraction by the Five Absorptive Factors
During the attainment of absorption, five distinct mental factors arise to directly oppose and suppress the five mental hindrances that corrupt ordinary consciousness:

1. **Sensual Desire versus Mental One-Pointedness:** Strong longing and craving for sensual objects binds beings to sensory existence and opposes concentration. When one withdraws into solitude with a suitable meditation subject, the factor of **mental one-pointedness** develops into firm concentration, suppressing sensual desire.
2. **Ill-Will versus Joyful Delight:** Intense anger, resentment, and hatred defile the mind. When consciousness is filled with the **joyful delight** born of meditation, anger finds no room to arise, and ill-will is suppressed through sustained suspension.
3. **Sluggishness-and-Torpor versus Initial Mental Application:** Sluggishness and mental dullness render the mind too weak and limp to engage a wholesome object. Just as an exhausted, collapsed man unable to carry his load is raised and energized by a strong man, the factor of **initial mental application** vigorously mounts and directs the mind onto the object, burning away sluggishness and torpor.
4. **Restlessness-and-Remorse versus Mental Ease:** The state of mental agitation and guilt-driven remorse is a form of mental distress. The subtle, exalted mental pleasure and well-being (**mental ease**) arising from steady focus replaces mental pain, thereby suppressing restlessness and remorse.
5. **Skeptical Doubt versus Sustained Examination:** Uncertainty regarding the teacher, the teaching, and the training obstructs wholesomeness. The factor of **sustained examination**, which continuously guides, moves, and rubs the mind on the contemplative object, allows thorough inspection, thereby entirely dispelling skeptical doubt.

### 4. The Progressive Fivefold Stages of Absorption and the Five Masteries
Through progressive practice, the practitioner ascends through five successive levels of absorption by shedding coarser mental factors:

* **The First Absorption (Five Factors):** Possesses initial application, sustained examination, joyful delight, mental ease, and mental one-pointedness. The practitioner masters this state through **five masteries**: the mastery of adverting to the absorptive factors at will, the mastery of entering the state instantly, the mastery of sustaining it for a predetermined duration, the mastery of emerging from it precisely at the intended moment, and the mastery of retroactively reviewing its constituent factors.
* **The Second Absorption (Four Factors):** Having mastered the first stage, sluggishness no longer threatens the mind. The practitioner perceives the lifting action of initial application as gross and unnecessary, discards it, and attains the second absorption consisting of *sustained examination, joyful delight, mental ease, and one-pointedness*, cultivating it through the five masteries.
* **The Third Absorption (Three Factors):** Recognizing that doubt has been eliminated and viewing sustained examination as gross, the practitioner discards it, attaining the third absorption consisting of *joyful delight, mental ease, and one-pointedness*.
* **The Fourth Absorption (Two Factors):** Viewing joyful delight as an agitating, gross factor, the practitioner abandons it, attaining the fourth absorption consisting solely of *mental ease and one-pointedness*.
* **The Fifth Absorption (Two Factors):** Recognizing that even mental ease is gross compared to pure peace, the practitioner discards both pleasure and pain, having already eliminated sorrow and mental excitement, and attains the fifth absorption consisting of *neutral equanimity and one-pointedness*.

### 5. Practical Progression and the Three Contemplative Signs
Taking an object such as an earth disk, the practitioner assumes a seated posture and meditates with continuous mental repetition on the element:
1. **The Preparatory Sign:** The external, physical concentration disk placed before the physical eyes.
2. **The Acquired Sign:** The mental image of the object that becomes so deeply impressed on the mind that it can be seen clearly even with the eyes closed. At this stage, the external disk is set aside, and the practitioner meditates purely on this internal mental impression.
3. **The Counterpart Sign:** Through continued meditation on the acquired sign, it breaks through into an exceptionally pure, radiant mental light (the counterpart sign). It first appears with the faint glow of a firefly and can be systematically expanded:
   * *Limited Expansion:* Expanded to the breadth of one or two inches.
   * *Medium Expansion:* Expanded to roughly twelve inches.
   * *Immeasurable Expansion:* Expanded outward boundlessly in all directions.

Fixing the mind upon this counterpart sign gives rise to the cognitive process of fine-material absorption.

### 6. Prerequisite Qualifications and Fatal Obstacles to Attainment
The attainment of absorption requires specific causal endowments and freedom from fatal obstacles:

* **Constitutional Rebirth Requirement:** The individual must possess a **triple-rooted rebirth consciousness** (a birth consciousness accompanied by native wisdom). 
  * *A Crucial Clarification:* Individuals born with double-rooted or rootless consciousness (lacking innate wisdom) cannot attain absorption in their present lifetime, even if they observe ethical purity and meditate under a master throughout their entire lives. If such persons do not understand this limitation, their failure to attain absorption may cause them to become disheartened, mentally deranged, or to adopt the destructive wrong view that the spiritual life is entirely futile, which leads to rebirth in states of misery. However, if they practice with the intention of securing future attainment, their effort is not wasted; they can obtain a triple-rooted rebirth in their second or third lifetime and quickly achieve absorption.
* **Freedom from Obstacles:**
  1. *Karmic Obstacle:* Having committed grave actions (such as killing a mother or father) irrevocably blocks absorption, even for a triple-rooted individual.
  2. *Defilement Obstacle:* Being bound by entrenched wrong views prevents attainment.
  3. *Resultant Obstacle:* Being born with a rootless or double-rooted constitution.
  4. *Disciplinary Obstacle:* An ordained monastic who commits a defeating transgression but remains in the order pretending to be pure cannot attain absorption (though if such a person disrobes and practices purely under lay precepts, attainment becomes possible).
  5. *Attachment to Wealth:* Intense greed and possessiveness toward material property blocks absorption.
* **Environmental Suitability:** Unsuitable locations—such as large, newly built, or dilapidated residences; places near highways, rocks, ponds, flowers, or fruit trees; crowded places; places near cities, ports, borders, or farmland; places with incompatible figures, persons, or non-human beings; or places devoid of good companions—must be avoided. The practitioner requires a suitable location that is neither too far nor too close, easy of access, quiet, and supported by wise spiritual companions.

### 7. Resultant and Functional Absorptive Consciousness
* **Resultant Fine-Material Consciousness (Five States):** When an individual attains a wholesome absorption and passes away without losing it, that consciousness directly causes rebirth in the corresponding fine-material realm of higher beings. In that realm, the rebirth-linking consciousness, the passive life-continuum, and the final death-consciousness all occur as this corresponding resultant absorption.
* **Functional Fine-Material Consciousness (Five States):** Fully liberated beings (who have eradicated all roots of future existence) also generate these five absorptions. In them, these states carry no karmic force to produce future results and are termed *functional*, experienced either for blissful dwelling in this life, for the attainment of total cessation, or as a basis for extraordinary direct knowledges.

### 8. The Five Extraordinary Direct Knowledges Born of the Fifth Absorption
Upon thoroughly mastering the fifth absorption through all masteries, a practitioner can generate five extraordinary direct knowledges:
1. **The Knowledge of Psychic Transformations:** The capacity to multiply forms, dive into the earth, or move through space. (This is cultivated by mastering eight material and color devices through fourteen complex methods across all eight meditative attainments, culminating in the fifth absorption and focused resolve).
2. **The Knowledge of Discerning Other Minds:** The power to directly know the mental states and thoughts of other beings (discerned through elevated vision by observing the physical color and condition of the heart-base).
3. **The Knowledge of Recollecting Past Lives:** The cognitive ability to trace back sequentially along one's own continuum of consciousness to recall past births, death-moments, and prior life circumstances.
4. **The Transcendent Eye:** The capacity to see distant and subtle forms far beyond normal physical sight, and to observe beings passing away and being reborn according to their moral actions.
5. **The Transcendent Ear:** The capacity to hear subtle and distant sounds across great distances.

**The Six Classes of Individuals and the Radiance of Their Knowledge:**
These direct knowledges can be attained by six types of individuals: (1) non-liberated ascetics (operating through the wholesome fifth absorption), (2) standard liberated disciples, (3) the eighty great liberated disciples, (4) the two chief disciples, (5) solitary non-teaching awakened beings, and (6) the supreme fully enlightened teacher (the latter five operating through the functional fifth absorption).

The clarity and illuminating power of their direct knowledge differs as follows:
* The knowledge of the non-liberated ascetic is like the **faint light of a firefly**.
* The knowledge of a standard liberated disciple is like the **light of an oil lamp**.
* The knowledge of the eighty great disciples is like the **flame of a torch**.
* The knowledge of the chief disciples is like the **light of the morning star**.
* The knowledge of a solitary awakened being is like the **light of the moon**.
* The knowledge of the supreme fully enlightened teacher is like the **boundless light of the sun**.
