### The Structure of Sensory-Bound Consciousness

Within the domain of sensory existence, consciousness operates primarily in relation to sensory objects and desires. This sphere of mind comprises fifty-four distinct states: twelve unwholesome states, eighteen rootless states (states devoid of underlying moral roots), and twenty-four wholesome and functional states (consisting of eight wholesome states, eight resultant states, and eight functional states). Together, the twelve unwholesome states and the eighteen rootless states constitute the non-elevated dimension of consciousness.

---

### The Nature of Unwholesome Consciousness and the Primacy of Delusion

An unwholesome state of consciousness is defined as any mental state that, upon arising within an individual’s continuous stream of experience, inevitably produces adverse, undesirable consequences in the future. These unwholesome states are generated through three fundamental negative roots: **attachment**, **aversion**, and **delusion**.

Among these, **delusion is the primary cause and ground of all unwholesome action**. Delusion acts like an inner darkness that obscures the true nature of the object of perception. A mind clouded by delusion cannot distinguish truth from falsehood, reality from unreality, or right from wrong, and it fails to recognize an action as a fault, an evil, or a detriment. Because an unwholesome mental state cannot form without this failure of understanding, the root of delusion is inherently present in every one of the twelve unwholesome states.

---

### How the Three Negative Roots Differentiate Consciousness

When a mind veiled by delusion encounters various objects of experience, its reaction produces three main classes of unwholesome consciousness:

1. **States Rooted in Attachment (Eight States):** When a deluded mind encounters an agreeable, pleasant, or attractive object—such as an appealing visual form or sound—craving, fondness, and longing arise toward it. Although delusion is present, attachment is the prominent operating factor. Furthermore, when a living being begins a new life, the initial transitional consciousness is followed by approximately fifteen or sixteen passive subconscious moments; then, an orientation of attention occurs, immediately followed by the mind's very first active cognitive series: seven consecutive moments of attachment-rooted consciousness that cling directly to that newly obtained existence.
2. **States Rooted in Aversion (Two States):** When a deluded mind encounters an disagreeable, unpleasant, or unwanted object, it reacts with anger, hostility, and resistance. In these states, aversion operates as the dominant factor alongside delusion.
3. **States Rooted Exclusively in Delusion (Two States):** When delusion is so overwhelming that the mind cannot even clearly determine or ascertain the object, neither clear attachment nor clear aversion can develop. Instead, the mind remains trapped in unsteadiness, confusion, and indecision, driven solely by the force of delusion.

---

### The Systematic Division of the Eight Attachment-Rooted States

The eight states of consciousness rooted in attachment are classified by three distinct factors: **affective tone (feeling)**, **the presence of distorted views (belief)**, and **the presence of volitional prompting (initiative)**:

* **Affective Tone:** Every object of experience carries a pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral quality. Experiencing the pleasant quality of an object produces *pleasure (joy)*, while experiencing its neutral quality produces *indifference*. (Unpleasant feeling or mental distress never coexists with attachment). Consequently, attachment-rooted states are divided into those *accompanied by pleasure* and those *accompanied by indifference*.
* **Cognitive Belief:** Attachment may arise coupled with a distorted philosophical view—such as the conviction that moral cause and effect do not exist, that there is no afterlife, or that actions have no ethical consequences. Alternatively, attachment may arise simply as desire without being linked to any such theoretical distortion. Thus, states are divided into those *associated with distorted views* and those *dissociated from distorted views*.
* **Volitional Initiative:** A state of attachment may arise spontaneously and naturally from the mind's own habit (*unprompted*), or it may be induced through external instigation or deliberate personal effort (*prompted*).

Combining these three factors produces the eight specific states of attachment-rooted consciousness:
1. Pleasurable, associated with distorted views, unprompted
2. Pleasurable, associated with distorted views, prompted
3. Pleasurable, dissociated from distorted views, unprompted
4. Pleasurable, dissociated from distorted views, prompted
5. Indifferent, associated with distorted views, unprompted
6. Indifferent, associated with distorted views, prompted
7. Indifferent, dissociated from distorted views, unprompted
8. Indifferent, dissociated from distorted views, prompted

### Manifestation in Action, Human Misconception, and Inevitable Consequences

These twelve unwholesome states of consciousness give rise to the ten courses of harmful action: the destruction of life, theft, sexual misconduct, falsehood, divisive speech, harsh speech, frivolous chatter, covetousness (longing for another's possessions), malice (wishing destruction upon others), and distorted views. When these actions are fully consummated as complete moral courses, they generate forces that determine both the realm of future rebirth and the adverse experiences encountered throughout life. When an action is incomplete, it produces adverse experiences only within the course of a lifetime.

In human life, even individuals considered highly educated, accomplished, or scientifically advanced frequently commit severe unwholesome acts due to delusion. Seeking physical comfort and pleasure, they kill animals, destroy eggs for food, engage in fishing and hunting for sport, consume intoxicants, gamble, and manufacture weapons capable of annihilating entire nations in an instant. Others adopt distorted dogmas such as divine creationism or an eternal heaven, completely denying the reality of moral actions and their fruits.

According to the laws of moral cause and effect, individuals who commit such actions out of delusion and ignorance inevitably experience profound suffering in the realms of misery after death. Even those who intellectually understand ethical teachings sometimes commit wrongdoing because they lack the deeply cultivated habits of wholesome character developed across past existences. Therefore, to overcome the blinding influence of delusion, avoid wrongdoing, and live in accordance with truth, the continuous cultivation of wholesome moral dispositions is essential.
