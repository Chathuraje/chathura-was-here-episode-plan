The teachings are structured into three main divisions: the discipline, the discourses, and the analytical higher doctrine. The first two divisions consist primarily of *conventional discourse*, which uses everyday language and concepts—such as sentient beings, individuals, men, women, trees, rocks, mountains, and rivers—without violating ordinary worldly communication. In contrast, the higher doctrine consists of *ultimate discourse*, which departs entirely from conventional usage to describe reality in terms of its ultimate constituents, such as fundamental aggregates, basic elements, and sensory bases.

To understand this distinction properly, one must grasp the difference between a conceptual construct (convention) and an ultimate reality.

---

### Meaning, Conceptual Constructs, and Ultimate Reality

Whatever is perceived, apprehended, or conceived by the mind—through hearing spoken words, seeing written or printed forms, or engaging in thought—is termed an "object of meaning." In this sense, "meaning" encompasses everything that can be cognized, and its varieties are countless. However, not everything apprehended by the mind exists in actual reality. Among the things perceived, some truly exist, while others do not. Most things taken as real by ordinary worldly perception do not exist in an ultimate sense.

#### Conceptual Constructs (Conventional Reality)
A *conceptual construct* is something that does not exist in ultimate truth, but is treated as real merely through a mental notion or label. Inanimate objects (such as tables, chairs, beds, houses, garments, and plants) and living beings (such as humans, deities, cattle, and elephants) are accepted as real solely through this conceptual attribution.

* **The Analogy of the Whirling Torch:** When a person rapidly whirls a burning torch in the dark, an observer at a distance sees a continuous "circle of fire." In reality, there is no circle of fire; there is only a single point of fire moving rapidly. The "circle" exists only as an appearance produced by rapid movement.
* **The Analogy of the Table:** An object is conventionally regarded as a "table." When analyzed, it consists of four legs, four joining planks, and several surface planks. None of these individual pieces is the table, nor is there a table separate from these pieces. If the pieces are dismantled or arranged differently, no table is found. The "table" is therefore not an independently existing entity, but a mental notion formed in response to a particular arrangement of parts.

#### Ultimate Reality (Irreducible Phenomena)
An *ultimate reality* is that which remains when an object is analyzed and broken down to the point where it can no longer be divided. It is the fundamental, irreducible truth. Ultimate realities are genuine phenomena that possess their own intrinsic nature, which never deviates from its inherent characteristic into something else.

If the wooden parts of a table are sanded down with abrasive paper, they become fine wood dust. Even a microscopic particle of wood dust, invisible to the naked eye, can still be divided further. Ultimate reality is the final, indivisible phenomenon reached at the end of such division.

The same analysis applies to a human being. Conventionally, a human is described as having two eyes to see forms, two ears to hear sounds, a nose to smell, a tongue to taste, a body to feel tangible sensations, a head and limbs, and a mind that knows things, walks, talks, and lives. However, when these parts and functions are examined separately, no independent entity called a "human" exists. Re-examining them reveals only microscopic physical elements and distinct mental activities. Thus, what is conventionally called a "human" is simply an assembly of physical and mental phenomena.

---

### The Four Ultimate Realities

In ultimate analysis, all existing realities are classified into four categories:

1. **Consciousness:** The fundamental act of being aware of or cognizing an object.
2. **Mental Factors:** The specific mental qualities and functions that arise alongside consciousness and shape its state.
3. **Physical Phenomena:** The fundamental material and energetic elements, whether visible or invisible.
4. **The Unconditioned State (Liberation):** The ultimate reality realized through the complete cessation and eradication of all mental defilements and suffering.

---

### The Practical Purpose of the Distinction

In ordinary life, people mistake conceptual constructs for ultimate truths, falsely believing in enduring persons and solid, permanent entities. This misunderstanding binds them to the cycle of existence and its attendant suffering.

When an individual undertakes analytical insight, they recognize that both within themselves and in the external world, there are no enduring persons, entities, or objects, but merely a continuous aggregation of physical and mental phenomena. 

By closely observing these physical and mental phenomena in terms of their three inherent characteristics—that they are impermanent, unsatisfactory, and devoid of an enduring self or essence—the practitioner systematically eradicates mental defilements. This analytical realization culminates in transcendent knowledge and liberation, permanently freeing the individual from cyclic existence and suffering. Understanding the distinction between conventional concepts and ultimate realities is therefore necessary for realizing the truth and achieving liberation.
