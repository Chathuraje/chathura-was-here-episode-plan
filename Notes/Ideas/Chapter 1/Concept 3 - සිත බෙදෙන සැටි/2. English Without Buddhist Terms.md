### 1. The Nature of Consciousness and the Common Misconception

Consciousness is the capacity or power to cognize an object. A living being is fundamentally a physical body endowed with consciousness. If consciousness departs from the body, that form is reduced to inanimate matter—a lifeless corpse—possessing no cognitive capability, just as inanimate objects like rocks, trees, and mountains lack consciousness.

A common misunderstanding in human experience is the belief that an individual possesses a single, unbroken mind or soul that endures continuously from birth to death. In reality, there is no singular, permanent entity; there is only an ongoing stream of consciousness composed of discrete, momentary mental events. Because each state of consciousness arises and dissolves with extreme rapidity, and because the succeeding state arises immediately without any gap or temporal interval, it produces the experiential illusion of a single, continuous consciousness. Within this process:
* Only one discrete state of consciousness arises at a time (two states never arise simultaneously).
* A single state of consciousness cognizes only one object at a time.
* A single state of consciousness apprehends only one specific aspect or mode of that object at a time (never two modes at once).

### 2. Conditions for the Arising of Consciousness and the Process of Continuity

A state of consciousness does not arise causelessly. Its arising requires two essential conditions:
1. **A Preceding Stream of Consciousness:** Just as a mango tree arises only from a preceding causal series of mango seeds and trees rather than from nothing, a state of consciousness arises only from a preceding stream of consciousness. Attempting to locate a first beginning of this continuum through an external creator is futile; the causal stream has operated across beginningless time.
2. **An Object of Awareness:** Just as an unsteady person requires a walking stick to stand, consciousness cannot arise in isolation; it must have an object to engage—such as a visible form, sound, odor, taste, tangible physical sensation, or a purely mental concept.

#### Continuity Across Lifetimes: Terminal, Transitional, and Life-Continuum States
The continuum of conscious existence across lifetimes is maintained through three functional states of consciousness:
* **Terminal Consciousness:** The final momentary state of consciousness that occurs at the moment of death in a given lifetime.
* **Transitional Consciousness:** The first momentary state of consciousness that arises in the subsequent lifetime, emerging instantly and without any temporal gap after the terminal consciousness, thereby connecting the past life to the new one.
* **The Life-Continuum Consciousness:** The passive stream of consciousness that flows during intervals when active cognitive processes are not occurring, maintaining the unbroken continuity of that individual life from its transitional beginning to its terminal end.

#### The Dying Process and the Transmission of the Object
Shortly before death, an individual undergoes a terminal cognitive process. In this process, the mind apprehends one of three possible objects:
1. **Past Volitional Actions:** Significant ethical or unethical deeds performed during life.
2. **Action-Related Signs:** Objects, instruments, or circumstances associated with those past deeds.
3. **Destinational Signs:** Perceptual signs or visions of the impending place of rebirth.

During this terminal process, five dynamic, active conscious moments experience this object, followed shortly by the terminal consciousness of that life. Immediately upon its cessation, the transitional consciousness of the next life arises, taking as its foundational object that exact same impression apprehended during the previous life’s dying process. The passive life-continuum that flows throughout that entire subsequent lifetime—as well as its eventual terminal consciousness—continues to hold that very same object derived from the previous existence, rather than an object acquired in the present life.

#### Sensory Doors and the Arising of Active Cognitive Processes
When a stimulus enters through one of the six sensory doors (the faculties of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, physical body, or the mental faculty), the passive life-continuum vibrates and subsides. An active cognitive process—a specific sequence of conscious moments—then arises to cognize and experience that new stimulus. Once this active sequence concludes, consciousness lapses back into the passive life-continuum.

This stream of consciousness continues without interruption, except in two temporary circumstances: when it is suspended during profound states of meditative cessation, and during existence in non-conscious planes of being. It ceases permanently and entirely only upon the final passing of a fully liberated being.

### 3. The Duration of Consciousness, Physical Bases, and the Misconception Regarding the Brain

#### The Lifespan of a Thought-Moment
The lifespan of a single state of consciousness is extraordinarily brief; within the span of a single finger-snap, millions of conscious moments arise and perish. Each individual moment of consciousness undergoes three sequential phases:
1. **Origination (Arising)**
2. **Static Persistence (Presence)**
3. **Dissolution (Cessation)**

By comparison, a single unit of physical matter endures for the duration of seventeen complete conscious moments (or fifty-one sub-phases). Therefore, physical matter is much coarser and more enduring than consciousness.

#### The Physical Bases of Consciousness
Embodied consciousness operates dependent upon six physical bases: the five sensitive physical structures of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body, and the internal physical heart-base. Basic sensory awareness (such as seeing or hearing) relies directly on the respective physical sense organ, whereas all other mental states and cognitive processes rely upon the heart-base.

#### Analysis of the Brain's Role
It is commonly assumed that consciousness resides in and is generated by the physical brain. A developmental examination clarifies this relationship:
* At the moment of conception (the arising of transitional consciousness), only three microscopic physical clusters arise through past volitional force: the bodily cluster, the sex-determination cluster, and the physical base cluster (comprising thirty physical qualities in total).
* The physical sense bases take approximately eleven weeks to form, and the brain takes approximately fourteen weeks to develop and become functional.
* The continuous stream of consciousness that operated during these initial weeks could not have resided in a brain that did not yet exist; rather, it functioned by relying on the physical heart-base that arose at the moment of conception.
* The brain is a physical regulatory organ necessary for sustaining the biological body—governing respiration, digestion, body temperature, organ function, and the transmission of physical sensations. When the brain or the body is damaged, the physical foundation is disturbed, causing the dependent mental stream to become weak or disordered. Thus, the brain is required for sustaining the physical body, while the fundamental physical base of the conscious stream remains the heart-base.

### 4. The Structural Classification of Consciousness

Consciousness possesses vast operational power: it can cognize past, present, future, and timeless objects; through concentrated absorption and insight, it can develop extrasensory capacities (such as perceiving other minds or past existences); and it is the primary determinant that leads an individual to states of suffering, happiness, or ultimate liberation.

While consciousness is uniform in its defining characteristic—the act of cognizing an object—it is classified into **89 fundamental states** (or **121 expanded states**) across eight structural criteria:

1. **By Existential Plane or Sphere (4 Categories):**
   * **Sensory-Sphere Consciousness (54 states):** States that predominantly occur within the eleven sensory realms (the four realms of suffering, the human realm, and the six sensory heavenly realms), primarily engaging sensory stimuli.
   * **Fine-Material Sphere Consciousness (15 states):** States of refined meditative absorption corresponding to the subtle material planes.
   * **Immaterial Sphere Consciousness (12 states):** Formless states of meditative absorption corresponding to purely non-physical planes.
   * **Transcendent Consciousness (8 basic or 40 expanded states):** Supramundane states that transcend the three worldly spheres, directly taking the unconditioned state of liberation as their object (divided into paths of direct realization and their corresponding fruits).
   *(Note: The term "transcendent sphere" does not denote a physical place, but states that transcend the conditioned world. Although higher absorptive states can occasionally arise in lower realms—such as absorption within a human being—these states are named after the planes where they predominantly manifest).*

2. **By Functional Class or Nature (4 Categories):**
   * **Constructive (Wholesome):** Ethically sound states that eradicate harmful tendencies and generate pleasant, beneficial future results.
   * **Destructive (Unwholesome):** Ethically harmful states that produce painful, disadvantageous results.
   * **Resultant:** Passive states that represent the experiential ripening or fruit of past constructive or destructive actions (analogous to the gradual maturing and ripening of fruit from a planted tree).
   * **Functional (Inoperative):** Purely operational states that perform cognitive tasks without generating future volitional consequences (predominantly experienced by liberated beings, with ordinary individuals experiencing only two basic types).

3. **By Affective Tone or Feeling (5 Categories):**
   * **Bodily Pleasure:** Physically agreeable bodily sensation (occurring exclusively as a constructive resultant).
   * **Bodily Pain:** Physically disagreeable bodily sensation (occurring exclusively as a destructive resultant).
   * **Mental Joy:** Gladness and pleasant mental feeling (occurring in both constructive and certain destructive states).
   * **Mental Grief:** Mental displeasure, resentment, and distress (occurring exclusively in destructive states).
   * **Neutrality (Equanimity):** Affective balance, being neither pleasant nor unpleasant (occurring in both constructive and destructive states).

4. **By Association (2 Categories):**
   * **Associated:** States of consciousness that operate in direct combination with specific mental factors, such as distorted views, wisdom, or aversion.
   * **Dissociated:** States of consciousness that function without those specific factors.

5. **By Volitional Prompting (2 Categories):**
   * **Unprompted (Spontaneous):** States that arise naturally, swiftly, and with immediate energy, without internal hesitation or external urging, when the mind and body are alert and agile.
   * **Prompted (Induced):** States that arise through conscious self-inducement, external encouragement, or when the mind and body are fatigued and sluggish.

6. **By Mundane vs. Transcendent Scope (2 Categories):**
   * **Mundane:** All 81 states belonging to the sensory, fine-material, and immaterial spheres that maintain existence within the cyclical continuum.
   * **Transcendent:** The 8 (or 40) states that break free from cyclical existence and take unconditioned liberation as their direct object.

7. **By Foundational Roots (2 Categories):**
   * **Rooted:** States that are firmly grounded and stabilized in their object by the presence of one or more of the six primary psychological roots—the three destructive roots (attachment, aversion, delusion) or the three constructive roots (non-attachment, non-aversion, non-delusion)—just as a tree is anchored to the earth by its roots.
   * **Rootless:** Cognitive states that operate without the presence of these six foundational roots.

8. **By Qualitative Nobility (2 Categories):**
   * **Noble (Qualitative):** States of consciousness accompanied by wholesome, morally sound, and elevated mental factors.
   * **Non-Noble:** States of consciousness that lack these wholesome, elevated mental factors.

### Summary

In this philosophical framework, consciousness is not an indivisible, permanent soul, but a rapid, causal stream of momentary cognitive events that animates physical matter. This continuum flows across lifetimes via the sequential links of terminal, transitional, and life-continuum states, depending on physical bases—specifically the heart-base—rather than originating in the brain. While uniform in its core definition of cognizing an object, consciousness diversifies into distinct qualitative states based on its existential sphere, ethical nature, affective feeling, and underlying mental components.
