### 1. The Relationship Between Transcendent Consciousness and Absorptive States

In comprehensive mental analysis, although transcendent consciousness is presented in the concise method as eight states (four transformative path states and four resultant fruition states), these states expand to forty in the detailed method according to their conjunction with the factors of deep mental absorption. Because the five progressive levels of absorption (first, second, third, fourth, and fifth) occur not only in elevated form-sphere consciousness but also within transcendent consciousness, the designation "absorptive states of consciousness" directly applies to transcendent states as well.

Within direct contemplative experience, this process occurs in two distinct ways:

*   **The Experience of Those Without Prior Absorptive Attainment:** When an individual who has not previously developed form-sphere absorptions develops direct analytical insight into reality and realizes the transformative path and fruition states, those transcendent states always and invariably arise endowed with the constituent factors of the *first level of mental absorption*.
*   **The Experience of Those With Prior Absorptive Attainment:** When a contemplative first attains a specific level of absorption, emerges from it, and directly investigates the constituent factors of that absorption through analytical insight to attain the path and fruition states, those transcendent states arise endowed with the specific structural factors of that corresponding absorption (for instance, a breakthrough based on the second absorption arises as a second-absorption transcendent state, while one based on the fifth absorption arises as a fifth-absorption transcendent state).

### 2. The Structural Derivation of the Forty Transcendent States

In the concise method, the initial stage of liberating breakthrough (the first path consciousness) is counted as a single state. Across the five levels of absorption, it branches into five distinct states:
1. Initial path consciousness functioning with first-level absorption factors
2. Initial path consciousness functioning with second-level absorption factors
3. Initial path consciousness functioning with third-level absorption factors
4. Initial path consciousness functioning with fourth-level absorption factors
5. Initial path consciousness functioning with fifth-level absorption factors

In the exact same manner, the remaining three progressive path states (the second, third, and fourth path stages of realization) each divide across the five absorption levels. This produces **twenty transcendent wholesome (path) states of consciousness** ($4 \times 5$).

Likewise, the four corresponding resultant fruition states (the realization states of the first, second, third, and fourth stages) each divide across the five absorption levels, producing **twenty transcendent resultant (fruition) states of consciousness** ($4 \times 5$).

Combining these twenty transformative path states with the twenty resultant fruition states yields **forty transcendent states of consciousness**. Systematically, these consist of eight first-absorption states, eight second-absorption states, eight third-absorption states, eight fourth-absorption states, and eight fifth-absorption states.

### 3. The Sixty-Seven Absorptive States and Their Affective Tones

The total number of absorptive states across all categories of consciousness is sixty-seven. This is formed by combining the twenty-seven elevated mundane states (fifteen form-sphere states and twelve formless-sphere states) with the forty transcendent absorptive states.

Across the five absorption levels, these sixty-seven states are distributed as follows:
*   First-absorption states = 11
*   Second-absorption states = 11
*   Third-absorption states = 11
*   Fourth-absorption states = 11
*   Fifth-absorption states = 23 (wherein the twelve formless-sphere states are classified under the fifth level of absorption)

**Classification by Affective (Feeling) Tone:**
The states belonging to the first, second, third, and fourth levels of absorption (including thirty-two transcendent states, totaling forty-four states) are accompanied by a **joyful or pleasant affective tone**. All twenty-three states belonging to the fifth level of absorption (including eight transcendent states) are accompanied entirely by an **equanimous or neutral affective tone**.

### 4. The Concise (89) and Elaborated (121) Systems of Consciousness

*   **The Concise System:** Formed by combining the eighty-one mundane states with the eight basic transcendent states, totaling **eighty-nine states of consciousness**.
*   **The Elaborated System:** Formed by combining the eighty-one mundane states with the forty differentiated transcendent absorptive states, totaling **one hundred and twenty-one states of consciousness**.

#### Systematic Divisions of the 121 States:
*   **By Plane or Domain:** 54 sensory-domain states, 15 form-sphere states, 12 formless-sphere states, and 40 transcendent states (Total: 121).
*   **By Functional Nature:** 12 unwholesome states, 30 wholesome (volitionally active) states, 52 resultant (experiential fruit) states, and 20 functional (non-karmic active) states (Total: 121).
*   **By Affective Tone:** 63 states accompanied by joyful/pleasant feeling, 55 states accompanied by equanimous/neutral feeling, and 3 states accompanied by aversive/distressful feeling (Total: 121).
*   **By Volitional Conditioning:** Elevated mundane and transcendent states are reckoned under the prompted (conditioned) category of volitional processing.

### 5. Source Comparison and Practical Clarification

The two source accounts complement each other directly. The first source focuses primarily on the lived practical process of the contemplative—distinguishing between those with and without prior absorptive mastery—and systematically enumerates how the forty transcendent absorptive states are generated. The second source expands upon this framework by classifying the resulting one hundred and twenty-one states across broad analytical categories (plane, functional nature, affective tone, and volitional conditioning).

This framework resolves a common misconception: the belief that individuals who have not attained elevated form-sphere absorptions cannot realize transcendent liberation, or that transcendent states exist entirely without absorptive factors. As the sources demonstrate, an individual attaining liberation through direct analytical insight naturally experiences transcendent consciousness endowed with the constituent factors of the first absorption, while those with prior absorptive mastery experience that liberation structured by the specific higher absorptive levels they have developed.
