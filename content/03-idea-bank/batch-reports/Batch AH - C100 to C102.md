## Held questions

1. **C101 backward recall of one's own day** (Pradeepika lines 130–132, PDF 370 / printed 360). Recalling the present posture, then entering the room, eating, washing on waking and the previous day, step by step backwards. This is a clear everyday memory practice, but in the source it is only the preliminary to past-life recollection, and the text gives no reason why it works. No card was made. Re-evaluate if a memory or recollection concept elsewhere (for example, mindfulness or recollection concepts) supports it independently.
2. **C100 canal-jump trembling** (Book 4 lines 44–46). "Unsteady on first landing because the object has not been repeated" is attractive, but it describes adjacent mind-moments, and the everyday reading overlaps C023-I02. Rejected, not held. Record it in case a coordinator prefers a card.
3. **C102 which moment precedes cessation.** Margaya says change-of-lineage (C102 file line 3; C101 file line 21), while Pradeepika says purification (C102 file line 87). This needs a specialist ruling but affects no card.
4. **C102 planes where cessation is possible.** Margaya line 9 says the sense and fine-material worlds. Pradeepika line 98 reports two differing opinions, and its own line 66 presupposes the fine-material planes. Unresolved.
5. **C100 dry-insight noble persons and fruition attainment.** Pradeepika line 200 infers that such a person must have developed absorption before the path, which sits uneasily with the definition at line 35. Unresolved.
6. **C100 fruition-attainment process.** Margaya lines 37–45 have no purification moment, while Pradeepika lines 188–196 and Book 4 line 224 include one. Unresolved.

## Content that must not be carried into ideas (flag for all downstream agents)

- **C101 Book 4 lines 58–70**: low birth, ugliness, poverty and little food attributed to past unwholesome action, and wealth and beauty to wholesome action. In everyday language this would blame the poor and disabled.
- **C101 Pradeepika lines 120–122**: reading another person's mind from the colour of the blood near the heart. This is a historical physiological account.
- **C101 Pradeepika line 63 and Book 4 line 134**: supernormal vision leading to fear and madness. It must not be used to frame mental illness.
- **C101 Book 4 line 102**: no action is more blameworthy than wrong view. **C101 Book 4 lines 84–88**: insulting a noble person blocks heaven and the path. Both are doctrinal.
- **C101 Pradeepika lines 140–152**: a ranking of how far other ascetics, disciples and Buddhas can recall. This is doctrinal and involves comparison with other traditions.
- **C100 Book 4 line 81 and lines 163–192**: the number of rebirths remaining for noble persons and where they attain final nibbāna.
- **C102 Pradeepika lines 50, 58–66 and 72–80**: protection of belongings by resolution, the seven-day limit without food, death being impossible during cessation, and bodily heat remaining. These are doctrinal claims, not physiology.
