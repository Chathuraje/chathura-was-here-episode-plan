## Held questions

- **The whole batch is karma-ripening doctrine.** Every card is framed as a limited analogy, and rebirth outcomes are excluded. A reviewer should confirm that C055-I01 does not imply that misfortune repays wrongdoing. Its sources use Aṅgulimāla's accidental injury and Ajatasattu's hell as the "limit" examples.
- **C056-I01.** The source story is about King Dutugemunu, a national historical figure. Should the project treat the reading of a merit book (පින් පොත) at a deathbed as a documented practice worth verifying? The card deliberately does not assert its current use, and C062-I01 already touches it.
- **C056-I03.** The source treats doubt after thirty years of fruitless practice as the practitioner's fault, with a crocodile rebirth as the result. The card keeps both readings open. Is this acceptable, or should it be held as too close to a devotional warning against doubt?
- **C057.** The four conditions for a gift ripening in this life (Pradeepika lines 31–37; Margaya line 14), namely intention pure before, during and after giving, may fit a concept on giving better. Should a later batch cross-reference them? C059-I01 already covers the before-and-after intention.
- **C057.** The fourth tetrad, action by place of ripening (විපාකස්ථාන), is only listed (C055 Pradeepika lines 50–55; C057 Margaya lines 32–36). No concept in this batch explains it. Coordinator: check whether another concept covers it.

## Source disagreements recorded (not errors)

- **C056, fixed wrong view.** Pradeepika line 37 says the result is certain. Margaya line 16 says the holder is freed if the view is abandoned before death.
- **C056, done-once action.** Margaya line 30 defines it as all residual action from this and past lives. Pradeepika line 113 defines it as done once and forgotten, able to give rebirth only with recollection support.
- **C057, present-life results.** Margaya line 12 says they are rootless only. Pradeepika line 39 says a qualified gift gives rooted wholesome results.
- **C057, later-life action.** Pradeepika lines 101–113 say it needs present-life action's support to ripen. Margaya does not say this.
- **C055, destructive action.** Margaya line 26 centres it on ending life early. Pradeepika line 107 centres it on displacing another action's result. On wholesome obstructive and destructive action, Margaya lines 24 and 26 call it rare, while Pradeepika lines 113–115 gives several examples.

## Possible overlaps

All three involve returning to a past act. The distinct point of C056-I02 is that recollection works like repetition and applies equally to cherished good deeds. A reviewer could merge its remorse half into C022-I01. Listed as related.

All concern the deathbed. C056-I01's mechanism is scale versus personal meaning in what is recalled. The reminding-friend element overlaps C062-I01 and was deliberately kept minor. Listed as related.
- **C055-I01** (*Trying to outweigh past harm with later good*) with **C059-I01** (*How later regret changes a past kindness*), the mirror case, and **C022-I01**. Related, not merged.
- **C056-I03** with **C016-I01** (*Mistaking a striking experience for arrival*). These are opposite errors on a long path. Related, not merged.
