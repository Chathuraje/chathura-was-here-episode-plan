# C059-I01 — How later regret changes a past kindness

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A generous act is not only the moment of giving. How a person prepared it, and how they look back on it afterwards, can change what the act means, including when they later think "that was foolish".

## Human mechanism and tension

The source divides giving into three intentions: while gathering what will be given, while letting it go, and when remembering it later. When the before and after are clouded, the act is weaker. One source gives concrete examples. A good deed is lessened if it is done to appear superior or to put others down, if resentful or grasping thoughts crowd in just before or after, or if the person later decides that giving was a stupid thing to do. A deed that is free of these, and that the person keeps recalling with gladness, is called superior. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that memory keeps working on an act after it is done. A person can come to resent their own generosity, or value it more over time. The tension is between an act fixed in the past and the changing way a person lives with it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who once gave something significant, such as a loan, land, labour, a donation to a temple or years of care, and who describe how their feelings about that gift have shifted since. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Can the way we remember a gift change what that gift was?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's claim concerns the strength of karmic result, not psychological wellbeing, and it does not say regret undoes the benefit to the recipient. Regret may be a reasonable response to being exploited or to real hardship, not a moral failing. Changing feelings about a past gift may reflect changed circumstances or relationships rather than a change in the original intention.
