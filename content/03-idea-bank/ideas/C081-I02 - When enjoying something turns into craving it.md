# C081-I02 — When enjoying something turns into craving it

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Feeling pleasure and craving more of it are two different things. There is a point where the first can turn into the second, and dwelling on the enjoyment tends to strengthen that turn.

## Human mechanism and tension

Both sources mark a "juncture" between feeling and craving. Feeling is a result that simply arrives; craving starts new causes. A related passage says feeling supports craving only as a background condition, not bound up with it, so a feeling need not become craving. The source adds that craving grows in someone who keeps wanting to taste pleasure again, like thirst growing in a person drinking salt water. My interpretation: pleasure is not the problem, but returning to it and replaying it can strengthen the pull instead of satisfying it. Unlike C073-I02, where wanting returns because satisfaction fades, here the tasting itself feeds the thirst.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who can describe when something they enjoyed, such as a food, praise, a phone feed or shopping, began to feel like a need, and people who kept enjoyment without that turn. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What makes one pleasure stay a pleasure while another becomes something we cannot leave alone?

## Limits and alternatives

The juncture belongs to a doctrinal model spanning three lives. The card claims no measurable moment of transition and does not say enjoyment is harmful. Growing desire may instead come from biological dependence, loneliness, boredom, product design or scarcity, and people may find no turning point.
