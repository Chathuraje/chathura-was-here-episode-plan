# C090-I02 — Unable to memorise, yet able to understand

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Failing at one way of learning, such as memorising words, can lead others to judge a person incapable. Yet understanding may come through a simple task and something seen directly.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source tells of a young monk who spent four months unable to learn a four-line verse. His elder brother, who was also his teacher, declared him unfit and sent him away, and he stood weeping at the gate. The Buddha told him that being unable to recite does not make someone incapable. He gave him a clean cloth to rub while repeating a short phrase. As he rubbed, the cloth grew soiled from the sweat of his hand. He saw that "the cloth is clean; the fault is in my hand", and this led him on to deeper understanding. Leaving aside the story's miraculous parts, the source contrasts two routes: repeating words, and noticing something through a physical, repeated act. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that one failed method can harden into a verdict on the whole person, from teachers and from the learner.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: learners who struggled with rote examinations but later understood a trade, a craft, farming or care work through hands-on practice, and teachers who changed methods for them. The inquiry would ask how being labelled affected them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How often is "cannot learn" really "cannot learn this way"?

## Limits and alternatives

The narrative is a canonical story with supernatural elements, including arahantship and a thousand created copies, and is not historical evidence. The source's insight concerns defilement, not general education. Some difficulties with memorisation reflect learning disabilities, trauma, hunger or poor teaching and need their own evidence. Hands-on learning is not a cure for them.
