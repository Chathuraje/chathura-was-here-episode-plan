# C009-I01 — Most of what meets the eye goes unseen

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Countless sights reach the eyes, but only those the mind turns toward are actually seen. Seeing depends on attention as much as on eyes and light.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source lists four conditions for seeing: a working eye, something to be seen, light, and attention. It then makes a striking point. The forms that meet the eye are beyond counting, and it is not possible to say that all of them were seen. When there is a wish to see a certain group of forms, attention to them arises, and only the wanted object is taken up. The same pattern, attention included, is repeated for hearing, smell, taste and touch. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that people tend to trust that they saw what was in front of them. The source suggests instead that what someone sees is shaped by what they were already inclined to look for. Two people in the same place may take in quite different things.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who notice what others walk past because of their work or interest. Examples could be a birdwatcher, a tracker, a craftsperson reading wood grain, or someone returning to a childhood place and seeing it differently. The inquiry would ask what they notice and when they learned to see it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What in a familiar place have we never actually seen?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's account of eye-sensitivity and the heart-base is traditional physiology and doctrine, not neuroscience, and should not be presented as matching modern findings. Failing to notice something may also come from poor visibility, fatigue, unfamiliarity or simply not knowing what to look for, rather than from where attention was directed. Skilled noticing may reflect trained knowledge more than desire to see.
