# C020-I01 — Knowing the sign without knowing the reason

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

We recognise things by marks that repetition has laid down. That recognition can become firm and quick without ever including an understanding of what the thing is or why it is so.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes recognition as a capacity present in every moment of experience. Each time something is recognised, the recognition leaves a mark. The next moment uses that mark to recognise the thing again and leaves a mark of its own. Over time, repeated marking makes recognition steady. The source then draws a limit. Recognition knows a thing by signs such as white, red or blue, but it does not know why the thing is white, red or blue. The tension, in my interpretation, is between familiarity and understanding. The more often we meet something, the more confidently we recognise it, and the easier it becomes to mistake that confidence for knowledge. The source does not draw this conclusion itself. It only separates recognising from knowing the cause.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who have recognised something for years, such as a plant, a weather sign, a sound in a machine or a symptom, and later learned what it actually was or why it happened. The inquiry would ask whether their earlier recognition was changed, deepened or contradicted. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What changes when something we have long recognised is finally understood?

## Limits and alternatives

The source analyses a mental factor within a doctrinal framework. It is not a cognitive science account of memory or pattern recognition and should not be equated with one. Long familiarity often does carry real practical knowledge that the source's simple example does not capture. An alternative reading is that a person who “only recognises” may understand a great deal but lack words or formal explanation for it.
