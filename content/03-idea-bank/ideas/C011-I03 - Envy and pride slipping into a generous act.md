# C011-I03 — Envy and pride slipping into a generous act

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A generous act that seems like one good intention from start to finish is made of many moments. Between the moments of attention to the giving, envy, pride or unrelated thoughts can arise.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source warns against assuming that from the decision to give until the giving is finished, or through a whole day of keeping precepts, only good mind-states arise. Good states arise only in the moments when the person attends to the deed. Between them, envy, conceit or unrelated and opposing thoughts can appear and weaken the deed. The source advises staying as mindful as possible, and elsewhere keeping the mind clean before and after the act. The mechanism is that intention is not a single switch; it has to be renewed. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people judge generosity as a whole, while the experience inside a long act of giving, such as organising a large offering, may include comparison, irritation or wanting credit alongside real care.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: extended acts of giving that last days or involve many people, such as community almsgivings, relief drives or hosting a large gathering. The research would ask participants what else they felt besides goodwill, and whether it changed the act for them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Over the long course of a generous act, what else do we feel, and does it change what the act was?

## Limits and alternatives

That stray thoughts "weaken" a deed concerns karmic strength and is doctrinal. Mixed feelings do not show a gift was insincere, and observers cannot see them. Pride or wanting recognition may sustain generosity that would otherwise not happen, and tiredness or social friction may explain irritation better than envy.
