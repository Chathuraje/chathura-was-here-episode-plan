# C061-I01 — Old deeds feeling new again near death

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Near the end of life, a significant past action, kind or harmful, may return to mind as vividly as when it was done, even if it had long been forgotten.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes, as doctrine, what appears to a dying mind. A past action can come up "as if it were being done anew", and the same quality of mind as at the time of the act arises again. It may recur on and off for several days before death. Objects linked to the act can also appear, such as the weapon used or the flowers offered. A second source adds a contrast. Deeds that are remembered return as recollection, while forgotten deeds come "like seeing a dream". It also says the mind leans toward whatever has appeared. My interpretation, which goes beyond the text, is that people often assume the past settles with time, yet at the end it may return with the weight of the present. The source does not speak of regret or life review; those are research questions.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: accounts from older people, hospice or palliative carers, and families about which memories of their own actions dying people bring up, whether these are generosity, harm or unfinished matters, and what objects seem to carry those memories. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Which of our own actions do we find we have never really left behind?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's purpose is to explain rebirth, which is a doctrinal claim and is not carried into this idea. It says the appearance is caused by past action, not by memory or emotion. Vivid memories near death may instead reflect medication, delirium, conversation with visitors or the natural review prompted by illness. Many dying people report no such return, and inner experience cannot be inferred from outside.
