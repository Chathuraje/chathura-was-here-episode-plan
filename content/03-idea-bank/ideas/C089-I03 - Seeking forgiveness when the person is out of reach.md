# C089-I03 — Seeking forgiveness when the person is out of reach

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A harsh remark is often made without knowing the other person's circumstances. The need to ask forgiveness does not end when that person can no longer be faced.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source tells of a young monk who saw an elder sit on a log to drink hot porridge and said the hungry old man had shamed them. He did not know the elder had stomach pain and needed it hot. Told he had wronged someone, he asked forgiveness. The source then covers other cases. If the person has gone elsewhere, go or send someone; failing that, confess before the monks where you are. If no one knows where they are, tell a learned monk that the memory brings remorse, receive reassurance, and bow towards where they went. If they have died, go to where they died or to the cremation ground. My interpretation is that apology is treated as an act, not a feeling: the harm is named aloud and the request made, even if the person never hears it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who wished to apologise to someone who had moved away, lost contact or died. The inquiry would ask what they did, such as visiting a grave, speaking to a relative or confessing to a teacher, and what it changed for them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What does asking forgiveness accomplish when the person we hurt can never answer?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's stakes are doctrinal. Insulting a noble one is said to block heaven and the path until forgiven, and that result cannot be carried over. The procedures belong to monastic etiquette of seniority. Apologising to someone absent may relieve the speaker without repairing anything for the person harmed or their family. Some people find peace through changed conduct, restitution or time rather than any ritual of apology, and others never do.
