# C100-I03 — Letting go only after searching every branch

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Some attachments do not end in a single moment of recognition. They wear out only after a person has checked, one part at a time, every place they hoped to find what they wanted, and found it absent in each.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source's simile is of a bat that settles in a tree with five branches, hoping for a flower or fruit. It searches the first branch and finds nothing, then the second, third, fourth and fifth. Only then does it conclude the tree has nothing to give, drop its attachment, climb the straightest branch, look up and fly to a tree that bears fruit. The source maps the branches onto the five groups that make up a person, each examined and found to hold nothing worth keeping. The mechanism differs from sudden recognition (C100-I01). Release depends on exhaustive checking, and attachment survives while one branch remains unsearched. My interpretation is that this describes why people often cannot leave something until they have tried every avenue. The bat also flies somewhere, rather than simply giving up.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who stayed with a livelihood, place or institution while trying one option after another before finally moving on. The research would ask what finally convinced them and what they turned toward. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What makes a search feel finished enough to let go of what we were searching?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy for a doctrinal insight that body and mind hold nothing to grasp. Exhaustive searching may also reflect stubbornness, a lack of alternatives or obligation rather than hope. People may leave before searching everything, or stay after searching everything. And a tree that seemed fruitless may simply have been out of season.
