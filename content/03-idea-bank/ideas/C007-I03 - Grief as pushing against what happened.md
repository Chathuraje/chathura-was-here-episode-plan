# C007-I03 — Grief as pushing against what happened

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Sorrow over a death, a loss or one's own serious mistake may share a movement with anger: the mind resisting something it cannot accept.

## Human mechanism and tension

Both sources place sorrow in the same category as anger, fear and aversion. One defines this category as the mind's opposition to its object, as if striking against it, and says that sorrow is the painful feeling itself. The occasions listed include the death of parents or children, other losses, one's own great mistakes, ageing, illness, being separated from loved ones and not getting what one wants. One source describes how this state shows in the body: weeping, harsh words, striking one's own head and chest. The human tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that grief is usually understood as love for what was lost, while this framing draws attention to a second strand inside it: refusal of what happened. The two may be tangled, which could help explain anger, blame or self-reproach in mourning.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how people describe the early period after a sudden loss, such as a death at sea, a disaster or an accident. The inquiry would ask whether anger, blame or disbelief sat alongside sorrow, and how that changed over time. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

In grief, how much is missing what was lost, and how much is refusing that it happened?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's classification of grief as unwholesome is doctrinal. It must never be used to judge, correct or morally label mourners. The idea is a limited analogy, not a theory of bereavement. Grief is widely understood as a natural expression of attachment and love, and anger in mourning can also come from real injustice or negligence. Only a person's own account, handled with great care, could suggest what their grief contained.
