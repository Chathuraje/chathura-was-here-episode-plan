# C074-I01 — A dormant urge that waits for its trigger

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

An urge can be absent from awareness for a long time and still be present, ready to rise when the right situation appears. Once it rises, it may fade in the mind or go on into words and actions.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources describe three stages of a desire, resentment or conceit. It lies dormant and unnoticed. When a suitable object appears, it rises and fills the mind. Then it may move body and speech into an act. One source adds that a risen urge sometimes subsides there and sometimes does not. Its example is a forest ascetic, long withdrawn and deep in meditation, who falls from that state on meeting a certain object, because the tendency had never gone. The tension, in my interpretation, is between believing "I am past that now" and discovering that distance only removed the trigger. The gap between rising in the mind and crossing into action also matters.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who have kept away from something for years, such as drinking, gambling or an old rivalry, describing what happened when circumstances brought it close again, and what happened between feeling the urge and acting. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When an old urge returns after years of absence, what decides whether it stays a feeling or becomes an act?

## Limits and alternatives

The claim that tendencies persist until removed by path knowledge is doctrinal. This card does not establish that urges are permanent or relapse inevitable. Renewed behaviour may come from new stress, social pressure, loss or physical dependency rather than a hidden tendency. Addiction needs specialist sources.
