# C087-I02 — Trust without judgement, cleverness without trust

**Status:** Merged into C076-I03 (*When trust outruns judgement, or the reverse*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards describe the same balance: trust without discernment misleads, and discernment without trust stalls. C087 adds the practice context of the kasiṇa chapter. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C076-I03 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Confidence in someone or something needs discernment to aim it, and discernment needs some trust to keep it honest. Either one alone goes wrong in its own way.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary treats faith and wisdom as faculties that must be kept in balance. When faith is strong and wisdom weak, a person places their confidence where it does not belong. When wisdom is strong and faith weak, a person drifts toward the side of the crafty or evasive, and the text says this is hard to remedy, like an illness caused by medicine itself. Only when the two are even does confidence settle in the right place. The source then adds a qualification: for someone developing concentration, somewhat stronger faith helps, because it keeps them practising before results arrive. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the medicine simile points to something specific: reasoning without any trust can argue its way around every correction, so the tool that should cure error becomes the obstacle. Trust without judgement, meanwhile, is easy to exploit.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations where people must decide whom to trust, such as choosing a healer, an investment scheme, a recruitment agent for work abroad or a teacher, and how people who were deceived and people who refused all help describe their reasoning. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What keeps trust from becoming gullibility, and cleverness from becoming a way to avoid being corrected?

## Limits and alternatives

"Faith" in the source means confidence in the Buddha's teaching, and "wisdom" means insight into things as they are; widening them to everyday trust and intelligence is an analogy. Being deceived often reflects fraud, poverty or lack of options, not weak judgement. Scepticism can be well-founded caution after past harm. Nothing here should be used to blame victims.
