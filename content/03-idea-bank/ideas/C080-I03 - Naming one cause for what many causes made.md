# C080-I03 — Naming one cause for what many causes made

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Whenever we say "this caused that", we have picked one cause out of many. Which one we pick depends on what we want the explanation to do.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources pause over their own formula, which names a single cause for each link. They state that nothing arises from one cause: many causes give many results, as seed, soil, water and season together give a sprout. One cause is named because it is the main one, because it is the most visible, because it is distinctive, or because it suits the listener. Margaya adds a habit of speech: we say rice is cooked by firewood, though it is the fire that cooks it; the cause of a cause gets named as the cause. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the same selection happens when families or villages explain a success, a failure or a quarrel. The chosen cause may be the easiest to see, or the one that places credit or blame where the speaker wants it. The tension is between the clarity a single cause gives and what it leaves out.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how different people explain the same local event, such as a failed harvest, a business closing or a child's exam success, and which single cause each one names. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we explain what happened with one cause, what decides which cause we choose to name?

## Limits and alternatives

The source discusses a doctrinal teaching method, not social blame; the extension is mine. Naming one cause is often honest shorthand, and one cause may truly dominate. This overlaps C060-I01, which concerns the grieving search for one reason after an early death; this card concerns the selection built into any causal statement. Stated causes do not reveal a speaker's motive for choosing them.
