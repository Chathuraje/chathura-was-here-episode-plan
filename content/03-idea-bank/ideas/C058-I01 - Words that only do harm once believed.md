# C058-I01 — Words that only do harm once believed

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A lie, a piece of gossip meant to divide people, or careless empty talk is not finished when it is spoken. Its full weight depends on someone else believing it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source lists the parts that must all be present before a harmful act of speech is complete. For lying, the last part is that others come to know and accept the falsehood. Words meant to split friends are complete only when the listeners believe them and are actually divided. Even idle, pointless talk becomes a serious act when listeners take it as true. The source also says the same lie is heavier or lighter depending on how much loss it causes. It names two motives: wanting gain or praise, and wanting to hurt or get even. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that harmful speech is shared work. The speaker starts it, but its force travels through trust. That places a quiet responsibility on the listener too, without reducing the speaker's.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how a rumour, a false accusation or a divisive story spreads through a workplace, a neighbourhood or a family, and what happens when some people believe it and others do not. It could also look at people who stopped a story by not passing it on. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When a story breaks a friendship, how much of the damage belongs to the teller, and how much to those who believed it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's framework concerns the moral weight of the speaker's intention, not shared blame. It does not say listeners are at fault. Its account of results after death is doctrinal. People may believe false stories because of prior conflict, fear or lack of information rather than gullibility. Someone may also repeat something they sincerely think is true, which the source does not count as lying.
