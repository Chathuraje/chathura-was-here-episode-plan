# C077-I02 — Sitting side by side yet feeling worlds apart

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Physical closeness does not make inner experience close. People in one room can be far apart in what they feel, while people far apart can be near in it.

## Human mechanism and tension

For material things, the source measures "near" and "far" by place: within hearing distance, the same house, village or country. For feeling, perception, intention and consciousness, it changes the measure. A painful feeling is "far" from a pleasant one, a settled mind's feeling is far from an unsettled one, and like is near to like. It says explicitly that three people on the same bench, one in pain, one at ease and one caught in harmful intent, have feelings far from each other. People in entirely different conditions who share the same kind of feeling are near. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that shared space is easily mistaken for shared experience. A family at one table may assume they are together while one member is grieving. Strangers who have lived through the same kind of loss may understand each other at once.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: gatherings where people share a place but not a mood, such as a wedding soon after a bereavement or a hospital waiting area, and people who feel better understood by distant others with a similar experience. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What makes two people close in what they are going through, if being in the same place does not?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's "near and far" is a classification device, and its examples include beings in other planes of existence, which is doctrinal context only. It says nothing about empathy or relationships. As an alternative, shared space often does create shared feeling through ritual, conversation and care. Outward behaviour cannot show how far apart people are inside.
