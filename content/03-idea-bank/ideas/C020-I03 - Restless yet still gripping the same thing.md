# C020-I03 — Restless yet still gripping the same thing

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A mind can be agitated and unsettled while still holding on to one object. Being restless does not mean being free of what one is fixed on.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source raises an apparent contradiction. In harmful states of mind, the capacity that keeps the mind on its object is present together with restlessness, which shakes the mind and prevents calm. How can both be there at once? Its answer is an image. A person riding a cart along a road full of potholes is jolted about, yet keeps travelling in the cart. In the same way, the harmful mind is unsettled but does not let go of its object. Because of the restlessness, the mind's other functions cannot work steadily. In beneficial states, the source says, this turbulence is absent, so the mind rests firmly on its object. My interpretation, which goes beyond the text, is that this describes a familiar experience: turning something over again and again, agitated, unable to settle on it calmly and unable to leave it. Holding on and being at peace with something are different.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations of waiting or unresolved trouble, such as waiting for exam results, a court date, news from a family member working abroad, or a debt. People describe being unable to think of anything else but also unable to think about it clearly. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What keeps us holding on to the very thing that will not let us settle?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes a momentary unwholesome mind-state in a doctrinal classification, not rumination, anxiety or any clinical condition. It must not be used to diagnose anyone or to suggest that worry is morally wrong. Returning again and again to a problem can also be necessary problem-solving, protective vigilance or grief, rather than agitation.
