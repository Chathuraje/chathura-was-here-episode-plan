# C097-I01 — Showing you need nothing in order to receive

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A display of not wanting can itself be a way of getting. Refusing, hinting and praising can bring in more than asking directly would.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source catalogues ways a monk who depends on gifts can obtain them without asking. In "pretended austerity", he turns down fine robes and food, saying a renunciant should wear rags and eat what he begs. Impressed donors then press gifts on him, and he accepts "only out of compassion, so that you gain merit". Soon cartloads arrive. Other tactics include walking or sitting so that people think he is advanced, and dropping hints such as "monks find requisites scarce today". Some flatter a donor's wealth and ancestry, or speak half-truths, "like mung beans, some cooked and some raw". Others belittle a donor until fear of blame makes them give. The source counts all of these as wrong livelihood. The line it draws is fine: it permits some hinting for shelter, but never asking outright. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that dependence on others' goodwill makes indirect asking attractive. The tension is that real modesty and strategic modesty can look identical, even to the person doing it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who depend on patrons, donors or employers, such as religious workers, artists, fundraisers or domestic workers, describing how they ask without seeming to ask, and how givers read those signals. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When someone declines what they are offered, how can either side tell whether it is modesty or a request?

## Limits and alternatives

The source addresses monastic rules, and its verdicts are doctrinal. In Sri Lanka and elsewhere, declining an offer at first is ordinary courtesy, not manipulation. Indirect requests may also protect dignity where open asking is shameful. Outside observation cannot reveal motive, so this card must never be used to call a documentary subject a hypocrite.
