# C088-I03 — Mastering a stage at will before moving on

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Reaching a level once is not the same as commanding it. The source treats a stage as secured only when it can be entered, held, left and reviewed whenever one chooses. Only then is the next stage attempted.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source names five masteries of a meditative state. The first is calling up its parts quickly. The second is entering it without delay. The third is staying in it for as long as one decides. The fourth is coming out at exactly the chosen time. The fifth is reviewing its parts when one wishes. A person who wants the next level "must practise" these first, and the same rule applies at every stage. The emphasis falls on control over starting, holding and stopping, not on intensity or a single breakthrough. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that many learners are tempted to push forward after a first success. A stage that is not yet reliable may give way under the next one. The tension lies between the eagerness to advance and the slower work of making what one has dependable.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: apprentices, musicians, dancers or divers whose teachers insist that a technique be repeatable on demand, including stopping cleanly, before the next is taught. The inquiry would ask how learners experience that insistence. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When is something we can do once really ours to build on?

## Limits and alternatives

The source concerns meditative absorptions and a doctrinal path. It says nothing about crafts or sport. Some learning advances better through varied practice or by attempting harder tasks early, so consolidation-first is one approach among several. Insistence on mastery may also reflect tradition or safety rules. Quick entry under pressure is a separate question, covered by C089-I01.
