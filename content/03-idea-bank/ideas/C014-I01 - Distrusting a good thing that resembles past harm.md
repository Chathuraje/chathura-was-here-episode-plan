# C014-I01 — Distrusting a good thing that resembles past harm

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

After turning from something that caused suffering, a person may distrust even a helpful thing of the same kind, and judge each new step by its closeness to what was left behind.

## Human mechanism and tension

In the source, a meditator has grown weary of the body, which must be maintained with difficulty and is exposed to ageing, illness and enemies. They have also relied on a mental image of light that helped quiet their defilements, which the source says is refined and not a cause of suffering. Yet because it is form of the same kind, the meditator wonders whether it too might bring fear, and works to remove it. At each later stage, an attainment is judged not sublime enough because it sits close to the coarser stage before it, and is given up. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that caution learned from real harm can spread to things that only resemble the harm. It may protect, or it may cost something that was helping. The source presents this as refinement, not error.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who left a harmful workplace, household or habit and became wary of helpful things that looked similar, such as a fair employer in the same trade. The inquiry would ask how they decided what to keep. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we tell what actually harmed us from what only shares its shape?

## Limits and alternatives

The source concerns meditative objects and a doctrinal wish for bodiless existence; disgust with the body is not offered here as a healthy attitude. Its suspicion is chosen and methodical, whereas fear after harm may be involuntary. Avoiding similar things may be a reasonable safety strategy rather than over-generalisation. People may also give up harmless things for reasons of identity or simplicity.
