# C020-I02 — Seeing only one side of something at once

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Anything we attend to has many aspects, but a single moment of attention can settle on only one of them. What a thing seems to be depends partly on which aspect the mind has settled on.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source says that even one object has many aspects the mind could take. Look at a thing from one place and it appears one way; from another place, another way; from a hundred places, a hundred ways. A single moment of mind cannot take several at once. It must settle on one, and the capacity that does this is present in every mind-state. Trying to take many at once, the source adds, means grasping none. The tension, in my interpretation beyond the text, is that people experience their view as the thing itself, while the source treats every view as one of many available aspects. Two people can both look accurately and still see different things.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: a shared object seen from different positions, such as a river valued by a farmer, a fisher, a builder and a pilgrim, or a house seen by the family who left it and the family who moved in. The inquiry would ask each what the thing is, and whether anyone tries to take another's aspect. No person, place, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How much of a disagreement is about the thing, and how much is about which side of it each person stands on?

## Limits and alternatives

The source is describing a factor of momentary mind, not a theory of social perspective or relativism. It does not say all views are equally true, and it does not address bias. Some differences in how people see a thing come from interests, power or misinformation rather than viewpoint alone.
