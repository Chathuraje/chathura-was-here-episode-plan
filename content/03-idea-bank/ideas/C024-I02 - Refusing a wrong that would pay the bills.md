# C024-I02 — Refusing a wrong that would pay the bills

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Declining a dishonest or harmful act is a different test when that act is how one would earn a living, rather than something done for pleasure or anger.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source singles out restraint defined by its motive: refraining from wrongs done *for the sake of a livelihood*. Its examples of wrong livelihood include pretending to virtues one lacks to win gifts, flattering people for profit, lending to someone in difficulty at unjust interest so as to take their land, and endangering others for a small gain. Honest trade is not itself this restraint; it exists only when a wrong way of earning is seen and turned down. Its example is a lawyer who accepts a bribe to win a field for a tenant trying to steal it, then returns the money, refusing first the lie and then a living made from lies. The tension, as I read it beyond the text, is that the need to earn can make a wrong feel like necessity, and refusing may cost one's household.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people in money-lending, land dealing, brokering, sales or professional services who describe an offer or practice they turned down because it would have depended on deceit or someone else's ruin, and what it cost them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What lets someone turn away money they need when earning it would mean deceiving or ruining someone else?

## Limits and alternatives

The source motivates refusal partly by fear of suffering in future lives, which is doctrinal. Occupations must not be treated as suspect. A refusal may also come from fear of legal risk, reputation, a better offer or simple inability to go through with it. Not every hard-earned compromise is the kind of wrong the source describes.
