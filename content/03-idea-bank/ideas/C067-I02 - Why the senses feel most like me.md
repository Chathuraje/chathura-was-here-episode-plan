# C067-I02 — Why the senses feel most like me

**Status:** Held. The source claim is clear, but it is a doctrinal ranking given in one or two sentences. It needs review before research. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

People do not claim every part of their body as "me" equally. The source says the feeling of "mine" is strongest towards the faculties through which we see, hear, smell, taste and touch.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source defines "internal" matter not by where it sits but by how intensely it is held. All bodily matter is ordinarily regarded as "I" and "mine". Among it, the five sense-faculties are singled out because the sense of ownership and craving towards them is very great, while other matter is not held so tightly. A related passage says the matter that makes up the living body is seized by craving and views as "mine" and "I". My interpretation, which goes beyond the text, is that people may identify most with the channels through which they meet the world, more than with a limb or an organ they cannot feel. The tension is that these channels are also the parts most exposed to change, ageing and damage.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how people describe themselves after a change in sight, hearing, taste or touch, compared with other bodily changes. The inquiry would ask whether they speak of losing something they had or of changing who they are. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Which parts of the body do we experience as ourselves, and which as things we merely have?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a doctrinal classification about craving, not a psychological or medical finding. The source gives no evidence and little explanation. Research must not treat disability or sensory loss as a loss of self, and must not suggest how people ought to feel. Strong reactions to sensory change may instead reflect loss of work or independence, stigma, pain or fear of illness. Many people identify strongly with hands, face, voice or skill instead.
