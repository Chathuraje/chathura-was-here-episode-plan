# C102-I01 — Deciding beforehand what may call you back

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Before withdrawing completely from others, a person can settle in advance which claims are weighty enough to bring them back, and arrange things so that their absence harms no one.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes four duties performed before entering the deepest withdrawal it recognises. The person makes sure belongings will not be lost. They resolve to come out before a messenger arrives if the community is waiting for them, and the same for a summons from their teacher. They check whether their remaining life will outlast the time they plan to be away, and fix that time in advance. If they made no resolution and the community sends for them, they still come out when the messenger speaks, "because the community is weighty". My interpretation, beyond the text, is that withdrawal and obligation are reconciled before leaving, not afterwards. The tension is between a real need for undisturbed stillness and the claims of those who rely on you. The source's way through is advance judgement about what may interrupt.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who periodically withdraw, whether on retreat, into long solitary work, into seasonal isolation or on pilgrimage, and how they arrange what happens if someone needs them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Before we step away from everyone, how do we decide who is allowed to call us back?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The attainment itself, the protection of belongings by resolution and the claim that death cannot occur during it are doctrinal claims. The duties are framed for a monastic hierarchy. Ordinary withdrawal may be unplanned, forced by illness or grief, or deliberately cut off. Some people cannot choose what calls them back, because caring duties or poverty decide it for them.
