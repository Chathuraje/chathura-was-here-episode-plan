# C066-I01 — Deciding to move is not yet moving

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Thinking about an action, even in detail, does not move the body. A further step is needed in which the intention becomes a force in the limbs, and the body's condition can allow or block it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source takes walking as its example. A person who wants to walk may think at length about how, where and in which direction to go, and still no walking happens. One foot must be lifted and set down, then the other, and that needs a force the mind itself generates in line with the intention. The force moves the limbs only if they are not weakened by illness, injury, or damage to or disconnection from the brain. Stopping is also active: a new intention to stand or sit replaces the force that carried the walk. The second source likens it to a match lighting a dark room. The tension, in my interpretation, is between how easy deciding is and how separate, sometimes difficult, it is for the body to begin.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people for whom starting or stopping a movement has become effortful or must be relearned, such as during recovery from injury, while learning a demanding physical skill or in a slowing older body. The research would ask what the moment of actually beginning is like. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What happens in the moment between having decided and actually beginning?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's "mind-born air element" is a historical account of movement and must not be equated with nerves or muscles. This card diagnoses no condition. A gap between deciding and moving may also come from fear, pain, low motivation or medical causes the source does not describe. The source concerns physical movement, so extending it to procrastination in general would go beyond the text.
