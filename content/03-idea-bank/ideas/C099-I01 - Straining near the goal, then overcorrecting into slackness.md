# C099-I01 — Straining near the goal, then overcorrecting into slackness

**Status:** Merged into C087-I01 (*When pushing harder keeps the goal away*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards cover straining as success appears, then overcorrecting into slackness. C099 adds the absorption-process framing. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C087-I01 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

When the first sign of success appears, eagerness can tighten effort until agitation spoils the attempt. A person who then sees that mistake may loosen too far and fail in the opposite way.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes a meditator close to a deep, settled state. Some, when the inner sign that it is near appears, think they will reach it quickly and grip their effort harder. The excess brings restlessness, and they fail. Others, having seen the fault in too much effort, fall into laziness, and also fail. Only someone who falls into neither arrives. The source adds that if the state does not come, one should keep trying with understanding, and later names effort "neither started beyond measure nor loosened beyond measure". As I read it, two things stand out. The danger is sharpest just as progress shows itself, when hope turns into pressure. And correcting one error can produce the other: the lesson "don't push" becomes the next failure. The right amount of effort cannot be fixed in advance.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: learners and practitioners approaching a threshold, such as a final exam, a first public performance or the last stage of a long craft apprenticeship, and how they describe the swing between forcing and backing off. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How does someone learn to keep going steadily at the very moment they can see the result?

## Limits and alternatives

The passage concerns meditation, not skills in general, and does not say how to find the balance. Failure near a goal may also come from fatigue, outside pressure or an unfinished skill. Slackening may be needed rest rather than overcorrection. C076-I02 covers alternating effort and restraint; this card is limited to the near-goal surge and the overcorrection.
