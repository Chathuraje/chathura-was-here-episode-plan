# C022-I01 — When regret keeps the wrong alive

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Burning regret over what one did, or failed to do, can feel like moral repair. Yet it may keep reopening the wrong and drain the energy needed to act differently now.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source separates remembering a past wrong, which is not the problem, from repeatedly burning over it. Its examples are someone who acted carelessly in youth and torments themselves in old age, and someone who now feels too weak to do the good they postponed. Many people, it notes, see this burning as a virtue that lessens wrongdoing. The source disagrees. It says the regret dirties the mind again, and that regret over good left undone creates no good and weakens the wish to act. When strong, the person cannot free themselves alone and needs good friends. The tension, as I interpret it, is that feeling bad can stand in for responsibility while blocking it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people later in life looking back on harm they caused or chances they let pass, and what, if anything, helped them move from self-reproach to some present action. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When does looking back at a wrong help someone change, and when does it hold them in place?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's framing is doctrinal, including the claim that remorse can make a light wrong heavy in its future result. It does not say accountability, apology or repair are wrong. Modern accounts of guilt, depression and trauma must not be equated with it. Persistent regret may reflect unresolved harm to others, unfinished apology, illness or grief rather than a habit of mind. It can also be an appropriate response. Outsiders cannot judge whether someone's regret is helpful.
