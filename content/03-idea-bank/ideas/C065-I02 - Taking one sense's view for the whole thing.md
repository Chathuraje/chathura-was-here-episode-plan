# C065-I02 — Taking one sense's view for the whole thing

**Status:** Merged into C020-I02 (*Seeing only one side of something at once*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards say that one moment or one sense reaches only one aspect of a thing. C065 adds that eye and hand meet different parts of an object. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C020-I02 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Each sense reaches a different part of a thing: the eye its colour, the hand its hardness. We join these into one object and trust that picture as if a single sense had given us the whole.

## Human mechanism and tension

One source puts it bluntly. What the hand grasps is hardness and what the eye sees is colour. The part the eye sees cannot be grasped, and the part the hand grasps cannot be seen. To think they are one and the same thing is called a mistaken notion. The other source adds that the colour we see is a blend of countless colours, and that the same object looks different at a distance or close up, in bright or dim light, and to different people. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is practical. People act on one confident picture of a gem, a piece of land or a fabric, although it is assembled from partial and shifting contacts. The source does not call the object unreal.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: trades where looking and handling can disagree, such as buying gems, textiles, timber or produce. The research would ask how people learn not to trust one sense alone. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we come to trust a thing we have only ever met one side at a time?

## Limits and alternatives

This is held because the viewpoint part duplicates C020-I02 (one aspect of an object at a time). Its only distinct point is that different senses meet different parts. The source's account of colour and light is historical, not scientific. Many would argue that perception combines the senses reliably and that treating an object as one thing is sensible. Misjudgements may come from haste, deception or inexperience instead.
