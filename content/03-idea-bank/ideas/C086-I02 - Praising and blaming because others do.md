# C086-I02 — Praising and blaming because others do

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Some judgements of good and bad are not formed by looking but taken over from the people nearby. Once taken over, they can be hard to put down.

## Human mechanism and tension

Describing the temperament dominated by delusion, one source says that when such a person sees something, they evaluate it only according to others' views: if others criticise, they criticise; if others praise, they praise. The other source says this person judges right and wrong, good and bad, by what others say, is easily deceived, and finds it hard to give up a wrong opinion once accepted. It contrasts the intelligent temperament, which acts after inquiry and does not accept something merely because others said it. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that two things are joined here: where a judgement comes from, and how firmly it is then held. Borrowed opinions are not necessarily held loosely; the person who never examined a view may be least able to revise it. The tension is that relying on others' judgement is also ordinary and often sensible, since nobody can check everything.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how opinions about a newcomer, a local official or a public figure spread through a neighbourhood or online group, and whether people can recall when they formed their own view. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we praise or condemn something, how much of the verdict did we reach ourselves?

## Limits and alternatives

The source ties this to a doctrinal temperament type caused, it says, partly by past-life conduct; that cause is not carried here, and no one should be called "deluded". Following others' views can reflect trust in expertise, respect for elders, lack of information or the social cost of disagreeing, rather than an inability to judge. People may also voice the group's verdict while privately holding another.
