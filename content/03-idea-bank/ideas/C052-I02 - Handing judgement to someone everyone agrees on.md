# C052-I02 — Handing judgement to someone everyone agrees on

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

When a group can no longer settle wrongdoing through advice or by punishing one another, it may agree to give one person the power to judge and support that person in return.

## Human mechanism and tension

In the source's origin story, neighbours are punishing thieves themselves. They then agree to appoint one person to speak out, censure and expel wrongdoers. They choose the person of finest appearance, who inspires confidence and has great presence, and offer him a share of the rice. He judges fairly and is named "the one chosen by the many". Two tensions sit inside the passage, and naming them is my interpretation. Authority here rests on agreement and payment, so it depends on continuing trust. And the qualities people chose by, looks, confidence and presence, are not the same as the fairness they needed, even though in this story they coincide.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how communities pick someone to settle disputes, such as a village elder, a society committee or a respected mediator, and what that person receives in return. Research would ask why they were trusted and how the arrangement holds when judgements are unpopular. No person, place, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What do people look for when they choose someone to judge them, and does it match what judging well requires?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a myth, not a history of government, and must not be used to legitimise any real ruler or institution. In practice authority is often inherited, imposed or seized. People may follow a mediator from fear, kinship or lack of options rather than consent. Presence can also reflect real skill in handling conflict.
