# C096-I01 — Knowing what helps, what harms, and what fits now

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Practical wisdom may be three skills, not one: knowing what makes good things grow, what makes them decline, and what this moment calls for. A person can have one without the others.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary names three kinds of skill. Skill in growth is knowing that, when certain things are attended to, harmful states do not arise or are abandoned, and good states arise and mature. Skill in decline is knowing the reverse. Skill in means is different in kind: wisdom that arises right there, fitting the situation, about the causes of welfare and happiness. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the first two are general knowledge that can be learned and stated, while the third cannot be fully stated in advance because it depends on the case. Someone may know well what generally helps and harms yet misjudge what to do now, or act well in the moment without being able to explain the pattern.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who look after something fragile over time, such as a village tank committee, a nurse on a long-term ward, a youth coach or a family running a small shop. The inquiry would ask what they know generally strengthens or weakens the thing in their care, and about moments where general knowledge did not settle what to do. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Why can knowing what generally helps still leave us unsure what to do today?

## Limits and alternatives

In the source, growth and decline concern wholesome and unwholesome mental states on a religious path, not success in work or community. It lists the skills as types of wisdom and does not claim they come apart in practice. Acting well in the moment may be experience or luck rather than a distinct wisdom. What counts as welfare is contested.
