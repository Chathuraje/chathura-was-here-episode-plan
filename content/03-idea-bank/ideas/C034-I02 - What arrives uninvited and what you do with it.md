# C034-I02 — What arrives uninvited and what you do with it

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Meeting something pleasant or unpleasant is one event. Acting on it is another, and the source places doing, and its moral weight, in the second.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source divides the work of a single moment of experience. Seeing, hearing, receiving and examining are described as weak results that take in what comes but do nothing with it. The active phase that follows “runs” with force and carries out bodily, spoken and mental action. One source states that knowing the object well, acting, and doing good or harm all happen there. The human tension, as I interpret it, lies between saying “it happened to me” and “this is what I did about it”. The source neither lets the first excuse the second nor blames someone for what reaches them. The line between impression and response may be where people locate their responsibility, or lose track of it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who repeatedly face provocations or temptations they did not choose, such as insults at a service counter, a sudden offer of easy money, or harsh news delivered by others. The inquiry would ask how they describe the gap, if any, between what came to them and what they did next. No person, place, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Where does something that happens to us become something we are doing?

## Limits and alternatives

In the source, what a person meets is framed as the result of their past actions, including past lives. That is a doctrinal claim and is not a premise here. The phases are said to be momentary, so an everyday “pause before responding” is a limited analogy. Reactions can be shaped by trauma, fatigue, illness or coercion, so a response need not reflect free choice. Outsiders cannot see where impression ended and response began.
