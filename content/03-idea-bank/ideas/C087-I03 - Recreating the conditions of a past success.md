# C087-I03 — Recreating the conditions of a past success

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A state that came once, such as deep focus, can be lost. One way back is to have noticed what was in place when it came, and to set those conditions up again.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary uses an archer. Having split a hair with an arrow, a skilled archer notes how his feet stood and how he held the bow, string and arrow, and repeats that stance so as not to miss again. In the same way, the meditator who first reaches absorption notes the food eaten, the people kept company with, the lodging and the posture. If the state is later lost, those conditions are set up again. The same text defines a suitable dwelling, village, conversation, person, food, weather and posture not by any fixed quality but by effect: a place is suitable if the image arises and holds there and the mind settles, unsuitable if it does not. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that this treats success as partly conditional rather than simply a personal achievement. The tension is that conditions noticed in hindsight may include coincidences, and repeating them can harden into superstition.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: craftspeople, athletes, writers or students who deliberately repeat a setting, time, food or posture that went with their best work, and how they decide which conditions really mattered. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When something went well once, how do we tell which of the surrounding conditions made the difference?

## Limits and alternatives

The source concerns regaining a meditative absorption, not general performance. Repeating conditions may work through confidence or habit rather than the conditions themselves. Some successes depend on things that cannot be recreated, such as another person's help or chance. Rituals copied without observation may become rigid.
