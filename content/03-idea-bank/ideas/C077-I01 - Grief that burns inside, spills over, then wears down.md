# C077-I01 — Grief that burns inside, spills over, then wears down

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Grief after a loss may take distinct forms: a burning kept inside, an outpouring in words and weeping, and a later heaviness when the outpouring has spent itself.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source separates three states that follow the loss of relatives, wealth or health. Sorrow is an inner burning that dries a person from within, like a heated dart. Lamentation is weeping aloud, calling the lost person's name, recounting their goodness and repeating half-finished words. Despair is deep fatigue and a sinking of the mind that also wastes the body. Its image is oil in a pot: on a low fire it cooks inside, on a high fire it boils over, and what remains keeps cooking until it is gone. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that these forms look unlike each other. Someone silent may be burning, someone loud may be emptying out, and someone who seems "over it" may be depleted. Others may read one form as the absence of grief.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how people describe the weeks and months after a death, a lost livelihood or a serious illness. This could include funeral customs that invite open lament, the quiet period after them, and what relatives notice in each other. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When grief stops showing, has it ended, or has it only changed form?

## Limits and alternatives

The source gives a simile, not a timetable, and does not say everyone passes through all three states in order. The same outward signs may come from shock, depression, illness or cultural expectations about mourning, and quietness can be acceptance. Its list of losses includes loss of virtue and right view, which is doctrinal. It mentions self-harm under despair, so research needs safeguarding.
