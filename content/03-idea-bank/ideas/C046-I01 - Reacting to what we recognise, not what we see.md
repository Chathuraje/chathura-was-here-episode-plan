# C046-I01 — Reacting to what we recognise, not what we see

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Liking, trust, irritation or confusion attach not to the bare sight of something but to what the mind has recognised it as.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes what follows a single sight. The mind first retakes what was just seen, then its colour, then recognises it under a concept such as a man, a woman, cloth or a vessel. Only after that recognition does a response of attraction, confidence, anger or bewilderment arise, and only that response carries ethical weight. A second account in a related source agrees that knowing "I saw such a thing" needs a name and its meaning to come first, though it counts the steps differently. My interpretation, beyond the source, is that the moment of recognition quietly decides the reaction. If a stranger is recognised as "threat" or "one of us", the feeling follows the label rather than the person. The tension is that recognition is fast and useful, yet it is also where a mistaken or inherited category can enter unnoticed.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: moments when people revise a first reaction after discovering they had recognised someone or something wrongly, such as a sound at night, a stranger at a gate or an unfamiliar food. The research would ask what they first took it to be and how the feeling changed. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How much of what we feel about something was settled by what we took it to be?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's moment-by-moment sequence is doctrinal analysis, not measured timing, and must not be presented as neuroscience. The two sources disagree on the steps. Some reactions, such as startle or disgust, may happen before any naming. People may also react to tone, context or memory rather than to a label. Observed behaviour cannot show which recognition someone made.
