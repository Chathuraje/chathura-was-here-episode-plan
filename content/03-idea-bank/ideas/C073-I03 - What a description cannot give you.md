# C073-I03 — What a description cannot give you

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Some things cannot be understood from another person's description. Without direct experience we rely on inference and trust, and even a little first-hand experience changes what a description can convey.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source distinguishes understanding built gradually by listening and reflecting from a direct seeing that happens at once. It says its subject cannot be known merely by hearing from others; each person must see it for themselves. Its illustration: colours described to someone who has never seen do not come across, while someone who once saw a little can grasp something. Meanwhile, it names three ways people accept what they have not experienced: the testimony of those who have, reasoning, and inference by contrast (where there is darkness, there is light). The tension, in my interpretation, is that people constantly act on second-hand understanding while sensing it is not the same as knowing, and must decide whom to trust and what small experience of their own can anchor the rest.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: passing on something known by experience, such as a craft, reading sea or weather, or surviving a disaster. The inquiry would ask what learners could take from being told and what changed once they tried it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we decide to trust an account of something we have never experienced?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's claim that nibbāna is reached only by supramundane insight is doctrinal and not endorsed here. Its blindness illustration must not become a metaphor for ignorance; people blind from birth have full understanding of their own. Much does transfer well through language and teaching, and failed learning may reflect poor teaching rather than a limit of description.
