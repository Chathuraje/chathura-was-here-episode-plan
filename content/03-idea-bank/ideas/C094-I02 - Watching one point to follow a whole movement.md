# C094-I02 — Watching one point to follow a whole movement

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Following a moving thing along its whole path can scatter attention. Holding attention at the single place where the movement passes can let the whole of it be known without chasing it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary warns that if the mind travels in with the breath towards chest and navel and back out again, it scatters. The practitioner stays instead at the nose-tip where the breath touches. A gatekeeper does not chase people inside or outside the city but checks each one who reaches the gate. A carpenter sawing a log keeps attention where the teeth meet the wood, not on the teeth moving back and forth, yet knows every tooth passes, sees his effort and sees the log cut through. The source adds that the fixed point, in-breath and out-breath are not one thought's object, yet none is unknown. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the urge to track everything competes with the steadier knowledge of staying where the action happens.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: skilled work where attention stays at one working point while a larger movement passes through it, such as a sawyer, a weaver at the loom or a traffic officer at a junction. The inquiry would ask what they watch and what they know without watching directly. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When does staying in one place let us see more than following everything?

## Limits and alternatives

The sources disagree: the lay manual says to try to see the breath from nose-tip to navel, while the commentary forbids following it. This card follows the commentary only. The similes serve meditation, not a theory of skill. Many tasks need attention that scans or anticipates, and fixed attention misses events elsewhere. Knowledge at a working point may come from trained feel rather than from where attention sits.
