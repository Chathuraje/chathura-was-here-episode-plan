# C022-I03 — Measuring yourself too low to try

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited reading. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Ranking yourself below others can shape what you attempt, just as ranking yourself above them does. The belief that "people like us" cannot learn, give or be happy can close doors before anyone else does.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source treats pride as a wrong *measurement* of one's own standing, not simply as arrogance. It names three forms: "I am better", "I am equal" and "I am lower". In the third, a person tells themselves they have no wealth, education, relatives or luck, and asks how lowly people like them could study, give, be content or rise. The source counts this as the same act of self-measurement as boasting, and says such a person declines. My interpretation, which goes beyond the text, is that the tension lies between an honest reading of real disadvantage and a self-image that turns disadvantage into a verdict on what one may attempt. The source does not discuss where such a self-image comes from.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: moments when someone decides whether a school, a skill, a public role or an act of giving is "for people like me". The research could include people who changed that judgement and what changed it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Where does the sense of what "people like us" can do come from, and what shifts it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source classification is doctrinal and moral, not a psychological account of self-esteem. Low self-estimates are often accurate readings of discrimination, poverty, caste or gender barriers. They are not personal faults, and this card must not blame people for structural exclusion. Declining to try may be caution, care for family or a sensible use of scarce resources.
