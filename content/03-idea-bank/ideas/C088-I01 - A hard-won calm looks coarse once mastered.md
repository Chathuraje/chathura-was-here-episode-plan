# C088-I01 — A hard-won calm looks coarse once mastered

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A state reached through long effort can feel complete at first. Only once it is familiar and fully in hand do its rough edges show, and moving on then means loosening a real attachment to it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes a meditator who has reached a settled state of mind and practised it until it is fully under control. Only then, on stepping out and reviewing it, does one of its parts appear coarse, and a quieter state beyond it appears peaceful. The text does not say the earlier state was bad. It names a "near enemy" close to it and an attachment (a longing for it) that must be given up before the next effort can begin. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that people often become fond of what they worked hardest to reach. Sometimes the flaw cannot be seen from inside the struggle. It becomes visible only after mastery and a deliberate look back. The tension lies between gratitude for an achievement and noticing that it is not the end.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who spent years reaching a stable income, a craft standard or a settled home life, and who later describe noticing what that stability still lacked. The inquiry would ask when and how the noticing came. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Why do the limits of something we fought for often become visible only after we have it firmly?

## Limits and alternatives

The source concerns absorption states in meditation, which are doctrinal and are not presented here as psychology. It does not say that every achievement should be surpassed. Seeing flaws later may instead come from boredom, changed circumstances, hedonic adaptation or outside comparison.
