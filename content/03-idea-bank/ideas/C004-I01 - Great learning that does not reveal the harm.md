# C004-I01 — Great learning that does not reveal the harm

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A person can be highly educated and skilled while a harm they cause does not register to them as harm. The kind of not-seeing involved is different from lacking information.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary makes delusion the root of every harmful state of mind: a darkness that hides the nature of what is in front of the mind, so fault and no fault are not seen. It insists this is not low intelligence. Many clever, highly educated people harm living beings for bodily pleasure or sport, and scientists build weapons that can destroy countries in a moment. They act, the text says, "without knowing it is a fault". It sets beside them a different case: people who know the teaching and do wrong knowing it is wrong. The tension, as I interpret it, is that "did they know?" has two meanings. One concerns facts and skill. The other concerns whether the harm is seen as harm at all. Expertise can grow in the first while the second stays dark.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: fields where skill is high and consequences fall on others, such as technical, industrial or scientific work, and moments when practitioners describe suddenly seeing an effect they had not counted before. The research would focus on changes in seeing, not on accusing anyone. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What makes a harm visible to someone who already knows all the facts?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's examples reflect its own doctrinal position, including rebirth in hell, which is not carried here. They must not be used to label people who fish, hunt, eat meat or work in science, many of whom act from livelihood or reasoned views. People may see a harm clearly and judge it justified, which is a disagreement, not blindness. Others may see it and act from pressure or fear.
