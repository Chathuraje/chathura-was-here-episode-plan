# C077-I03 — Pleasure measured against what you are used to

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Whether something feels fine or poor depends partly on what a person is accustomed to. Great comfort can make good things seem dull, and hunger can make plain food taste wonderful.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source needs a standard for calling sights, sounds, smells, tastes and touches "desirable" or "undesirable". It refuses to use the very wealthy, because to them even something equal to a divine object may seem disagreeable. It also refuses the very poor, who struggle to get rice and water, because to them broken-rice gruel and spoiled meat taste very sweet. The measure is set instead by people in the middle, who meet both pleasant and unpleasant things and can tell the difference. The source adds that momentary liking can be distorted, delighting in what is poor or resenting what is fine. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that expectations quietly reset what counts as enjoyable. A hard life can make small things precious, and rising comfort can make each gain feel ordinary.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people whose circumstances changed sharply, such as families who moved from scarcity to relative plenty or the reverse, or elders comparing food from lean years with food today. The research would ask what used to feel like a treat and what feels like one now. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Does having more change what we enjoy, or only what we notice?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's purpose is classifying objects for karmic result, and it holds that an underlying desirable quality stays fixed, so it does not endorse pure relativity. Its examples must not romanticise poverty or suggest poor people are easily satisfied. As alternatives, enjoyment is also shaped by health, company, culture and memory, and people may recall past pleasures through nostalgia.
