# C013-I03 — Answering each inner obstacle with its opposite

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Different inner obstacles give way to different counter-states. Resentment gives way to gladness, heaviness to active engagement, and doubt to close examination. No single remedy fits all of them.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source pairs five obstacles to steady attention with five qualities that push each out. Anger does not rise while one is glad, so gladness counters ill-will. Heaviness and sluggishness are like a faint man lying on the ground unable to lift his load; the quality of directing the mind onto the object is like a strong man who comes, rouses him, and helps him carry the load to where he wants to go. Restlessness and remorse fade when a subtle ease and well-being arise. Doubt is removed by turning the object over and examining it thoroughly. Steadiness of mind counters sensual craving. The tension, as I read it beyond the text, is that people often try one general remedy, such as rest, distraction or willpower, for every inner difficulty. The source suggests matching: a doubting mind is not helped by cheerfulness, and a heavy mind is not helped by more scrutiny.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how teachers, coaches, carers or counsellors describe telling apart a student or client who is stuck through doubt, through heaviness or through agitation, and what different response each needs. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we tell what kind of stuckness we are in before choosing how to move?

## Limits and alternatives

The pairings are a doctrinal analysis of factors in meditative absorption, not a clinical model, and the displacement lasts only while the counter-state is present (C013-I02). Heaviness may come from illness, grief or depression, which need care, not engagement alone. Doubt in the source means doubt about the Buddha and the teaching; widening it is interpretation.
