# C074-I03 — When giving turns into outdoing others

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited application. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A generous act can quietly become a comparison. Watching others give, a person may want their own offering to stand higher, often without noticing that the wish has changed.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes conceit as a tendency that lies unnoticed and surfaces when conditions suit, "mostly without the person knowing". Its concrete example: after seeing others perform meritorious deeds, a person wants to arrange their own to be grander, and this mostly comes from latent conceit. Conceit rests on wealth, family, virtue or knowledge, and takes three forms: better, equal or lesser. The last two show that comparison, not only superiority, is the mechanism. The tension, as I interpret it, is that the act stays genuinely generous and useful to those who receive it, while the motive underneath shifts from giving to ranking. Neither giver nor observer can easily separate the two.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: communal occasions where giving is visible, such as temple festivals, almsgivings, funerals or charity drives, asking givers how they describe the pull between offering and being seen to offer more. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we give where others can see, how do we know whether we are giving or competing?

## Limits and alternatives

This rests on one explicit sentence within a doctrinal classification. It does not establish that public giving is usually competitive. Larger offerings may reflect gratitude, a vow, family custom or duty in proportion to wealth. Outsiders cannot judge a giver's motive, so the card must not be used to label people as proud.
