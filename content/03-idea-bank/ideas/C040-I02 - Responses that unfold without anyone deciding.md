# C040-I02 — Responses that unfold without anyone deciding

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A response to something we sense can run through a whole series of steps, from noticing to checking to enjoying, without any inner person choosing each step.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source asks how the ordered chain of mind-states in a cognitive process comes about. Its answer: there is no maker or doer. Once the object strikes the sense, the steps arise by their own nature, as creepers wind round a nearby tree. Its simile: a man asleep under a mango tree hears a ripe fruit fall, wakes, sees it, picks it up, squeezes and smells it to decide it is ripe, eats it, swallows the lingering taste and sleeps again. A related source adds that no act, such as speaking, giving or understanding a sentence, is done by one mind-state. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that everyday responses often run as sequences nobody consciously directs, even when they include something that looks like judging. The tension is ethical: if no one inside decides each step, how do people still own what they do?

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: skilled or habitual routines people perform fluently and only afterwards describe, such as judging fruit by touch and smell or reacting on a familiar road. The inquiry would compare what people do with how they explain it. No person, event or outcome is established.

## Open human question

When a response runs smoothly without our choosing each step, in what sense is it still ours?

## Limits and alternatives

The no-doer claim is doctrinal, not a finding about free will or automaticity. The simile's stages stand for momentary mind-states, not deliberate acts or conscious checking. The source does not say people lack responsibility; it does not discuss responsibility at all, so that question is my extension. Fluent action may also reflect earlier deliberate training that made the steps automatic.
