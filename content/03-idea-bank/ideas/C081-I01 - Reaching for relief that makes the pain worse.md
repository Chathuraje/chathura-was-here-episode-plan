# C081-I01 — Reaching for relief that makes the pain worse

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

The wish to be rid of a pain can lead a person to grab whatever seems to soothe it. When the chosen remedy does not suit the wound, it inflames what it was meant to heal.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source explains a chain of experience through a boil. Pain is its ache. Craving is the wish to treat it. Clinging is seizing unsuitable remedies, and what follows is like smearing on the wrong ointment, bringing swelling and bursting. A second image agrees: a cow tormented by fire and blows drinks hot water that gives no relief and harms it. The sequence is the source's; the application is my interpretation. The wish for relief is reasonable, even urgent. The trouble lies in grabbing a remedy without seeing what it will do, so the escape from pain becomes its next cause.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who describe a way of coping that first felt like relief and later deepened the trouble, such as borrowing to cover an earlier debt or drinking to quiet grief, and how some noticed the pattern. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How does someone tell, in the middle of pain, a remedy that heals from one that only soothes for now?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The similes illustrate a doctrinal chain across lives; "unsuitable remedy" means clinging and kamma, not medicine or coping. Behaviour that looks self-defeating may be the only option available, a rational trade-off, or the effect of addiction, poverty or illness rather than a failure to see.
