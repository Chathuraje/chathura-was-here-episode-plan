# C028-I01 — Doubt that leaves no room to decide

**Status:** Held. C008's sources restrict this doubt to religious and doctrinal doubt and explicitly exclude ordinary uncertainty, so a practical-choice reading would alter the teaching. If it is revived, consider merging it into C021-I01 (deciding as the opposite of doubt). No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

While a mind is caught in doubt, the capacity to settle on something is absent. So is the plain wish to act.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source's table of mental factors makes a specific causal remark. In the mind-state joined with doubt, the factor of decision or firm settling is *not* present, *because* doubt is present. The same state also lacks desire-to-act, the simple wish to do something. In the neighbouring state of restlessness, decision can be present, yet desire-to-act is still missing. As I interpret it, doubt is not just a slow route to a decision. For as long as it lasts, it occupies the place where a decision would stand. Energy and thought are still present in the doubting state, so the mind is busy rather than idle. The tension is between that activity and the missing ability to commit. The source does not say how doubt ends. It describes only what cannot share the moment with it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction (held, not for use): moments where people describe being "stuck" on a choice they have thought about at length. Examples could be whether to migrate for work, sell family land, accept a marriage proposal or leave a vocation. The research would ask what finally let a decision become possible, and what the doubting period felt like from inside. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What has to change before someone who keeps thinking about a choice can actually settle on it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source classifies momentary mind-states. It does not describe long periods of indecision, which consist of many moments of different kinds. The C008 source note records that Pradeepika (C008, line 36) excludes ordinary doubt about an unknown matter. The doubt meant is doubt about the Buddha, the teaching, the community, moral conduct, rebirth and dependent origination. Applying it to practical choices would therefore change the teaching. That is why this card is held. Delay in deciding may come from a lack of information, real risk, conflicting obligations, depression, or deference to family authority, not doubt as such. Outward hesitation does not show what a person is experiencing.
