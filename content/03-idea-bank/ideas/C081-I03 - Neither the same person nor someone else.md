# C081-I03 — Neither the same person nor someone else

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A changing life can be misread in two opposite ways: as one unchanged self, or as a string of unconnected people. The source proposes a continuity in which each stage grows out of the last without being identical to it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source gives two ways of viewing a chain of causes. Seen as continuity, like a seed becoming sprout, trunk and branches, nothing is simply cut off; misread as sameness, this becomes belief in something permanent. Seen as distinctness, each stage has its own character; misread as separation, one continuous series is treated as different beings. Within the chain, it says, there is "neither oneself nor another". My interpretation, beyond the text, is that people face this choice about their own past. "That was not me" loosens the link to what they did; "I am exactly who I was" denies real change. Forgiveness, responsibility and hope turn on it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people looking back across a large change, such as release from prison, recovery or old age, and how they and their families describe the link between who they were and who they are. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How much of who we were do we carry, and who gets to decide?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The source applies these methods to rebirth and to refuting eternalism and annihilationism; that use is not carried over, and no claim about personal identity in philosophy or psychology is made. A strong sense of a continuing self is a reasonable view, and saying "I am a new person" may be strategic rather than a view about identity.
