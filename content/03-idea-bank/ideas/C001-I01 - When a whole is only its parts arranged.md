# C001-I01 — When a whole is only its parts arranged

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Many things we treat as solid wholes, such as a table, a person or perhaps a place or community, may be names for parts held in a particular arrangement. When the arrangement changes, the “thing” can seem to vanish.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source offers two images. A burning stick whirled in the dark appears as a ring of fire, but only the moving stick is there. A table is several pieces of wood. No single piece is the table, and if the same pieces are scattered or rearranged, there is no table. The name is useful. It points to how the parts are placed, not to something extra. The source applies the same analysis to a human being: body parts and a knowing mind that, examined closely, are a collection of changing phenomena. The human tension, which is my interpretation beyond the text, is that people love, defend and mourn wholes such as a home, a family or a village. They experience those wholes as real. Yet what they lose when things change is often the arrangement, while the parts carry on elsewhere.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: places or groups whose “parts” remain while the whole dissolves or reforms. Examples could be a house taken down and its timber reused, a team or troupe whose members scatter, or a village relocated for a development project. The inquiry would ask what people feel has really been lost or kept. No person, place, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When the parts are still here but the arrangement is gone, what exactly have we lost?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The source's aim is ultimate analysis for liberation, not a theory of community or grief. Its claim that conventional things “do not truly exist” is doctrinal, and the analysis must not be presented as physics. Many philosophical traditions treat wholes as real in their own right. People's sense that a place or relationship is more than its parts is a legitimate view, not an error to correct.
