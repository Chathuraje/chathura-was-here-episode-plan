# C091-I02 — Imagining an admired person present before a wrong

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Someone who habitually keeps an admired person in mind may, at the moment a harmful act occurs to them, feel as if that person were standing in front of them, and hold back out of shame.

## Human mechanism and tension

Among the benefits of recollecting the Buddha, the commentary lists two linked effects. The practitioner comes to feel "I live together with the Buddha". And as soon as the thought "I will do a wrong" arises, the Buddha seems present before them, so they feel shame and fear about doing it. The source describes not a rule or a threat but an imagined presence, built by repeated recollection, that arrives exactly when a harmful intention appears. The tension, in my interpretation beyond the text, is that this restraint rests on a relationship held in the imagination. It may be powerful because it is personal, and fragile for the same reason: if admiration fades, the restraint may go with it. It sits between holding back for self-respect and holding back for how one looks to others.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who say they still ask themselves what a dead parent, a former teacher or a mentor would think before acting, for example when tempted by a shortcut at work or in a family dispute. The inquiry would ask when the imagined presence appears, what it feels like, and whether it ever stops working. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What happens to our restraint when it rests on someone we only imagine watching?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes recollecting the Buddha specifically; extending it to parents or mentors is interpretation. Restraint may instead come from settled values, fear of consequences or lack of opportunity, and people may credit an imagined figure afterwards. Imagined disapproval can become oppressive. An imagined presence cannot be checked from outside.
