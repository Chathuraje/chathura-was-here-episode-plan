## Central meaning

The 28 kinds of material form (රූප) are first described as all sharing eight features: rootless (අහේතුක), conditioned (සප්පච්චය / සඞ්ඛත), object of the taints (සාසව), mundane (ලෞකික), sense-sphere (කාමාවචර), not taking an object (අනාරම්මණ) and not to be abandoned (අප්පහාතබ්බ). They are then split into nine or more pairs: produced/unproduced (නිෂ්පන්න/අනිෂ්පන්න), internal/external (අජ්ඣත්තික/බාහිර), base/non-base (වස්තු), door/non-door (ද්වාර), faculty/non-faculty (ඉන්ද්‍රිය), gross/subtle (ඖදාරික/සූක්ෂ්ම, also near/far, impinging/non-impinging), grasped/ungrasped (උපාදින්න/අනුපාදින්න), visible/invisible (සනිදස්සන), object-taking/non-object-taking (ගෝචරග්ගාහික) and inseparable/separable (අවිනිබ්භෝග). A full chart (Pradeepika lines 260–305) marks each of the 28 forms against 24 columns.

## Essential distinctions

- **What must be abandoned is not matter but the wanting for it.** Pradeepika lines 88–92: matter is not a thing to be abandoned; what is to be abandoned is desire, passion, delight and craving concerning matter. When craving for matter is gone, matter is said to be abandoned. Margaya line 3 gives only the short form: matter is not to be abandoned the way defilements are.
- **Internal vs external is defined by ownership-feeling, not location.** Pradeepika line 120: the five sense-faculties are called internal because the sense of "mine" and craving for them is very great; other matter is not held so intensely and is called external. Margaya line 7 adds that all bodily matter is generally regarded as "I, mine", but the sense-faculties are singled out because self-regard and craving are strongest there.
- **Grasped (උපාදින්න)** matter is kamma-born matter that is seized by craving and views as "mine" and "I" (Pradeepika line 199; Margaya line 21, where the grasping is said to be strongest for kamma-born matter as the main part of the body).
- **Eye and ear take distant objects; nose, tongue and body need contact** (Pradeepika line 238; Margaya line 27 with the eyelid example: what touches the eye cannot be seen).
- **Doors.** The five sense-faculties are doors for sense-consciousness; bodily and vocal intimation are doors for bodily and verbal action (Pradeepika line 153–157; Margaya line 11).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on all counts (8/20 inseparable, 12/16 gross/subtle, 18/10 grasped, etc.). Margaya's subtle list says "විකාර රූප පසය" (five, including the two intimations) where Pradeepika lists the two intimations and three alteration forms separately (line 195); totals agree at 16. Pradeepika alone gives the "abandon the craving, not the matter" passage in full, the chart, and the "abandoning" modes (තදඞ්ග, විෂ්කම්භණ, සමුච්ඡේද). Margaya alone gives the eyelid and sound-direction examples. No genuine disagreement found.

Suspected text errors: Pradeepika line 157 "කර්මවාර" is presumably කර්මද්වාර. Line 78 (සාසව) is grammatically garbled ("තමාම අරභයා උපන් ආශ්‍රව සහිත"), though the meaning is recoverable. Line 238 "සොත ප්‍රසාද රූපසද" is presumably රූපයද.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The eightfold classification, the kamma-born/other split, the heart-base (හදය වස්තු) and the sex-faculty matter are doctrinal physiology and must not be presented as anatomy or biology. The perception account (eye does not touch its object) is a historical theory of perception, not a finding. The idea that the sense-faculties are held as "self" most strongly is a doctrinal ranking, not a measured psychological result.

## Human meaning paragraph

Two threads carry human meaning. First, the source locates the problem not in things but in the wanting directed at them: matter is not something to get rid of; desire and delight concerning it are. My interpretation is that a person may try to solve a craving by removing or destroying the object, whereas the text points at the relation. Second, the source grades how strongly different parts of the body are held as "me" and "mine", naming the sense-faculties as most intensely claimed. The everyday extension, which goes beyond the text, is that the parts of ourselves through which we meet the world may feel most like our self.

## Idea decisions

- **C067-I01 — Giving up the wanting, not the thing.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 88–92, supported briefly by Margaya line 3.
- **C067-I02 — Why the senses feel most like me.** Held. The source gives a clear claim (Pradeepika line 120; Margaya lines 7 and 21), but the ranking is doctrinal, explained in a single sentence, and an everyday reading risks medicalising sensory loss.
- Rejected candidate: "Too close to see" (Margaya line 27, eyelid example). This is a perception theory, and turning it into a metaphor would be invented rather than sourced.
- Rejected candidate: the gross/subtle and visible/invisible pairs. Pure classification with no human mechanism.
