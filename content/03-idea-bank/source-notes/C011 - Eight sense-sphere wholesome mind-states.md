# C011 — Eight sense-sphere wholesome mind-states

## Central meaning

Of the 54 sense-sphere mind-states, 24 are "beautiful" (කාමසොභන) because factors such as faith and mindfulness accompany them (Pradeepika line 25). The eight great wholesome states (මහා කුසල්) arise in ordinary good deeds: giving, keeping precepts, meditation, service, listening to and teaching the teaching, sharing merit (Pradeepika line 51; Margaya line 18 lists the ten meritorious deeds). They are divided by three pairs: joyful or neutral feeling (සොමනස්ස / උපෙක්ඛා), with or without knowledge (ඤාණ සම්පයුත්ත / විප්පයුත්ත), and unprompted or prompted (අසංඛාරික / සසංඛාරික). Margaya line 16 notes they are called "great" not because they are higher but because they are common to most people everywhere.

## Essential distinctions

- **Joyful versus neutral.** Joy arises when conditions are favourable: strong faith, a suitable gift and worthy recipient, right time and place, good friends, knowing the benefits, freedom from illness, and a joyful rebirth-consciousness (Pradeepika lines 53–66; Margaya line 3). If any is missing, the good act is done with neutral feeling. Line 101: even when obstacles prevent being pleased, a good act can still be done with understanding.
- **With or without knowledge.** Knowledge here means believing in action and its result, in cause and effect, understanding the teaching well, and knowing how to avoid wrong (Pradeepika line 70). Pradeepika contrasts this sharply with worldly learning, which can serve killing, weapon-making and poison-making; the wise person in the teaching's sense tries to separate from the unwholesome, does good for self and others and strives to rise in virtue, not only intellect (line 70). Good deeds done by habit, without reflecting on results, and children following parents to the temple, arise without knowledge (line 85; line 97).
- **Prompted versus unprompted.** Unprompted: giving with non-greed and compassion, by one's own hand, with firm conviction. Prompted: giving with the idea of pleasing others or on another's order (Pradeepika line 89).
- **A good act is not one continuous state.** From the intention to give until the giving ends, or through a day of precepts, it should not be thought that only these wholesome states arise. They arise only in the moments of mindful attention to the deed. Envy (ඊර්ෂ්‍යා), conceit (මාන) or unrelated contrary thoughts arise between them and weaken the deed (Pradeepika line 111). Line 74 adds that the mind should be kept clean before and after the deed and not surrounded by greed, hatred or delusion.
- **Strength of intention.** Margaya lines 20–26: intention (චේතනා) turns the mind; a sharp, large intention makes a deed strong. Example: the same mind-state gives a cent one day and a rupee another, and the rupee intention is greater. Margaya line 28: knowledge is the main factor in a deed's excellence; feeling and prompting are secondary.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both agree on the eightfold structure and on examples. Pradeepika gives conditions, the worldly-learning contrast, the habit examples and the warning about interruption by envy and conceit. Margaya gives the ten meritorious deeds, the role and strength of intention, and a ranking (knowledge-associated always higher; joyful above neutral and unprompted above prompted "in general"). Pradeepika gives no such ranking, so this is complementary, not conflicting. One tension within Pradeepika: line 74 says a person who serves others with great respect, food, drink and housing but does not believe in karmic result has only knowledge-dissociated good states. This ties "knowledge" to doctrinal belief.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- "Knowledge" includes belief in karma and its results. It cannot be equated with general wisdom, intelligence or secular ethics without labelling the move as analogy.
- Rebirth-consciousness as a condition for joy or knowledge, and past-life merit as a source of wisdom (lines 59, 74, 78), are doctrinal.
- The claim that kind people who do not believe in karma do lesser good (line 74) must not be used to judge real people.
- Strength of result by amount given (Margaya line 26) concerns karmic result, not a measure of generosity.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe good action as varied inside. The same outward deed can be done gladly or flatly, with an understanding of why it matters or out of habit, of one's own accord or because someone asked. Knowing a great deal is not the same as the understanding that counts, because learning can serve harm. And a good deed is not one steady state: envy, pride and stray thoughts arise between the moments of attention to it. The everyday readings below are interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C011-I01 — Knowledge that serves harm as easily as good.** Held. Based on Pradeepika line 70. The mechanism substantially overlaps C004-I01 (Great learning that does not reveal the harm; same book, same weapons example) and C019-I01 (Abilities that take the colour of their aim). Recommended: merge into C004-I01, keeping C011 as a supporting reference for the definition of beneficial understanding.
- **C011-I02 — Doing good by habit without asking why.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 85 and 97, Margaya lines 28 and 36.
- **C011-I03 — Envy and pride slipping into a generous act.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika line 111 and line 74. Distinct from C074-I03 (comparison as the starting motive of giving): this card concerns interruption during the act.
- Rejected candidate: *Doing good when it brings no joy* (line 101 and the conditions for joy). Sourced, but the conditions list is mostly doctrinal and the source gives little mechanism. Kept as a held question in the batch report.
- Rejected candidate: *Giving because someone asked* (prompted wholesome states, line 89). Same mechanism as C006-I03, which covers prompting; referenced there rather than duplicated.
- Rejected candidate: *A rupee means more than a cent* (Margaya line 26). It concerns karmic strength of result and would invite judging gifts by amount.
