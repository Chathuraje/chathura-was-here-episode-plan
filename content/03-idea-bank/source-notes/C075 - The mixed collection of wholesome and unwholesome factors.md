# C075 — The mixed collection of wholesome and unwholesome factors

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–438). Frontmatter declares Book 2, chapter 7, PDF 197–227 (printed 186–216). Page markers run from line 43 (PDF 198 / printed 187) to line 436 (PDF 227 / printed 216).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–138). Heading `# 2. මිශ්‍ර‍ක සංග්‍ර‍හය`, with lesson markers at lines 3, 35 and 96. No page locators.

## Central meaning

The "mixed" collection groups factors that can be wholesome, unwholesome or neither. It has seven parts. There are six roots (greed, hatred, delusion and their opposites) and seven absorption factors (initial and sustained application, rapture, one-pointedness, and three feelings). There are twelve path factors: eight right ones and four wrong ones (wrong view, intention, effort and concentration). There are twenty-two faculties (sense bases, sex, life, mind, feelings, faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom and three supramundane knowledges) and nine powers (faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, shame, fear of wrongdoing, shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing). There are four dominants (desire-to-act, mind, effort and investigation) and four nutriments (food, contact, volition and consciousness).

## Essential distinctions

- **The same factor serves either direction.** Initial application, effort and one-pointedness are "common" factors. They are path factors of a good path in wholesome minds and of a bad path in unwholesome ones (Pradeepika lines 146 and 204, Margaya lines 26–28). Margaya (line 30) says an act of killing contains a four-factor "absorption" and a three-factor wrong path. Pradeepika (lines 142–144) defines wrong effort as the exertion put into completing killing and similar acts, and wrong concentration as strong, harmful one-pointedness on an object while doing them.
- **Powers are measured by what cannot shake them.** Each power suppresses its opposite and cannot be shaken by it (Margaya line 100, Pradeepika line 332). Shame and fear of wrongdoing are powers because shamelessness cannot move them. Equally, shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing are powers because shame and fear cannot move them (Margaya line 100, Pradeepika lines 362–368). Powers exist only where effort is present (Margaya line 100, Pradeepika line 344).
- **Dominants versus faculties.** A faculty governs only within its own function, like a vassal king. A dominant overrides the others, like an emperor (Margaya line 119, Pradeepika line 376). Only one of desire-to-act, mind, effort or investigation dominates at a time, even when several are present (Margaya line 117, Pradeepika line 376). Margaya (line 121) glosses desire-to-act as a strong wanting to get something done: "if there is the wanting, nothing cannot be done". It can drive wholesome or unwholesome minds.
- **Faculties.** Margaya (line 41) explains that eye-consciousness follows the eye's sensitivity: when that is defective, things look smaller, larger or differently coloured. Margaya (line 91) says the faculties are taught to show that no inner self does their work.
- **Nutriments.** Contact "feeds" feeling, which leads to craving (Margaya line 132, Pradeepika lines 426–430). Volition "feeds" rebirth (line 432). Food sustains the body (lines 418–424).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the seven groups, their members and their reduction to underlying factors. Pradeepika adds characteristic, function and manifestation for each path factor and a doctrinal etymology of "faculty" (lines 224–238). Margaya adds clearer explanations of how faculties and dominants govern, and the killing example.

Genuine disagreements and differences:
- **Wrong concentration and doubt.** Margaya (line 24) says wrong concentration is one-pointedness in unwholesome minds *except* the doubt-accompanied mind, whose one-pointedness does not even reach faculty or power level (lines 24 and 73). Pradeepika (line 144) says wrong concentration is one-pointedness in *all twelve* unwholesome minds. However, its line 150 assigns wrong concentration to "the remaining eight" in a way that does not address the doubt mind.
- **Scope of dominants.** Margaya (lines 109–115) restricts desire-to-act, mind and effort dominants to two- and three-rooted impulsion (javana) minds. Pradeepika (line 385) says desire-to-act can dominate in 69 minds (all except the two delusion-rooted and eighteen rootless), and for effort (line 405) it gives the 73 minds in which effort *occurs*. It gives 52 impulsion minds for the mind dominant (lines 389–397). Pradeepika's figures appear to describe where the factor is present rather than where it can dominate. This is left unresolved.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- Pradeepika's roots passage (lines 45–53) says that weak past good kamma leads to rebirth as a lowly person or "someone else's slave", living with difficulty. This is doctrinal, and in everyday language it would blame poverty on the poor. It must never be used.
- The sex faculties: Margaya (line 57) says men's and women's thoughts differ because of the sex faculty, and Pradeepika (line 268) lists girls' play with sewing and cooking pots as "female activity". These are doctrinal and historical claims. They must not become premises and must not be presented as biology.
- The eye-defect explanation (Margaya line 41) is a traditional physiological account, not modern optics.
- Rebirth nutriments, path-stage knowledges, Brahma worlds and the Buddha's eight roots at conception are doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources insist that capacities people usually admire, such as focused attention, sustained effort, careful thought and a strong will to get something done, are not good in themselves. The same capacities appear in harmful acts and make them effective. They also describe inner forces as competing for control. At any moment one drive leads and the others follow, and a moral sense and its absence can each be firm enough that the other cannot move it. The reading below is interpretation: what matters is the aim a capacity serves and which force is in charge, not how strong the capacity is.

## Idea decisions

- **C075-I01 — When focus and effort serve a harmful aim.** Accepted. Based on wrong effort and wrong concentration and the killing example, with C074's "cleverness in harm is not wisdom" (Pradeepika C074 line 434) as supporting material.
- **C075-I02 — Which inner drive leads a hard undertaking.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the four dominants, only one leading at a time.
- **C075-I03 — When shamelessness is as steady as conscience.** Held. The source gives only definitions of mutual overpowering, with no example or explanation of how shamelessness becomes strong, and the card carries a high risk of morally labelling documentary subjects.
- Rejected candidate: *contact feeds feeling, feeling feeds craving* (nutriments). The rebirth framing is doctrinal, and the chain is too generic to form a distinct mechanism. It is better handled under dependent origination concepts elsewhere.
- Rejected candidate: faculties shaping perception (eye defects). This is a physiological claim, not a human tension.
- Rejected candidate: sex faculties. This would reproduce gender stereotypes as doctrine.
- Rejected candidate: roots as a tree bearing fortune. This is doctrinal and ethically harmful (see above).
- Rejected candidate: faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration and wisdom as faculties. These are definitions only, with no mechanism beyond the list.
