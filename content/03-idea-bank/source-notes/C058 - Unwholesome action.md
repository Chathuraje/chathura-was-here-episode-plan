# C058 — Unwholesome action

## Central meaning

Unwholesome action (අකුසල කර්ම) is the intention (චේතනා) present in unwholesome mind-states. It is grouped by "door": bodily (killing, stealing, sexual misconduct), verbal (lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle chatter) and mental (covetousness අභිද්ධ්‍යා, ill will ව්‍යාපාද, wrong view මිථ්‍යාදෘෂ්ටි). These are the ten unwholesome courses of action (අකුසල කර්මපථ). Each has a set of factors (අඞ්ග). When all are complete the act becomes a full "course of action" that, in the doctrine, can give a rebirth result. When a factor is missing it gives only lesser results during life (Pradeepika lines 45–49).

## Essential distinctions

- **Door versus action.** An action typical of one door can occur through another. Ordering someone to kill or steal is verbal (lines 68, 88). A lie or slander written on paper or signalled by hand is bodily (lines 147, 166, 183, 210). Mental actions are strongest when they stay purely in the mind (line 220).
- **Factors that complete an act.** Killing needs five: a living being, knowing it is one, the intention to kill, effort, and death (lines 53–57). Stealing is parallel (lines 82–86). Lying needs four, the last being that others come to know or accept it (lines 159–162). Divisive speech is complete only when the others believe and are divided (lines 178–181). Idle chatter becomes a full course of action only when listeners take it as true (line 208).
- **Weight.** The gravity of killing varies with the virtue and body size of the being killed. With a large animal, the effort and time are longer, so killing-intentions repeat until death (line 74). The gravity of a lie varies with the harm caused and the virtue of the person deceived (line 164). Harsh speech varies with harshness and the target's virtue (line 199).
- **Roots.** Lying, slander and idle chatter arise from greed when done for gain or praise, and from hatred when done for revenge, anger or to harm (lines 170, 187, 212–216). Stealing likewise (line 88). Killing always involves aversion, even when done "smiling", because the being's life is an obstacle to one's purpose (line 70).
- **Thresholds in the mental actions.** Covetousness as mere relish for another's property is an action; the strong wish to make it one's own is the full course (line 229). Mere anger gives only minor results; wishing a being's destruction is the full course of ill will (line 239).
- **Wrong views.** Pradeepika lists the 62 views (lines 253–397). Among them, four kinds of "eel-wriggling" (අමරා වික්ෂේප, lines 299–309): refusing to affirm or deny anything, out of fear of lying, fear of attachment, fear of being cross-examined by the wise, or plain dullness. Fixed wrong view (නියත මිථ්‍යාදෘෂ්ටි: no cause, no result of action, no efficacy of action) is a course of action (lines 399–409). Belief that good outcomes come from auspicious sights, sounds, times, planets or offerings "apart from the results of action" is also classed as wrong view (lines 413–417).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the ten courses, the three doors and the roots (Margaya lines 3–13; Pradeepika lines 421–425). Pradeepika adds the factors, weights, results and the 62 views. It also adds delusion-originated variants of the hatred-rooted and greed-rooted acts (lines 421–425), which Margaya handles by saying delusion is the root of all unwholesome acts (line 13). Margaya's file also covers material that overlaps the Pradeepika extract for C059: sense-sphere wholesome action (lines 15–21), exalted wholesome action (lines 23–25) and results of unwholesome action (lines 27–33). Its note that even Buddhas experience unwholesome-result consciousness when seeing unpleasant sights (line 31) is not in either Pradeepika extract. Its point that restlessness-associated intention is too weak to cause a woeful rebirth (line 33) is not in the C058 Pradeepika extract; the C059 Pradeepika extract states the exclusion without the reason (line 367). No direct disagreement found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

All statements about results — rebirth in woeful states, being reborn poor, female or "neuter" for sexual misconduct (lines 139–141), cosmic durations — are doctrinal. The claims about sex and gender as punishment are doctrinal, dated and must not become premises. The list of twenty "protected women" (lines 105–133) reflects historical social arrangements, treats women as protected by or belonging to others, and is not a usable framework. The weighting of killing by the victim's virtue and body size is a karmic claim, not a moral measurement. The wrong-view section on auspicious times and planetary rites (lines 413–417) is a doctrinal judgement about practices still common in Sri Lanka; it must not be used to portray communities as mistaken. The 62 views rest on meditation, past-life recall and cosmology.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source treats a harmful act as something assembled from parts: what is there, what the person knows, what they intend, what effort they make and what actually happens. For speech, one part lies outside the speaker: the listener has to believe. The mental actions describe a threshold between feeling something (relish, irritation) and settling on a wish (this should be mine; that person should be ruined). Separately, the "eel-wriggling" views describe a person who avoids every clear answer, and the source names the fears behind it. The everyday reading is interpretation: harm through words depends on uptake by others, hostile feeling becomes something more serious when it hardens into a wish, and evasiveness can be driven by fear rather than openness.

## Idea decisions

- **C058-I01 — Words that only do harm once believed.** Accepted. Based on the "others know or accept" factor for lying, divisive speech and idle chatter.
- **C058-I02 — When annoyance hardens into wishing someone harm.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the thresholds in covetousness and ill will.
- **C058-I03 — Dodging every answer for fear of being wrong.** Accepted. Based on the four kinds of eel-wriggling.
- Rejected candidate: *Seeing another's life as an obstacle* (line 70). Striking, but it is a claim about which mind-state must be present, and turning it into a statement about people who kill would be diagnosis. The nearby human mechanism is already covered by C007-I01 (When an obstacle to wanting becomes an enemy).
- Rejected candidate: *Same theft, greed or revenge* (line 88). This shares a mechanism with C005-I01 (Same act, different reason).
- Rejected candidate: *Trusting auspicious times over one's own actions* (lines 413–417). The text is a doctrinal judgement on living cultural practice; a card would risk moralising and stereotyping.
- Rejected candidate: *An act is complete only when intent, knowledge and outcome line up* (lines 49–57). Legal-moral classification; it could support later research but it is too close to a doctrine of culpability to stand alone as a human mechanism. Partly reflected in C058-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Harm scaled by who is harmed* (lines 74, 164, 199). Doctrinal weighting.
