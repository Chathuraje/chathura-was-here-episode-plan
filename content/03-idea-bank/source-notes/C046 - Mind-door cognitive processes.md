## Central meaning

A mind-door process (මනෝද්වාර වීථිය) is a series of mind-moments that takes an object through the mind alone, not through the five senses. The sources classify the sense-sphere (කාම ජවන) mind-door processes by the object's lifespan and clarity. A present material object that lasts seventeen mind-moments gives five "clear" (විභූතාරම්මණ) processes and two "faint" (අවිභූතාරම්මණ) processes, depending on how many moments have already passed. Past or future matter, mind, mental factors, nibbāna and concepts have no fixed lifespan and give one clear and one faint process. Clear processes end with two registration moments (තදාරම්මණ); faint processes do not. Margaya gives the total as nine sense-sphere mind-door processes (line 3).

## Essential distinctions

- **Clear and faint objects.** Clarity decides whether registration (තදාරම්මණ) follows the seven impulsion moments (Pradeepika lines 63, 67, 79–81; Margaya lines 31, 52).
- **The sequence after a sense contact** (Pradeepika line 117 and lines 119–151). After one eye-door process, four mind-door processes follow. The first takes the object as past. The second takes it by its colour (නීලාදී). The third takes a concept such as "woman", "man", "cloth" or "vessel", and "it is in this process that one recognises what the object is" (line 143). Only the fourth mind-door process has impulsions that like, delight, become angry or become confused, and only its impulsions are "action-effective" (අර්ථ සාධක) and can complete wholesome or unwholesome courses of action (lines 117, 149). The 28 impulsions of the earlier four processes give only minor results (line 149).
- **Wise and unwise attention** (Pradeepika line 117). Reflecting on the object as impermanent gives a wholesome impulsion process; taking it as permanent gives an unwholesome one.
- **Margaya's technical point** (lines 52–54): registration can only take sense-sphere objects, so the faint process without registration actually covers more kinds of object.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the five clear and two faint seventeen-moment processes and on the two processes for objects without a fixed lifespan. Pradeepika alone gives the five-process recognition sequence ending in reaction. Margaya's parallel account appears in C048 (`Abhidharma Margaya.md` of Concept 48, lines 23–27). It has one sense process followed by three mind-door processes (past object, name, meaning) and calls this "one teacher's view", noting that books differ. This is a genuine disagreement in the number and content of steps and is recorded, not averaged. Margaya (line 3) also divides mind-door processes into sense-sphere and absorption (අර්පණා) impulsion; Pradeepika's extract here treats only the sense-sphere processes.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Mind-moment counts, seventeen-moment lifespans and the chart notation (ති න ද ම ජ ත භ) are doctrinal analysis, not measurable timings, and must not be equated with neuroscience or reaction times. "Action-effective" refers to karmic result. The claim that registration occurs only for sense-sphere beings (Pradeepika line 115) is cosmological.

## Human meaning paragraph

In Pradeepika's account, seeing something does not immediately produce liking or anger. The mind first holds what was just seen, then its colour, then recognises it under a name such as a person or an object, and only then responds with attraction, trust, irritation or confusion. Only that final response is treated as ethically weighty. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that our reactions attach to what we have recognised and named, which may differ from what was actually there. The source gives no timing that ordinary experience could check.

## Idea decisions

- **C046-I01 — Reacting to what we recognise, not what we see.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika line 117 and lines 137–149, with Margaya C048 lines 23–25 as supporting parallel.
- Rejected candidate: *Faint impressions leave no after-taste* (faint objects lack registration). The distinction is a technical moment count; turning it into a claim about weak memories would invent a mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: *Wise attention changes the response* (line 117). A single sentence, and it overlaps with attention ideas likely drawn from earlier concepts; not developed here.
