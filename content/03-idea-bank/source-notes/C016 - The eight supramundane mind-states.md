## Central meaning

Supramundane consciousness (ලෝකෝත්තර සිත්) takes nibbāna as its object and carries a person beyond the world. There are four path states (stream-entry, once-return, non-return, arahantship) and four fruition states, making eight. Each path arises only once in a person and cuts off particular defilements so that they never arise again (සමුච්ඡේද ප්‍රහාණය). Fruition follows immediately and calms the "fatigue" of the cut-off defilements. There are no supramundane functional states because a path never recurs (Margaya line 21; Pradeepika line 54).

## Essential distinctions

- **Simultaneity.** The eight path factors arise together in one moment (Pradeepika line 54), and the path performs four functions at once, like a lamp that burns the wick, dispels darkness, spreads light and consumes oil "without before or after" (line 56). Margaya (line 3) adds that the eight factors develop gradually in the meditator before the path.
- **Mundane versus supramundane wholesome states.** Mundane wholesome states can displace or suppress defilements but cannot carry one beyond saṃsāra (lines 25–29). Present generosity and virtue are not themselves the path but become a supporting condition if aimed at nibbāna (lines 60, 68).
- **Long preparation.** Attainment requires perfections built over at least a hundred thousand aeons (line 62). Those who awakened quickly on hearing a little teaching had long prior preparation (line 84). Even so, arahantship cannot be reached at once; the paths come in order (line 84).
- **Seven purifications** (lines 64–74), including the purification that distinguishes path from not-path: during insight, ten "corruptions of insight" such as illumination (ඕභාසාදි දශ විධ විදර්ශනොප ක්ලෙශ) arise, and a meditator may take them for the path and go the wrong way (line 70).
- **Balance of calm and insight** yoked equally like two oxen in the path moment (line 76).
- **How defilements are removed** (lines 78–82): not by destroying something present in the path moment, but by making those that "have found ground" in the aggregates unable ever to arise again.
- **Types of noble persons and their rebirths** (lines 108–160), long lists of abandoned defilements (lines 96–100, 126, 142–146, 172), the treasure-house simile (line 184).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the eight states, the four paths occurring once each, the absence of functional supramundane states, and the immediacy of fruition (Margaya line 14: it gives its result at once, unlike mundane wholesome states; Pradeepika line 106: අකාලික). On the path factors, Margaya stresses gradual development before the path, Pradeepika stresses simultaneous arising in the path moment; these are complementary, not contradictory. Pradeepika gives detail absent from Margaya: purifications, defilement lists, persons, similes and the dependency of stream-entry on hearing from another (line 86). Margaya adds the total of 89 consciousnesses and exercises (lines 23–27).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Nibbāna, the cutting off of defilements "never to arise again", rebirth counts (lines 112–118), Pure Abodes, preparation across aeons, the statement that stream-entry is impossible in formless realms because there are no ears (line 86), and the gender-specific rebirth rule for female non-returners (line 154) are doctrinal. Permanent eradication of anger or greed must not be presented as an achievable psychological outcome.

## Human meaning paragraph

On a demanding path of practice, vivid experiences can arise that feel like the goal itself, and taking them for arrival can turn a person in the wrong direction; the source treats the ability to tell such experiences apart from real progress as its own stage. Separately, what looks like sudden understanding is described as resting on long preparation that cannot be seen at the moment it happens. The first reading is a limited analogy; the second depends on preparation across lives and is held.

## Idea decisions

- **C016-I01 — Mistaking a striking experience for arrival.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika line 70.
- **C016-I02 — Quick understanding built on long unseen preparation.** Held. Based on Pradeepika lines 62 and 84, but the preparation the source describes spans previous lives and aeons. A within-one-life reading would substantially alter the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: *Seeing and letting go in the same moment* (lamp simile, line 56). It describes a single path mind-moment; everyday use would force a doctrinal event into a psychological claim.
- Rejected candidate: *A change that never needs repeating* (cutting off, lines 21 and 54; Margaya line 21). Permanent eradication is doctrinal; the suppression side is already covered by C013-I02, whose Limits note the contrast.
- Rejected candidate: *Steadiness and insight pulling equally* (two oxen, line 76). Too close to generic balance advice without further support.
- Rejected: defilement lists, persons and rebirth types (technical classification).
