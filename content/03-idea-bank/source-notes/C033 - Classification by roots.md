## Central meaning

There are six roots (හේතු / මූල). Three are unwholesome: greed, aversion and delusion. Three are wholesome, or neutral in resultant and functional states: non-greed, non-aversion and non-delusion (wisdom). Both sources stress that "root" does **not** mean the cause that makes a mind-state arise. It means the factor that keeps a mind-state firmly planted on its object, as roots keep a tree standing (Pradeepika lines 27, 81; Margaya line 5). Margaya contrasts this with the ordinary sense of cause, as fire causes smoke. Pradeepika names the actual conditions for arising: a stream of mind produced by ignorance and craving, meeting an object, and a physical base (line 81).

## Essential distinctions

- **How a root holds** (Margaya line 7). Greed "bites hard" (තදින් කා වැදේ) into the desirable object. Aversion bites hard into its object. Through them, the associated mind stays on the object without wavering (නො සැලී).
- **Strength** (Pradeepika line 35; C029 line 29). Rootless states are weak, inferior and unstable. Rooted states are strong and stable.
- **Combinations** (Pradeepika lines 43–65; Margaya lines 11–13). An unwholesome state has one or two roots, never three. Greed and aversion never occur together. A wholesome state has two or three roots, never one. Non-greed and non-aversion always occur together, while wisdom is optional.
- **Relative power** (Pradeepika line 83). Roots are stronger than other factors. Wholesome roots are stronger than unwholesome ones and can destroy them: non-greed destroys greed, non-aversion destroys aversion, and wisdom destroys delusion. When strong wholesome results arise, unwholesome result-power is suppressed.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the definition, the tree image and the counts (18 rootless; 2 one-root, 22 two-root, 47 three-root). Margaya gives the vivid "biting into the object" description. Pradeepika gives the conditions for arising, the relative strength and power of destruction, the tree's roots drawing nourishment to bear fruit as an image of result-giving (line 79), and a 42-fold enumeration (lines 67–77, 107–130). No disagreement was found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Result-giving power (rebirth-linking and life-continuum results, line 79) and the suppression of unwholesome results (line 83) are karmic doctrine. "Wholesome roots are stronger" is a doctrinal evaluation, not an empirical finding.

## Human meaning paragraph

What keeps attention fixed on something is a different question from what brought it there. The source says that craving and hostility can hold the mind as steadily as generosity, goodwill and understanding do. Unrooted moments are thin and unstable. It also claims that the opposing qualities do not merely coexist with craving, hostility and confusion but undo them. Everyday readings are interpretation. Steadiness here means holding to an object, not calmness.

## Idea decisions

- **C033-I01 — What keeps the mind gripped on something.** Accepted. Based on the root-as-anchor definition, the explicit contrast with the cause of arising, and Margaya's "biting into the object". It is related to, but distinct from, C020-I03 (one-pointedness holding despite restlessness) and C075-I01 (capacities serving harmful aims).
- **C033-I02 — Loosening a grip by growing its opposite.** Merged into C013-I03 (Answering each inner obstacle with its opposite), which has the same counter-state mechanism. The preserved difference: C033 pairs the three roots and claims the wholesome roots are stronger and destroy the unwholesome ones (Pradeepika line 83). The karmic-result part is excluded.
- Rejected candidate: *Wanting and hating never share a moment*. It is a placement rule without explanation, and human mixed feelings are more complex than the classification addresses.
- Rejected candidate: *Generosity and goodwill always arrive together*. It is a combination rule only. The "understanding optional" part overlaps C030's rejected wisdom candidate and C011.
