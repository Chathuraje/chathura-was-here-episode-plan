## Central meaning

Matter arises from four causes: kamma (past wholesome or unwholesome volition), mind (චිත්ත), temperature/heat (ඍතු, the fire element) and nutriment (ආහාර, ඕජා). Kamma produces matter at every sub-moment from rebirth until the seventeenth mind-moment before death. Seventy-five types of mind produce matter at their arising moment only. Heat in any material cluster produces further matter at its static moment. Nutriment produces matter when internal and external nutritive essence meet.

## Essential distinctions

- **Mind moves and holds the body.** Margaya line 13: the body does not move by itself, and the non-material mind cannot move it directly; all going, coming, lifting and lowering happen through mind-produced matter. Standing and sitting upright are maintained by it. Pradeepika lines 117–119 and Margaya line 15: life-continuum (භවාඞ්ග) mind-states are too weak to hold posture, which is why a person who falls asleep sitting or standing falls; absorption states hold posture but cannot initiate movement.
- **Laughter and crying.** Margaya lines 19–21; Pradeepika lines 150–165. Thirteen joyful mind-states produce laughter. Ordinary people laugh from four greed-rooted joyful states and four wholesome joyful states; trainees from fewer; arahants from functional states. Pradeepika line 169: every laughter-producing state must be joyful, but not every joyful state produces laughter. Line 165: crying is produced by the two hate-rooted (දෙෂමූල) javana states.
- **Voice.** Pradeepika line 165: mind-produced sound arises together with vocal intimation when one wishes to make one's meaning known to another; stomach noises and sounds during sleep are temperature-born, not mind-born.
- **Nutriment needs both sides.** Margaya line 25; Pradeepika lines 291–299: external food cannot produce matter without the body's own nutritive essence, and vice versa. Pradeepika line 297: no food produces nutriment-born matter in a corpse; plants grow by temperature-born matter, not nutriment-born.
- **Kamma acts only after it has ceased** (Pradeepika lines 42–46), and matter born of kamma is sustained by life-faculty "like a wet-nurse" after the birth-mother dies (line 54).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the four causes, the 75 matter-producing minds, the exclusions (two-fold fivefold sense consciousness, four immaterial resultants, rebirth consciousness, arahant's death consciousness), the 32 intimation-producing minds and the 13 laughter-producing minds. Margaya gives the ending of kamma-born matter as "very close to death"; Pradeepika specifies the seventeenth mind-moment before death (lines 46, 62): complementary precision. Margaya line 11 records a teacher's view that no death-consciousness produces matter; Pradeepika does not mention it. Margaya line 27 records a teacher's view that external food only supports internal nutriment; Pradeepika does not. Pradeepika alone adds that Buddhas laugh with knowledge-associated states and records a dispute about hasituppāda (line 161), and alone attributes crying to hate-rooted states.

Suspected text errors, recorded in the source's own comments or noticed on reading: Pradeepika line 333 prints "(375)" beside words meaning 357 (flagged in the file's comment, lines 335–340). The chart at lines 179–198 gives 35 intimation-producing states against 32 in the text (flagged at lines 207–212). Pradeepika line 99 "විතක්ෂිය" / "විතක්කී" appear to be misprints for විතක්ක. Margaya line 25 "ඒජාවය" is presumably ඕජාවය.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The whole framework is doctrinal physiology: kamma producing bodily matter, counts of clusters (153, 357), the 77th-night appearance of sense clusters, matter lasting 17 mind-moments, and the claim that mind-produced matter holds posture. None of it may be presented as anatomy, neuroscience or nutrition science. The laughter classification by attainment (ordinary person, trainee, arahant, Buddha) is doctrinal and must not be used to judge anyone's laughter.

## Human meaning paragraph

The only passages that describe a human process are the laughter and crying lines. Laughter always comes from a joyful state, but joy does not always become laughter; and the gladness behind a laugh may be tied to wanting, or it may be wholesome. The source does not explain what turns joy into laughter, and it gives a classification rather than a mechanism. Any everyday reading is interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C068-I01 — Not every joy turns into laughter.** Held. The distinction is explicit (Pradeepika line 169; Margaya lines 19–21), but the text gives lists, not explanation, and the attainment ranking is doctrinal. It partly overlaps C005-I01 (outwardly same act, different inner source).
- Rejected candidate: "Staying upright takes a waking mind" (posture held by mind-born matter). This is a historical physiological account, and a human idea would require scientific claims the source cannot support.
- Rejected candidate: "Speaking because you want to be understood" (Pradeepika line 165). One clause only, too thin for a card.
- Rejected candidate: food needing the body's own nutriment. Physiology, not a human mechanism; any metaphor would be invented.
- Rejected candidate: kamma bearing result only after it ceases. Rebirth doctrine; cannot be an idea premise.
