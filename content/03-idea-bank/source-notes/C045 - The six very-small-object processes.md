## Central meaning

- **Small-object processes (පරිත්ත).** When the base, object, light and attention are weaker, four to nine past life-continuum moments pass before the object registers. The process reaches determining, but no impulsion can arise. Determining repeats, then the mind falls back into the life-continuum. These are "determining-ending" (වොට්ඨපනවාර) processes, and because there is no impulsion, "there is no kamma in them" (Pradeepika lines 25–61).
- **Very-small-object processes (අතිපරිත්ත).** When weakness is extreme, ten to fifteen past moments pass. The object only makes the life-continuum vibrate twice, and no process mind arises at all. These are called "futile" or "empty" processes (මොසවාර / හිස්වීථි) (Pradeepika lines 63–81; Margaya lines 3–5).
- **Totals.** Each door has 15 processes, making 75 across five doors (Pradeepika line 99; Margaya line 18). Only 54 sense-sphere minds and only present objects occur in them (Pradeepika line 103; Margaya line 24).
- **Strength of knowing** (Margaya line 20):
  - In very-great-object processes, sights and sounds are felt very well.
  - In great-object processes, they are felt well.
  - In small-object processes, because no impulsion arises, knowing is very weak, "like something seen in a dream, as if seen and not seen", or "as if heard and not heard".
  - In very-small-object processes, there is no knowing of the object at all. "Those processes have no use."
- **Good and bad objects** (Margaya lines 28–34). Result minds follow the object's actual quality whatever the person thinks of it. Impulsion follows the person's disposition. Whether an object is good or bad is hard to settle, and the commentary takes ordinary people's judgement as the standard.

## Essential distinctions

The key distinction is between contact and engagement. Contact can occur (the life-continuum is disturbed) without any registering. Registering can occur without full engagement (no impulsion), which leaves only a faint, dream-like trace and, per Pradeepika, no ethically weighty action.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- The sources agree on the ranges (4–9 and 10–15 past moments) and the double vibration.
- Pradeepika adds that in near-death, fainting, drowning, unconscious states and birth, five or six impulsions may arise (line 27). Doctrinal.
- Margaya alone gives the phenomenological description ("seen-not-seen") and the discussion of good and bad objects.
- Pradeepika's small-object charts (lines 51–56) appear to drop one cell per row relative to its own text. See the batch report.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The moment counts, the life-continuum, "no kamma" as a karmic-result claim, and beings of the fine-material and immaterial realms (Pradeepika line 101) are doctrinal. The dream-like description is a traditional account of weak cognition. It is not evidence about subliminal perception or modern attention research, and it must not be equated with either.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source describes a gradient. Some things are fully taken in. Some are registered and then dropped before any real response forms, leaving a "was it there or not?" trace. Some touch the senses without ever being noticed. The cause lies in the conditions — the sense, the object's strength, light and attention — not in the object alone. As an interpretation, people constantly pass through things they half see or half hear, and the question of what they "saw" or are responsible for noticing is more uncertain than it seems.

## Idea decisions

- **C045-I01 — When something is half seen and half heard.** Merged into C040-I01 (*Not everything that reaches us gets noticed*), which has the same mechanism. The card keeps its ID and preserves the "seen and not seen, as in a dream" description (Margaya line 20) and the question of later recall. Evidence: Margaya line 20; Pradeepika lines 25–27, 61, 65 and 81; C044 Margaya line 3.
- Rejected candidate: *Good and bad are what ordinary people agree on* (Margaya line 30). This is interesting, but the passage is about fixing a doctrinal result classification. Its response side is already covered in C043-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Noticing without acting carries no weight* ("no kamma" without impulsion). Ethically suggestive, but it is a karmic claim and risks becoming a moral rule about responsibility. It is noted as an open question in C045-I01 rather than made into a card.
