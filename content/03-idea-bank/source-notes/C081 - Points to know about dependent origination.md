# C081 — Points to know about dependent origination

- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–63). Heading `# 4. පටිච්චසමුප්පාදය ගැන දත යුතු කරුණු`, sub-headings for the three periods, twelve factors, four groups, three junctures, twenty modes, three rounds and two roots. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Dependent origination (පටිච්චසමුප්පාදය) is analysed as a chain of twelve factors (අඞ්ග), from ignorance (අවිද්‍යාව) and formations (සංස්කාර) through consciousness, mind-and-matter, the six bases, contact, feeling (වේදනා), craving (තෘෂ්ණා), clinging (උපාදාන) and becoming (භව) to birth, ageing and death. Both sources arrange the factors across three lives (past, present, future), four groups (සංක්ෂේප), three junctures (සන්ධි), twenty modes (past causes, present results, present causes, future results) and three rounds (වට්ට/වෘත්ත): defilement, action and result, which turn into each other so that saṃsāra continues. Ignorance and craving are the two roots. Cutting them breaks the wheel (Margaya lines 59–61).

Pradeepika adds much more:
- four directions of teaching, compared to four men gathering a vine (lines 33–78);
- one-, two-, threefold analyses of each factor (lines 84–148);
- why sorrow and lamentation are mentioned (lines 152–158);
- definitions of each factor as past or present cause (lines 197–273), with a worked example of giving (lines 215–223);
- the chain as truths, functions and wrong views refuted (lines 317–396);
- two series of similes (lines 398–456): a blind man stumbling, and a boil treated with the wrong medicine; then a film over the eye, a magician, fire-sticks, salt water, a fish taking the hook, a seed and a falling tree;
- four kinds of depth (lines 458–521);
- four methods (නය) with their right and wrong readings (lines 523–548).

## Essential distinctions

- **Not a first cause.** Both sources say ignorance is placed first for teaching, not as the beginning of the world. The cycle has no known start (Margaya line 63; Pradeepika line 66).
- **Result versus cause.** Feeling belongs to the present results. Craving begins the present causes. The juncture between them is the "result–cause juncture" (Pradeepika line 173; Margaya line 33).
- **Preparatory intention versus the act.** The intentions of someone preparing a gift for a month or a year are formations (ආයූහන සංස්කාර). The intention at the moment the gift is placed in the recipient's hand is kamma-becoming (Pradeepika lines 215–219).
- **Continuity without identity.** The unity method (එකත්තනය) sees an unbroken series, like seed to sprout to tree. Read rightly, it removes the view that things are cut off. Read wrongly, as sameness, it supports eternalism. The diversity method (නානත්තනය), read rightly, removes eternalism. Read wrongly, as though one series were many beings, it supports annihilationism (lines 533–539).
- **No agent.** Ignorance does not intend to produce formations (අව්‍යාපාරනය). Read wrongly, this becomes the doctrine that action does nothing (lines 541–545).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the twelve factors, three periods, four groups, three junctures, twenty modes, the "no beginning" point and the two roots. They differ in detail:
- **Rounds.** Margaya's result round includes rebirth-becoming, birth and ageing-death (line 57). Pradeepika's lists only the five present-result factors (line 313). This is a difference of scope, not a real conflict.
- **Sorrow and lamentation.** Margaya says sorrow and the rest are not counted as factors because not everyone has them (line 15). Pradeepika says they are mentioned to show the wheel keeps turning, because sorrow means ignorance persists (lines 152–158). These are complementary reasons.
- **Refuted views.** Margaya says the past causes refute chance arising and creation by a god, the future results refute "no rebirth", and the cause–result series refutes self-view (line 11). Pradeepika assigns a refuted view to each link (lines 374–396). The two accounts are compatible.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The three-life structure, rebirth-linking, "womb-entry" imagery, destinations, the claim that no one escapes the fear of saṃsāra without path knowledge (line 550), and the refutation of creator and self doctrines are doctrinal. They cannot be idea premises. "Craving for existence" as the special cause of wholesome action (line 72) concerns rebirth and is not used. The similes may be used only where they describe a process that also makes sense within one lifetime, and then only as limited analogies.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe experience as a chain in which what is felt can become what is wanted, what is wanted can be gripped, and gripping can drive new action. They repeatedly use images of relief-seeking that does not relieve: a tormented cow drinking hot water, a sufferer putting harmful ointment on a boil, a thirsty man drinking salt water. As interpretation beyond the text, three human processes can be drawn out. Attempts to soothe pain can make it worse. Savouring something can feed the wanting rather than settle it. A life that changes is neither a fixed self nor a series of strangers. Nothing here establishes rebirth or the three-life model.

## Idea decisions

- **C081-I01 — Reaching for relief that makes the pain worse.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the boil simile (lines 414–424) and the cow drinking hot water (lines 66–70).
- **C081-I02 — When enjoying something turns into craving it.** Accepted. Based on the result–cause juncture, the salt-water simile (line 448) and line 74. C083 (Pradeepika line 307) is a supporting concept: feeling conditions craving only as a decisive support, not by arising together with it.
- **C081-I03 — Neither the same person nor someone else.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the unity and diversity methods (lines 533–539) and the "neither self nor another" passage (line 295). It explicitly excludes the rebirth application.
- Rejected candidate: *Preparation as part of the gift* (lines 215–223). The distinction is clear, but the worked example immediately ties craving to wishing for a future birth. It also overlaps the existing giving-motive cards C011-I03, C012-I01 and C074-I03. Held as a question.
- Rejected candidate: *No one steering, yet action still matters* (lines 541–545). This is too close to C040-I02 (Responses that unfold without anyone deciding). Recommended as a supporting reference for that card.
- Rejected candidate: *Specific causes, specific results, not fate* (milk to curd, line 547). This overlaps C060-I02 (Leaving to fate what care could change). Could support it.
- Rejected candidate: *Pain leads to self-punishment, pleasure to indulgence* (line 78). The mechanism is compressed into one sentence and framed around ascetic practice. It is mentioned in C081-I02 only as context, not as a separate card.
- Rejected candidate: *Grief that keeps ignorance going* (lines 154–158). This is doctrinal, and the human side overlaps C007-I03 and C022-I01.
- Rejected candidate: the four directions of teaching (vine-gatherers). This concerns teaching method and has no human mechanism.
