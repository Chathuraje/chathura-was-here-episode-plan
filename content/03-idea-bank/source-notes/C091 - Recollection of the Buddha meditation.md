# C091 — Recollection of the Buddha meditation

## Central meaning

Recollection of the Buddha (බුද්ධානුස්සතිය) is a calm-meditation subject in which the practitioner, seated in seclusion with firm confidence, calls to mind the Buddha's qualities. Pradeepika lists and explains the nine qualities of the formula `ඉතිපි සො භගවා ...` (lines 26–50) one by one (lines 54–356): arahant, fully self-awakened, endowed with knowledge and conduct, well-gone, knower of worlds, unsurpassed trainer of those to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed. As the qualities are recalled, the mind is not oppressed by lust, hatred or delusion, becomes upright, the hindrances are suppressed, strong gladness arises, then tranquillity, ease and concentration (line 362). Because the qualities are deep and varied, the meditation reaches only access concentration, not full absorption (line 362). Eleven benefits follow (lines 364–388).

Margaya gives a practical, lay-oriented account. There are two methods: visualising a Buddha image one finds pleasing, part by part, until it can be pictured with closed eyes (line 7), and recalling qualities (lines 9–25). One should pick one quality, or three or four, or seven or eight, that one's own mind grasps clearly (line 9). It gives sample reflections in plain language (lines 11–23) and ends: recalling the qualities in whatever way one likes is the meditation, but reciting memorised qualities from books that one does not understand is not (line 25).

## Essential distinctions

- **Understanding versus recitation.** Margaya line 25 separates genuine recollection from repeating uncomprehended words. Pradeepika presents a detailed scholastic account but also frames it as something the meditator "should reflect on" (e.g. line 76).
- **Access, not absorption.** The object's depth and variety keep the mind moving among many reflections (line 362), so it steadies but does not fully absorb.
- **Two meanings of "blessed"** (lines 316–318): fortune (appeal to householders, relief of bodily distress, worldly happiness) and broken defilements (appeal to renunciants, relief of mental distress, supramundane happiness).
- **Speech criteria under "well-gone"** (lines 154–164): what is untrue and harmful is not spoken, whether pleasant or unpleasant; what is true and beneficial is spoken at the right time, whether pleasant or unpleasant.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree that the practice is recalling qualities while seated in seclusion and that it brings gladness and some concentration (Margaya line 7; Pradeepika line 362). Margaya adds the image-visualisation method, which Pradeepika does not mention, and a freedom to choose a few understood qualities. Pradeepika gives the full nine-quality exegesis, the access-only limit and the benefits list, none of which Margaya gives. There is no direct contradiction. There is a difference of emphasis: Margaya treats any sincere, understood form of recollection as valid, while Pradeepika's account is formula-based and technical.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Omniscience (Margaya line 11; Pradeepika lines 248–250), supernormal powers and the eight worldly "powers" such as becoming minute or light (lines 322–326), taming nāgas, yakkhas and Brahmas (lines 226–230), the frog reborn as a deity through hearing the Buddha's voice (line 236), countless world-systems (line 216), the practitioner's body becoming worthy of worship like a shrine (line 380) and assured good rebirth (line 388) are doctrinal claims. The detailed defilement lists (lines 284–308) are classificatory. The claim that the mind becomes free of lust, hatred and delusion while recollecting is a doctrinal description, not a measured effect. None of these may become idea premises.

## Human meaning paragraph

Calling to mind the good qualities of someone one deeply respects can change the state of mind one is in: agitation and resentment lose hold, the mind straightens and a quiet gladness appears, which then supports calm. Margaya insists this works through understanding, not through repeating words one does not grasp. Pradeepika adds two further effects that are closer to everyday experience: a sense of living in that person's company, and a sense of shame and fear about wrongdoing because the admired figure seems to be present when a harmful thought arises. Separately, one quality recalled, the right use of speech, offers a clear account of how truth, benefit and pleasantness relate when deciding what to say. The everyday readings below are interpretation, and the object of recollection in the source is specifically the Buddha, not any admired person.

## Idea decisions

- **C091-I01 — Recalling someone's goodness in words you understand.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Margaya lines 9 and 25 and Pradeepika line 362. Related to C085-I02, which concerns recalling one's own conduct.
- **C091-I02 — Imagining an admired person present before a wrong.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika benefits 7 and 10 (lines 378, 386). Related to C023-I01.
- **C091-I03 — Knowing when to say a true but unwelcome thing.** Accepted for research. Based on Pradeepika lines 154–164. Note that it is drawn from a quality recollected, not from the meditation's mechanism, and the source's list is a compressed form (see batch report).
- Rejected candidate: *Holding an image until it appears with closed eyes* (Margaya line 7). This is a visualisation technique with no human tension beyond sustained attention, which overlaps C013-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Why a deep subject never lets the mind settle fully* (access only, line 362). The explanation is technical and too thin to carry a mechanism beyond the text.
- Rejected candidate: *Teaching by gentle and harsh means* (line 230). Only a list of taming stories, mostly involving non-human beings; no mechanism is given.
