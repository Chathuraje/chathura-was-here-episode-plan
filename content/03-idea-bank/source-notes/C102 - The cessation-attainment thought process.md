# C102 — The cessation-attainment thought process

## Central meaning

Cessation attainment (නිරෝධ සමාපත්ති, also called the cessation of perception and feeling) is the non-occurrence of mind and mental factors for a pre-set period. Only non-returners and arahants who have all eight attainments and both calm and insight can enter it. They enter each absorption in turn and review its formations as impermanent, suffering and non-self up to the base of nothingness. Then they perform four preliminary duties, enter the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception for one or two moments, and the mind-stream stops. At most seven days is possible in beings who live on food (Pradeepika line 66; Book 4 line 24). On emergence, one moment of the person's own fruit consciousness arises, and the mind afterwards inclines toward nibbāna.

## Essential distinctions

- **The four preliminary duties** (Pradeepika lines 43–62). (1) Resolving that belongings not attached to the body will not be destroyed by fire, water, wind, thieves or animals. (2) Resolving to emerge before a messenger comes if the monastic community is waiting for one; otherwise one emerges when the messenger speaks, "because the community is weighty". (3) The same for a summons from the Teacher. (4) Checking whether one's life span will last through the chosen period, because death cannot occur within cessation and one must emerge first.
- **Why calm alone or insight alone is not enough** (Pradeepika lines 35–41). Calm alone reaches the fourth formless absorption; insight alone reaches fruition attainment; both together are needed for cessation.
- **Motive** (Pradeepika line 33). Weariness with the arising and breaking up of formations, and the wish to dwell at ease in peace here and now.
- **Cessation versus death** (Pradeepika lines 72–80). In both, breathing, speech formations and mental formations have ceased. In the dead, life faculty, bodily heat and sense faculties have also gone; in the one in cessation they remain.
- **Status of cessation** (Pradeepika line 82). It is neither conditioned nor unconditioned, neither mundane nor supramundane, but is called "produced" because it belongs to the person who enters it.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on who can enter, the progression through the absorptions with insight, the two moments of the highest formless absorption and the single fruit moment on emergence.

Disagreements:
- **Change-of-lineage or purification.** Margaya's diagram (C102 file line 3) and prose (C101 file line 21) name change-of-lineage (ගෝත්‍රභූ) before the absorption moments. Pradeepika's diagram (line 87) has purification (වො). Since only noble persons enter, purification looks consistent with C100, where purification replaces change-of-lineage after the first path.
- **Planes where cessation is possible.** Margaya (line 9) says only the sense and fine-material worlds, not the formless. Pradeepika (line 98) reports two opinions: some hold only the sense sphere, and some teachers say not in the formless world only. Pradeepika's own line 66 (unlimited duration in fine-material planes) presupposes that it is possible there.
- **Number of absorption moments.** Margaya (C101 file line 21) says twice; Pradeepika line 64 says one or two, but its diagram shows two.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The attainment itself, the seven-day limit without food, the protection of belongings by resolution, the claim that death cannot occur within cessation and the classification of noble persons are doctrinal claims. They must not be presented as physiology or as achievable states. The motive of "weariness with formations" is not ordinary boredom or depression.

## Human meaning paragraph

The only human process with enough support is in the preliminary duties. Before withdrawing completely, the practitioner decides in advance which claims on him are weighty enough to bring him back (the community's need, a summons from his teacher, the limit of his own life) and arranges things so that his absence does no harm. The source treats the community's call as overriding the withdrawal even when no provision was made. Read as a limited analogy, this describes someone who steps away from others while deciding beforehand what would justify being called back. That reading is interpretation beyond the text.

## Idea decisions

- **C102-I01 — Deciding beforehand what may call you back.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 43–66.
- Rejected candidate: *Stillness that is not the same as death* (lines 72–80). It depends on doctrinal physiology.
- Rejected candidate: *Needing both calm and insight to go further* (lines 35–41). This is a doctrinal ranking of attainments without an explained human mechanism, and it may overlap future cards on calm and insight.
- Rejected candidate: *Withdrawing out of weariness with constant change* (line 33). There is too little explanation, and there is a risk of equating it with depression or escapism.
