## Central meaning

The "collection of all" regroups every conditioned reality, plus nibbāna where relevant, in four overlapping ways. There are five aggregates: matter, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness. There are five clinging-aggregates, which are the mundane portion that craving and views grip as "mine" and "I". There are twelve sense bases (six internal, six external) and eighteen elements, which split mind into seven consciousness-elements. Finally there are the four noble truths: suffering, its origin in craving, its cessation (nibbāna) and the eightfold path. Margaya (lines 24, 49, 60) says each division exists to loosen the sense of self for a different kind of listener. Pradeepika (3023–3031) says the aggregates are taught for those confused about mind, the bases for those confused about matter, and the elements for those confused about both.

## Essential distinctions

- **Eleven ways of dividing each aggregate** (Pradeepika 44–57 and each aggregate section): past, future and present; internal and external; gross and subtle; inferior and superior; far and near. For feeling, perception, formations and consciousness, "far" and "near" mean *likeness of kind*, not distance. People on one bench can have feelings "far" from each other, while people in different realms can have "near" ones (373–401; also 472–534, 605–669, 730–790). For matter, far and near are also spatial (217–223).
- **Inferior and superior objects are measured against a middling person** (183–207). For the very rich, even objects fit for gods can seem unpleasant. For the very hungry, broken-rice gruel and rotten meat taste very sweet. The standard is therefore set by people such as generals, ministers and merchants, who meet both. The source still holds that the *resultant* consciousness registers desirable and undesirable objects correctly, while impulsion can like the undesirable and resent the desirable through distorted perception (193–199).
- **Gross and subtle feeling** (273–333). Pleasant and painful feelings are coarse because they agitate. Neutral feeling is subtle because it is peaceful. The unsettled person's feeling is coarse because it is scattered over many objects (301).
- **Hidden and open suffering** (2444–2448). Earache, toothache and the inner burning of lust or hatred must be learnt by asking, and their cause is unclear. Suffering from torture is evident without asking. Similarly, what a person perceives through the five doors can be guessed by watching, though the story at 452 shows a glance misread. What a person is thinking can only be known by asking (458).
- **Sorrow, lamentation and despair** (2664–2726) are distinct. Sorrow burns and dries the mind from inside like a heated dart. Lamentation repeats the lost person's name and virtues in empty, half-finished words. Despair is exhaustion and sinking of mind. The simile at 2726 is oil cooking inside a pot on a low fire, boiling over on a high fire, and the remainder cooking inside until it is used up.
- **Three marks concealed** (1389–1399). Impermanence is hidden by continuity. Suffering is hidden by changing postures. Not-self is hidden by treating a compact mass as one thing.
- **Old age as suffering** (2584–2590). It is not itself pain but causes pain. It brings weakness and loss of memory, learning and effort, and sometimes contempt from one's own children, who no longer honour and serve the old person as before.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the membership of all four schemes and on counts: the truth of suffering covers mundane consciousness, mental factors except greed, and matter (Margaya 103; Pradeepika 2174). Pradeepika adds the eleven-fold division, the sixteen-fold miscellany (792–962), long body-part meditations (1489–1683), similes (1012–1086, 1869–1895, 2204–2220) and a full treatment of the twelve sufferings.

Genuine disagreements:
- **Why the bases and elements are taught.** Margaya 49 says bases are for those who hold mental functions (knowing, tasting, liking, opposing) as self. Pradeepika 3029 says bases are for those confused about *matter*. On elements, Margaya 60 and Pradeepika 1853 agree (strong self-notion in consciousness), but Pradeepika 3031 says elements are for those confused about both.
- **The three kinds of suffering.** Margaya 83 explains "formation suffering" as the effort of obtaining a human existence (making merit, avoiding evil) and of keeping it going (food, protection). Pradeepika 2442 explains it as neutral feeling and all formations being oppressed by arising and passing. Margaya 85 explains "change suffering" as decay and destruction, and fear and sorrow at signs of it. Pradeepika 2440 applies it to pleasant feeling that causes pain when it changes.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- Rebirth-linking, embryology (832–936), hells, ghosts and cosmic planes (103–119, 2523–2531), the size of the embryo and "eighty families of worms" (1044) are doctrinal or historical-physiological claims.
- The hierarchy of bodies from animals through village headmen and kings to Brahmās (203–207) ranks beings and must not become a premise about social worth.
- The desirable-object passage is tied to karmic result. Its "middling person" standard must not be used to rank poor or rich people.
- Margaya 83 links a human existence to merit, which would imply blame if carried over.
- Suffering in the womb and at birth (2503–2513) is traditional description, not obstetrics.
- Self-harm and suicide are listed as expressions of despair and dejection (2515, 2692, 2710). Any research touching them needs safeguarding.

## Human meaning paragraph

Across this long chapter, a few passages describe processes that people recognise without doctrine. Grief takes more than one form: a burning held inside, a spilling-over into words and weeping, and a later exhaustion. Two people close in space can be far apart in what they are going through, and people far apart can be close. Whether something feels pleasant or poor depends partly on what a person is used to. The reading below is interpretation. The source's own purpose is to dissolve the sense of a self by sorting experience into impersonal groups.

## Idea decisions

- **C077-I01 — Grief that burns inside, spills over, then wears down.** Accepted. Based on the definitions and oil simile for sorrow, lamentation and despair. It is related to C007-I03 but has a distinct mechanism.
- **C077-I02 — Sitting side by side yet feeling worlds apart.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on "far" and "near" defined by likeness of kind rather than place.
- **C077-I03 — Pleasure measured against what you are used to.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the "middling person" standard for desirable objects.
- Rejected, overlap: *Some suffering is known only by asking* (2444–2448, 450–458). It is a genuine mechanism, but it largely overlaps C047-I02 (sensing another's pain you cannot see) and C005-I01. Recorded as a held question for possible addition to C047-I02's trace.
- Rejected, overlap: signs of ageing seen only in broken teeth and grey hair (2547–2560). This duplicates C066-I03.
- Held question, not carded: *Old age and the loss of regard* (2588–2590). This is a real tension, but the source gives only one clause, and a card would rest on thin text.
- Rejected: changing posture hides constant discomfort (1391, 1397). The claim is doctrinal (all conditioned things oppress), and the human reading would overstate it.
- Rejected: concentration supported by effort and mindfulness, shown by three friends reaching flowers (2384–2387), and understanding by turning a coin over (2392, 2933). These overlap C013-I01 and C075-I02.
- Rejected: seeing a festival crowd while destitute and wounded deepens dejection (2706). This is a single illustrative sentence and sits inside the grief card's area.
- Rejected: "the dog bites the stone, the lion attacks the hunter" (2844). It is used polemically against other teachers, and its message about causes is generic.
- Rejected: the body-part meditations (1489–1683), where parts "do not know" each other. This is meditation instruction and overlaps C001-I01.
- Rejected: Margaya's "labour of keeping a life going" (83). It is framed by merit and rebirth.
