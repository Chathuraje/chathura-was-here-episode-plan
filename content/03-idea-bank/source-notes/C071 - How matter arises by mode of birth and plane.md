## Central meaning

The concept explains in which worlds, for which kinds of beings, and at what moments the groups of matter (රූප කලාප) arise. It covers the three worlds (කාම, රූප, අරූප), the four modes of birth (අණ්ඩජ egg-born, ජලාබුජ womb-born, සංසේදජ moisture-born, ඕපපාතික spontaneous), whether a being is born with complete or incomplete faculties, and two phases of material arising: at rebirth (පටිසන්ධි) and during life (ප්‍රවෘත්ති). For womb-dwellers (ගබ්භසෙය්‍යක) only body, sex and heart-base decads arise at conception. Eye, ear, nose and tongue decads appear on the 77th day (end of the eleventh week). Pradeepika (lines 161–165) gives the embryonic stages කලල, අර්බුද, පේසී, ඝන, පසාඛ at weekly intervals. Kamma-, mind-, temperature- and nutriment-born matter then continue "like a lamp flame or a river's current" until death (Pradeepika line 157).

## Essential distinctions

- Rebirth-moment matter versus matter arising during life (Pradeepika lines 73–77).
- Kamma-born matter of the new being versus matter from the parents' bodies: the kalala is the being's own kamma-born matter, supported by the fused egg and sperm (Pradeepika line 123).
- Order of onset: kamma-born from the arising moment of rebirth consciousness; temperature-born from its static moment; mind-born from the first bhavaṅga; nutriment-born once nutritive essence spreads (Margaya line 19; Pradeepika lines 127–155). In the womb, nutriment comes from food the mother eats (Pradeepika line 159; Margaya line 23).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the four modes of birth, the seven decads available to moisture-born and spontaneous beings, the three decads at conception for womb-dwellers, and the 77th day. Margaya adds that short-lived beings stay briefly in the womb and gain the four decads sooner (line 17). It notes other teachers' views on the 77th day. It also gives a cause for missing faculties: weak rebirth-producing kamma, or an unwholesome kamma blocking them (line 7). Pradeepika gives the embryonic week stages and the growth chart. On which beings may lack which decads, the accounts broadly agree (Pradeepika lines 106–115; Margaya lines 11–13). Pradeepika says a spontaneous being in a bad destination never lacks the nose decad; Margaya says no spontaneous being lacks it. This is a slight difference of scope, not a conflict.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Almost all of it. Modes of birth, planes, rebirth-moment matter and kalāpa counts are doctrinal cosmology and historical physiology. They must not be presented as embryology. The week-by-week stages and the 77th day are traditional accounts, not modern findings. The claim that blindness, deafness or intersex conditions result from weak or obstructing kamma (Margaya lines 7–9; Pradeepika lines 85, 106–115) is a doctrinal claim with real potential to stigmatise disabled and intersex people. It must never become an idea premise.

## Human meaning paragraph

No supported human process emerges. The text is a classification of how matter is arranged across kinds of beings and moments of life. The one evocative image is that the body continues like a lamp flame or a river (Pradeepika line 157). It is only an image, and the mechanism it points to (a whole that is a continuing process of parts) is already carried by C001-I01.

## Idea decisions

- No idea cards.
- Rejected candidate: *A body that is a flame, not a thing* (continuity image, Pradeepika line 157). It is one sentence of illustration, and it duplicates the mechanism in C001-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *What a parent's food gives an unborn child* (Pradeepika line 159; Margaya line 23). This is a physiological claim, not a human process of intention, feeling or attention.
- Rejected candidate: any idea based on missing faculties. The source explains them by kamma, and a secular rendering would either distort the text or stigmatise people.
