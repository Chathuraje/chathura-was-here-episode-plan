# C083 — The modes of conditionality in brief

## Central meaning

The concept summarises how the twenty-four conditional relations (ප්‍රත්‍යය ශක්ති) apply. Pradeepika goes link by link through dependent origination. It names which relations make ignorance a condition for meritorious, demeritorious and imperturbable formations (lines 27–73), formations for consciousness (75–79), consciousness for mind and matter (81–125), and so on through feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth and ageing-death (307–367). Both sources then give the six-part summary:
- mind conditions mind in six ways;
- mind conditions mind-and-matter in five;
- mind conditions matter in one (post-nascence);
- matter conditions mind in one (pre-nascence);
- concepts, mind and matter condition mind in two ways (object and decisive support);
- mind-and-matter condition mind-and-matter in nine.

(Pradeepika lines 369–417; Margaya lines 5–29.) Margaya adds that every phenomenon, past, present, future or timeless, inner or outer, is a condition in some relation. Everything except nibbāna and concepts is also conditioned (line 31). It also explains the terms "nāma" and "rūpa" (line 35).

## Essential distinctions

- **Feeling to craving** is only a decisive-support condition (උපනිශ්‍රය), a single relation (Pradeepika line 307). Craving conditions sensual clinging only by decisive support, but conditions the three view-based clingings in seven ways, including co-nascence and root (lines 309–325).
- **Proximity, contiguity, absence, disappearance and repetition** (අනන්තර, සමනන්තර, නත්ථි, විගත, ආසේවන) link one mental moment to the next. Repetition applies only between successive javana moments (Margaya line 9).
- **Nāma** here covers the 89 cittas, 52 cetasikas and nibbāna. They are called "name" because they have no shape and are known through their names (Margaya line 35).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The six-part summary agrees in both sources. Pradeepika alone gives the link-by-link application. Margaya alone gives the statements about universal conditionality and the definition of nāma and rūpa. See the batch report for a probable text error (Pradeepika line 357) and an unclear phrasing (line 395).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The relation names are technical terms of the Paṭṭhāna and have no everyday equivalent. Rebirth-linking, the non-percipient beings (අසංඥසත්ව), the formless and five-aggregate planes, and kamma-born matter are doctrinal. "Every phenomenon is a condition" is a metaphysical claim, not a finding.

## Human meaning paragraph

As interpretation: the one point with human weight is that the text treats a felt pleasure or pain as only a *supporting* condition for craving. The feeling does not arise bound together with the craving. That point is used as supporting evidence for C081-I02. The rest of the concept is a classification of relation types and gives no mechanism a storyteller could explore without the technical system.

## Idea decisions

- **No suitable idea.** This concept is a technical classification.
- Used as a supporting concept for **C081-I02** (Pradeepika line 307).
- Rejected candidate: *Repetition makes the next moment easier* (āsevana, Margaya line 9; Pradeepika lines 49, 361, 379). This is a tempting "habit" reading, but the source uses it only for successive javana moments within one cognitive process, a span far too short for habit in the everyday sense. It would misstate the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: *Everything conditions something* (Margaya line 31). This is a broad metaphysical statement, not a mechanism, and would become a slogan.
- Rejected candidate: *Names for things with no shape* (Margaya line 35). This is too thin, and it overlaps C084.
