## Central meaning

Paṭṭhāna is the most detailed of three ways the tradition teaches cause and effect (Pradeepika lines 25–29). A condition (ප්‍රත්‍යය) is anything that helps another thing arise, continue or grow. Every kind of reality, including nibbāna and concepts, can act as a condition. Only mind, mental factors and matter are conditioned (lines 91–95). Nothing arises from one condition alone. A single thing can help others in many distinct ways, called conditioning forces (ප්‍රත්‍යය ශක්ති) (Pradeepika line 97; Margaya lines 3–7, 204–208). Margaya's image is a match: the head and the box must each help in a different way, or either would light the flame alone (line 5). Each condition is then set out as a triad of conditioning, conditioned and excluded states, followed by the canonical answer-sets (වීථි) that apply it to wholesome, unwholesome and indeterminate states.

## Essential distinctions

- **Roots** (හේතු, lines 103–143): greed, hatred, delusion and their opposites grip an object as tree roots grip soil. Rootless states are called very weak (line 143).
- **Object** (ආරම්මණ, lines 403–1633): any state can be an object. The answer-sets show the same object taken up by very different minds. A gift given can be reviewed with a wholesome mind (lines 571–579), or savoured as "I made great merit", clung to, misread as "I did merit", doubted ("was it merit or wrong?"), or followed by displeasure (lines 783–801). A harmful state taken as object breeds more of itself (lines 989–1023). The same harmful state can be the object of a clear, wholesome review (lines 1051–1095).
- **Predominance** (අධිපති, lines 1635–2216): an object can pull the mind back again and again, like a gem (line 1648). Greed-rooted minds esteem things for their enjoyability, as a dog esteems filth; wholesome minds esteem them for their excellence (line 1721). Among desire, mind, effort and investigation, only one leads at a time, and only when strong (line 1740).
- **Contiguity, co-arising, mutuality, support** (lines 2218–4333): moment-to-moment succession. Things arise together, or lean on each other like sticks propped together (line 3346), or rest on a base like a painting on paper (Margaya line 96).
- **Decisive support** (උපනිශ්‍රය, lines 4335–5719): the strongest condition. It has three kinds: esteemed object, immediately preceding mind, and natural or habitual support (පකතූපනිස්සය), meaning qualities, acts, persons, bodily pleasure and pain, climate, food and lodging cultivated or met with earlier (lines 4357–4359). The answer-sets contain the most human material. Faith, virtue, learning, generosity and wisdom support good acts (lines 4585–4657), but also conceit and wrong view (lines 4913–4945) and self-imposed hardship (lines 5009–5041). Harmful acts support further harmful acts, and one killing leaves the mind unrestrained so that other wrongs become "very easy" (lines 5155–5219). A grave act supports the same act in four ways, including seeing another do it and thinking it right (lines 5227–5239). Harmful states also support good acts, for example giving in order to escape the results of harm (lines 5313–5363). Bodily pleasure, pain, climate, food and lodging support both good and harmful acts (lines 5495–5719).
- **Pre-nascence, post-nascence, repetition, kamma, result, nutriment, faculty, jhāna, path, association, dissociation, presence, absence, disappearance, non-disappearance** (lines 5721–9073). Repetition (ආසේවන) lets each impulsion moment strengthen the next of the same kind, like a first coat of paint helping the second (Margaya line 126; Pradeepika lines 6157–6181). Intention (කර්ම) conditions co-arising states now and results later (lines 6353–6367). Path factors lead the mind toward good or harm (lines 7574–7619).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Agreement.** Both sources give the same 24 conditions, definitions and most counts, for example the object-predominance sets (Margaya lines 64–66; Pradeepika lines 1656–1680).
- **Complementary detail.** Margaya is a compact lesson with similes (match, tree roots, painting on paper, coats of paint) and one worked example of greed as both condition and conditioned (lines 206–208). Pradeepika gives every triad and answer-set with the canonical illustrations. Margaya omits the object-base pre-nascent condition as "hard to understand" (line 100). Pradeepika explains it through the heart-base arising 17 moments before death (lines 3624–3630).
- **Genuine differences.** (1) For co-nascent faculty, Margaya lists seven mental faculties (line 156) and Pradeepika lists eight, adding life faculty (lines 7134, 7164). (2) On presence versus non-disappearance, Margaya says the two do not differ and are the same teaching phrased for different listeners (line 200). Pradeepika agrees that their terms are identical but gives each a distinct emphasis: presence versus not having ceased (line 9032). Neither difference affects the cards.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth-linking, planes, supernormal knowledges, path and fruition attainments, the Buddha's rays (line 35), certain rebirth in hell after the five heinous acts or fixed wrong view (lines 5285–5289), and the heart-base are doctrinal. The whole scheme concerns momentary mental events in a technical sense. "Decisive support" is not statistical causation and must not be presented as psychology. The heinous-act examples (matricide, wounding a Buddha, splitting the Sangha) are extreme doctrinal illustrations and must not be applied to real people.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source insists that nothing comes from one cause and that the same thing can help very different outcomes. A remembered good deed can renew goodness or feed self-congratulation. A disliked inner state, when turned toward, can multiply, or it can be seen clearly. Real strengths such as faith, learning or generosity can support further good or become grounds for pride. One harmful act can loosen restraint in other areas, yet harm can also push a person toward good. Everyday comforts and hardships can tip either way. In interpretation beyond the text, what a person does with a condition matters, and conditions strongly support outcomes without fixing them. The sources give no order in time for co-arising factors. The sequences used in the cards come from the answer-sets that explicitly describe one mind taking an earlier one as object or support.

## Idea decisions

- **C082-I01 — When a troubled state becomes its own fuel.** Accepted, with a caution about equating it with clinical rumination. Based on the harmful-to-harmful object answer-sets (lines 989–1023, 1206–1210) and the contrasting harmful-to-wholesome review (lines 1051–1095).
- **C082-I02 — How one crossed line makes the next easier.** Accepted. Based on decisive support among harmful acts (lines 5155–5219, 5227–5239), with the counter-case of harm supporting good (lines 5337–5363) kept in Limits.
- **C082-I03 — Real virtue turned into a sense of superiority.** Accepted. Based on faith, virtue, learning, generosity and wisdom as decisive support for conceit and wrong view (lines 4913–4945), and on savouring past merit (lines 783–799, 4893–4899).
- Rejected: *Comfort and hardship can push conduct either way* (lines 5495–5719). This is plausible, but the harmful side is a bare list with no explanation, and it overlaps C086-I03 and C080-I01. Keep it as supporting material for those cards.
- Rejected: *A fault that pushes someone toward its remedy* (lines 5313–5363). This overlaps C055-I01 (amends) and C033-I02 or C013-I03 (growing the opposite).
- Rejected: *Recalling a good deed can renew or inflate it* as a separate card (lines 571–579 against 783–801). The mechanism is folded into C082-I03. The regret side is already covered by C059-I01, and recollection as habit by C056-I02.
- Rejected: *Each repetition lends strength to the next* (repetition condition). This concerns micro-moments within one cognitive series, so a habit reading would be a stretch. It overlaps C056-I02 and C023-I02.
- Rejected: *Nothing comes from a single cause* (Margaya match simile). It duplicates C080-I03.
- Rejected: *Only one inner drive leads at a time* (co-nascent predominance). It duplicates C075-I02.
- Rejected: *Effort and focus on a harmful road* (wrong path factors). It duplicates C075-I01.
