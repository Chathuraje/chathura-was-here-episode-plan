# C072 — How matter ceases at death and in other planes

## Central meaning

At the approach of death, new kamma-born matter stops arising from the 17th mind-moment before the death consciousness (චුති චිත්තය). The last kamma-born matter ceases together with the death consciousness. Mind-born and nutriment-born matter cease shortly after. After death only temperature-born matter (උතුජ) continues in the corpse (Pradeepika line 16; Margaya line 3). The concept then lists which matter occurs in the fine-material Brahma world (23 kinds; smell, taste, body sensitivity and the two sex-faculties are absent) and among non-percipient beings (අසඤ්ඤසත්ත; 17 kinds, only the life-nonad at rebirth). No matter exists in the formless worlds.

## Essential distinctions

- Margaya line 3 makes a causal point. Kamma-born matter does not stop *because of* the death consciousness. Rather, because kamma-born matter stops, there is room for only sixteen more minds, so the seventeenth becomes the death consciousness.
- Rebirth-moment versus life-time matter: certain kinds never occur at rebirth (Pradeepika lines 63–72; Margaya line 15).
- Brahmas lack smell, taste and touch sensitivity because these are rooted in sensual lust, which their meditation removed (Pradeepika line 22).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the 17-moment cessation, on temperature-born matter continuing in the corpse, and on the 28 / 23 / 17 counts per plane.

**Genuine disagreement:** the kinds of matter not obtained at rebirth. Pradeepika (lines 63–72, PDF 122 / printed 111) lists **nine**: sound, the five mutable kinds (විකාර), continuity (සන්තති), decay (ජරතා) and impermanence (අනිච්චතා). Margaya (line 15) lists **eight**: sound, two intimations, three lightness-group, decay and impermanence. It omits continuity. Both totals are stated explicitly. This looks like a real difference of reckoning rather than a copying error, and it is recorded, not resolved.

**Complementary:** Pradeepika (lines 78–80) adds a legendary account. Only the Bodhisatta in his last life (and a "thoroughbred" chief bull) are born with intimation and lightness-group matter. This is how the newborn walks seven steps and declares "අග්ගොහමස්මි ලෝකස්ස". Margaya has no parallel.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Everything here is doctrinal physiology and cosmology: a mind-moment count for dying, matter in Brahma and non-percipient planes, the birth legend. It must not be presented as a medical account of dying or of what happens to a body after death.

## Human meaning paragraph

No supported human process emerges. The concept describes the order in which kinds of matter stop at death and which kinds exist in other planes. It says nothing about how a dying person or those around them notice, feel, intend or respond. The only image with human resonance is that the body continues changing after the life has ended. The source states this as a classification of temperature-born matter, not as an experience.

## Idea decisions

- No idea cards.
- Rejected candidate: *A body that goes on after the person has gone.* This would rest on a doctrinal claim about matter, and any human content (how families relate to a body after death) would be invented, not sourced.
- Rejected candidate: *Losing the senses rooted in desire* (Brahmas lack smell, taste, touch). This is cosmology; it would turn a plane-of-existence claim into a premise.
