# C096 — Insight meditation subject

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika Book 4.md`: read in full (lines 1–246). Frontmatter declares Book 4, chapter 9, PDF 432–445 (printed 413–426). Page markers run from line 32 (PDF 433 / printed 414) to line 242 (PDF 445 / printed 426). The extract starts mid-page on PDF 432 / printed 413 (line 22). Sinhala commentary only.
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–5). Heading `# 12. විදර්ශනා කර්‍මස්ථානය`, lesson 12. One paragraph. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Insight meditation (විපස්සනා) is the development of wisdom (ප්‍රඥා) for realising the four truths, undertaken after conduct is purified and the mind is concentrated by a calm-meditation subject (Pradeepika line 26). Pradeepika defines wisdom as insight-knowledge in a wholesome mind (28), distinguishes it from recognition (සංඥා, knowing blue or yellow) and consciousness (විඥාන, which adds knowing characteristics) (30), and gives its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause (34–40): penetrating the nature of things, dispelling the darkness of delusion that hides it, non-confusion, and concentration. It then lists many classifications of wisdom (42–184), including wisdom from reflection, from hearing and from meditative development (58–62); skill in progress, decline and means (66–72); and the four analytical knowledges with conditions that make them clear (76–182). Finally it names the "ground" of insight (aggregates, sense bases, elements, faculties, truths, dependent origination), its "roots" (purity of conduct and of mind) and its "body" (five further purifications) (186–246), and explains why the Buddha taught the same analysis in different forms for different people (190–226).

Margaya line 5 only defines insight meditation as the practice that yields the four paths and fruits and frees beings from saṃsāra, and names the topics to learn: seven purifications, three characteristics, three contemplations, ten insight knowledges, three liberations and three doors to liberation. Pradeepika's extract stops before these topics.

## Essential distinctions

- **Recognition, consciousness, wisdom** (line 30): the same object known as a colour, as having characteristics, and with a capacity to bring about the path.
- **Three sources of wisdom** (58–62): one's own reflection without hearing from others (including wisdom in livelihood, crafts and sciences), hearing or instruction, and meditative development.
- **Three skills** (66–72): skill in growth (knowing what stops unwholesome states arising and makes wholesome ones grow), skill in decline (knowing what brings the reverse) and skill in means (situation-fitting, on-the-spot wisdom about the causes of welfare).
- **Teaching fitted to the hearer** (192–226): aggregates for those confused about mental phenomena, sense bases for those confused about matter, elements for those confused about both; the same ordering by sharp, middling and dull faculties and by preference for brief, medium or detailed teaching; faculties for those holding a creator-god view of self.
- **Aids to analytical knowledge** (146–176): previous practice, broad learning, knowledge of languages, knowing the texts, asking about meaning "even of one verse", attainment, relying on learned teachers, and good friends.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources do not conflict. Margaya is an outline of topics; Pradeepika's extract is an introduction to wisdom that covers none of Margaya's named topics in detail (they belong to later concepts, notably C097). Pradeepika's classifications are complementary detail only.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Liberation from saṃsāra and the path-and-fruit attainments (Margaya line 5; Pradeepika lines 46, 56, 124, 134, 182), previous practice under former Buddhas (144), the claim that Magadhi is the "natural language" of meaning (118), and the teaching aimed at creator-god believers (222) are doctrinal or polemical. The ordering of people by "sharp" and "dull" faculties must not become a way of ranking documentary subjects. Wisdom here is a specific religious insight, not general intelligence.

## Human meaning paragraph

The commentary treats understanding as layered and as something that grows under conditions. Knowing a thing's name or look is not yet knowing its nature. Understanding may come from one's own reflection, from being taught or from practice. Part of practical wisdom is knowing what makes good things grow, what makes them decline and what the present situation calls for. The same truth, it says, was taught in different shapes because people are confused about different things, prefer different lengths of explanation and differ in readiness. Two of these are carried into ideas below as limited analogies. The "three sources of wisdom" overlaps C073-I03 and is not made a separate card.

## Idea decisions

- **C096-I01 — Knowing what helps, what harms, and what fits now.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Pradeepika lines 66–72.
- **C096-I02 — Fitting an explanation to where someone is confused.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Pradeepika lines 190–226.
- Rejected candidate: *Knowing from hearing versus knowing from practice* (lines 58–62). Shares its mechanism with C073-I03 (what a description cannot give you) and partly with C037-I01. Recorded as a possible supporting concept for C073-I03 rather than a duplicate card.
- Rejected candidate: *Recognising a thing versus understanding it* (line 30). Overlaps C036-I01, C042-I01 and C046-I01. The source gives only definitions, without the fuller simile tradition.
- Rejected candidate: *Understanding sharpened by questions and good company* (lines 146–176). A list of aids with no explanation of how they work; the one concrete detail (asking about the meaning of even one verse, line 170) is too thin for a card.
