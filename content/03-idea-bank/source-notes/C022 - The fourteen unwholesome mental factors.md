## Central meaning

Fourteen unwholesome mental factors (අකුසල චෛතසික) accompany the twelve unwholesome mind-states. Four occur in all of them (අකුසල සාධාරණ): delusion (මෝහය), shamelessness about wrong (අහිරිකය), fearlessness about wrong (අනොත්තප්පය) and restlessness (උද්ධච්චය). The other ten occur only in some: greed (ලෝභය), wrong view (දිට්ඨිය), conceit (මානය), aversion (දෝසය), envy (ඉස්සාව), stinginess (මච්ඡරිය), remorse (කුක්කුච්චය), mental sluggishness and torpor (ථීනය, මිද්ධය) and unwholesome doubt (විචිකිච්ඡාව). Pradeepika (lines 25–31) frames all of them as arising when the object is taken wrongly, with delusion as the root that hides the true nature of things (lines 35–51).

## Essential distinctions

- **Shamelessness vs fearlessness** (Pradeepika 53–69; Margaya 11–15). One is the absence of inner recoil or disgust at wrong; the other the absence of alarm at its consequences. Both sources call them the two great powers on the unwholesome side. Their wholesome counterparts are treated in C023.
- **Conceit is mis-measuring oneself** (Pradeepika 101–113; Margaya 31). It has three forms: "I am better", "I am equal", "I am lower". Pradeepika gives lineage, virtue and merit-based pride as forms of "better" (line 107). It describes "lower" conceit as seeing oneself as having no wealth, learning, relatives or fortune and asking "how can lowly people like us learn, give, be happy or rise?" (line 111). Margaya adds that conceit should be understood as like madness (line 31). Conceit and wrong view never occur in the same mind-state (line 113).
- **Envy vs stinginess** (Pradeepika 123–135; Margaya 37–45). Envy takes others' prosperity as its object (external). Stinginess takes one's own possessions (internal). Pradeepika says envy takes pleasure in others' misfortune and recasts others' giving, virtue and meditation as hypocrisy or a scheme for gain (line 129). Stinginess refuses to let others own, use, match or even know about one's wealth. It also blocks giving things one has no use for, and even others' giving (line 133). Both occur only with aversion-rooted states.
- **Remorse** (Pradeepika 139–157; Margaya 49). It means burning over wrong done and good left undone. Pradeepika insists that simply recalling a past wrong is not remorse. It says that burning regret, which many people treat as a virtue that lessens wrong, instead defiles the mind again. It also says regret over good left undone produces no good and weakens the mind that wanted to act (line 143). A third form is regretting past good deeds after taking up a wrong view (lines 145–149). When strong, remorse makes self-generated good impossible without help from admirable friends (line 151).
- **Sluggishness and torpor** (Pradeepika 159–175; Margaya 53). Mental drowsiness counts as unwholesome. Ordinary sleep from bodily tiredness or illness does not (line 171). Drowsiness when listening or watching unwillingly, or doing something without wanting to, is attributed to this factor (line 175).
- **Doubt** (Pradeepika 177–183; Margaya 57). The unwholesome factor is doubt that obstructs the path, above all about the Buddha and the teaching. Ordinary uncertainty about unknown matters is excluded.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the list, the four universal factors and the core definitions. Pradeepika adds similes, occurrence rules, path-stage removal and extended examples. Margaya is a short gloss list. Complementary details: Margaya lists grief as arising with aversion (line 35), while Pradeepika lists fear, disgust, sorrow, discontent and wanting revenge as modes of aversion (line 121). **Minor disagreement:** on doubt about unknown matters, Margaya says flatly that it is not the unwholesome factor (line 57). Pradeepika only says it *may* not be unwholesome (නොවන්නටද පිළිවන, line 179). **Possible text error:** Margaya line 43 describes stinginess as the state that "reveals" (හඟවන්නා) one's own wealth. Pradeepika line 133 says it cannot bear others even knowing of one's wealth. "සඟවන්නා" (hides) is likely intended.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth, hell-realm consequences, "habit carried through saṃsāra" and path-stage eradication are doctrinal. The claim that remorse can turn a light wrong into one that leads to hell (line 143) is doctrinal. The list of wrong views includes theistic creation and denial of other worlds (lines 41, 97). These must not become premises or be used to judge believers of other faiths. Pradeepika's claim that equal-conceit includes the view that distinguishing rich and poor is unjust (line 109) is doctrinally loaded. It must not be carried into ideas as a judgement on egalitarian beliefs. The pig-and-dog simile (line 59) and the "lower" examples must not be used to stigmatise poor people or people with low self-worth. Moral classification of "unwholesome" is not a psychological diagnosis.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source describes several forces that shape wrongdoing and failure to do good. Among those with a recognisable everyday texture, three stand out. Regret can keep reopening a past wrong instead of freeing a person to act differently. Envy can recast someone else's good conduct as calculation. And a person can measure themselves so low that learning, giving or happiness seem closed to "people like us". The source presents these as mental factors present in a mind-state, not as a sequence or a personality. The everyday readings in the cards are interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C022-I01 — When regret keeps the wrong alive.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika's remorse section (lines 141–153), with Margaya line 49.
- **C022-I02 — Explaining away someone else's goodness.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika's envy section (lines 125–129), with Margaya line 39.
- **C022-I03 — Measuring yourself too low to try.** Accepted for research as a limited reading, with ethical cautions. Based on Pradeepika line 111 and Margaya line 31.
- Rejected candidate: *Tiredness that comes from not wanting to* (line 175). It is a genuine observation, but it rests on one sentence with no development. Note for possible future use.
- Rejected candidate: *Guarding what you do not even need* (stinginess, line 133). The mechanism is real. It is held back to stay within three cards and because it partly overlaps C005-I02 (holding on). It could be revisited.
- Rejected candidate: shamelessness and fearlessness as ideas. They are better handled from the positive side in C023-I01.
- Rejected candidate: wrong view. It overlaps existing C006-I02 (the belief that makes taking seem fair).
- Rejected candidate: delusion, restlessness and doubt. They are too broad or doctrinally bound (doubt is defined by reference to the Buddha and teaching).
