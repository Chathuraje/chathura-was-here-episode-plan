# C088 — The second and higher absorptions

Sinhala title: ද්විතීයාදි ධ්‍යාන.

## Central meaning

A meditator who has the first absorption (ධ්‍යානය) must first master it in five ways (පඤ්ච වශීතා): adverting to its factors, entering it quickly, staying in it for a chosen time, leaving it at a chosen time, and reviewing its factors. Only then do they reflect that the current absorption has a "near enemy" and a coarse (ඖදාරික) factor, give up their attachment to it (නිකාන්තිය), and resume the same meditation object to reach the next level. Step by step, applied thought, sustained thought, rapture (ප්‍රීතිය) and bodily-mental pleasure (සුඛය) are dropped until only equanimity and one-pointedness remain. The formless absorptions follow the same logic but surpass objects rather than factors: the kasina image, then boundless space, then the consciousness of space, then the absence of that consciousness, and finally a perception so subtle it is "neither perception nor non-perception".

## Essential distinctions

- **Mastery before progress.** Book 4 lines 26–40 and Margaya line 5 require all five masteries before seeking a higher level. Book 4 line 54 repeats this for the second absorption ("ප්‍රගුණ වූ කල්හි").
- **Coarseness is seen only in review.** Book 4 lines 44, 58 and 112: after emerging and reviewing, a factor appears coarse and the next state appears peaceful. Margaya lines 7 and 9: after mastery, the factor "appears" coarse.
- **Rapture versus pleasure.** Book 4 line 54: rapture is coarse "because it makes the mind bubble up" (සිත් උල්ප වන්නාක් බැවින්). Line 101: third-absorption pleasure is "extremely sweet" yet there is no longing for it, hence equanimity. Line 97 defines absorption-equanimity as not producing partiality even toward the highest absorption bliss.
- **Ten kinds of equanimity** (Book 4 lines 66–99): a list, with one-line definitions. Effort-equanimity (line 85) is effort neither over-started nor over-slackened.
- **Formless absorptions surpass objects, not factors** (Book 4 line 276). All four have the same two factors, but each is more refined (the four-storey palace simile, line 278, and the cloth of finer thread, lines 280–284).
- **Neither perception nor non-perception** (lines 246–272): perception is present but too subtle to do its work, like oil smeared in a bowl that is "there" but too little to pour.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Counting method.** Margaya follows the fivefold method (the second absorption keeps sustained thought, and there is a fifth absorption). Book 4's main text uses the fourfold method and then explains the fivefold one (line 124). This is a difference of presentation, not a conflict. Margaya's "fifth absorption" as the base for the formless states (line 15) equals Book 4's "fourth absorption" (line 139).
- **Holding mastery.** Margaya line 5 defines it as being able to keep the absorption "even for a second or two". Book 4 line 32 says "for as long as one wishes". These are complementary emphases. Margaya's reviewing mastery is so fast that seven impulsions do not occur. Book 4 line 28 speaks of four or five sense-sphere impulsions for adverting. The detail differs, and it is not resolved here.
- **Motive for the formless states.** Only Book 4 (lines 139–157) gives the motive: the dangers of physical form (weapons, quarrels, a thousand diseases), with the snake and enemy similes and the pavilion simile (lines 286–290). Margaya line 17 says only "to move further from form".
- Margaya gives the formless recitations (lines 15–21) in the same order and with the same objects as Book 4 (lines 159, 208, 222–226, 240).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Absorption states, the formless attainments and "neither perception nor non-perception" are meditative and doctrinal claims. They are not psychological findings and have no modern equivalent. Rebirth in the Brahma world (line 173), the claim that even Sariputta could not analyse the fourth formless state item by item (line 266), and the stories of Moggallana are doctrinal. The dangers of the body that motivate the formless states are a renunciant framing, not a general judgement on physical life. Existing cards C014-I01 (the snake and enemy similes) and C014-I02 (the pavilion simile) already cover the similes found here, from Concept 14's sources.

## Human meaning paragraph

As an analogy only: a condition that someone works hard to reach can at first feel like the goal. Once it is familiar and fully under their control, the same condition can reveal a roughness that was invisible before, and a quieter alternative becomes noticeable. The texts insist that this recognition comes after mastery and on reflection, not in the first flush of reaching it. They also separate an excited, bubbling gladness from a calmer ease that is enjoyed without leaning toward it. Finally, being able to reach a state once is treated as different from being able to enter, sustain and leave it at will. Any application to crafts, relationships or ordinary contentment is my extension.

## Idea decisions

- **C088-I01 — A hard-won calm looks coarse once mastered.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Mechanism: familiarity and review reveal flaws in an attained state, and progress requires letting go of attachment to it.
- **C088-I02 — When delight itself unsettles a quieter contentment.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Mechanism: excited gladness disturbs a steadier ease, and very sweet pleasure can be felt without craving.
- **C088-I03 — Reaching a state once versus entering it at will.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Mechanism: mastery is defined as control over entry, duration, exit and review, not as a single success.
- Rejected: *snake and enemy similes* (fear spreading to resemblances). Duplicates C014-I01.
- Rejected: *pavilion simile / relying on a flawed king for livelihood* (line 290). Largely duplicates C014-I02, and the king detail is a single clause.
- Rejected: *oil in the bowl, water on the path* (lines 258–270). These are about the logic of "present but too faint to function". They are an interesting image but not a human process the text develops.
- Rejected: *the king who admires a craftsman without wishing to become one* (line 254). A simile for surpassing, too thin on its own.
- Rejected: *sound as a thorn to the first absorption* (line 177). Technical and doctrinal.
- Rejected: *ten kinds of equanimity* (lines 66–99). A definitional list. Effort-equanimity (balanced effort) has only one line.
