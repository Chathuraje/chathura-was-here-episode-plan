## Central meaning

The seven purifications (විශුද්ධි) are a graded path of insight meditation:

1. Purification of conduct (ශීල විශුද්ධිය).
2. Purification of mind (චිත්ත විශුද්ධිය): concentration strong enough to keep the hindrances out.
3. Purification of view (දිට්ඨි විසුද්ධිය): seeing the person as only mental and material phenomena (නාම-රූප), which removes the belief in a self.
4. Purification by overcoming doubt (කඞ්ඛාවිතරණ): seeing the causes of those phenomena across past, present and future.
5. Purification by knowing path and not-path (මග්ගාමග්ග ඤාණදස්සන): recognising that the ten "corruptions of insight" are not the path.
6. Purification by knowing the way (ප්‍රතිපදා ඤාණදස්සන): the nine insight knowledges, from rise and fall through to conformity.
7. Purification by knowledge and vision (ඤාණදස්සන විසුද්ධිය): the four supramundane paths.

The first two are the ground of insight and the last five its body (Margaya line 46).

## Essential distinctions

- **Purification of conduct** is the only purification with rich everyday material. Pradeepika spends lines 24–1022 (PDF 445–506) on it. It sets out nineteen classifications of conduct and the fourfold purity (restraint by the monastic code, sense restraint, pure livelihood, and reflective use of requisites), with long catalogues of wrong livelihood (lines 355–515). It adds a simile of the defiled virtue (lines 861–897) and the dangers of broken conduct (lines 919–996). The fourfold purity is supplied respectively by faith, mindfulness, effort and wisdom (lines 589–597).
- **Wrong livelihood** (මිථ්‍යාජීව) covers more than breaking rules. It also covers indirect ways of getting support: pretended austerity (කුහනා, lines 375–399), flattery and talk (ලපනා, lines 401–431), hinting (නෙමිත්තකතා, lines 433–455), belittling people so they give out of fear of blame (නිප්පෙසිකතා, lines 459–485) and using one gift to get a larger one (lines 487–489). Margaya compresses all of this into one clause (line 16).
- **Conduct with a limit** (සපරියන්ත සීලය, Pradeepika line 97) is kept for gain, fame, relatives, a limb or life. When the gain comes, the conduct is dropped. Conduct kept for praise, self-exaltation or rebirth is called low or "grasped" (lines 89, 103, 115, 869, 895, 915).
- **Using what is given.** A person without conduct who uses gifts given "to the virtuous" is "using by theft". Using them without reflection is "using on debt" (lines 709–715). The Aggikkhandhopama sūtra passage (lines 972–996) ends with the charge to make givers' help fruitful (line 994).
- **Sense restraint** takes place at the impulsion (ජවන) moment, not in the seeing itself (lines 329–349). This repeats C009, C040 and C043 material.
- **The corruptions of insight** arise only for someone practising well (Margaya line 103; Pradeepika line 2103). The real corruption is subtle craving (නිකන්ති) for the light, joy and calm. The other nine are corruptions only because they feed it (Margaya line 123; Pradeepika line 2167). Each can be grasped three ways: "it arose for me" (view), "a fine light arose" (conceit) and relish (craving) (Pradeepika lines 2171–2173). The story of Mahānāga (lines 2133–2137) tells of an elder who believed for sixty years that he was fully liberated until fear exposed him.
- **The insight knowledges** describe fear and disenchantment. Pradeepika insists that the "knowledge of fearfulness" is a judgement, not being afraid (lines 2311–2313).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Agreement.** Both sources agree on the sevenfold order, the four kinds of conduct, the name-and-form analysis, the causes, the ten corruptions with craving as the real one, the nine knowledges, and the way change-of-lineage leads to the path.
- **Complementary detail.** Pradeepika supplies the classifications of conduct, the wrong-livelihood catalogues, the stories, the eighteen- and forty-fold contemplations, the seven material and seven immaterial septads, the table of what each path abandons, and the analysis of what is "arisen". Margaya supplies the four aspects (characteristic, function, manifestation, proximate cause) with the fire simile (lines 32–44). It notes that faith can suppress wrong view (line 52), that skipping reflection on food is no obstacle for lay people (line 18), and that it leaves out the classification of liberations as unhelpful for ordinary readers (line 185).
- **Differences.** Margaya says pure-insight practitioners gain their purification of mind from a concentration that arises within insight itself (line 23). Pradeepika defines purification of mind only as access concentration plus the eight attainments (line 1026), although it later mentions the dry-insight worker (line 2609). Margaya counts ten insight knowledges, starting with comprehension (line 132), but places only nine in the sixth purification. Pradeepika gives the nine directly (lines 2193–2205). This is a difference in counting, not substance. Pradeepika's numbered list of corruptions (lines 2110–2119) is in a different order from its own verse (lines 2105–2106) and from Margaya (line 103).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- Rebirth, heavens, hells, the path attainments, supernormal light that reaches for leagues, a created elephant, a deity holding a lamp and a truth-act that heals a tumour (lines 638–650, 2127–2137) are doctrinal or miraculous claims.
- Several passages are harmful if lifted out. Pradeepika lines 241–285 list widows, unmarried older women, paṇḍakas, nuns and "faithless families" as unsuitable company. Lines 938–970 heap disgust on the person without conduct (an outcaste-boy simile at line 968; see also line 2363). Lines 972–996 say it is better to embrace fire than a woman when without conduct. Lines 607–613 praise monks who let themselves die rather than cut a living creeper. Lines 2293–2305 use similes of a mother watching her sons beheaded and of nine dead children. Line 1266 describes the womb as a filthy place.
- The detailed matter counts (lines 1050–1078) and the tables of cognitive processes are technical doctrine, not physiology.

## Human meaning paragraph

The purification of conduct describes goodness that is not simply present or absent. The same outward restraint can be a performance aimed at receiving more. It can be a bargain dropped once its reward is secured. It can be sustained by gifts and respect that others give because they believe the recipient is good. The source treats all three as defilements, not as ordinary goodness. The later purifications add a further point: signs that effort is working can become the thing a person wants. This reading is interpretation. The source is written for monks seeking liberation, and its ethical verdicts are doctrinal.

## Idea decisions

- **C097-I01 — Showing you need nothing in order to receive.** Accepted. Based on the wrong-livelihood catalogue, especially pretended austerity. Hinting, flattery and belittling are included as related tactics.
- **C097-I02 — Accepting care meant for who you appear to be.** Accepted as a limited application. Based on using gifts "by theft" or "on debt", the Aggikkhandhopama charge and the Ambakhādaka Mahātissa story.
- **C097-I03 — Goodness that ends once its reward arrives.** Accepted. Based on conduct with a limit, low and grasped conduct, and conduct defiled by expectation of gain.
- **Rejected for overlap:** *Enjoying signs of progress until the work stops* (craving for the corruptions of insight, "my insight is good", lines 1967–1971 and 2103–2177). This shares its mechanism and question with **C016-I01 — Mistaking a striking experience for arrival**. The fuller material here (PDF 571–577) should be offered to the coordinator as extra source trace for C016-I01, not as a new card.
- **Rejected:** *Keeping the rule while rehearsing the wish* (the seven bonds of sexuality, lines 879–897). The mechanism is real, but the material is monastic and gendered, and a secular rendering risks moralising private thought. Item 7 (celibacy for a divine reward) is used in C097-I03 instead.
- **Rejected:** *Small shifts that hide steady discomfort* (suffering concealed by changing posture, line 2215). It rests on one sentence and is bound to the doctrine of the three characteristics.
- **Rejected:** *A suppressed urge returning later* (lines 3152–3158: a sight by day inflames at night, and a singing voice stirs an elder with the attainments). This is covered by C074-I01, C013-I02 and C048-I01.
- **Rejected:** *Keeping at it when clarity does not come* (mirror, sesame oil and clearing-nut similes, lines 1148–1162). This is close to C013-I01.
- **Rejected:** *Judging a danger without being afraid of it* (lines 2311–2313). The passage is thin and tied to insight stages.
- **Rejected:** decades of life (lines 1822–1855), the blind and lame man (lines 1234–1238) and the vina (line 2047). These are analogies for non-self doctrine. The first is close to C066-I03.
