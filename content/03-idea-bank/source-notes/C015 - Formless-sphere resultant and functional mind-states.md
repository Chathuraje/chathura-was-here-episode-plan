# C015 — Formless-sphere resultant and functional mind-states

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–49). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 1, PDF 100–101 (printed 89–90). The extract starts mid-page on PDF 100 / printed 89 (line 21). Page marker: line 43 (PDF 101 / printed 90).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–24). Heading `# 16. අරූපාවචර විපාක, ක්‍රියා`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

The four formless-sphere resultant states (base of infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, neither-perception-nor-non-perception) mirror the four formless wholesome absorptions. One who dies without losing the absorption is reborn in the matching formless plane, where the same state serves as rebirth, life-continuum and death consciousness. No body arises there, only mind, because the absorption was developed through revulsion toward form. Life spans are 20,000, 40,000, 60,000 and 84,000 great aeons (Pradeepika line 30; Margaya lines 3–12). The four formless functional states are the same absorptions arising in arahants, who produce them in order to enter the attainment of cessation (නිරෝධ සමාපත්ති) (Pradeepika lines 39, 49; Margaya line 14). All twelve formless states count as fifth-absorption states with neutral feeling (Pradeepika line 41; Margaya line 16).

## Essential distinctions

- **Quick and slow learners** (Pradeepika line 45): those of sharp wisdom need three preparatory moments before absorption, those of duller wisdom four. The Buddha taught four absorptions for the former and five for the latter (line 47). The eight and nine attainments are named (line 47).
- **Happiness needing nothing external** (Margaya line 12): sense-sphere beings suffer endlessly through maintaining a body and get pleasure by gratifying the senses with outside things. Formless beings need nothing from outside. Analogy: sense-sphere existence is like plain rice, tasteless unless something is added; formless existence is like a piece of cake, tasty on its own.
- **Exalted (මහග්ගත) states** (Margaya line 18): the 27 fine-material and formless states, "belonging to higher persons, not to ordinary people".
- **Counts** (Margaya lines 20–24): 81 mundane states, with feeling breakdown.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the four resultant and four functional states, the fifth-absorption classification and the purpose of the functional absorptions (cessation). Pradeepika adds life spans, the quick and slow learner distinction and the names of the attainment sets. Margaya adds the rice-and-cake analogy, that consciousness in sense and fine-material worlds depends on a body, that higher formless states are more refined, that non-returners as well as arahants can enter cessation, and the counts. Pradeepika line 49 mentions only arahants for cessation; Margaya line 14 includes non-returners. This is a difference of scope in the extract rather than a stated disagreement.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth into formless planes, bodiless existence and aeon-long life spans are cosmology. Cessation attainment is a claim about realised persons. The comparison of happiness applies to beings in those planes and cannot be transferred to human life without changing its meaning.

## Human meaning paragraph

No supported human process could be extracted without converting cosmology into a premise. The only passage that touches ordinary experience is Margaya's rice-and-cake analogy: pleasure that needs something added from outside versus a state that is satisfying in itself. In the source, however, the second term is existence without a body in a formless plane, not a human possibility.
