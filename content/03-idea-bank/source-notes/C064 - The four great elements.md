## Central meaning

All matter is built from four inseparable "great elements" (මහාභූත): earth (පඨවි, hardness and softness), water (ආපෝ, cohesion and flowing), fire (තේජෝ, heat and cold, ripening) and air (වායෝ, distension, pushing, motion). They always arise together in a material group (කලාප), support each other, and vary only in which one predominates. Different proportions yield different substances, colours, tastes and functions (Pradeepika lines 112–122; Margaya line 21). The concept extract also covers the five sensitivities: a special clear "sheen" (ඔපය) of karma-born elements located in eye, ear, nose, tongue and body, which serves as the base of the five sense-consciousnesses (Pradeepika lines 124–217; Margaya lines 23–47).

## Essential distinctions

- **Characteristic vs. function** for each element: earth, hardness / supporting co-arisen matter (Pradeepika line 56); water, fluidity / binding (line 74); fire, heat / ripening (line 92); air, distension / motion (line 110).
- **Four senses of each element word** (Pradeepika lines 46–54, 62–72, 80–90, 98–108): the physical mass (සසම්භාර), the meditation object (ආරම්මණ), the named deity of a Brahma realm (සම්මත) and the characteristic itself (ලක්ෂණ).
- **Hard and soft are one quality in degree.** Margaya line 7: softness is only mild hardness. "Hard" and "soft", like "big" and "small", are names given by comparison.
- **Fire both grows and wears down.** Margaya line 15 and Pradeepika line 78: the same ripening matures and strengthens bodies and plants while young, and after maturity the continued ripening makes them decay.
- **Water cannot be touched.** What the hand feels in water is earth, fire and air (Pradeepika line 74), a point developed in C065.
- **Sense-sensitivity vs. sense organ.** The fleshly organ (සසම්භාර චක්ඛු) differs from the sensitivity (ප්‍රසාද චක්ඛු) located in a tiny region (Pradeepika lines 142–171; Margaya line 31).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the four elements, their marks, their inseparability, and the locations and images of the sensitivities (for example "oil soaked into seven layers of cotton", Pradeepika line 157 and Margaya line 31; the nose region shaped like a goat's hoof, Pradeepika line 189 and Margaya line 39). Pradeepika adds folk etymologies of "great element" (lines 20–36: magicians and deceiving demonesses who conjure attractive forms), the fourfold meanings, the 14/44 constituents of the eye (lines 144–155), and the 17-mind-moment lifespan of eye-decad groups (line 165). Margaya adds the relativity of hard and soft (line 7), and an alternative opinion on the size of the eye-sensitivity region (a mung-bean husk, line 31). No genuine disagreement was found. The tongue region is compared to a water-lily petal in both (Pradeepika line 201 උපුල්පතෙක; Margaya line 43 මානෙල් මල් පෙත්තක).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The element theory is a historical account of matter. It must not be equated with chemistry, physics or anatomy. The claim that sense-sensitivities are produced by past karma rooted in the craving to see, hear and so on (Pradeepika lines 126, 167–171; Margaya line 27) is a rebirth doctrine. The Brahma-realm "element deities", the demoness etymology and the mind-moment lifespans are doctrinal or cosmological. The stated embryology and physiology (for example the digestive-fire zone excluded from body-sensitivity, Pradeepika line 213) are historical accounts, not medical facts.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept is almost entirely a technical description of matter and of the physical base of the senses. The only passages that touch a human process are side remarks: that hard and soft are judged by comparison, and that the same ripening which strengthens a young body later wears it down. The first is a general point about relative words rather than a mechanism of experience. The second is better developed, with the source's own discussion of visible ageing, in C066 (C066-I03), where this concept is recorded as supporting.

## Idea decisions

- **No idea card.** The concept is a classification of matter. Forcing a human premise from it would misrepresent the text.
- Rejected candidate: *What grows us also wears us down* (fire ripens and then decays). Supported, but as a physical doctrine. It is used as supporting material for C066-I03 instead of a separate card, which would duplicate that mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: *Hard and soft exist only in comparison* (Margaya line 7). This is a linguistic point about relative terms, too general to form a specific human mechanism, and extending it to social judgement ("strict", "gentle") would go beyond the source.
- Rejected candidate: *Beauty conjured from mere elements* (Pradeepika lines 22–30, elements "deceive the senses" by appearing as gold, gems and attractive bodies). This is a folk etymology with demoness imagery. The underlying point overlaps C001-I01 (a whole is its parts arranged).
- Rejected candidate: *Senses shaped by the desire to experience* (Margaya line 27). The mechanism is karmic rebirth and cannot be a premise.
