## Central meaning

When the object strikes the sense after two or three past life-continuum moments, the process runs through seven impulsions but has no room for the two registration moments. The first great-object process ends with one life-continuum moment. The second ends at the seventh impulsion. There are two per door and ten across the five doors (Pradeepika lines 25–35; Margaya lines 3–9). These are also called "impulsion-ending" (ජවන වාරය) processes (Pradeepika line 35).

## Essential distinctions

- **Why the delay.** Pradeepika (line 27) says it comes from weakness in the base, object, light or attention. Margaya (line 3) gives concrete causes: the object is small, far away even if large, or the light is dim.
- **Why no registration.** Margaya (line 7) says the object is in its last moment and too weak to condition registration, and notes that teachers disagree on registration (these views are omitted "as a bother for beginners"). Pradeepika (lines 31–33) says registration must come in a pair, so with only one moment left a life-continuum moment arises instead.
- **Small-object processes (in Margaya).** After four to nine past moments, not enough time remains for seven impulsions. A mind-order rule says impulsion arises seven times unless there is a special reason, so no impulsion arises and determining repeats once or twice (lines 29–37). Margaya answers the question "why not six impulsions?" (line 37). All minds in one process must share an object that is the same in time as well as in kind, and impulsion cannot take a past object here.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the structure and the counts. They give different reasons for the missing registration: weakness of the dying object (Margaya) versus the pair rule (Pradeepika). These are complementary, not contradictory. Pradeepika line 57 adds that these processes occur in sense-sphere and fine-material beings.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The moment counts, the pair rule and the idea of an object's seventeen-moment lifespan are doctrinal technicalities. The physical causes in Margaya line 3 (small, distant, dim) are ordinary observations, but the source uses them only to explain a timing classification.

## Human meaning paragraph

The human-relevant point is modest. Whether an experience is taken in fully depends on conditions such as size, distance, light and attention, and a slightly weaker contact is still fully responded to but leaves less behind. The fuller treatment of faint perception is in the small and very-small object processes, carded under C045.

## Idea decisions

- No card for C044. Its human content (weak conditions reduce how fully something is experienced) shares the mechanism of C045-I01. It is used there as supporting evidence rather than duplicated.
- Rejected candidate: *Responding fully but not lingering*. The difference between great and very-great processes is only the registration moments, which the sources treat as a timing and doctrinal matter, not as a lived difference.
