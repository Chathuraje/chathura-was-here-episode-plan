## Central meaning

The last cognitive process of a life has only five impulsion moments instead of the usual seven, because the heart-base is very weak (Pradeepika line 25; Margaya file for C061, line 13). It may run through any of the five sense doors or the mind door, and in four patterns depending on whether death consciousness follows immediately after the impulsions, after a life-continuum moment, after two registration moments, or after registration and a life-continuum moment: 6 doors × 4 patterns, which Pradeepika counts as 24 (five sense doors × 4 = 20, plus 4 at the mind door). The near-death impulsions are always sense-sphere ones: greed-, hatred- or delusion-rooted, or great wholesome (line 104). Their object is an action, a sign of action or a sign of destination. Pradeepika then maps what kind of impulsions lead to what rebirth (lines 106–130) and enumerates 24 rebirth-linkings by plane and object (lines 132–192).

## Essential distinctions

- **A present sense object can be the sign of action.** Margaya line 3: sometimes a thing actually in front of the eyes at death is the sign of action; rarely even a destination sign may be seen. Margaya line 25 gives concrete cases: a sound-door process for someone who dies listening to the Dhamma, a smell-door process for someone who dies smelling incense offered in worship, a taste-door process for someone into whose mouth something has been placed, a touch-door process for someone who dies while receiving someone's touch.
- **Timing of objects across death** (Margaya line 9; Pradeepika line 194). A present visible object has a life of seventeen mind-moments, so it can still be the object of the rebirth-linking consciousness and first life-continuum moments of the next life.
- **Roots and outcomes** (Pradeepika lines 106–130). Greed impulsions tend to the ghost realm, hatred impulsions to states of loss, delusion impulsions mostly to animal birth.
- **Arahants** (Pradeepika line 200): functional impulsions contemplate formations as impermanent, suffering and non-self; no rebirth follows.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both give the four patterns and the doorway structure. Margaya counts the categories as six doors (line 3) while Pradeepika counts 24 processes (line 29); this is a presentational difference. Pradeepika adds the root-to-realm mapping and the 24 rebirth-linkings table; Margaya adds the concrete deathbed examples (line 25), the object-timing explanation (line 9), the asañña and immaterial transitions (lines 37–41), the bhavaṅga explanation (line 43), and a closing reference to cutting craving (line 47). **Numerical disagreement:** Margaya line 7 says the new life's first mind-door process after 15 or 16 life-continuum moments has greed impulsions arising seven times; Pradeepika (C061 file, line 87) says five. Not resolved here.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Mind-moment counts, the seventeen-moment life of matter, the heart-base, the mapping of roots to realms, the 24 rebirth-linkings and every rebirth transition are doctrinal and technical. The claim that the object at death determines the next birth must never be presented as a fact or used to judge how someone died.

## Human meaning paragraph

Most of this concept is a technical table with no human mechanism. One passage has a human side: the source expects that what is actually present around a dying person (a sermon or chanting being heard, the smell of offered incense, something placed in the mouth, another person's touch) can become the last thing the mind takes up. Read alongside C061, where the same author tradition says such objects are not brought by relatives but arise through the person's own past action, it points to a real tension that the everyday reading below explores as interpretation: those who sit with the dying often try to shape the final surroundings, while what the dying person actually takes in remains beyond their control and largely beyond anyone's knowledge.

## Idea decisions

- **C062-I01 — Trying to shape a dying person's last impressions.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Margaya lines 3 and 25, with C061 Pradeepika lines 29 and 51 as supporting.
- Rejected candidate: the root-to-realm mapping (Pradeepika lines 126–130). Doctrinal outcome claims; turning them into a card would imply judging the dying by their final state.
- Rejected candidate: the five weakened impulsions as "a fading mind". A technical count with no described experience.
- Rejected candidate: the arahant's final contemplation (line 200). Doctrinal attainment, no general human process given.
