## Central meaning

The 25 beautiful factors appear only in the 59 (or 91) beautiful consciousnesses, by four methods: the 19 beautiful universals in all 59; the three abstinences in 16 (fixed and together in the 8 supramundane, variable and separately in the 8 great wholesome); compassion and gladness in 28 (variable, separately); wisdom in the 47 knowledge-associated states. The section also defines fixed (නියතයෝගී) and variable (අනියතයෝගී) factors: 41 fixed, 11 variable (Margaya line 23; Pradeepika lines 72–88).

## Essential distinctions

- **Abstinence only in the act of refraining** (Margaya line 17; Pradeepika line 41). Wholesome consciousness in giving, worship, preaching or listening to the teaching has no abstinence. Abstinence occurs only when a person completes virtue by refraining from misconduct.
- **One abstinence at a time** (Margaya lines 5, 17). Refraining from a verbal wrong is not refraining from a bodily wrong; refraining from lying is not refraining from slander. In ordinary wholesome consciousness only one abstinence occurs at a time. In path consciousness all three occur together because the defilements that cause wrongdoing are destroyed (Margaya line 19).
- **Compassion and gladness separately** (Margaya line 21). Feeling for the suffering and rejoicing with the happy are "two different jobs", so both never occur in one mind-state.
- **Teacher's opinion** (Margaya line 7). Some hold that compassion and gladness occur only with joyful feeling, which would give 20 rather than 28 consciousnesses. Pradeepika (lines 47–49, 65) gives only 28 and excludes the fifth jhāna because of equanimity.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree (Margaya's 47 for wisdom = 12 + 15 + 12 + 8; Pradeepika's = 12 + 27 + 8). Genuine disagreement recorded: Margaya line 7 reports the minority view (20 consciousnesses) that Pradeepika does not mention. Pradeepika line 72 contains a garbled sentence (see batch report).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Supramundane path consciousness destroying defilements and jhāna counts are doctrinal. "One abstinence at a time" is a claim about single mind-moments, not about a person's overall character or a day's conduct.

## Human meaning paragraph

The everyday thread is that declining to do harm is a specific act tied to a specific occasion, not a general glow of goodness produced by doing good deeds. A person who gives generously has not thereby refrained from anything; a person who declines to lie has not thereby declined to gossip. This is my interpretation of a moment-level classification.

## Idea decisions

- **C027-I01 — Not harming is a different act from helping.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Margaya line 17, supported by C024 Pradeepika lines 27, 33, 37. Related to C024-I01, which concerns *what* holds a person back rather than the distinctness of the act.
- Rejected candidate: *You cannot pity and rejoice in the same moment* (Margaya line 21). A single-sentence reason about mind-moments; not enough to support a human mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: fixed and variable factors. Purely technical.
