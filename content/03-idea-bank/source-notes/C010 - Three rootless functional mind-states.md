# C010 — Three rootless functional mind-states

## Central meaning

The three rootless functional (අහේතුක ක්‍රියා) mind-states have none of the six wholesome or unwholesome roots and produce no karmic result; they are "mere function" (Pradeepika line 25; Margaya line 3).

1. **Five-door adverting (පඤ්චද්වාරාවජ්ජන)** turns the stream of mind toward a sight, sound, smell, taste or touch that has struck a sense door. It arises and ceases before eye-consciousness and the rest. It has no power to know the object; the following sense-consciousness knows it. It is weak and has neutral feeling (Pradeepika lines 29–33; Margaya lines 11–15).
2. **Mind-door adverting (මනොද්වාරාවජ්ජන)** turns the mind toward objects taken by the mind itself. In a five-door process the same state performs "determining" (වොට්ඨපන), fixing the object as "such" after investigation (Pradeepika line 37; Margaya lines 13–15). Margaya says the two can only be properly understood in the later chapter on cognitive processes (line 15).
3. **Smile-producing (හසිතුප්පාද)**, with joy, arises only in arahants and does nothing but produce a smile (Pradeepika lines 41–45; Margaya lines 17–19).

## Essential distinctions

- **Turning toward versus knowing.** Adverting only turns the mind; recognising belongs to a later state (Pradeepika line 33). Margaya words the five-door adverting as the mind that knows *that* an object has struck a door (line 13), not *what* it is.
- **Other smiling states.** Pradeepika line 47 lists twelve further joyful states that can produce a smile (four greed-rooted, four wholesome, four great functional) and which types of person have which. Margaya line 17 says those states do more than smile; hasituppāda only smiles.
- **Six kinds of laughter** (Margaya lines 19–25), from a poetics treatise: slight eye-opening (සිත), a silent smile showing tooth-tips (හසිත), soft audible laughter (විහසිත), laughter shaking head and shoulders (උපහසිත), laughter with tears (අපහසිත), whole-body convulsive laughter (අතිහසිත). The text assigns the first two to Buddhas and similar beings, the middle two to good ordinary people and learners, and the last two to those "with low intentions who have not developed virtue".
- All eighteen rootless states are counted as unprompted (Pradeepika line 47; Margaya line 27). Pradeepika lines 55–72 and Margaya lines 29–31 give the totals of the thirty "non-beautiful" states.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the three states, their feelings and their functional character. Pradeepika adds the physical basis (heart-base), the arahant's reason for smiling (joy on seeing another being's saṃsāric condition and knowing oneself freed of it, line 41) and the full count of smiling states. Margaya adds the explanation of the word "functional", the door model and the six kinds of laughter. There is no genuine disagreement. Margaya's reason for the name hasituppāda (it *only* smiles) and Pradeepika's (it arises only for smiling in arahants) are complementary.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- The heart-base (හෘදය වස්තු / හෘදය කෝෂය) as the seat of mind is a historical physiological doctrine, not anatomy.
- Adverting is a single mind-moment in a doctrinal model of cognition. It must not be presented as equivalent to findings in attention research.
- The arahant's smile and its reason are claims about realised beings.
- The six-laughter ranking links ways of laughing to moral development. Carried over, it would shame people for loud or tearful laughter and could map onto class or decorum norms. It is not used.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source separates the bare turning of the mind toward something that has arrived from the later knowing of what it is, and it says the turning has no power to know. As a limited analogy only, this points to an ordinary experience: the moment of being pulled toward a sound, movement or sudden thought before it is recognised, and the separate moment when it is fixed as "this". The source describes single momentary events inside a doctrinal model of cognition, so any everyday version stretches its timescale. That extension is interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C010-I01 — Noticing something before knowing what it is.** Held. The distinction is clearly sourced, but the source concerns single momentary mind-states within a model of cognition the text itself defers to a later chapter. Turning it into an everyday idea risks implying scientific equivalence. A reviewer should decide whether it can stand as a limited analogy. It also largely duplicates C034-I01 (Seeing before knowing what you see) and C042-I01 (A first glance is not yet knowing), drawn from the cognitive-process chapters. Recommended: merge into C034-I01 with C010 as a supporting reference.
- Rejected candidate: *How you laugh shows who you are* (Margaya lines 19–25). The ranking is moral and hierarchical, drawn from a poetics treatise, and would stigmatise ordinary laughter.
- Rejected candidate: *A smile that wants nothing* (hasituppāda). It is a claim about arahants only; there is no ordinary-person mechanism in the text. Related existing card from another batch: C068-I01 (Not every joy turns into laughter).
