## Central meaning

Matter (රූප) is the third ultimate reality after consciousness and mental factors. Everything seen, heard, smelled, tasted and touched, from the earth and ocean to drums and voices, is "a modification of a collection of forces arising from various conditions", changing under opposing conditions such as cold and heat (Pradeepika line 20). Matter arises only from four conditions (action, mind, temperature, nutriment), passes through arising, presence and dissolution, and lasts seventeen mind-moments (fifty-one sub-moments) (line 26). A thing looks stable while its producing condition stays the same, because a similar series keeps arising; fire changes the condition and the object burns to ash (line 26). There are 28 kinds: four great elements and 24 derived kinds, grouped eleven ways (lines 30–69). Margaya groups them as 18 concretely produced (නිෂ්පන්න) and 10 non-concretely produced (අනිෂ්පන්න) (lines 3–7).

## Essential distinctions

- Tangible object is not a separate kind of matter; it is earth, fire and air elements, so it is not counted again (Pradeepika line 49; Margaya line 11).
- Four great elements are primary; the other 24 depend on them (Pradeepika line 30; Margaya line 13).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both agree on the total and the primary/derived division. Margaya lists five "modification" kinds including bodily and vocal intimation (line 5), whereas Pradeepika separates intimation (2) from modification (3) (lines 61–65); the totals agree. Pradeepika names space element as "delimitation matter" (පරිච්ඡේද රූපය, line 55). Margaya instructs the reader to memorise the list (line 9). No substantive disagreement.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The four-condition theory, mind-moment lifespans of matter, heart-base and sex-faculty matter are doctrinal analyses and must not be equated with physics, chemistry or biology. Pradeepika's phrase "subtle atomic forces" (line 24) is not modern atomic theory.

## Human meaning paragraph

This concept is a classification list. The only passage with a possible human reading is that things appear stable only while their conditions remain steady (line 26). That is a general observation about change and is already covered in spirit by C001-I01 (wholes as arranged parts). It does not supply a distinct human mechanism, tension or decision.

## Idea decisions

- Rejected candidate: *Things look stable only while conditions hold* (Pradeepika line 26). Too general, a physical claim rather than a human process, and it overlaps C001-I01. Any use would risk presenting doctrine as physics.
