# C094 — Mindfulness of breathing meditation

Page markers run from line 28 (PDF 200 / printed 181) to line 499 (PDF 240 / printed 221). The extract starts mid-page on PDF 199 / printed 180 (line 22). Sinhala commentary only.
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–7). Heading `# 10. ආනාපානසති භාවනාව`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Mindfulness of breathing (ආනාපානසති) sets the mind on the in-breath and out-breath. Pradeepika praises it as peaceful and refined from the very start, needing no preparatory "sweetening", and says it stills distracting thoughts (lines 26–42). It explains the setting (forest, tree root, empty place; line 52–56), posture (lines 62–64), and the sixteen-step method in four tetrads: body (lines 72–250), feeling (252–327), mind (331–399) and phenomena or insight (403–471), followed by benefits (475–501). Only the first tetrad is given to a beginner; the other three are for those who already have absorption (lines 112, 283).

The practical core for a beginner is: count breaths (lines 118–128), then follow them with continuous mindfulness (130), attending at the nose-tip where the breath touches (132–148). As body and mind settle, the breath becomes so fine that one must search for it (222–244). If it seems to vanish, one should not get up but return to the nose-tip (154–158). A sign arises that appears differently to different people (158). The teacher should neither confirm nor deny that it is the sign (164).

Margaya gives a short lay account: sit upright, find the place the breath touches, try to be aware of the breath's beginning, middle and end from nose-tip to navel (line 5), let the breath be natural, count to ten and restart, then drop counting (line 7).

## Essential distinctions

- **Natural versus forced breathing.** Margaya line 7: breathing with effort leads to fatigue and possibly stomach or chest trouble. Pradeepika describes breath becoming subtle through settling, not through control (lines 218–224), and the swing-watcher making no special effort (138).
- **Following versus staying at the point of contact.** Pradeepika: do not let the mind travel in and out with the breath (126, 130); attend where the breath touches, like a gatekeeper or a carpenter watching where saw teeth meet wood (140–146). The mind still knows the whole breath (146).
- **Counting as a support, not a goal.** Count only up to ten, do not skip or go beyond, and stop once the mind can stay without it (118–128).
- **Coarse and subtle.** Each settled stage makes the prior stage look coarse (228–250).
- **Knowing a state through its object and through insight** (lines 289–297, 307): a feeling is known "by object" when absorbed and "by non-confusion" when examined as passing.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on sitting upright, placing the mind at the point of contact, counting up to ten and restarting, and later dropping the count. Margaya's warning about forced breathing has no exact parallel in Pradeepika, which stresses settling instead; this is complementary.

**Genuine disagreement:** Margaya (line 5) tells the practitioner to try to see the breath's beginning, middle and end, following the air from nose-tip to navel. Pradeepika (lines 126, 130, 148) says the mind must *not* follow the breath's beginning, middle and end inward and outward, because this scatters the mind; one attends only at the nose-tip. Pradeepika's third step (line 206) does speak of knowing the beginning, middle and end of the whole breath, but while the mind stays at the nose-tip. These are recorded, not reconciled.

Second tension: Margaya (line 3) says the practice is done by many both within and outside the Buddhist dispensation. Pradeepika (line 46) says the sixteen-step practice succeeds for a monk within this dispensation, not holding outside views.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rising into the air (line 154), absence of breath in the fourth absorption, in the womb and for the dead (154, 493), rebirth-related "last breaths" (483–495), the elder who chose his moment of death while walking (497–501), the claim that all Buddhas awaken through this practice (42), matching dwellings to humours and temperaments (58–62), the insight-knowledge sequence to arahantship (166–170) and the medical effects of forced breathing (Margaya line 7) are doctrinal or unverified. They are not idea premises. The practice is not to be presented as a proven therapy.

## Human meaning paragraph

A person trying to attend to something as quiet as breathing meets several ordinary difficulties. The mind, used to more stimulating objects, keeps running off (lines 46–50). The wish to observe well slides into controlling what is observed, which exhausts. Following something along its whole path scatters attention, while staying at one point where it passes lets the whole movement be known. As agitation settles, what is watched becomes fainter, and it is easy to conclude that it has gone. At the point of first success, a teacher's confirmation or denial can itself stop progress. The ideas below take three of these. They are interpretations of a meditation manual, not claims about breathing physiology.

## Idea decisions

- **C094-I01 — Watching the breath without taking control of it.** Accepted for research. Margaya line 7; Pradeepika lines 138, 154, 218–224.
- **C094-I02 — Watching one point to follow a whole movement.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Pradeepika lines 136–148. The disagreement with Margaya line 5 is stated in its limits.
- **C094-I03 — When a teacher's verdict stops the learner.** Accepted for research. Pradeepika line 164.
- Rejected candidate: *A mind used to stimulation refusing a quiet task* (the untamed calf tied to a post, lines 46–50). A strong image, but the mechanism of pulling back a wandering mind to one object is already covered by C013-I01 and related cards. Cited as context rather than a separate card.
- Rejected candidate: *Counting as scaffolding you must later drop* (lines 118–128). Technical; the human claim (supports become the object if overused) is thin in the source.
- Rejected candidate: *Knowing one's last breath* (lines 483–501). Rebirth and death-timing claims; doctrinal. Related to C062-I01 only as context.
- Rejected candidate: *The sign appears differently to each person* (line 158). Interesting, but the source gives only a one-line cause (differences of perception and wisdom); folded into C094-I03's context.
