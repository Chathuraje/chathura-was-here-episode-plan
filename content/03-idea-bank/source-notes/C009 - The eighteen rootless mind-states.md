## Central meaning

"Rootless" (අහේතුක) states contain none of the six roots: greed, hatred, delusion and their opposites. Margaya (line 3) stresses that this does not mean they arise without cause. There are eighteen: seven results of unwholesome action, eight results of wholesome action and three functional states. The results include the ten sense consciousnesses (seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, one pleasant and one unpleasant version each), receiving (සම්පටිච්ඡන) and investigating (සන්තීරණ) states.

## Essential distinctions

- **Conditions for seeing** (Pradeepika lines 79–90): an intact eye-sensitivity, a visible object, light and attention (මනසිකාරය). Parallel lists for hearing (open ear passage, lines 106–111), smell (air, lines 117–124), taste (moisture, lines 130–137) and touch (solidity, lines 149–154).
- **Attention selects** (line 81): countless forms meet the eye and one cannot say all are seen. When there is a wish to see a certain group of forms, attention arises and only the wanted object is taken.
- **Why bare sensing is neutral** (line 92): the colour meets the eye as softly as cotton touching cotton, so seeing itself cannot be pleasant or painful. Only after the object is well recognised do joyful or displeased states arise, depending on whether it is agreeable. Hearing, smell and taste are also neutral for similar reasons (lines 102–106, 117, 139).
- **Touch is different** (lines 143–147): like a hammer striking through a tuft of cotton onto an anvil, touch strikes hard, so bodily consciousness is pleasant or painful.
- **Agreeable versus disagreeable** (lines 94–98): judged by what ordinary "middling" people have long accepted, but perception can be inverted, so agreeable things may be taken as disagreeable and the reverse. One object can be agreeable to one sense and disagreeable to another (a sword pleasing to look at but painful to the body; a fine substance pleasant to touch but foul-smelling). The same filth produces aversion in an ordinary person, greed in a pig, reflection on the body in a wise person, and a purely functional response in an arahant.
- **Receiving and investigating** (lines 158–166): like taking an object in hand and then examining it before deciding what it is. A very agreeable object is investigated with joy; a middling one with neutral feeling (also Margaya line 52).
- **Rebirth functions** (lines 168–178): the neutral investigating states serve as rebirth-linking consciousness for lower realms or for humans born with disabilities or hardship; they also serve as life-continuum and registration.
- **Why unwholesome action has only rootless results** (lines 184–190).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the count and composition (Pradeepika 25–69; Margaya 3–46). Margaya adds the crucial clarification that "rootless" does not mean causeless (line 3), explains that the sense states are named by location and the heart-based ones by function (line 11), and notes there are rootless wholesome results but no rootless wholesome states (line 48). Pradeepika gives the physiology, analogies, conditions and rebirth functions. The two list the joyful and neutral investigating states in opposite order (Pradeepika 58–59; Margaya 43–44); this is not a disagreement.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claim that the agreeable or disagreeable objects a person meets are results of past action, the rebirth-linking functions, and especially the statement that people born blind, deaf or mute reflect weak wholesome action (lines 168–172) are doctrinal. The last must never be carried into ideas and is ethically harmful if applied to real people. The eye-sensitivity, heart-base and "four great elements" accounts are traditional physiology, not modern science.

## Human meaning paragraph

The Pradeepika describes perception as layered. A sense contact, needing conditions such as light and attention, first registers without liking or disliking. The object is then received and examined. Only after recognition does a pleasant or unpleasant response follow, and that response can differ between people, between senses, and when perception is distorted. Attention decides which of countless available sights are taken up at all. The everyday reading is interpretation: much of what people call a reaction to a thing is a later response shaped by who is meeting it.

## Idea decisions

- **C009-I01 — Most of what meets the eye goes unseen.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 81 and 87–90.
- **C009-I02 — Liking and disliking come after the seeing.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 92 and 94–98, and lines 158–166.
- Rejected candidate: *Receiving before judging* (lines 158–166). Overlaps C009-I02; folded into it.
- Rejected candidate: *Touch strikes harder than sight* (lines 143–147). Traditional physiology with little human tension; mentioned inside C009-I02 only.
- Rejected candidate: anything built on rebirth-linking or disability as result of action. Doctrinal and ethically harmful.
