# C041 — The very-great-object eye-door process

## Central meaning

When a visible object strikes the eye-sensitivity, a fixed series of seventeen mind-moments unfolds. It starts with the life-continuum (භවඞ්ග) that is "past", "vibrating" and "cut off", then five-door adverting (පඤ්චද්වාරාවජ්ජන), eye-consciousness (චක්ඛු විඤ්ඤාණ), receiving (සම්පටිච්ඡන), investigating (සන්තීරණ), determining (වොට්ඨපන), seven impulsion moments (ජවන) and two registration moments (තදාරම්මණ). Then it lapses back into the life-continuum. This happens when the object is "very great", which means it strikes after only one moment has passed. Pradeepika (line 25) says it depends on four strong factors: the eye-sensitivity, the object, light and attention (මනසිකාර). The object and the eye-sensitivity cease together with the second registration moment (Pradeepika line 56; Margaya line 7).

## Essential distinctions

- Which moments are result (විපාක), functional (ක්‍රියා) or impulsion (Pradeepika lines 34–48). Determining is the mind-door adverting consciousness performing a determining function (line 44). Impulsion is one of the 29 sense-sphere wholesome, unwholesome or functional types (line 46; Margaya line 7, "එකුන් තිස්").
- The same pattern holds for the ear, nose, tongue and body doors (Pradeepika line 52). These processes are also called registration-ending processes (line 54).
- Margaya (line 9) explains the names past, vibrating and arrest life-continuum. It gives a memorised sequence (line 11), counts fourteen process minds from adverting to the second registration (line 7), and describes the life-continuum "shaking" as the object strikes (lines 3–5).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the order and the seventeen-moment lifespan of the object. Pradeepika adds the four enabling factors, the base (heart-base versus eye-base) of each moment and the classification of each moment. Margaya adds a functional gloss on each stage (turning toward, seeing, accepting, examining, judging) and the "shaking" image. There is no substantive disagreement. The abbreviation for "past life-continuum" differs (`ති` in Pradeepika line 30; `තී` in the C042 Margaya chart).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The object of the life-continuum being taken from the previous life (Pradeepika line 34), the heart-base (හදය වස්තු) and the counted mind-moments are doctrinal or historical-physiological claims. They are not cognitive science and must not be presented as a measured timeline of perception.

## Human meaning paragraph

Read cautiously, the passage describes perception as a series of stages, not a single flash. Contact first draws attention, then bare seeing occurs, then acceptance, examination and settling, and only then a full engagement with the object that can be wholesome or unwholesome. It also says that a full experience depends on several conditions together: a working sense, a strong object, light and attention. Both points are developed more fully, with similes and human consequences, in C042, C043, C044 and C045. Cards are placed there to avoid duplication.
