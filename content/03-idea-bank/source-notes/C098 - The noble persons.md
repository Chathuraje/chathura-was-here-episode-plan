# C098 — The noble persons

## Central meaning

Those who have produced the supramundane paths and fruits through insight meditation are noble persons (ආර්‍ය්‍ය පුද්ගල); everyone else is an ordinary person (පෘථග්ජන). There are eight: a path-moment person and a fruit person for each of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning and arahantship. The path moment lasts an instant, so the names are used mostly for fruit persons (Margaya line 7). Each path abandons a defined set of defilements; the remaining defilements are listed by mind-state (Margaya lines 11–27) and by ten or more classified groups in a four-column chart (Pradeepika lines 24–135). The sources then describe the attainments available to noble persons: fruition attainment, and cessation attainment with its four preliminary resolves (Margaya lines 31–45; Pradeepika lines 283–326), their worthiness of offerings (Pradeepika line 332), and subtypes of stream-enterers, once-returners and non-returners defined by the number and place of remaining rebirths (Pradeepika lines 334–378).

## Essential distinctions

- **What "abandoning" acts on** (Pradeepika lines 143–145). The path does not abandon past defilements (already ceased), future ones (not yet arisen) or present ones (no path development occurs while a defilement is present). It abandons what would otherwise arise, like a young tree cut at the root before it fruits.
- **Kinds of "arisen"** (lines 151–191): present, arisen-and-gone, kamma that has made room for results, and "ground-obtained" (unabandoned tendencies lying in one's own aggregates). A second fourfold set separates the currently occurring, what arises later once an object has been gripped (example: Mahātissa, line 185), what has not been suppressed, and what has been suppressed but not uprooted (example: an elder with the eight attainments stirred by a woman's song, line 189).
- **Three abandonings** (lines 205–249): suppression by worldly concentration, abandoning "by the opposite factor" through each insight knowledge (lines 213–245), and cutting off by the noble path.
- **The four kinds of bias** (අගති, line 50): doing what should not be done and not doing what should be done, through desire, hatred, delusion or fear. All four are abandoned by stream-entry (chart line 112).
- **The eight worldly conditions** (ලෝක ධර්ම, line 38): gain, loss, disrepute, fame, blame, praise, pleasure, pain. In the chart (line 95) the non-returner's path abandons loss, disrepute, blame and pain; the arahant's path abandons gain, fame, praise and pleasure.
- **Order of abandoning greed and hostility.** Hostility (දෝස / පටිඝ / ව්‍යාපාද) ends at the non-returner's path; greed (ලෝභ), covetousness and the remaining attachments end at the arahant's path (chart lines 92–93, 111, 133–134; Margaya lines 21–27).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- The sources agree on the eight persons, on stream-entry removing view and doubt, on non-returning removing sense desire and ill-will, and on arahantship removing attachment to form and formless existence, conceit, restlessness and ignorance.
- Margaya gives the abandoning in terms of the twelve unwholesome mind-states (lines 13–27). Pradeepika gives it by eighteen defilement groups in a chart and adds the analysis of "arisen", the three abandonings, the four functions of path knowledge (lamp simile, line 199) and the subtypes of persons. These are complementary.
- **Possible tension.** Margaya line 21 says the non-returner's path removes sense desire (කාමරාග) completely. The Pradeepika chart places clinging to sense pleasures (කාමුපාදන, line 131), covetousness (අභිජ්ඣා, lines 111 and 134), greed as a defilement (ලොහ, line 93) and attachment to gain and pleasure (line 95) at the arahant's path. This may be a difference of classification (greed there covers attachment to existence as well), but neither source explains it. Recorded as a held question.
- Margaya line 37 restricts cessation attainment to non-returners and arahants with the eight attainments; Pradeepika line 326 refers back to an earlier description not in this extract. No conflict observed.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The eight persons, the number of remaining rebirths (lines 334–378), the pure abodes, worthiness of offerings, cessation attainment, the claim that the body cannot be harmed by fire or water during cessation (Margaya line 41) and the Buddha's summons are doctrinal claims and must not become idea premises. The chart is a record of doctrinal ordering, not a measured developmental sequence. The examples of monks stirred by "an opposite-sex form" and a woman's song (lines 185, 189) frame women as triggers; they are not carried into cards. No card may present any real person as "noble" or "ordinary".

## Human meaning paragraph

Two small passages describe processes a non-Buddhist reader can recognise. First, the text names four ways a person goes wrong in what they do or fail to do: favouring what they want, acting from hostility, acting from confusion, and acting from fear. Fear stands beside favour as a source of bias, and failing to act is counted alongside wrong action. Second, the chart separates reactions to unwelcome conditions (loss, disrepute, blame, pain) from attachment to welcome ones (gain, fame, praise, pleasure), and places the release of the first set before the release of the second, in step with hostility ending before greed. Read as interpretation beyond the text, this suggests that no longer being stung by blame and no longer being drawn by praise may be separate achievements, and the second may be harder. The source gives no reason for either ordering; the bridge to everyday life is mine.

## Idea decisions

- **C098-I01 — Unfairness that comes from fear, not favour.** Accepted for research. Based on the definition of the four biases (line 50). Thin source, clearly limited to one definition; no doctrinal premise is needed.
- **C098-I02 — Blame stops stinging before praise stops pulling.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the chart's split of the worldly conditions (line 95) together with the order of abandoning hostility and greed. The explanatory bridge is interpretation.
- Rejected candidate: *Change works on what has not yet happened* (young-tree simile, lines 143–145). Interesting, but the mechanism overlaps C013-I02 (suppression versus uprooting) and C074-I01 (dormant urge), and the passage is tied to path attainment.
- Rejected candidate: *A sight that stirs trouble only later, once gripped* (line 183, Mahātissa, line 185). Overlaps C048-I01 and C043-I01, and the example frames women as triggers.
- Rejected candidate: *Stepping away while staying on call* (the preliminary resolves before cessation, Margaya lines 39–45). The setting is a supernormal attainment with protective powers and a summons by the Buddha; any human reading would substantially alter the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: the three abandonings (lines 205–249). Already the basis of C013-I02.
- Rejected candidate: five kinds of stinginess (line 42). Covered by C026-I01.
- Rejected: fruition attainment, cessation, the subtypes of noble persons and worthiness of offerings. Doctrinal classification with no supportable human mechanism.
