## Central meaning

Direct knowledges (අභිඥා) are seven: the divine eye, the divine ear, psychic powers, knowing others' minds, recollecting past lives, knowing beings' passing according to their action, and knowing the future. The last two are included in the divine eye when only five are counted. A person who has mastered the eight attainments enters the base absorption (fifth absorption in the fivefold count of Book 1 and Margaya; fourth in the fourfold count of Book 4), emerges, resolves on what is to be known or done, enters the base absorption again, emerges, and then a mind-door process arises: preparation (dull only), access, conformity, change-of-lineage, and a single direct-knowledge moment.

## Essential distinctions

- **Graded preparation for the divine ear** (Pradeepika lines 75–83; Book 4 lines 38–48). Begin with coarse sounds that ordinary hearing already catches (animals in the forest, thunder), move closer to temple sounds, speech, birds, wind, drying leaves, insects, and from coarse to subtle. Attend by direction. Then widen a measured area from one finger-width outwards. Without separating them, all sounds become one clamour. To tell each sound apart, one must return to the base absorption for each.
- **Knowing others' minds** (Pradeepika lines 118–126). Uses the divine eye to see the colour of blood near the heart: red for a joyful mind, dark for a displeased one, clear like sesame oil for a neutral one, by inference, repeated a hundred thousand times.
- **Past-life recollection** (Pradeepika lines 130–152). Begins by recalling one's own actions backward from the present moment: sitting down, entering the dwelling, eating, washing the face on waking, the previous day, then months and years.
- **Seeing passing and arising** (Book 4 lines 54–56). One who sees only death takes an annihilationist view; one who sees only arising thinks beings are new; seeing both frees one from both views.
- **Wrongly accusing a noble person** (Book 4 lines 80–96). This blocks heaven and the path, but, unlike the heinous deeds, can be forgiven. A novice mocked an elder who drank hot gruel sitting on a log because he had stomach wind. The elder asked whether the novice had a foothold in the teaching, and the novice apologised. The text sets out how to apologise: in person, by messenger, before other monks, to a learned monk and toward the direction the person went, or at the deathbed or cremation place.
- **Danger to an ordinary practitioner** (Pradeepika line 63; Book 4 line 134). Frightening sights may unsettle the mind, cause loss of absorption and even madness. So one should not be content with the divine eye but develop insight.
- **Torch simile** (Book 4 lines 124–130). A traveller relights a torch that keeps going out until the sun rises. Likewise the kasiṇa light must be renewed until it becomes firm.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The three sources agree on the list of seven and the basic process. Book 1 and Margaya use the fivefold absorption count and Book 4 the fourfold count; this is a reckoning difference, not a disagreement. Margaya (line 17) says the psychic-power knowledge is based on the fifth absorption on the earth kasiṇa, while Pradeepika (lines 87–104) requires attainments on all eight kasiṇas and fourteen ways of taming the mind. That is complementary at most and possibly a real difference. Book 4 alone contains the wrongful-accusation passage and the classes of beings seen. Book 1 alone contains the heart-blood method and the table of how far six kinds of person can recall.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

All the powers are doctrinal claims and cannot be idea premises. The heart-blood colour method is a historical physiological account. Book 4 lines 58–70 attribute low birth, ugliness and poverty to past unwholesome action; in everyday language this would blame the poor and disabled and must not be carried forward. The claim that wrong view is the most blameworthy action (Book 4 line 102) and the special weight of insulting noble persons are doctrinal. The forgiveness procedure is monastic etiquette within that doctrine; only the human act of seeking forgiveness is usable.

## Human meaning paragraph

Two ordinary processes sit inside this technical material. First, a person who has wronged someone by judging them without knowing their situation can repair the wrong by asking forgiveness. When the person cannot be faced, the source still expects some act directed toward them: through others, toward where they went, or at their grave. Second, the divine-ear training begins with a plain discipline of attention: start with what is loud and obvious, move step by step to what is faint, widen the field gradually, and return to a settled state to tell sounds apart. Both readings are limited analogies drawn from passages whose purpose is doctrinal.

## Idea decisions

- **C101-I01 — Asking forgiveness from someone you cannot reach.** Accepted. Based on Book 4 lines 84–96. The act of apology is human and is not premised on the powers.
- **C101-I02 — Hearing the faint by starting with the loud.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 75–83 and Book 4 lines 38–48.
- Rejected candidate: *Judging an act without knowing the pain behind it* (the novice and the elder). It is folded into C101-I01 as the trigger, and its mechanism overlaps C005-I01 (Same act, different reason).
- Rejected candidate: *Remembering the day backward, step by step* (past-life recollection preliminaries). The ordinary part is only the preparation for a doctrinal claim, and the source gives no reason why backward recall works. It is recorded as a held question in the batch report.
- Rejected candidate: *Seeing only the ending or only the beginning* (death-only and birth-only views). It concerns rebirth views, and the everyday version overlaps C020-I02 (Seeing only one side of something at once).
- Rejected candidate: *A power that can unsettle an unprepared mind*. Its premise is the doctrinal divine eye.
- Rejected candidate: *Relighting the torch until the sun rises*. The mechanism overlaps C013-I02 (Calm that lasts only while it is kept up).
