## Central meaning

The five mundane supernormal knowledges (අභිඥා) are psychic power (ඉද්ධිවිධ), the divine ear (දිබ්බසොත), knowing others' minds (පරචිත්ත විජානන / චෙතොපරිය), recollecting past lives (පුබ්බේනිවාසානුස්සති) and the divine eye, which sees beings die and arise (දිබ්බචක්ඛු / චුතූපපාත). Book 4 adds two knowledges that come with the divine eye, knowing where beings go according to their kamma (යථාකම්මුපග) and knowing the future (අනාගතංස), which makes seven (lines 734–752). A supernormal knowledge is the highest fine-material absorption mind, given special power (Margaya line 7). It does not come merely from attaining absorption or even arahantship. It must be developed afterwards through separate preliminary work for each knowledge (Margaya lines 17, 25). The exceptions are people with strong past practice and bodhisattas (Margaya line 17; Book 4 lines 98, 112–116).

## Essential distinctions

- **Mastery in fourteen ways** (Book 4 lines 43–96; Pradeepika lines 71–86; Margaya line 21). The meditator enters absorptions on eight kasinas in forward and reverse order, skipping stages, switching factors and objects, and defining factors and objects.
- **Graded difficulty** (Book 4 lines 98 and 104; Pradeepika lines 88–92). Each stage is "one in hundreds of thousands": the learning sign, the counterpart sign, the knowledge, the fourteenfold taming, transformation, quick entry (ඛිප්පනිසන්ති), and last, being able to protect others in danger. Stories illustrate this: Buddharakkhita creating a mountain (lines 100–104), an elder stopping a rain of coals "before blinking" (line 108) and Moggallāna, where the other monks could perform everything except enter absorption quickly enough (lines 398–402).
- **Preparation → determination** (Book 4 lines 224, 274; Pradeepika line 94; Margaya line 23). The meditator enters the base absorption, emerges, does preliminary work in a sense-sphere mind ("may I become a hundred"), enters again, emerges and makes the determination. If it does not work, they repeat (line 224). Merely thinking or saying it achieves nothing (Margaya line 25).
- **Four bases of power** (Book 4 lines 160–182). Desire-to-act, effort, mind and investigation, each joined with concentration. Neither desire nor concentration works alone.
- **Divine ear method** (Book 4 lines 476–490; Pradeepika lines 59–67). The meditator attends first to coarse, distant sounds, then gradually nearer and subtler ones, then sounds by direction. The range is extended from one finger-width outward. Heard all at once, sounds become one uproar. To tell them apart, the meditator must re-enter the base absorption for each sound.
- **Mind-reading method** (Book 4 lines 500–516; Pradeepika lines 104–110). The meditator first *infers* feeling from the colour of heart-blood seen with the divine eye, then strengthens this into direct knowing. Book 4 lists sixteen kinds of mind (lines 518–566).
- **Past-life recollection method** (Book 4 lines 616–632; Pradeepika line 116). The meditator recalls the present sitting, then each prior action of the day in reverse, then previous days, months and years back to conception. Anything unclear is met by re-entering the base absorption. Recollection within this life is only "preliminary concentration knowledge" (line 632). An axe simile covers the gap at conception. The woodcutter resharpens the dulled axe again and again and never recuts what is already cut (lines 624–630). The meditator is told not to give up effort (line 622).
- **Dangers of the divine eye** (Book 4 line 730; Pradeepika line 47). An ordinary person may see fearsome beings, fall from absorption and go mad. The meditator should not be content with the divine eye but develop insight.
- **Seeing only death or only birth** produces annihilationism or eternalism (Book 4 line 652).
- **Insulting a noble one** (Book 4 lines 680–692). It blocks heaven and the path but can be forgiven. The story: a novice mocked an elder who drank hot porridge sitting on a log because of stomach wind. The detailed procedure for asking forgiveness covers the person being present, gone elsewhere, whereabouts unknown or dead.
- **Object classifications** of the seven knowledges (Book 4 lines 754–972) are technical.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Absorption count.** Margaya and Book 1 Pradeepika speak of the *fifth* absorption (fivefold method), and Book 4 of the *fourth* (fourfold method). Margaya line 19 explains the two counting methods, so this is not a real disagreement.
- **Divine eye.** Book 1 (line 29) gives a four-step process, entering the fifth absorption on fire, white or light kasina. Book 4 (lines 714–728) adds that for the divine eye the kasina light is extended at access level without producing full absorption on it, and gives a torch simile (lines 722–726). The two are complementary.
- **Heart-blood colour for an unhappy mind.** Book 1 line 106 compares it to a ripe *mango* (අඹ), described as black. Book 4 line 506 compares it to a ripe *rose-apple* (දඹ). This looks like a genuine textual discrepancy, probably an error in Book 1, since a ripe mango is not black.
- **Order of the difficulty chain.** Book 4 line 98 places obtaining the knowledge *before* the fourteenfold taming, while line 43 and Book 1 line 88 put taming first. Book 1 has "attaining absorption", not "the knowledge", at that step. This is a possible slip in Book 4.
- **Past-life recollection.** Both sources list the six kinds of recollectors and their ranges (Book 1 lines 124–136; Book 4 lines 570–586). Only Book 4 adds the lamp and path similes (lines 588–614).
- **Book 4 exclusives.** The Cūḷapanthaka, Nandopananda and other miracle stories and the insult-and-forgiveness passage appear only in Book 4.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Every power itself: multiplying the body, walking on water, touching the sun and moon, hearing gods, reading minds, recalling past lives, seeing rebirth. These are doctrinal claims and must not be premises. The heart-blood colour method is a historical physiological account, not science. Book 4 lines 654–666 attribute low birth, ugliness and poverty to past greed, hatred and delusion. Used in everyday language, this would blame the poor and disfigured, so it must not be carried over. The warning that the divine eye causes madness concerns doctrinal visions of demons, not mental illness. The karmic weight of insulting noble ones (line 684) is doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

Beneath the doctrinal powers, the sources describe how a trained capacity is built and used. The steps are repeated practice in many orders, a return to a steady base before each act, and effort renewed at each failure. The sources also say how rare it is to reach the last layers, being able to act quickly and to protect others when danger arrives unannounced. The past-life method rests on an ordinary technique: recalling one's own recent actions backwards, one linked step at a time, returning to steadiness when a step is dark. A side passage treats a hurtful remark about someone whose circumstances the speaker did not know, and lays out how to seek forgiveness even when the person cannot be faced. The ideas below are interpretation and limited analogy. None assumes that any power is real.

## Idea decisions

- **C089-I01 — Having a skill but not reaching it in time.** Accepted as a limited analogy. It draws on the graded-difficulty chain and the quick-entry stories.
- **C089-I02 — Remembering backwards one step at a time.** Accepted as a limited analogy. It uses only the within-life preliminary recall and the axe simile. The past-life goal is excluded.
- **C089-I03 — Seeking forgiveness when the person is out of reach.** Accepted. It draws on the novice's insult and the forgiveness procedure.
- Rejected candidate: *Many sounds heard as one noise until each is separated* (divine-ear method). It is a real attention mechanism, but it is tied closely to a doctrinal power and overlaps C040-I01 and C013-I01. Recorded as a held question.
- Rejected candidate: *Judged incapable by one kind of test* (Cūḷapanthaka, lines 228–264). He fails rote learning, is expelled, and understands through rubbing a cloth. The mechanism is strong, but the resolution relies on a Buddha's intervention with a created cloth and instant arahantship. It is better placed in a concept on meditation subjects or temperament if one exists. Recorded as a held question.
- Rejected candidate: *Knowing another's mind by inference first* (heart-blood colour). It rests on unsupported physiology and overlaps C047-I02.
- Rejected candidate: *Seeing only one end of a process and concluding wrongly* (death-only or birth-only views). It is rebirth-based and overlaps C020-I02 and C065-I02.
- Rejected candidate: *Extraordinary perception that overwhelms the untrained* (divine eye dangers). The visions are of demons and it overlaps C016-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Hiding one's ability out of modesty* (line 286, becoming one again so as not to be recognised). The passage is too thin.
