# C014 — The twelve formless-sphere mind-states

## Central meaning

Formless-sphere consciousness (අරූපාවචර සිත්) is developed only by someone who already has the fifth form-sphere absorption. Four wholesome, four resultant and four functional states make twelve. The four differ only by object (Margaya line 12): boundless space (ආකාසානඤ්චායතන), boundless consciousness (the first formless consciousness taken as object), nothingness (the absence of that first consciousness), and neither-perception-nor-non-perception (the third formless consciousness taken as object, contemplated as "peaceful, sublime"). All have the two factors of equanimity and one-pointedness, so they count as fifth absorption (Pradeepika line 44).

## Essential distinctions

- **Motive** (Pradeepika line 29; Margaya line 12). The meditator sees the body as the root of suffering: it must be maintained with great difficulty, is subject to ageing, illness and enemies (Pradeepika line 50), and bodily misconduct leads to lower rebirths. Hoping for a bodiless existence, they turn away from form.
- **Suspicion by resemblance** (Pradeepika line 50). Even the kasiṇa light, which helped suppress defilements and is "not a cause of suffering like the body", is suspected because it is form in the same way, and "might it also produce fear like the body?" The meditator works to remove it. Wanting to see again the stars the light has covered, they drop their liking for it, and it vanishes from one area "like opening a window".
- **Judging each attainment by its nearness to the coarser one** (Pradeepika lines 56, 60, 66). The first formless state is judged not sublime because it is close to the fifth form absorption; the third is given up because it is close to the second.
- **Dependence on the previous stage.** Each of the second and fourth takes the preceding formless consciousness as its object (Pradeepika lines 56, 66, 84, 88; Margaya lines 20, 28). The pavilion simile (lines 68–88): in a desert, a pavilion floor covered in filth; four exhausted men arrive. The first hangs from a corner of the roof; the second hangs from the first man's feet; the third hangs from the other corner; the fourth hangs from the third man's feet. None touches the floor.
- **Extreme subtlety** (Pradeepika line 66; Margaya line 28). The fourth state is so subtle it is hard to know whether perception is present or absent, whether one "lived with a mind or without".

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the count, the four objects, the prerequisite of the fifth absorption, and the body-weariness motive. They describe the removal of the kasiṇa differently. Pradeepika (line 50) has the meditator spread the light, then wish to see the stars it covers, so that the light opens like a window. Margaya (line 14) has the meditator attend to the kasiṇa sign as "space" rather than as form until space appears where the sign was. These are complementary accounts of one step, not clearly in conflict. Margaya alone states that the space is only a concept with no beginning or end edge (line 16) and that absorption consciousness is not constantly present even in attainers (line 24). Pradeepika alone gives the pavilion simile and the "nearness" reasoning.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Formless worlds, bodiless rebirth, and the desert as saṃsāra are doctrinal. Disgust at the body is presented as a meditative motive toward bodiless existence; it must not be turned into advice about the body or into a mental-health claim. The state "neither perception nor non-perception" is a doctrinal description of a meditative attainment and cannot be equated with any known psychological state.

## Human meaning paragraph

When a person has escaped something harmful, a new good thing can come under suspicion simply because it is of the same kind as what harmed them, and each stage reached can be judged less by what it is than by how close it still sits to what was left behind. At the same time, each step up still rests on the step before, as the second man hangs from the first man's feet: rising above a thing does not free you from depending on it. Both readings are interpretation beyond the text, which concerns meditative attainments.

## Idea decisions

- **C014-I01 — Distrusting a good thing that resembles past harm.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika line 50 (kasiṇa light suspected by resemblance) with lines 56, 60 and 66 (nearness to the coarser stage).
- **C014-I02 — Moving beyond something while still hanging from it.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the pavilion simile (Pradeepika lines 68–88) and the object structure (Margaya lines 20 and 28).
- Rejected candidate: *Tired of looking after a body* (line 29; Margaya line 12). The source aims this at bodiless rebirth; a secular reading about weariness with bodily care would change its point and risks health framing.
- Rejected candidate: *When your own light hides the stars* (line 50). A single image whose function in the text is a meditative technique; kept inside the C014-I01 trace rather than made a card.
- Rejected candidate: *So quiet you cannot tell if you were aware* (line 66; Margaya line 28). Doctrinal meditative claim with no everyday bridge the source supports.
