## Central meaning

The five sense objects (ගෝචර / විෂය රූප) are what the five sensitivities (C064) take up: visible colour (වර්ණ), sound, smell, taste and the tangible. The tangible is not a separate kind of matter. It is three of the great elements, earth, fire and air, since water cannot be touched (Pradeepika lines 96–100; Margaya line 23). With the sex faculties, heart-base, life faculty and nutritive essence, these complete the eighteen "concretely produced" (නිෂ්පන්න) material phenomena (Pradeepika line 241; Margaya line 43).

## Essential distinctions

- **Only colour is seen.** Of the 28 material phenomena the eye sees only colour (Margaya line 7). What the hand grasps is the earth element, what the eye sees is colour, and "to think that what the hand grasps and what the eye sees are the same thing is a mistaken notion arising from not knowing the analysis of phenomena" (Margaya line 25).
- **Appearance varies with conditions.** The colour met by the eye is a blend of thousands of colours, not individual colour elements. The same object appears differently from far and near, in strong and dim light and to different people, while "true colour" is not seen by the fleshly eye (Pradeepika lines 28–34).
- **Reaching vs. non-reaching senses.** Sight and hearing take objects that do not physically reach the organ. Smell, taste and touch take objects that do (Pradeepika lines 36–38, 56, 72, 90, 106).
- **Selection in touch.** Although earth, fire and air strike the body at once, a single body-consciousness takes only one, the most prominent. When a person touches something expecting hardness or heat, the expected element is what is registered. Heat and cold are felt relative to one's own body temperature, and both are not felt at once (Pradeepika lines 100–104).
- **Taste needs moisture.** A dry hard object on the tongue produces only touch, yet it is not to be called tasteless (Pradeepika line 88; Margaya line 19).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the five objects, on the tangible as three elements, and on faint smells and tastes that exist but are not perceived (Pradeepika lines 68, 88; Margaya lines 15, 19). Pradeepika adds the account of light, sound "shadows", wave motion and the thunder-and-lightning delay (lines 40, 56–60), the expectation effect in touch (line 100), and long accounts of the sex faculties, heart-base and nutriment. Margaya gives the sharpest statement of the hand/eye distinction (line 25). On nutriment, Margaya (line 41) says each being eats food suited to its body. Pradeepika (lines 235–239) ranks the coarseness of food from crocodiles through animals, frontier-dwellers, villagers and kings to gods. This is complementary rather than contradictory, but the ranking is hierarchical and must not be carried forward. On the heart-base, Margaya states it briefly (line 33). Pradeepika argues against those "holding modern scientific views" that mind arises in the brain (lines 158–162).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- The heart-base doctrine and Pradeepika's rejection of the brain as the seat of mind (lines 158–162) are doctrinal and conflict with modern neuroscience. They must not be presented as fact or used as a premise.
- The claim that anger and fear darken the blood and body, joy and faith redden it, and grief dries it, and that a mind-reader can see another's mind in heart-blood colour (lines 168–170), is a historical physiological and supernormal account.
- The sex-faculty section (lines 110–138) lists gendered tasks and play as marks of womanhood and manhood, and attributes sex change to strong unwholesome karma. These are doctrinal and culturally dated claims. They must not become premises and could harm people if carried forward.
- The food hierarchy (lines 235–239), mind-moment lifespans, and rebirth-linked causation are doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

The usable material concerns perception. The source says each sense reaches only one aspect of a thing, and the mind joins these into "one object" that is, strictly, an assumption. How a thing looks shifts with distance, light and the viewer. In touch, many qualities strike at once, yet only one is registered, and which one depends partly on what the person was expecting and on their own body's state. My interpretation, which goes beyond the text, is that ordinary judgements of objects and situations are built from partial, selected contacts that feel complete. The source does not frame this socially. The applications in the cards are hypothetical.

## Idea decisions

- **C065-I01 — Feeling what you reached for.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika's account of touch: one element registered among several, expectation selecting it, and heat and cold relative to one's own body (lines 100–104). Related to C009-I01 (attention takes up only the wanted sight), which covers the selective role of wanting in sight. This card is kept separate for touch and for the body as its own baseline.
- **C065-I02 — Taking one sense's view for the whole thing.** Held as a possible merge into C020-I02 (seeing only one side of something at once), which already carries the viewpoint mechanism. Based on Margaya line 25 (hand and eye meet different aspects) and Pradeepika lines 28–34 (appearance varies with distance, light and viewer). Its only distinct point is that different senses meet different parts of a thing. It differs from C001-I01 (parts arranged).
- Rejected candidate: *Emotions showing in the body's colour* (Pradeepika line 168). Historical physiology tied to a supernormal claim.
- Rejected candidate: *Food that suits one body and not another* (Margaya line 41). Too general, and Pradeepika's version is a hierarchy of beings and social classes.
- Rejected candidate: *Taste that is there but not felt* (Pradeepika line 88). An interesting point about undetected qualities, but it is largely covered by C065-I02's concern with partial perception and has little human tension on its own.
- Rejected candidate: anything from the sex-faculty or heart-base sections, for the reasons above.
