## Central meaning

This tetrad classifies action by when it ripens. The basis is the seven impulsion moments (ජවන) in a cognitive process that performs a deed:

1. **දිට්ඨධම්ම / දෘෂ්ටධර්ම වේදනීය (this life).** The intention in the first impulsion moment. It is weak because it lacks repetition support, so it ripens only in the present life or lapses (Pradeepika line 25; Margaya line 12).
2. **උපපජ්ජ / උපපද්‍ය වේදනීය (next life).** The seventh impulsion moment, supported by the six before it (Pradeepika line 85; Margaya line 22).
3. **අපරාපරිය / අපරාපර්‍ය්‍ය වේදනීය (third life onward).** The five middle moments. They ripen whenever an opening arises, until final liberation (Pradeepika line 93; Margaya line 26).
4. **අහෝසි (lapsed).** Action that never ripens because its window passed (Pradeepika line 117; Margaya line 30).

Margaya line 8 explains the strength curve. The first moment is weakest, the moments grow stronger up to the fourth and then weaken, and the seventh is weak but stronger than the first. Margaya lines 32–36 also list the fourth tetrad, by place of ripening, and total the sixteen kinds of action.

## Essential distinctions

- **Four conditions for a gift to ripen in this life** (Pradeepika lines 31–37; Margaya line 14). A worthy recipient, a righteously obtained gift, the recipient freshly emerged from cessation (Pradeepika only) and a giver pure in intention before, during and after the gift.
- **Hunter simile** (Pradeepika line 41). A shot that hits drops the deer. A miss lets it escape for good. Present-life action either ripens now or never.
- **Present-life harms.** Offences against the Buddha, arahants, parents and helpful friends are said to ripen in this life (Margaya line 16; Pradeepika lines 57–77, with stories of the earth opening).
- **Unnoticed ripening.** Margaya line 18 says ordinary actions may also ripen in this life, but the doer does not notice it.
- **Old potential needing present support.** Pradeepika lines 101–113 say later-life action cannot ripen on its own and needs support from present-life action. It gives similes of green wood thrown into hot embers, dry wood on a weak fire, rain putting out a weak fire, and a timid person who can enter a cremation ground only with a friend.
- **Aṅgulimāla** (Pradeepika line 121). After many killings he became an arahant. His next-life and later-life unwholesome actions lapsed. Stones and clubs thrown at dogs and crows in the villages still struck his head, and this is read as the present-life result of the killings.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Agreement.** Both sources give the same four kinds and the same mapping to impulsion moments.
- **Complementary detail.** Margaya explains *why* the moments differ in strength (line 8). Pradeepika gives the conditions, similes and stories.
- **Disagreement on the kind of result.** Margaya line 12 says the first-moment intention can produce only rootless (අහේතුක) results in the present life. Pradeepika line 39 says a fully qualified present-life gift can give rooted (සහේතුක) wholesome results to the same aggregates. This is a genuine difference.
- **Claim found only in Pradeepika.** Pradeepika lines 101–113 say later-life action cannot ripen without support from present-life action. Margaya does not say this. Pradeepika line 99 also says there is no later-life course of action that gives life-course results without first giving a rebirth result. This is technical and unclear, and it is not addressed in Margaya.
- **Wording of the next-life condition.** Pradeepika line 85 treats next-life action as "the same as generative action". Margaya line 22 stresses the seventh moment's weakness. The two are compatible.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The concept is a timing model for moral results across lives. It relies on impulsion-moment counts, the earth swallowing wrongdoers, sudden wealth after a gift to an arahant, a shipwreck explained as a thousand people's past actions (Pradeepika line 99), and accidental injury read as deserved (line 121). Carried into everyday language, it would imply that sudden fortune or disaster reveals a person's past deeds. That is exactly the premise the project must not adopt.

## Human meaning paragraph

The only human material that is not purely doctrinal is thin. Some opportunities to act or to receive the effect of an act pass and do not return (hunter simile). Old dispositions may need present circumstances to activate (fire similes). A person who has changed deeply may still live among the effects of past harm (Aṅgulimāla). The first two are generic images of timing and conditions rather than specific human tensions. The third shares the mechanism and question of C055-I01, where it is used as supporting material. No separate card is justified.

## Idea decisions

- No card created. Status: no suitable idea.
- Merged as supporting material into C055-I01: *Still living among the effects of past harm after changing* (Aṅgulimāla, Pradeepika line 121). As a standalone card it would rest on reading an accident as deserved consequence, which cannot be carried over.
- Rejected candidate: *Chances that pass and do not return* (hunter simile, Pradeepika line 41; lapsed action). It is a generic timing image, and the doctrinal point concerns karmic lapse, not human opportunity.
- Rejected candidate: *Old tendencies that need present fuel* (fire and timid-friend similes, Pradeepika lines 103–113). A human reading would recast karmic ripening as psychology of habit, which alters the teaching, and C056-I02 already covers the defensible habit mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: *Giving with a pure mind before, during and after* (Pradeepika line 37). This is interesting, but in this source it serves as a condition for miraculous present-life reward. It may fit better with a concept on giving (දානය), if one exists in the corpus.
