# Philosophy Map

This map groups the 148 accepted idea cards into 14 human territories. The territories came from reading the cards, not from the nine Abhidhamma chapters. Several territories draw on cards from four or five different chapters.

A territory describes a family of human questions. It does not describe a type of person, and no real person should ever be placed in one. The Abhidhamma source for each card stays in the card's source trace. Nothing here adds to or changes the idea bank.

**How to read the entries**

- **Primary cards** have this territory as their main home. Every accepted card has exactly one primary territory.
- **Secondary cards** have their main home elsewhere but are useful when researching here. No card has more than two secondary territories.
- Card titles are in [the index](../03-idea-bank/index.md). Scores and tiers are in [documentary-potential-matrix.md](documentary-potential-matrix.md). Near-duplicates and lead cards are in [overlap-map.md](overlap-map.md).
- *Possible forms of reality* lists kinds of situation where a question could appear. These are research directions. None of them is a verified story, person, place or event.

## T01 — Noticing and overlooking

**Definition.** What reaches a person's attention, what passes them by, and how attention is trained or scattered by work, hurry and familiarity.

**Central tension.** People trust that they saw what was in front of them. Yet what gets noticed depends on interest, training, pace and how prominent a thing is, not on how much it matters.

**Major open questions**
- What in a familiar place have we never actually seen? (C009-I01)
- What do we only notice once we have stopped looking for something else? (C013-I01)
- What do we miss experiencing when we are busy getting through it? (C008-I01)
- How do we learn to hear what is quiet without being overwhelmed by everything else? (C101-I02)

**Primary cards:** C003-I01, C008-I01, C009-I01, C013-I01, C036-I02, C040-I01, C065-I01, C094-I01, C094-I02, C101-I02

**Secondary cards:** C009-I02, C032-I02

**Important internal differences**
- *Interest versus prominence.* C009-I01 is about attention following what a person already wants to see. C040-I01 is about attention following what is large, near or well lit. The two can produce the same blind spot for different reasons.
- *Sustained attention versus divided attention.* C013-I01, C101-I02, C065-I01 and C094-I02 describe attention trained over time on one object or point. C003-I01 and C008-I01 describe attention split by many demands.
- *Watching versus steering.* C094-I01 asks how to observe a process without taking control of it. That is a different skill from noticing more.
- *Senses in the present, thought elsewhere.* C036-I02 separates where the body is from where the mind has gone. It fits research about places more than research about skill.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Missing something is not a moral failing. None of these cards may be presented as neuroscience or perception science. Traditional accounts of eye physiology and mind-moment counts are doctrine. Skilled noticing often comes from training and tools, not from any inner quality.

**Possible forms of reality.** Work that depends on reading faint signals by sight, sound or touch. Places people pass daily while slow changes happen in them. Returns to a place known in childhood. Busy occasions such as festivals, markets and household events.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Attention can be shown rather than explained. The camera can look where an experienced person looks, and the audience can notice with them. A journey through a place can gradually reveal what the first glance missed, without any narration about attention.

## T02 — What we take things to be

**Definition.** How recognition, labels, categories and first impressions settle what something is to us before we have examined it, and how reactions such as liking, disgust, trust or suspicion follow from that.

**Central tension.** Recognition is fast and useful. It is also where a mistaken or inherited category can enter unnoticed, so that a person reacts to a label rather than to what is in front of them.

**Major open questions**
- How much of a disagreement is about the thing, and how much is about which side of it each person stands on? (C020-I02)
- What changes when something we have long recognised is finally understood? (C020-I01)
- How much of what we feel about something was settled by what we took it to be? (C046-I01)
- How much of our disgust or delight belongs to the thing, and how much to us? (C009-I02)
- When does describing what someone did become deciding who they are? (C084-I01)

**Primary cards:** C009-I02, C020-I01, C020-I02, C034-I01, C034-I02, C036-I01, C043-I01, C046-I01, C084-I01, C090-I01, C093-I02

**Secondary cards:** C014-I01, C047-I02, C090-I02

**Important internal differences**
- *Recognition versus attention.* This territory concerns what a thing is taken to be once it has been noticed. T01 concerns whether it is noticed at all.
- *Recognising versus understanding.* C020-I01 separates knowing a thing by its signs from knowing why it is so. Long familiarity can carry real practical knowledge, so the card does not rank one below the other.
- *Viewpoint versus label.* C020-I02 is about different people each seeing one real side of a shared thing. C046-I01 and C084-I01 are about a category that may be wrong or may outlast its reason.
- *Impression versus response.* C034-I02 and C043-I01 ask where meeting something ends and acting on it begins. They carry the ethical weight of the territory and are also the hardest to observe.
- *Disgust.* C009-I02, C090-I01 and C093-I02 all involve disgust, with different mechanisms: a response that follows contact, a frame deliberately changed, and a reaction to a believed origin. C093-I02 is not advanced (see the processing report).

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Categories such as "threat" or "one of us" must never be illustrated through real communities. Disgust questions often lead to stigmatised work, such as waste collection, fish processing or care of the dead. Research must centre the workers' own accounts, not other people's reactions to them. Labels must not extend to race, appearance, caste or disability.

**Possible forms of reality.** A landscape or resource valued differently by the people who use it. Traditional signs of weather, plants or animals, and how they are explained now. Nicknames and reputations that outlast the act they came from. Work in which insiders and outsiders react to the same materials very differently.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Several people standing before the same river, field or building can be filmed from their own positions, and the audience can feel the thing change as the viewpoint changes. Chathura's own first impression of a place can be revised on screen as he learns what others see in it.

## T03 — Preoccupation and old pulls

**Definition.** What holds the mind on something for hours or years, what keeps an echo sounding after a brief contact, and what happens when an urge that seemed gone meets its old trigger.

**Central tension.** Holding on to something is not the same as being at peace with it, and distance from a pull is not the same as being free of it. People often discover this only when circumstances bring the thing close again.

**Major open questions**
- What keeps us holding on to the very thing that will not let us settle? (C020-I03)
- When something holds our attention for years, is it the same thing that first caught it? (C033-I01)
- Why do some brief moments keep sounding in us while most fade at once? (C048-I01)
- When an old urge returns after years of absence, what decides whether it stays a feeling or becomes an act? (C074-I01)

**Primary cards:** C013-I02, C020-I03, C033-I01, C038-I01, C040-I02, C048-I01, C074-I01, C082-I01

**Secondary cards:** C047-I01, C056-I02, C058-I02, C081-I01, C095-I02

**Important internal differences**
- *Unresolved waiting versus chosen devotion.* C020-I03 concerns agitation around something not yet decided, such as a result, a verdict or news. C033-I01 concerns attention sustained for years, whether by care, longing or grievance.
- *Latency versus suppression.* C074-I01 asks what triggers a dormant urge. C013-I02 asks what had been holding it away. They are researched together but ask different things.
- *Echo versus loop.* C048-I01 concerns one contact that keeps producing thought. C082-I01 concerns looking at one's own feeling in a way that feeds it.
- *Self-knowledge.* C038-I01 and C040-I02 are lenses on how people explain their own inner life. They are not story starting points.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** No card here describes anxiety, rumination, obsession or addiction in a clinical sense, and none may be used to diagnose. Relapse and addiction need specialist sources and safeguarding. Worry and persistence can be necessary and protective. A long preoccupation may be kept alive by a legal process, a debt or family expectation rather than by anything inside the person.

**Possible forms of reality.** Waiting for exam results, a court date, a visa or news from a relative working abroad. A decades-long effort to restore, protect or reclaim something. People who kept away from a habit or rivalry for years, then returned to the setting where it began.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Waiting has a built-in structure: something is going to happen and has not yet. Places of long devotion or dispute show years of attention in their physical state. The outcome is unknown at the start of filming, which leaves room for reality to decide.

## T04 — Wanting, having and enough

**Definition.** How wanting behaves before and after something is obtained: how hardship shapes what people come to want, how possession changes the grip, how satisfaction fades, and how people part with things or the wanting attached to them.

**Central tension.** Getting something is expected to settle the want. Often it renews the want, tightens the grip or raises the fear of loss. Letting go of a thing and letting go of wanting it are different acts.

**Major open questions**
- When we get rid of something we crave, what happens to the craving? (C067-I01)
- How much of what we long for is shaped by the discomfort we once had to bear? (C080-I01)
- Why can something we have finally obtained hold us more tightly than it did while we were still searching? (C080-I02)
- Where does the wish to make something end, and the need to own it begin? (C021-I02)
- What makes the ending of a trouble worth more to us than the things we enjoyed? (C073-I01)

**Primary cards:** C006-I01, C021-I02, C067-I01, C073-I01, C073-I02, C077-I03, C080-I01, C080-I02, C081-I01, C081-I02, C088-I02, C100-I01

**Secondary cards:** C005-I02, C007-I01, C024-I02, C026-I01, C032-I01, C052-I01, C100-I03

**Important internal differences**
- *Desire versus the wish to act.* C021-I02 separates wanting to do or make something from needing to possess it. The source treats the first as necessary and without clinging.
- *Pleasant feeling versus the wanting that follows it.* C081-I02 places a turning point between enjoying and craving. C073-I02 describes wanting returning because satisfaction fades. C080-I01 describes wanting produced by pain. These are three different routes to the same outward behaviour.
- *Knowing the cost.* C006-I01 is about wanting that continues after its cost is understood. It sits between this territory and T09.
- *Relief versus pleasure.* C073-I01 and C081-I01 both concern escaping pain. The first is about what people will give up for relief. The second is about relief that deepens the trouble.
- *Releasing by recognition versus by giving up.* C100-I01 describes a grip loosening when something turns out not to be one's own. C067-I01 describes the wanting being given up while the thing may stay.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Seeking relief from hardship is usually necessity, not craving. Poverty must not be romanticised, and people who sold land or went into debt must not be presented as foolish or wise. No livelihood or community, including gem mining, should be read through a simile. Ownership disputes must not be adjudicated on film.

**Possible forms of reality.** Families deciding whether to keep or sell land, a house or an heirloom. Elders comparing food, comfort and wants in lean years and now. People who found or inherited something valuable. Craftspeople and makers describing their relation to finished work.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Objects and land carry the question physically. A house, a field or a stored heirloom can be filmed before and after a decision. Wanting becomes visible in what people keep, repair, lock away or give up.

## T05 — Effort, readiness and the conditions for acting

**Definition.** What makes action come easily or require pushing: fatigue, weather, food, practice, encouragement, other people's sacrifice, and the judgement of when to push, when to ease off and when to leave things alone.

**Central tension.** Needing a push is often read as unwillingness, and more effort is often assumed to be better. The cards suggest that readiness depends on conditions, and that too much effort at the wrong moment can prevent the result.

**Major open questions**
- What happens to our effort when we learn what someone else went without for us? (C076-I01)
- How do we tell when something needs more effort from us and when it needs us to stop interfering? (C076-I02)
- When we need to be pushed, is it the will that is missing, or the strength and practice? (C003-I02)
- When something hard gets done, what was really in charge: the wanting, the effort, the resolve or the understanding? (C075-I02)

**Primary cards:** C003-I02, C006-I03, C013-I03, C021-I03, C066-I01, C069-I01, C075-I02, C076-I01, C076-I02, C086-I03, C087-I01, C087-I03, C096-I01

**Secondary cards:** C001-I03, C013-I02, C023-I02, C075-I01

**Important internal differences**
- *Habit versus deliberate choice.* C003-I02 and C069-I01 concern bodily and practical conditions that make acting easy or hard. C006-I03 concerns another person's voice, or one's own self-persuasion, tipping a hesitant decision.
- *Difficulty of doing good.* C021-I03 attributes it to a claimed lean of the untrained mind. C023-I02 (T07) attributes it to lack of practice. They must not be merged.
- *Balance and timing.* C076-I02 describes alternating effort, cooling and watching. C087-I01 narrows this to the moment just before a goal. C013-I03 matches a remedy to the kind of obstacle. C096-I01 separates general knowledge of what helps from judging what fits today.
- *Motivation from outside.* C076-I01 concerns effort given in response to someone else's sacrifice. It can steady a person or weigh on them.
- *Deciding versus moving.* C066-I01 concerns the step between intention and bodily action. It needs particular care in illness and disability settings.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Tiredness must never be attributed to attitude. Traditional accounts of "wind" in the body and mind-born matter are not physiology. Individual-fit ideas (C086-I03) rest on temperament typology and must not be used to type people or justify poor conditions. Obligation to family must not be presented as a virtue that excuses pressure on young people.

**Possible forms of reality.** Seasonal and outdoor work under heat and changing food. Kilns, hearths, boats and workshops where timing matters. Coaches and teachers before a performance. Students and workers whose start in life was paid for by a family's sacrifice.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Effort is physical and has a rhythm. Firing, waiting, cooling and checking can be filmed, and so can the difference between a rested morning and a heavy afternoon. A person's sense of obligation often leads the camera back to a home and the people who made the effort possible.

## T06 — Learning, mastery and judging progress

**Definition.** How knowledge and skill are passed on and secured, how learners and teachers judge whether something has been reached, and how people read a long effort that has or has not produced what they hoped.

**Central tension.** People want a clear sign that they have arrived or failed. Yet a first success is not yet mastery, a struggle is not proof of incapacity, and a teacher's verdict can stop the learning it was meant to guide.

**Major open questions**
- What turns something we know how to do into something we can do the moment someone needs it? (C089-I01)
- How do we decide to trust an account of something we have never experienced? (C073-I03)
- How often is "cannot learn" really "cannot learn this way"? (C090-I02)
- After years of effort with nothing to show, how does a person decide whether the path failed or they did? (C056-I03)
- When is something we can do once really ours to build on? (C088-I03)

**Primary cards:** C001-I02, C016-I01, C056-I03, C073-I03, C085-I01, C088-I01, C088-I03, C089-I01, C090-I02, C093-I01, C094-I03, C096-I02, C100-I02

**Secondary cards:** C004-I01, C011-I02, C014-I02, C020-I01, C022-I03, C091-I01, C102-I01

**Important internal differences**
- *Understanding consequences versus acting on that understanding.* C073-I03 and C093-I01 concern the gap between being told and knowing by experience. C089-I01 concerns the further gap between knowing and acting in the first seconds of an emergency.
- *Opposite errors on a long path.* C016-I01 is taking a striking experience for arrival. C056-I03 is taking a missing result as proof the path is false. They are researched together.
- *Consolidation versus readiness.* C088-I03 asks when a skill is secure enough to build on. C089-I01 asks whether it can be reached under threat.
- *Learner's struggle versus teacher's response.* C085-I01 and C090-I02 concern the learner. C094-I03 and C096-I02 concern the teacher's verdict and explanation.
- *Credentials versus change.* C001-I02 asks what learning changes apart from what it earns. It connects to T07 and T09 more than to skill.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Learners must not be ranked as sharp or dull. Learning difficulties, trauma and hunger need their own evidence; hands-on learning is not a cure. Children and people with disabilities need specific consent. Congenital blindness must not be used as a metaphor for ignorance. Freezing in an emergency is a normal stress response.

**Possible forms of reality.** Apprenticeships in crafts, music, dance or boats. Knowledge of sea, weather or land passed between generations. Lifeguards, rescue volunteers, midwives and crews who must act instantly. Adults returning to learning. Teachers of mixed groups.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Learning has visible stages: watching, trying, failing and trying again. Chathura can be a genuine learner beside someone skilled, which gives the audience the experience of learning rather than a lecture about it.

## T07 — Giving and what moves it

**Definition.** The motives, feelings and memories inside acts of giving: what is given and what is kept, how giving is learned, how it becomes comparison, how it feels the first time and later, and how the giver and receiver remember it.

**Central tension.** A gift can be generous and useful while the motive inside it shifts toward comparison, habit, credit or regret. Neither the giver nor an observer can easily separate these, and the receiver may notice something the giver does not.

**Major open questions**
- When we keep doing a good thing learned in childhood, what do we understand about it now that we did not then? (C011-I02)
- Does what we keep for ourselves say more about a gift than what we hand over? (C059-I02)
- Can the way we remember a gift change what that gift was? (C059-I01)
- When we give where others can see, how do we know whether we are giving or competing? (C074-I03)
- What makes the first act of giving harder than the tenth? (C023-I02)

**Primary cards:** C005-I01, C005-I03, C011-I02, C011-I03, C023-I02, C059-I01, C059-I02, C074-I03, C097-I01, C097-I02

**Secondary cards:** C001-I02, C030-I01, C056-I01, C082-I03, C097-I03

**Important internal differences**
- *Pleasant feeling versus beneficial action.* C005-I03 concerns the pleasure of watching a recipient. C030-I01 (T10) concerns good done without any pleasant feeling. The cards separate how giving feels from whether it helps.
- *Motive at the start versus during the act.* C074-I03 is comparison as the reason for giving. C011-I03 is envy or pride arising in the middle of a long act of giving.
- *Habit versus understanding.* C011-I02 concerns giving learned by custom. C023-I02 concerns giving that becomes easier through practice. These are different mechanisms.
- *Before, during and after.* C059-I01 treats memory as part of the gift. C059-I02 treats what is kept back as part of it.
- *The receiver's side.* C097-I02 concerns receiving care meant for the person one appears to be. C097-I01 concerns declining as a way of asking.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** No giver may be labelled proud, competitive or insincere. Large offerings can come from vows, gratitude or custom. Declining an offer at first is ordinary Sri Lankan courtesy, not manipulation. Monks and religious workers must not be implied to obtain gifts by deceit. Giving that people cannot afford must not be praised.

**Possible forms of reality.** Almsgivings, roadside drink and food stalls on festival days, temple and community kitchens, harvest sharing, relief drives, feeding workers and guests, and giving practices carried from one generation to the next.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Giving is prepared, cooked, carried and handed over. Its logistics and hospitality are visual. The receiver's face and the giver's kitchen are different places, and moving between them lets a journey reveal what a single scene cannot.

## T08 — Comparison, resentment and reconciliation

**Definition.** How other people's fortune, sharing or goodness is compared with one's own, how a passing irritation becomes a lasting grievance, and how people approach goodwill toward someone who hurt them.

**Central tension.** Resentment can feel like loyalty to oneself or to justice, and suspicion can feel like shrewdness. Yet a long grievance can cost the person carrying it more than the person it is aimed at, while the original harm may still be real.

**Major open questions**
- What turns a passing flash of resentment into a wish we keep carrying? (C058-I02)
- Why can someone else's good fortune feel like something taken from us? (C007-I02)
- Can goodwill toward someone who hurt us be approached gradually, and what happens when it is forced? (C095-I01)
- Where does the sense of what "people like us" can do come from, and what shifts it? (C022-I03)

**Primary cards:** C007-I01, C007-I02, C022-I02, C022-I03, C026-I01, C058-I02, C082-I03, C086-I01, C095-I01, C095-I02, C098-I02

**Secondary cards:** C007-I03, C033-I01, C058-I01, C074-I03, C092-I02

**Important internal differences**
- *Desire turning hostile.* C007-I01 describes wanting that becomes hostility toward whatever blocks it. C058-I02 describes irritation hardening into a held wish for harm. C095-I02 describes a grudge that ends up serving the rival. All three can occur in one long dispute, but they ask different questions.
- *Envy versus stinginess.* C007-I02 looks outward at another's fortune. C026-I01 concerns resisting others sharing what is one's own.
- *Suspecting goodness versus performing it.* C022-I02 (here) and C023-I03 (T14) are mirror cases and must stay separate.
- *Low self-measure.* C022-I03 treats ranking oneself low as a form of comparison. It is also where the gap between source and reality is widest, because low estimates are often accurate readings of exclusion.
- *Typology.* C086-I01 and C098-I02 come from temperament and attainment schemes and serve only as private lenses.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** No person, family or community may be called envious, resentful, proud or hateful. Lasting anger may be a response to real injustice, and research must not suggest victims stop seeking justice. Reconciliation may be unsafe and must never be pressed on anyone. Caste, class and gender barriers must not be recast as personal self-limitation.

**Possible forms of reality.** Long disputes over land, boundaries, water or inheritance. Returning migrant workers and changing relations with neighbours. Families or groups attempting reconciliation after a rift. Young people deciding whether a school, job or public role is open to them.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Disputes leave marks on land: fences, boundary stones, court files, unused paths. Reconciliation, when it happens, is a journey between two homes. The outcome is genuinely uncertain, and reality may contradict any expectation of resolution.

## T09 — Conscience and restraint

**Definition.** Why people hold back from harm when they could act, what makes harm visible or invisible to people who hold values, and how skill, belief and reward can carry conduct in either direction.

**Central tension.** Restraint rests on different supports, such as self-respect, reputation, a promise, an imagined witness or a goal. Each can hold, and each can give way when conditions change. Harm is often done by people who know better in general but do not see it in the moment.

**Major open questions**
- What keeps someone from doing harm when no one they respect will ever find out? (C023-I01)
- What lets someone turn away money they need when earning it would mean deceiving or ruining someone else? (C024-I02)
- What makes a harm visible to someone who already knows all the facts? (C004-I01)
- What keeps us behaving well after the thing we were behaving well for has been gained? (C097-I03)

**Primary cards:** C004-I01, C006-I02, C023-I01, C024-I01, C024-I02, C026-I02, C027-I01, C075-I01, C082-I02, C091-I02, C097-I03

**Secondary cards:** C006-I01, C055-I01, C060-I02, C074-I01, C076-I03, C095-I01, C097-I02, C098-I01

**Important internal differences**
- *Restraint versus indifference.* The restraint cards (C023-I01, C024-I01, C024-I02, C091-I02) describe an active holding back, not an absence of interest.
- *Three ways harm happens despite values.* C006-I01 (T04) acts while knowing the cost. C006-I02 holds a belief that removes the sense of wrong. C026-I02 has shame and caution go quiet in the moment. These must not be treated as one idea.
- *Skill and harm.* C075-I01 concerns capacities that serve any aim. C004-I01 concerns whether a harm registers as harm at all.
- *Supports for restraint.* C023-I01 contrasts self-respect with reputation. C024-I01 contrasts the moment with a prior promise. C091-I02 adds an imagined admired person. C097-I03 adds a reward that, once gained, removes the reason.
- *Not harming versus helping.* C027-I01 separates declining a specific wrong from general generosity.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Occupations must never be treated as suspect, including fishing, money-lending, brokering and science. Research must not invite people to incriminate themselves on film. Source examples of lineage and status must not become caste or class reasoning. No subject may be framed as being on a slope toward harm (C082-I02).

**Possible forms of reality.** People moving from close villages to anonymous cities. Trades and services where a dishonest option pays well. Practitioners who changed a practice after seeing its effect. Commitments kept for a job, a marriage, a vote or a visa, and what happened afterwards.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Restraint is usually invisible, which makes it difficult but distinctive. It becomes filmable through the place where the choice was made, the money or object refused, and the person's own account. The documentary can let the audience weigh a choice without being told what to think.

## T10 — Care, obligation and the limits of helping

**Definition.** How love, care and duty are given, felt and limited: care that also holds on, help that continues without feeling good, suffering others cannot see, closeness that is not shared experience, and when stepping back is still care.

**Central tension.** Care is judged by how it feels and how visible it is, yet steady care may feel flat, deep distress may not help, and the most caring act may be to stop intervening. Duties to others also compete with a person's need for inner space.

**Major open questions**
- In caring for the people we love, how can we tell what we give for them from what we hold on to for ourselves? (C005-I02)
- If helping no longer feels good, what tells us it still matters? (C030-I01)
- What suffering around us goes unknown simply because no one thinks to ask? (C032-I02)
- How do we know when stepping back is still a way of caring? (C095-I03)
- Before we step away from everyone, how do we decide who is allowed to call us back? (C102-I01)

**Primary cards:** C001-I03, C005-I02, C024-I03, C030-I01, C030-I02, C032-I02, C047-I02, C077-I02, C095-I03, C102-I01

**Secondary cards:** C003-I02, C005-I03, C020-I03, C059-I02, C062-I01, C069-I01, C076-I01, C077-I01, C089-I01, C090-I01

**Important internal differences**
- *Love versus possession.* C005-I02 holds care and clinging together without calling care selfish. C095-I03 describes a later stage, when care means not interfering.
- *Pleasant feeling versus beneficial action.* C030-I01 separates the goodness of care from how it feels.
- *Physical pain versus mental distress.* C032-I02 concerns both kinds of hidden suffering. The card that tried to distinguish them (C032-I03) is held and is not used.
- *Distress versus compassion.* C024-I03 separates being overwhelmed by someone's pain from wanting steadily for it to end. It must not become a judgement on tears.
- *Asking versus sensing.* C032-I02 is about asking. C047-I02 is about inferring another's pain, which can be wrong.
- *Duty versus solitude.* C001-I03 and C102-I01 concern space for an inner life within or away from obligation.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Family care must never be described as greed or selfishness. Carers' exhaustion and support needs are real and not a mindset. Health information requires consent and must not be diagnosed. The source's basis for equanimity, that people receive what their past actions earned, must not be carried into research.

**Possible forms of reality.** Adult children leaving home or country. Long-term care of an ageing parent. Weekly service in a community kitchen or water scheme. Chronic pain in heavy manual work. Pilgrimages and retreats taken by people with dependants.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Care is made of repeated small acts, such as cooking, carrying, washing and waiting, which the camera can observe without interpretation. The question of what care means can emerge from routine rather than from a crisis.

## T11 — Grief, mortality and the end of life

**Definition.** How people meet death: the forms grief takes, the explanations sought for a death, what returns near the end, the rituals around the dying, how voices and objects remain, and how people reflect on their own mortality.

**Central tension.** People need a clear story and a fitting farewell. Causes are often many and partly unknowable, what the dying person takes in is beyond anyone's control, and grief continues after it stops showing.

**Major open questions**
- When we replay someone's voice, what do we receive, and what do we supply ourselves? (C066-I02)
- When we arrange the last moments of someone we love, who are we arranging them for? (C062-I01)
- What does a grieving person gain, and lose, by settling on one reason for a death? (C060-I01)
- When grief stops showing, has it ended, or has it only changed form? (C077-I01)
- What changes when we stop quietly believing our strengths or standing put our death further off? (C092-I02)

**Primary cards:** C007-I03, C056-I01, C060-I01, C060-I02, C061-I01, C062-I01, C066-I02, C077-I01, C092-I01, C092-I02, C092-I03

**Secondary cards:** C022-I01, C024-I03, C052-I03, C073-I01, C080-I03, C089-I03, C091-I02

**Important internal differences**
- *Before and after a death.* C060-I02 concerns precaution and fate before a death. C060-I01 concerns explanations after it.
- *Forms of grief.* C077-I01 describes inner burning, outpouring and depletion. C007-I03 frames grief as resistance to what happened and is the riskier card.
- *The dying person versus those around them.* C061-I01 and C056-I01 depend on the inner experience of dying people. C062-I01 can be researched through carers and practices.
- *Objects and traces.* C066-I02 concerns recorded voices, which also exist in archives. It does not require contact with the recently bereaved.
- *Reflecting on one's own death.* C092-I01 to C092-I03 concern relationships, status and time horizons. C092-I03 carries the highest risk.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** This territory needs a safeguarding protocol before any fieldwork. The source material mentions suicide and self-harm (C060, C077, C092). No mourner's grief may be classified, judged or corrected. Rebirth, near-death objects and fixed lifespans are doctrine only. Deaths must not be blamed on the dead, their families or their beliefs. Vulnerable people must not be approached because their situation is dramatic.

**Possible forms of reality.** Families and archives that keep recorded voices. Funeral and deathbed customs as described by those who carry them out. Communities after sudden deaths at sea, on roads or in floods. Reactions to the deaths of prominent local figures.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Death leaves places, objects and sounds, including graves, rooms and recordings, which can carry a film without intruding on private grief. The audience can experience absence through what remains.

## T12 — Regret, amends and past selves

**Definition.** How people live with what they have done: regret that repairs or traps, amends made over years, forgiveness sought from people who cannot answer, caution learned from harm, and the question of how much of a former self a person still carries.

**Central tension.** Feeling bad can stand in for responsibility, and doing good afterwards can change the weight of the past without erasing it. Saying "that was not me" can loosen accountability, and saying "I am who I was" can deny real change.

**Major open questions**
- What does asking forgiveness accomplish when the person we hurt can never answer? (C089-I03)
- How much of who we were do we carry, and who gets to decide? (C081-I03)
- When someone spends years doing good after causing harm, what changes, and what stays? (C055-I01)
- When does looking back at a wrong help someone change, and when does it hold them in place? (C022-I01)
- How do we tell what actually harmed us from what only shares its shape? (C014-I01)

**Primary cards:** C014-I01, C022-I01, C055-I01, C056-I02, C081-I03, C085-I02, C089-I03

**Secondary cards:** C052-I03, C059-I01, C095-I03

**Important internal differences**
- *Regret versus recollection.* C022-I01 concerns regret that keeps a wrong alive. C056-I02 concerns any single act, good or harmful, that gains weight by being revisited.
- *Amends versus apology.* C055-I01 concerns long repair through conduct. C089-I03 concerns the act of asking forgiveness when the person is absent or dead.
- *The one harmed versus the one who harmed.* C014-I01 is about caution after being harmed. Most other cards here are about having harmed.
- *Identity across change.* C081-I03 is about persons. C001-I01 and C039-I01 (T13) are about places and things.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** Research involving people who caused harm must include, or at least respect, those who were harmed. Nothing may suggest that misfortune repays wrongdoing. Former prisoners and people in recovery need consent and protection from stigma. Regret may be appropriate and must not be treated as a disorder.

**Possible forms of reality.** Visits to graves or former homes to say something unsaid. People whose lives changed sharply after prison, recovery or a turning point. Long service or giving that followed a known harm. People who became wary of an employer, trade or place after an earlier bad experience.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Apology and repair often involve travel: returning to a village, finding a family, standing where something happened. The journey is part of the act, and the audience can travel it with Chathura.

## T13 — Change, continuity and memory of place

**Definition.** How places, communities, bodies and practices persist and change: wholes that dissolve while their parts remain, structures renewed piece by piece, lessons that fade across generations, endings of good periods, and what memory holds when people return.

**Central tension.** People experience continuity and loss in wholes, such as a village, a house or a way of life. What actually changes may be an arrangement, a generation or the person remembering, while much else carries on.

**Major open questions**
- When the parts are still here but the arrangement is gone, what exactly have we lost? (C001-I01)
- What keeps a lesson learned through loss alive once the people who saw the loss are gone? (C052-I03)
- When we long for a place, which part of the memory are we actually longing for? (C047-I01)
- If everything in something has been replaced bit by bit, what is it that we still feel is the same? (C039-I01)
- What in our past are we still holding on to in order to stand where we are now? (C014-I02)

**Primary cards:** C001-I01, C014-I02, C032-I01, C039-I01, C047-I01, C052-I03, C066-I03, C089-I02, C091-I01, C100-I03

**Secondary cards:** C011-I02, C036-I02, C066-I02, C067-I01, C080-I02, C081-I03

**Important internal differences**
- *Arrangement versus replacement.* C001-I01 concerns a whole lost when its parts are rearranged. C039-I01 concerns a whole that seems unchanged while every part is replaced. The same place can show both.
- *Place versus experience of place.* C047-I01 separates remembering a place, having been there, and how it felt.
- *Generations.* C052-I03 concerns what passes from witnesses to descendants. C014-I02 concerns new practices standing on old ones.
- *Endings.* C032-I01 concerns the pain of a good period ending. C100-I03 concerns leaving only after every option has been tried.
- *Remembrance.* C091-I01 and C089-I02 concern how people recall, in their own words or by reconstructing backwards.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** A sense that a place is more than its parts is a legitimate view, not an error to correct. Displacement and relocation often involve injustice and state power, which must be researched as facts, not philosophy. Disaster and war memory is politically and ethnically sensitive in Sri Lanka. Backward reconstruction can produce false memories. Ageing must not be presented only as decline.

**Possible forms of reality.** Villages relocated for reservoirs or development. Houses dismantled and their timber reused. Boats, temples or bridges maintained piece by piece. Coastal communities after a tsunami, and other places shaped by flood, epidemic or conflict. Return journeys to schools, homes and towns.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** This is the territory closest to the channel's principle of Story → Place → Experience. The place is the evidence. Chathura can travel to it, walk it with people who remember it, and let the audience feel what persisted and what did not.

## T14 — Trust, talk and fairness in shared life

**Definition.** How communities form beliefs, share resources, choose who judges, spread or stop stories, and decide when to speak or stay silent.

**Central tension.** Shared life depends on trust in other people's words and judgements, yet beliefs spread before they are checked, divisions that feel prudent change how neighbours see each other, and fear or timing decides what is said.

**Major open questions**
- When does saving for tomorrow start to change how we treat what we once shared? (C052-I01)
- How do we tell the difference between waiting for the right moment and avoiding a hard truth? (C091-I03)
- When a story breaks a friendship, how much of the damage belongs to the teller, and how much to those who believed it? (C058-I01)
- What do people look for when they choose someone to judge them, and does it match what judging well requires? (C052-I02)
- When we stay silent or step back, how do we tell prudence from fear deciding for us? (C098-I01)

**Primary cards:** C021-I01, C023-I03, C052-I01, C052-I02, C058-I01, C058-I03, C076-I03, C080-I03, C086-I02, C091-I03, C098-I01

**Secondary cards:** C006-I02, C020-I02, C026-I01, C060-I01, C084-I01

**Important internal differences**
- *Belief formation.* C021-I01 concerns feeling sure before checking. C086-I02 concerns verdicts borrowed from others. C058-I01 concerns belief completing a harmful story. C076-I03 concerns trust and doubt out of balance.
- *Speaking.* C091-I03 concerns the timing of true, unwelcome words. C058-I03 concerns evasion from fear of being wrong. C098-I01 concerns fear bending a judgement or causing silence.
- *Commons and authority.* C052-I01 concerns shared resources becoming divided. C052-I02 concerns handing judgement to an agreed person. Both come from one origin myth and must be read as analogy.
- *Explanation.* C080-I03 concerns which single cause people name for an event.
- *Display.* C023-I03 concerns trust in people who speak well about goodness. It carries the highest defamation risk here.

**Recurring ethical cautions.** False or harmful claims must not be repeated on film, even as examples. No one may be called a hypocrite, gullible or deluded. Customary rights and disputes over commons may be legitimate and must be researched historically. The origin myth must not legitimise any ruler, rank or caste.

**Possible forms of reality.** Village tanks, grazing land and fishing grounds and how their use has been divided. Local mediators, committees and elders. Doctors, inspectors and elders delivering unwelcome news. Rumours in a neighbourhood and people who stopped them.

**Why it may suit cinematic documentary.** Commons are landscapes, such as tanks, channels, shorelines and pastures, whose use and division can be filmed. Speech and silence become visible through meetings, notices and the places where decisions are announced.
