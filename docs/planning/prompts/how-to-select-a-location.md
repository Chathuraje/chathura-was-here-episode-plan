# How to select a location for one idea

Paste this into a fresh chat, then name the idea. It is self-contained: it assumes no memory of any previous
session. Written 2026-09-26 from the GRP-01 method and the corrections of the GRP-02/GRP-03 rework.

---

You are choosing the real shooting location for **one film** in *Chathura Was Here*, a 98-film documentary
series on Abhidhamma philosophy, shot entirely in Sri Lanka. The repo is
`/Volumes/192.168.1.5/chathura-was-here-notes`. Work only in local files and web research. **Create no
artifacts on any external service, no MCP integrations, and no files outside this repo.**

The idea you are working on is: **IDEA-XXXX**  ← *(replace this)*

## Read before you search

- `development/ideas/IDEA-XXXX.json` — the record. Read all of it.
- The digest for **each concept in its `concept_links`**, in `development/digests/`. Read `summary_en`,
  `human_interpretation_en`, `key_terms`, `does_not_transfer`, `uncertainties` and `evidence`.
  **Skip `sinhala_explanation`.**
- `docs/planning/prompts/footage-led-idea-brief.md` — the house style.
- `docs/planning/10-standing-creative-decisions.md` — why the style is what it is. Binding.
- `docs/planning/08-the-footage-led-slate.md` — every other film. You need this twice: for the **Films per
  district** table and for the **archetypes** already used.

## The one rule that governs everything else

**The concepts are fixed. The location is free.**

The 98 films exist to cover a syllabus. The concepts in `concept_links` were assigned to this slot before any
location was, the first is primary, and **you may not change, reorder, add or drop them** to make a location
work. If a location only fits after you have adjusted the concepts, you have found the wrong location.

Everything else about the record — the title, the place, the beats, the treatment — is yours to rewrite from
scratch. **Ignore the location currently in the file completely.** Do not ask what can be salvaged from it.
Reading it first and then searching is how every weak location in this project survived: you end up arguing
for what is already there. Form your answer to the question below *before* you look at what the file says.

## The question to start from

> **What does this concept need in order to be *seen*?**

Not illustrated. Not resembled. Seen. A camera cannot photograph a mental state, so the concept has to be
carried by something outside a person: a landscape, a building, a document, an institution, a boundary, a
piece of engineering, a ruin. Work out what kind of *thing* would carry it before you think about any place
in Sri Lanka.

## The two-part test. Both halves are required.

**1. A documented or genuinely disputed story that already asks the concept's question.**

Not a story you can read the concept into — one that actually turns on it. The best locations are the ones
where a specific finding in the digest is the *only honest reading* of what the place is. A real example: a
digest says the fault is not recalling a past wrong but the churning regret that believes guilt cleanses;
the location is a shrine built in dressed stone as atonement for a killing most of the evidence says never
happened, by a king whose own monks told him no atonement was possible. The doctrine and the document say the
same thing. That is the standard.

A **dispute** is as good as a fact, often better — a place whose own inscription contradicts its founding
legend, a boundary claimed by two authorities, a building whose roof cannot be reconstructed. Disputes are
filmable because they are about the limits of knowing, which is frequently what the concept is about too.

**2. Scale, relief, light, weather or ruin worth travelling to photograph.**

The series is footage-led. A place with no frames in it is not a location, however good the argument.

**Both, or reject it.** A great story in a shed is rejected. And this next one is the specific failure that
forced a whole group to be redone, so hold it in front of you: **a perfect doctrinal fit in an ordinary
location is not a location.** When you notice you are excited about how neatly the philosophy lands and vague
about what the camera would actually see, you have hit it. Stop and start again.

Prefer **destinations** — landmarks are allowed, and were once wrongly discouraged. Small working places
(a workshop, a depot, a yard) are almost always the failure above wearing a disguise.

## The research standard

Assume **at least one thing you first read is wrong**, and find which. Across this project, writers checking
briefing notes found errors in almost every film — nine in one record. These are the recurring kinds. Test
for each one by name:

1. **A chronicle that does not contain the story attributed to it.** Download the translation and search it.
   One film's central story was credited to a chronicle in which the relevant word never appears.
2. **A founding legend carried only by texts written centuries later** — sometimes by the site's own monks.
3. **A figure repeated verbatim across dozens of pages with no source.**
4. **A claim that belongs to a different site with a similar name.** Sri Lankan site names collide constantly.
5. **A date impossible on its own terms** — a king credited with a tank "in the 12th century BC"; a man dead
   at 85 who was born 80 years earlier.
6. **An argument from silence reported as testimony.** Sources that do not mention an event have not denied
   it. Never count silence as a denial.
7. **"Scholars attribute…" naming no scholar** — and often contradicting the same literature's own
   description of the object.
8. **A chronicle passage told halfway.** A source gives a wound, then an astrologer, a prince and witchcraft;
   the modern retelling keeps only the wound, because only the wound sounds like history. Take the whole
   passage or none of it.

And ask of every story, always:

> **Who first wrote this down, and when, and what did they stand to gain?**

Name the **earliest attestation you can actually reach**. Twice in this project a "chronicle account" turned
out to enter the record through a modern newspaper travel article that miscredited the chronicle and was then
copied everywhere. In the best case that question is not an obstacle to the film — **it is the film**: one
location's whole subject became the fact that the accusation against a king was first attested at the court
with the clearest motive and first written down under the king who reversed his religion.

Separate your findings into **firm / disputed / could not confirm**, and *never average a variant*. Two dates
for a building means two dates in the record, not a midpoint. **Verify the district yourself** — online
sources get Sri Lankan districts wrong constantly, and one site sat across a river from a town in another
district and is misfiled everywhere because of it.

## Differentiation

- **No archetype already on the slate may be repeated.** Compare *kinds of place and story shapes*, not
  place names. "A Hindu temple destroyed by a colonial power and later restored" is one archetype however many
  such temples exist — the second one is a repeat. Forts, lighthouses, inscription-led films, hill shrines in
  use, documents films and so on are each used up once they are used.
- **No two films in a group share a visual approach.** Decide time of day, lens, movement or its absence, and
  the payoff, and check all of them against every other film in the group and against the slate's nearest
  neighbours. Say explicitly how yours differs.
- Watch for repeating a *method* rather than a place — e.g. keeping the named instrument out of frame. Twice
  in one group is a repeat.
- Do not let one historical person be named in the voice of more than one film, or the group reads as a
  biography.

## The district budget

Read the **Films per district** table in the slate. It is generated from the records, so it is authoritative
for what is *written* — but if a group is mid-rework its rows may still hold the old locations. Check.

Treat **7 films in a district as closed** and **4 or fewer as preferred**. Choose against the budget as an
input; do not pick first and audit after. If you must spend a district up to 7, say so in plain words and
give the reason, in `suggested_location.research_note`. Never force a weaker location to protect a count —
but never pretend the count did not move either.

## The floors — these are not preferences

- **No place name anywhere outside `suggested_location`.** Historical persons, kings, dynasties, chronicles,
  colonial powers and deities may be named in the voice; places may not.
- **No identifiable person may be framed or narrated as being in a mental state** — angry, greedy, envious,
  proud, resentful, afraid, deluded. You cannot read a state off a face and the film must not pretend to.
  Move the charge into landscape, architecture, documents or an institution.
- **No bereavement, funerals or private grief. No remembrance register.**
- **Sri Lanka's civil war is out of bounds as subject matter.** If a location's story is the war, it is not a
  location, whatever else is there.
- **Kamma is not fate.** Never narrate an outcome as deserved, and never use material that reads that way
  however good it is — one chronicle line perfect in every other respect was cut for this.
- **Whatever the digest's `does_not_transfer` forbids.** Read it as a list of things the film may not say.
  It usually also contains the film's best protection: these lists routinely *exempt* the honest case —
  ordinary uncertainty is not the unwholesome doubt, bodily tiredness is not the unwholesome sluggishness —
  and the exempt case, filmed as exempt, is a better film than an accusing one.
- **Never narrate traditional teaching as observable fact** — e.g. the bodily effects of anger.
- **No living community is a subject or an exhibit**, and no live ownership, religious or territorial dispute
  is entered. A sentence like "control was exercised through such-and-such a community" never enters the voice
  in any form.

## One thing to look for and never force

The camera is in the room. Several films independently found that the charge in their digest lands on
**looking** — on the observer, not on anyone in frame — and turned it onto the camera itself. Where the
sources put the observer inside the frame, that is the best available answer for any concept that is a
defilement. Where they do not, leave it alone.

## Deliver this, and do not sell it

1. **One recommendation**: place, district (with its current film count and where it moves to), approximate
   coordinates, and why it is there.
2. **The story**, with firm / disputed / could-not-confirm separated, and the earliest attestation named.
3. **The argued concept fit** — which *specific* digest finding it carries, quoted, and why this place is the
   way to see it. If you cite digest evidence lines, note that citations are machine-checked and range-aware:
   only cite lines you have read and verified carry the claim, and an off-by-one range fails the build.
4. **A treatment**: time of day, lens, movement or its absence, sound, and the payoff shot or cut — with an
   explicit comparison against the other films in the group and the slate's nearest archetypes.
5. **The guards** — what this film must not do, drawn from the floors and from the location's own hazards.
6. **The practical position**: access, permissions, season, and what would kill the shoot.
7. **One fallback in a different district.**
8. **Your honest doubts.** Name what is weakest about your own recommendation and what you could not verify.
   State the strongest objection to it yourself. A report that argues only one side is not usable.

**If nothing passes both halves of the test, say so and stop.** Do not let a weaker place through to fill the
slot, and do not pad a thin one. Proposing to reopen the slot, or to relax the district rule for a genuinely
first-rate story, is a legitimate answer. Recording a refusal with its reason is a result.

## When you write the record

Only after the location is agreed. Model depth and field order on `development/ideas/IDEA-0001.json`; a thin
rewrite is a failure. Keep `id`, `record_type`, `group_id`, `created_at`, `created_by`, `schema_version` and
the concepts in `concept_links` unchanged; bump `version`; set `updated_at` and `updated_by`; reset `review`
to pending with a full paragraph. `suggested_location` carries name, region (district named early),
coordinates, why_here, what_to_film, access, best_time, images, sources, research_note and a
`historical_thread` block (`story` / `how_it_binds` / `what_is_certain` / `what_is_disputed` / `sources`).
5–8 beats, each with both `on_screen` and `voice`.

Reference images: Wikimedia Commons search API (`generator=search&gsrnamespace=6`) with a **descriptive user
agent** — a bare `Mozilla/5.0` returns HTTP 429. Confirm every file with `action=query&titles=File:…
&prop=imageinfo` before writing its URL, as `Special:FilePath/<FILE>`. **Never write an unconfirmed URL.** An
empty array is an honest answer; explain it in `research_note`. If you carry an image that is not the site,
caption it in capitals saying so, and never substitute another site's material.

Write with Python `json.dumps(obj, indent=2, ensure_ascii=False) + "\n"` so Pali diacritics survive. Then run
`cd app && node scripts/check-data.mjs` and do not stop until it prints that all checks passed. Never
`git add` or `git commit`, and never touch `content/`, app source, or another idea file.
