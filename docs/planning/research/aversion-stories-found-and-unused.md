# Two chronicle stories of aversion, found and not usable here

Recorded 2026-09-26 during the GRP-03 slot-0037 search. Both are first-rate matches for C007 — the
hate-rooted minds — and both were rejected for reasons that may not hold for a later group. Preserved so the
research is not done twice.

## Coranaga, and eighteen unnamed vihāras

**Mahāvaṃsa ch. 34** contains it, verified in the chronicle rather than in summary:

> "Those places, where he had found no refuge during the time of his rebellion, eighteen vihāras, did this
> fool destroy."

A man returns to power and destroys the places that would not shelter him when he was a fugitive. It is
C007's occasion — a thing asked for and not given — carried out later, deliberately, at eighteen addresses.

**Why it could not be used: not one of the eighteen is named.** The story has no location, so it cannot be
filmed under a footage-led method. It would work only as an inscription- or manuscript-led film, or attached
to a site that can be independently shown to be one of the eighteen — which no source does.

## Mahāsena, Saṅghamitta, and the food that was forbidden

The Mahāvihāra monks refused the Vaitulya doctrine; the king forbade anyone to give them food on pain of a
fine; they left the capital; and the buildings were pulled down for their materials — **at Saṅghamitta's
urging.**

This is the most precisely documented **prompted** hate-rooted mind in the chronicles: the distinction between
the two minds of C007 is *only* spontaneous versus prompted, and here the chronicle names the prompter. The
starvation order is also C007's occasion in its purest administrative form — a refusal enacted as a rule about
food rather than as an act of violence.

**Why it could not be used:** the site is Anuradhapura, which stands at 9 films and is closed; and
**IDEA-0002 already stands on the Lovamahapaya that this story destroys**, so the two films would contradict
each other's subject at the same coordinates.

If a later group has a concept that this serves better than C007 — and the prompted/spontaneous distinction
suggests it might — the constraint to solve is the district, not the story.

## A name collision to know about

The Department of Archaeology's **Puttalam District** protected list includes a **"Mulgirigala Raja Maha
Vihara" at Nawagattegama**. This is a different site from **Mulkirigala in Hambantota**, which IDEA-0005 uses.
Anyone searching the name will pull Hambantota material onto a Puttalam site, or the reverse.

## Two founding legends with nothing behind them

- **Kuveni's palace, Wilpattu:** no archaeological or literary evidence connects the ruins to Kuveni. The
  plan, the five surrounding buildings and the stupa mound indicate a Buddhist monastery.
- **Anawilundawa**, the Puttalam Ramsar wetland, is described in state-adjacent sources as a tank cascade
  built by Parākramabāhu "around the **12th century BC**" — impossible on its own terms, and repeated verbatim
  across tourism pages. A live specimen of the date-that-cannot-be-right failure mode.
