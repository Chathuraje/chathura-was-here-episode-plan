# The Mihintale tablets of Mahinda IV — research note

Held for a GRP-06 film that was drafted and then paused on 2026-09-26. **Nothing here has been written into
an idea record yet.** Kept because the primary edition was obtained and read in full, which is expensive to
repeat.

## The edition, and where to get it

The text is **Epigraphia Zeylanica I, no. 7**, "The Two Tablets of Mahinda IV at Mihintale", ed. and trans.
Don Martino de Zilva Wickremasinghe, pp. 75–113 (issued 1904–12). Two independent online witnesses agree
word for word:

- **Internet Archive, Digital Library of India copy** — `archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.506674`,
  with whole-volume OCR at `.../2015.506674.Epigraphia-Zeylanica_djvu.txt`. English prose OCRs well; the
  Sinhala-script pages do not. **Warning:** the other Archive copy (`binm_epigraphia-zeylanica-vol-1-...`)
  is only 202 leaves and does **not** contain pp. 75–113.
- **Zenodo / Siddham** (Beyond Boundaries, Exeter), record IN03030 — translation at
  `zenodo.org/records/3540913` (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.3540913; the full Wickremasinghe translation sits in the
  `main_translation_copy` field of the metadata JSON) and the romanised transcript at
  `zenodo.org/records/3539313` (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.3539313).

Not obtained: Gunawardana, *Robe and Plough* (1979) — the standard scholarly treatment of the servants'
status, lending-only on Archive.org; Ranawella, *Inscriptions of Ceylon* V.ii (2004) pp. 272–285; the
Archaeological Survey reports describing the refectory excavation.

## The clause a film would be built on

Slab B, ll. 22–25, Wickremasinghe's translation:

> "…to **a servant who procures firewood and cooks food, three *aḍmanā* of rice** [daily]; to **a servant
> who brings firewood but does not cook**, and to **a servant who goes on errands, two *aḍmanā* of rice
> each**; to **a servant who [only] cooks on firewood fetched [by others], one *aḍmanā* of rice**."

A threefold pay gradient by **combination of tasks**, not by task. The twelve regular cooks are paid in land
instead (one *kiriya* and two *paya* each), their foreman in rice on top. The refectory itself is ⟨bat-ge⟩,
"rice-house". Wickremasinghe's own summary of Slab B: *"no service whatever was exacted without paying for
it."*

Slab B runs to roughly 4,000 words and names, among many others: an administrator of monastic regulations, a
steward, a clerk, a registrar and keeper of caskets, an almoner, a watchman, a master of festivals, a
calf-rearer, a head painter and eleven painters, granary caretakers, a refectory warder, twenty-four hired
⟨mīṇ̆ḍi⟩, thatchers, five potters, an alms-bowl maker, a water-strainer supplier, an astrologer, a barber,
florists, a lily-keeper, a chief master-artisan, eight carvers, brick-layers, two master-lapidaries (the
largest per-head land grant in the list), blacksmiths, lime-burners, six cartmen, twelve piece-workers,
stupa warders, sweepers and two laundrymen.

## Three things that must be held as disputed

1. **The reign dates.** Every popular source gives 956–972. Wickremasinghe gives none of that: he prints
   "a.d. 975–91, W.; 997–1013, T." and then argues from South Indian synchronisms that the accession must
   fall **between 1019 and 1029**. Siddham records 975–991. The conventional 956–972 rests on a later
   revised chronology. A ~60-year spread, almost never flagged.
2. **The servants' status.** Wickremasinghe translates ⟨dasun⟩ as **"serfs"** throughout and glosses
   *su-dasun* as "well-conducted serfs". **Ranawella argues the opposite** — that ⟨vehera dasun⟩ means
   domestic servants "who were not serfs or slaves". Müller rendered ⟨mīṇ̆ḍi⟩ "slave", Gunasekara "female
   servant"; Wickremasinghe declined to translate it. **Unresolved; record both, adjudicate neither.**
3. **The commonest unit of pay is the least understood.** ⟨vasag⟩ appears constantly. Müller and Gunasekara
   read it as "a farm from Damiya"; Wickremasinghe rejects that and reads "a measured quantity of
   provisions"; others "a meal", "a monk's cell".

Also disputed: whether the slabs belong to the refectory or flank the relic-house entrance (Wickremasinghe
says "near … the **so-called** Bhojana-sala" and hedges the building's identification); the slabs'
dimensions (7 × 4 × 2 ft per Wickremasinghe, 6 × 2 ft elsewhere); and the large rice trough's length
(41 ft 9 in / 12.81 m in two sources, 23 ft in another).

## Facts that are firm

Two granite slabs, each **58 lines** (116 total), lines about **109.2 cm** long, letters about **2.22 cm**,
standing upright to the left of the third flight of steps on the western ascent, well below the summit
terraces. Sinhala characters of the late tenth and early eleventh centuries. Wickremasinghe's text was
checked letter by letter against the stone by the Archaeological Commissioner, H. C. P. Bell.

**Publication history is older than usually stated:** Armour's translation appeared in the *Ceylon Almanac*
for **1834**; Goldschmidt printed the first nine lines in 1876 and first dated it palaeographically; Müller
published the full text in 1880 and again in 1883 ("not very accurate" — Wickremasinghe); Wickremasinghe's
edition followed.

## The floor, if this is ever used

The medical provision is **two clauses out of roughly 4,000 words** — a physician, a leech-doctor, and
possibly a preparer of decoctions — bracketed between a water-strainer supplier and a barber. The
inscription mentions **no hospital, infirmary or nursing attendant**; the famous excavated hospital at the
foot of the hill is not referred to in it. Any film must keep illness to a single item in a list and must
not make care of the sick its subject.

The penalties are real and must be recorded without relish: employees who infringe are fined and dismissed;
wages of "those who are refractory" are appropriated by the monastery; tenants' fines are commutable to
tank-digging; and anyone who alienates monastery land is to be **"banished to a foreign country"**.
