## Idea

### Premise

Follow one person or a small working group through a real, bounded task that demands repeated noticing, recognition, adjustment, and care. The task—not a philosophical lesson—supplies the story: something must be made, repaired, prepared, moved, or completed, and reality decides whether it succeeds. Close observation attends to contact with materials and people, moments that feel easy, painful, or ordinary, the features workers recognize, and the choices that redirect the work. The film never claims to see inside a mind; actions, natural sound, and participants’ own words carry what can be known.

### Central human question

When real work changes from moment to moment, how does a person recognize what is happening, live with how it feels, and decide what to do next?

### Concept connections

- **Primary: C020 — සර්ව චිත්ත සාධාරණ චෛතසික සත.** The seven co-present functions can sharpen editorial attention to contact, feeling tone, recognition, intention, holding to an aspect, momentary maintenance, and directing the mind. Do not turn them into a seven-step plot or diagnose them on camera.
- **Supporting: C032 — වේදනා සංග්‍ර‍හය.** Its pleasant, painful, and neutral distinction—and its separation of bodily and mental forms—can make questions about experience more precise. Do not equate `වේදනා` with every emotion or attribute a feeling without the participant’s account.

### Movement and stakes

## English human interpretations

### C020 — several jobs occur together

Meeting a situation involves more than attention. Something makes contact with experience; it has a pleasant, painful, or neutral tone; features are recognized; activity is organized; the mind holds to some aspect; the moment is sustained; and the mind is directed. The source presents these together, not as a universal seven-stage sequence. Using them to choose interview questions or notice edits is an editorial application; a camera cannot prove seven hidden mental factors. The source meanings of intention/karma and mental life faculty do not transfer fully into ordinary English without context.

### C032 — feeling tone is narrower than emotion

An experience can feel pleasant, painful, or neither, with the source also distinguishing bodily from mental forms. This is not a list of every emotion. A film may ask what feels easy, painful, unwelcome, or ordinary and how that may influence a response, but it must not claim a universal chain or scientific validation. The participant’s account and observable context are evidence; an edit or expression is not enough. The source’s broader `දුක්ඛ` account cannot be reduced to “everything feels bad.”
