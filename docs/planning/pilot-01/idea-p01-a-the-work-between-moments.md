# IDEA-P01-A — The Work Between Moments

## Premise

Follow one person or a small working group through a real, bounded task that demands repeated noticing, recognition, adjustment, and care. The task—not a philosophical lesson—supplies the story: something must be made, repaired, prepared, moved, or completed, and reality decides whether it succeeds. Close observation attends to contact with materials and people, moments that feel easy, painful, or ordinary, the features workers recognize, and the choices that redirect the work. The film never claims to see inside a mind; it lets actions, natural sound, and participants’ own words carry what can be known.

## Central human question

When real work changes from moment to moment, how does a person recognize what is happening, live with how it feels, and decide what to do next?

## Concept connections

**Primary — C020, සර්ව චිත්ත සාධාරණ චෛතසික සත.** Its seven co-present functions offer an editorial checklist for attending to contact, feeling tone, recognition, intention, holding to an aspect, momentary maintenance, and directing the mind. It is not imposed as a seven-step plot or a diagnosis of participants [C020 dossier](c020-universal-mental-factors.md).

**Supporting — C032, වේදනා සංග්‍ර‍හය.** Its distinction between pleasant, painful, and neutral feeling—and between bodily and mental forms—helps prevent the film from treating all difficulty as one emotion. A participant’s account is required before the film attributes a feeling [C032 dossier](c032-feeling-classification.md).

C084 is deliberately not attached. The idea does not need all three pilot concepts.

## Unverified possibilities to investigate

- A person or small team carrying out a skilled task with a visible beginning, constraints, corrections, and finish.
- A task in which touch, sound, timing, repetition, fatigue, uncertainty, and learned recognition can be observed without staging them.
- A participant willing to describe specific moments in their own language without being asked to endorse the source framework.

These are search criteria, not discovered people, confirmed access, or actual events.

## What the camera could observe

- Hands, tools, materials, faces, pauses, repeated attempts, checks, corrections, and cooperation.
- Changes in pace, sound, light, weather, material condition, or other real constraints if they occur.
- The full natural-sound texture of the work, including quiet intervals.
- Participant explanations tied to concrete actions: what they noticed, recognized, expected, or changed.

The edit must not present facial expression alone as proof of an inner feeling or intention.

## Movement and stakes

The task itself can create movement: a real requirement, limited material or time, an error, a changing condition, or responsibility to another person may matter if research verifies it. The stakes should come from what completion or failure means to the participant, not from manufactured urgency. If no consequential task or meaningful uncertainty exists, the idea is too slight.

## Possible change in viewer understanding

The viewer may begin by seeing only a finished product or routine action and come to notice the many kinds of adjustment involved. The real outcome is unknown; the film must not promise mastery, transformation, or success before observation.

## Location requirements

- An active, ethically filmable working environment where a bounded task unfolds through observable action.
- Sufficient visual and natural-sound variation without interrupting or reshaping the work.
- Safe camera access and informed participant consent.
- No location is proposed or selected; selection remains exclusively Chathura’s.

## Research questions and evidence needed

- What real task has a clear beginning, development, and possible consequence?
- Who performs it, who is affected by it, and how do they describe its importance?
- What access, consent, safety, privacy, and work-disruption limits apply?
- Which moments recur naturally, and which changes or corrections can actually be observed?
- How do participants describe comfort, pain, neutrality, recognition, and decision without the interviewer supplying those answers?
- What footage, field notes, interviews, and contextual records support each eventual claim?

## Why this deserves a film

The independent story is the completion of consequential work and the relationships and judgment it requires. C020 and C032 can sharpen observation, but the film should remain worthwhile if their terminology is never spoken. It earns a film only if a real person, real task, and meaningful stake sustain attention.

## Link to IDEA-P01-B

This idea looks closely at how a person meets changing work. [IDEA-P01-B](idea-p01-b-the-name-in-use.md) looks outward at how a shared name organizes action among people. A question can carry forward without merging them: how much of what people do comes from the immediate situation, and how much from the meaning already attached to it?

## Revise or abandon if

- The task has no independent human stake beyond illustrating C020 or C032.
- Access would require staging actions or disrupting livelihoods.
- Participants do not want inner experience discussed, or the film can only infer it from images.
- The work is visually repetitive without real development, relationship, or consequence.
- Research reveals that the combined framework distorts how participants understand their own activity.
