### Differences, extraction issue, and limits

C084-P prints the heading `අට ප්‍රඥප්ති` but supplies only seven numbered items—aggregate, configuration, beings, direction, time, space, and sign—before the page ends. The extract flags this explicitly [C084-P:L27–L45], and BOOK-3 confirms the same gap [BOOK-3:L12202–L12220]. No eighth category is invented here.

C084-M organizes the material as `අර්ථ ප්‍රඥප්ති` and `නාම ප්‍රඥප්ති`, gives a cloth/thread example, and explicitly distinguishes the physical sound from the name and meaning it occasions [C084-M:L5–L21]. Its visible meaning-concept list is not identical to C084-P’s seven-item list: it does not separately show space and sign in that short section. The sources’ schemes therefore remain side by side rather than being forced into a single complete taxonomy.

## English human interpretation

### Names organize a shared world

People act through names, categories, directions, times, roles, and recognized shapes. A spoken sound and the meaning people take from it are not the same thing in the source’s analysis [C084-M:L21–L21]. A name can therefore become visible in practice: it can guide where people go, what they maintain, whom they ask, and what they think an object or place is for.

This does not mean that everything is imaginary. A building, path, tool, or person is not erased because its name is conventional. The source makes a technical distinction between conventional wholes and an ultimate analysis of constituent phenomena [C084-P:L23–L41; C084-M:L5–L19]. The editorial application is to observe what a shared name allows people to do and where different names create different expectations.

### Meaning is not a fixed camera fact

A film could compare the words people use with the actions, materials, and relationships in front of the camera. It should not announce that one participant’s label is false merely because another label differs. The story would need evidence for who uses each term, in what context, and with what practical effect.

### What does not transfer cleanly

The source’s `existent/non-existent` combinations depend on its own account of ultimate and conventional truth. Turning them into the slogan “language constructs reality” would overreach and lose the sixfold technical distinction. This pilot carries across only a modest editorial question: how do names and categories shape shared action?
