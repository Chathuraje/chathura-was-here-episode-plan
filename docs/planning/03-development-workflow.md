# Episode-development workflow

The workflow is gated, reviewable, and reversible. Reality may change a location, participant, route, object, event, or captured scene; downstream records must then be revised without rewriting original sources or erasing earlier approved versions.

## Stage gates

| Stage | Inputs | Outputs | Ready to advance when | Chathura reviews |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Source audit | Original documents, source maps, extracts, extraction guidance | Inventory, provenance links, coverage/gap notes, audit limits | Files and identifiers are known; gaps and uncertain routing are named; originals remain untouched | Source scope, provenance gaps, and whether further verification is required |
| 2. Concept explanation | Approved concept record and its cited extracts | Clear Sinhala explanation preserving source meaning and Buddhist terms; source-teaching claims linked to passages | Every material statement is supported or explicitly marked uncertain; explanation does not add documentary claims | Accuracy, terminology, omissions, and whether the explanation represents the source |
| 3. Human interpretation | Approved Sinhala explanation | Separate simple-English interpretation without Buddhist jargon | It is understandable on human terms, remains meaningfully connected to the source, and is clearly editorial interpretation | Tone, faithfulness, and whether the interpretation opens a genuine human question |
| 4. Candidate ideas | One or more approved interpretations; known project constraints | Unnumbered idea records, concept links, documentary possibility, risks, unknowns, and location requirements | Each idea can stand as a film; people/events are not doctrine props; concept combinations are meaningful | Which ideas to reject, hold, revise, combine, or explore |
| 5. Ten chronological groups | Approved candidate ideas and continuity constraints | Ten connected groups, flexible membership, group arcs, Object 1–10 assignments, provisional chronology | The 98 development-film slots can be distributed across ten groups with no ten-film ceiling; connections and object acquisition logic are coherent | Group themes, connections, flexible sizes, first chronological episodes, and object logic |
| 6. User location selection and research | Approved idea, explicit location requirements, AI suggestions kept separate | Chathura’s selected location (or an explicit “not selected”), research plan, evidence records, permissions/safety/logistics unknowns, reveal policy | Chathura—not AI—has made any required selection; location-dependent claims have sufficient verified research | Location choice, disclosure timing, access, ethics, feasibility, and research sufficiency |
| 7. Episode briefs | Approved idea/group placement, selected location where required, research, continuity state | Complete internal brief with story question, people, place, experience, sources, interpretation, evidence, object/continuity notes, unknowns | Core real-world premise is supportable; dependencies and unknowns are explicit; no permanent release number is required | Whether the film should enter slate planning and what must change first |
| 8. Release planning | Approved briefs, audience arc, continuity constraints | Release-series/season membership and provisional release positions, separate from chronological and filming positions | Release logic is coherent and does not overwrite story chronology; framing Episodes 1 and 100 remain fixed | Public sequence, series/season boundaries, and unresolved older distribution rules |
| 9. Treatments | Approved brief and research | Prose treatment describing dramatic movement, observation, narration approach, sound, ending, and alternatives | The story works independently and honors Story → Place → Experience | Story shape, point of view, ethics, access assumptions, and ending |
| 10. Scene outlines | Approved treatment, chronology, continuity and evidence | Scene records with purpose, chronological key, dependencies, footage needs, sound, narration intent, timings, and continuity | Required and optional scenes are distinguishable; shared scenes and exact known boundaries are referenced | Scene order, rhythm, shared footage, object/location state, and gaps |
| 11. Production screenplays | Approved scene outline; selected location; adequate research | Versioned pre-filming screenplay covering purpose, footage, sound, narration, timing, research gaps, and continuity | It is practical to film, factually bounded, and does not invent participant speech/events | Approval to film, production risks, and remaining conditional passages |
| 12. Post-filming revisions | Production screenplay, footage log, transcripts/releases, field notes, verified events | Separate version grounded in what was actually captured; variance log; revised edit/continuity notes | Every retained event/dialogue claim is supported by footage or records; planned-but-uncaptured material is removed or clearly noted | Editorial truth, consent/ethics, final continuity, and lock readiness |

## Review and status rules

Every record should move through explicit statuses such as `draft`, `in_review`, `approved`, `needs_revision`, `on_hold`, and `retired`. Approval applies to a named version, not forever to a mutable file. Each review decision records reviewer, date, outcome, note, and the version reviewed.

No downstream approval rewrites upstream evidence. For example:

- a compelling idea cannot alter what a source passage says;
- a suggested location cannot become selected without Chathura’s decision;
- a planned participant statement cannot become authentic dialogue without a recording or transcript;
- release order cannot overwrite chronological order;
- a post-filming screenplay cannot pretend planned footage was captured.

## Revision loops when reality changes

When field reality changes, create a change note, identify affected records, and reopen only the necessary gates. A changed location may reopen research, brief, treatment, outline, and production screenplay while leaving the source explanation intact. A real change in object ownership updates the object-event ledger and all later continuity projections. New footage may change scene order or meaning but must preserve the earlier production screenplay as a distinct version.

If evidence contradicts an approved documentary possibility, evidence wins. The possibility is revised, held, or retired; people and events are never manipulated to rescue it.

## Count and structure controls

- The active development slate targets exactly 98 films across ten chronological groups.
- Group sizes are validated by sum only: each group must contain at least one film, and no ten-film maximum is imposed.
- Framing Episodes 1 and 100 are held separately and bring the public-film target to 100.
- Internal IDs survive movement between groups or changes in chronology, release order, season, or filming schedule.
- Permanent release numbers are assigned only at an approved release-planning gate.
