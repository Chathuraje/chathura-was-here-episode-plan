## Central meaning

Each link of the twelve-factor formula is explained. Ignorance (අවිද්‍යාව) is not-knowing the four truths, past and future, and conditioned arising; it "covers" the true nature of things. Formations (සංස්කාර) are the 29 mundane wholesome and unwholesome volitions, which through the ripening of action give 32 kinds of resultant consciousness at rebirth and during life. Consciousness conditions mind-and-matter, which conditions the six sense bases; these condition contact, contact conditions feeling, feeling conditions craving (තණ්හාව), craving conditions clinging (උපාදානය: sense-desire, views, rites and rules, self-doctrine), clinging conditions becoming (කර්ම භවය, the action done; උපපත්ති භවය, the resulting existence), becoming conditions birth, and birth conditions ageing, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair. The whole is a beginningless wheel with two roots (ignorance, craving) across three times, with no maker and no separate experiencer behind it.

## Essential distinctions

- **Many causes, one named** (Margaya line 31; Pradeepika lines 538–558). No result comes from one cause; many causes give many results (seed, earth, water and season give a sprout, line 550). A single cause is named because it is principal, obvious, distinctive, or suited to the listener (line 552). Margaya line 111 adds that a cause's cause is often spoken of as the cause: "rice is cooked by firewood", though fire cooks it.
- **Unlike causes** (Margaya line 33; Pradeepika lines 560–568). Cause and result need not match: a fragrant lotus grows from foul mud; bright fire gives dark smoke; earlier training conditions later skill; milk conditions curd. This is how unwholesome ignorance can condition wholesome action.
- **All three feelings condition craving** (Margaya line 91; Pradeepika line 1292). Pleasant feeling is wanted more; a person in pain longs for pleasure; neutral feeling is wanted because it is peaceful.
- **Craving versus clinging** (Margaya line 97; Pradeepika lines 214–222, 1300, 1311, 1380). Craving from feeling is weak; clinging is craving made strong, "not releasing" what it has taken. Simile: a gem-miner's wanting before the gem is found versus the craving for a valuable gem already in hand.
- **Clinging leads to action** (Margaya line 111; Pradeepika lines 1525–1547). With clinging, one begins doing many things to maintain, please and secure the "I and mine". Pradeepika's harsh image: the ordinary person, like a madman, acts without asking what is fitting (line 1525).
- **Sorrow and its types** (Pradeepika lines 260–298, 1577–1611; Margaya line 125). Sorrow is inner drying and burning, going over virtues and faults with regret; lamentation is repeatedly calling out names and virtues; despair is heavy exhaustion. Sorrow arises when a mind stuck on objects is separated from them, "like a deer struck by a spear" (line 1603), and when a body taken as "me, mine" changes (line 1605).
- **Neither the same nor another** (Pradeepika lines 929–969). Rebirth-consciousness does not travel from a past life, yet does not arise without its causes; milk and curd, seed and sprout; a child's schooling benefits the adult body and no one else.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on every link and on the key similes they share (lotus/mud in Margaya; unlike-cause examples in Pradeepika). Margaya is a simplified teaching text; Pradeepika is a dense commentary with the formal 24-condition analysis for each link.

Genuine differences:
- **Which feelings condition craving.** Margaya line 89 counts all 81 feelings in mundane consciousness. Pradeepika line 1290 says the exposition intends only resultant pleasant feeling, then (line 1292) admits all three feelings. Line 1248 restricts `ඵස්ස පච්චයා වෙදනා` to the 32 resultant feelings, as does Margaya line 81 for that link.
- **Rūpa in "mind-and-matter → six bases."** Margaya line 63 lists eleven kinds and notes that "some books" count twelve with nutritive matter; Pradeepika line 1159 lists the same eleven without the variant.
- **Who can have view-clinging.** Margaya line 99 says view-clinging and rites-and-rules clinging cannot arise in "true Buddhists". Pradeepika instead says these are removed at stream-entry (line 1368). Different framings, not necessarily a conflict.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth-linking, the 32 resultant consciousnesses, deathbed signs, the twins who differ "because of kamma" (Margaya line 117), rebirth as the handicapped for weak wholesome kamma (Pradeepika line 741), planes of existence, the three-lives division of the twelve factors, and the dying devas' five signs (line 1609) are doctrinal claims. The characterisation of ordinary people as "blind fools" or "like madmen" (lines 590, 1525) is sermon rhetoric and must not be applied to real people. Lamentation "only for one confused by delusion" (line 1601) must not become a judgement on mourners.

## Human meaning paragraph

Inside the doctrinal wheel, three passages describe processes people can recognise. First, feeling of any kind can turn into wanting: discomfort makes a person long for comfort, comfort makes them want more of it, and calm is wanted for its peace. Second, wanting has grades: the loose hope before something is obtained is weaker than the grip on something valuable once it is in hand, and that grip "does not let go" and drives action to keep and protect. Third, as a matter of method, the sources warn that whenever we say "this caused that", we have chosen one cause from many, perhaps because it is the main one, the obvious one or the one that suits our listener, and we sometimes name a cause's cause instead. These are interpretations beyond the text's aim, which is to show how suffering continues across lives.

## Idea decisions

- **C080-I01 — When discomfort turns into wanting comfort.** Accepted. Margaya line 91; Pradeepika lines 1258–1294, 204–212.
- **C080-I02 — Wanting tightens once the thing is in hand.** Accepted. Margaya lines 97, 101, 111; Pradeepika lines 214–222, 1296–1311, 1380.
- **C080-I03 — Naming one cause for what many causes made.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Margaya lines 31, 111; Pradeepika lines 538–558. Flagged overlap with C060-I01, which is about the grieving search for one reason after an early death; I03 is about the selection built into any causal statement.
- Rejected candidate: *Good things growing from unlikely roots* (lotus from mud; ignorance conditioning merit). The human application slides into moralising about mixed motives, already covered by C005-I01, C011-I02 and C012-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *A childhood lesson that lives in no one else's adult body* (Pradeepika lines 961–965). Its purpose in the text is rebirth continuity, and the human point overlaps C039-I01 and C001-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Sorrow measured by how tightly something was held* (Pradeepika lines 1603–1605). Plausible, but close to C007-I03 and C005-I02, and the text frames grief and lament as products of delusion, which would risk judging mourners. Listed as a held question in the batch report.
- Rejected candidate: *Acting without asking what is fitting, like a madman* (line 1525). Sermon rhetoric, not a mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: deathbed relatives offering flowers, music and incense to guide the dying mind (lines 817–821). Duplicates C062-I01 and rests on rebirth doctrine.
