# C001 — Abhidhamma, conventional names and ultimate reality

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–59). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 1, PDF 24–28 (printed 13–17). Page markers appear at line 29 (PDF 25 / printed 14), line 35 (PDF 26 / printed 15), line 45 (PDF 27 / printed 16) and line 57 (PDF 28 / printed 17).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–56). It has two headings: `# 1. අභිධර්මයේ වැදගත්කම` and `# 2. පරමාර්ථය හා ප්‍ර‍ඥප්තිය`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

**Pradeepika** opens with an exhortation. The teaching is the only refuge. Learning must lead to practice (virtue, concentration, wisdom). Reading history and biographies, merely avoiding wrongdoing, doing social service without understanding, or studying the teaching for gain, honour, degrees and positions all fall short (lines 31, PDF 25). Laypeople with jobs and households have very little leisure, so study is hard. They must still make room for it without neglecting their duties (line 39, PDF 26). It then separates conventional teaching (සම්මුති දේශනා), which uses the world's terms such as person, man, woman, tree or river so that particular listeners understand, from ultimate teaching (පරමාර්ථ දේශනා), which describes aggregates, elements and bases (lines 51–53). A “human being” is a conventional name for a collection of parts. On analysis it is a mass of mental and material phenomena (lines 55–59).

**Margaya** asserts that Abhidhamma is needed to understand rebirth, merit and demerit, unseen beings and nirvāṇa (lines 3–19). It then explains ultimate and conventional reality through analogies. A whirled firebrand appears as a ring of fire that does not exist (line 42). A table is only pieces of wood in an arrangement. Neither any single piece nor the pile of pieces is the table, and scattered pieces are no table (line 46). A conventional thing (ප්‍රඥප්තිය) is what disappears on division. The ultimate (පරමාර්ථ) is what remains indivisible at the end of analysis (lines 48–56).

## Essential distinctions

- A conventional truth is not a lie. The sources treat it as a legitimate mode of teaching adapted to the listener (Pradeepika line 51).
- “Does not truly exist” (Margaya) is a technical claim about ultimate analysis, not a denial that tables are usable or people matter.
- Wood particles are not ultimate either (Margaya lines 54–56). So this is not simple physical atomism.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the conventional/ultimate distinction and the human-as-parts analysis. Pradeepika frames it through two modes of teaching and relates it to insight meditation. Margaya gives the firebrand and table analogies and a divisibility test. There is a difference in tone rather than in doctrine. Pradeepika says a human being is “nothing but” a collection of mental and material phenomena. Margaya says conventional things “do not truly exist” and are “mere perceptions” (හැඟීම් මාත්‍රයෙකි). Both statements are doctrinal.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claims about rebirth, unseen beings, merit's power and the need for Abhidhamma to avoid being a “blind worldling” are doctrinal assertions. They should not become idea premises. Pradeepika's claim that ultimate phenomena are known only to omniscient knowledge is doctrinal. The divisibility analysis must not be equated with particle physics. Margaya's assertion that people who do not venerate images hold wrong views is sectarian and is not used.

## Human meaning paragraph

Everyday life runs on names for wholes: a table, a person, a village, a family. The source proposes that such names are useful ways of speaking about parts in a particular arrangement, not independent things. The whole appears the way a spinning flame appears as a ring. Separately, Pradeepika raises two practical human tensions about learning. Studying something can be driven by status rather than by the change it is meant to bring. People with jobs and families struggle to find time for the inner work they value. The applications to identity, community and busy lives below are interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C001-I01 — When a whole is only its parts arranged.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the table, firebrand and human-as-parts analysis.
- **C001-I02 — Learning for a title instead of for change.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika's critique of study for gain and position.
- **C001-I03 — Making room for an inner life within a crowded day.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika's remark about laypeople's scarce leisure.
- Rejected candidate: *Speaking in the listener's language* (conventional teaching adapted to people). Interesting, but the source gives one sentence with no mechanism. Hold it as a possible future supporting reference.
- Rejected candidate: the need to learn Abhidhamma to understand rebirth. This is a doctrinal premise, not a human idea.
