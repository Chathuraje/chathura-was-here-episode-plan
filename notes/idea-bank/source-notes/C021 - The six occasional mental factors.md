# C021 — The six occasional mental factors

## Central meaning

Six factors are “scattered” (ප්‍රකීර්ණක). They arise in some wholesome and some unwholesome mind-states but not in all. They are wholly wholesome, wholly unwholesome or neutral according to the mind-state they join (Pradeepika line 25; Margaya lines 29–31).

- **Initial application (විතක්ක).** Lifts or carries the mind to the object, and is what is called thinking (කල්පනා). Pradeepika: like a man who helps lift a load onto someone's shoulder, a first bell-strike, or a hawk flapping to climb (line 33). Margaya: weak initial application is not obvious. It becomes obvious when deliberately produced, as the effort to reach where the mind has not reached and to know what is not known (line 7).
- **Sustained application (විචාර).** Rubs or spreads over the object, making its nature clear. Like the resonance after the bell, a hawk gliding, or polishing a metal bowl held firmly in one hand (Pradeepika lines 37–41; Margaya line 11). Margaya warns that the bell simile may suggest a sequence, but the two arise in the same moment (line 11).
- **Decision (අධිමොක්ඛ).** Settles or determines the object, removing wavering. Pradeepika: it grips whatever the object is without distinguishing true from false or good from bad, and so often believes the untrue as true (“wrong decision”). It is opposed to doubt and absent from the doubt-associated state (line 45). Margaya: because of it the mind does not go two or three ways, and takes the object one way, rightly or wrongly (line 15).
- **Energy (වීරිය).** Supports the mind so it does not retreat, like food for the body (Pradeepika lines 49–53). Margaya: not retreating from a task, continuing until what was begun is done. Mental energy is distinct from bodily strength (line 19). Pradeepika adds an asymmetry. In an ordinary mind bent toward wrong, energy moves easily to the unwholesome side, like a boat on water flowing downhill. The wholesome must be done by suppressing the unwholesome and shaping the mind with effort. To a person with defilements, giving and keeping precepts seem a great burden, like climbing a hill carrying a load (line 53).
- **Rapture or joy (පීති).** Refreshes or gladdens the mind, like a shrunken, suffering body filling out on getting something desired. Pradeepika lists five grades, from goosebumps to pervading joy, and attributes laughter and dancing to wind-element produced by joy (lines 57–63). Margaya: swelling like a flower opening (line 23).
- **Wish-to-do (ඡන්ද).** Pradeepika: the wish to take the object, not a force that sticks to it. Sticking is greed, craving or longing. It is absent from the two delusion-rooted states because agitation or doubt prevents any wish (line 69). In greed-rooted states it supports greed. In wholesome states it helps suppress greed and, developed, supports higher wholesome acts, absorption and nirvāṇa (line 75). Margaya: the wish or need to do things. It looks a little like greed but lacks greed's clinging, being “a wanting free of attachment”. In greedy states it accords with greed, and in states opposed to greed it opposes greed (line 27).

## Essential distinctions

- Wish-to-do versus greed: wanting to act or to take up an object is not the same as clinging to it (Pradeepika line 69; Margaya line 27).
- Decision versus correctness: settling on an object is not the same as being right about it (Pradeepika line 45; Margaya line 15).
- Initial versus sustained application are simultaneous functions, not steps (Margaya line 11; Pradeepika line 41, “always arise together”).
- Mental energy versus bodily strength (Margaya line 19).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the six factors, their basic functions and their variable ethical quality. Pradeepika gives much fuller similes and the energy asymmetry. Margaya adds the explicit warning against reading the bell simile as a sequence, and the distinction between bodily and mental energy. There is a difference of emphasis on decision. Pradeepika says decision “mostly” (බොහෝ සෙයින්) believes wrongly, while Margaya is neutral (“rightly or wrongly”). This should be recorded, not averaged.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claim that divine-ear powers can hear the “sound” of thinking (Pradeepika line 33), joy producing bodily movement through the wind element (line 61), absorption factors, perfections and nirvāṇa (lines 33, 41, 53, 65, 75) are doctrinal. The five grades of rapture describe meditative and devotional experience and should not be presented as physiology. The claim that the unwholesome is “downhill” for ordinary people is a doctrinal view of human nature, not an empirical finding.

## Human meaning paragraph

Several capacities that people often lump together are distinguished here. Wanting to do something differs from clinging to it. Becoming sure of something differs from being right. Effort itself has no direction, but, the source claims, it flows more easily toward harm than toward good for most people, so good action feels like carrying a load uphill. In everyday terms (my interpretation), this gives three separate human tensions: certainty without checking, motivation without possessiveness, and the uneven cost of acting well.

## Idea decisions

- **C021-I01 — Being sure without checking whether it is true.** Accepted. Based on decision (Pradeepika line 45; Margaya line 15).
- **C021-I02 — Wanting to do without needing to possess.** Accepted. Based on wish-to-do versus greed (Pradeepika lines 69, 75; Margaya line 27).
- **C021-I03 — Why doing good can feel uphill.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the energy asymmetry (Pradeepika line 53). Related to, but distinct from, C006-I03 (needing a push).
- Rejected candidate: *Reaching for a thought and staying with it* (initial and sustained application). The similes are rich, but the sources insist the two are simultaneous within one mind-moment. An everyday “first grab, then explore” reading would impose the sequence Margaya explicitly warns against. The functions also recur in later absorption (jhāna) concepts.
- Rejected candidate: *Joy that shows in the body* (rapture grades). The bodily explanation is doctrinal physiology, and the grades concern meditative experience.
