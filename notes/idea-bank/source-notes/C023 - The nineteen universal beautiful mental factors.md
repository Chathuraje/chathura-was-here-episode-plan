# C023 — The nineteen universal beautiful mental factors

## Central meaning

Twenty-five "beautiful" mental factors (සොහන චෛතසික) accompany wholesome and other beautiful mind-states. Nineteen of them occur in every beautiful mind-state (සොහන සාධාරණ). They are faith (සද්ධා), mindfulness (සති), shame at wrong (හිරි), fear of wrong (ඔත්තප්ප), non-greed (අලෝභ), non-aversion (අදෝස), balance (තත්‍රමජ්ඣත්තතා), and six pairs, each applying to the mental factors (කාය) and to consciousness (චිත්ත): tranquillity, lightness, malleability, wieldiness, proficiency and straightness. Pradeepika presents each as a positive force that suppresses a specific unwholesome factor. It is not just the absence of that factor. It says the untrained mind naturally drifts toward the unwholesome, so good action takes effort (lines 55, 109).

## Essential distinctions

- **Non-greed and non-aversion are active powers, not absences** (Pradeepika 89, 99). Giving even to a disliked recipient or an enemy requires suppressing aversion (lines 93, 99–103).
- **Shame vs fear of wrong** (Pradeepika 65–85; Margaya 15). Shame (හිරි) is inward-referenced. It guards self-respect (ආත්මගෞරවය), and a person refrains by considering their lineage, age, courage or education ("an educated person like me should not do what the ignorant do"). Fear of wrong (ඔත්තප්ප) is outward-referenced. It guards regard for the world (ලොකගෞරවය), through fear of punishment and of blame from the virtuous and the learned, or awareness that one lives among good people. Together they are called "world-protecting", said to govern the world better than punishment laws (line 85).
- **Proficiency** (පාගුඤ්ඤතා, Pradeepika 151–157; Margaya 47). Good action needs practice as any work does. People who have practised giving find it easy. Those who have not are reluctant, fearful or embarrassed to give (line 157). Wieldiness (කම්මඤ්ඤතා, line 149): when weak, a person prepares to do good but quickly slides back.
- **Straightness** (උජ්ජුකතා, Pradeepika 159–179; Margaya 51). It opposes concealment of one's faults (මායා) and display of qualities one lacks (සාඨෙය්‍ය). There are three kinds of crookedness. The "cow-urine" kind does wrong while denying it. The "crescent-moon" kind does wrong while telling others to fear wrong. The "plough-tip" kind does not claim innocence openly but implies it indirectly, explaining the evils of wrongdoing to others so that listeners think them upright.
- **Mindfulness is not memory** (Pradeepika 55). Ordinary remembering is described as a process across many mind-states, not this factor.
- **Faith is defined doctrinally** (Pradeepika 41–51; Margaya 7). It is confidence in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha. Devotion to defiled persons, or harmful acts done eagerly as though good, is "false resolve" (මිථ්‍යාධිමොක්ඛය) or "bewildered devotion", not faith.
- **Balance** (තත්‍රමජ්ඣත්තතා, lines 109–115) is not neutral feeling (line 115).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the list and each definition. Margaya gives one-line glosses and the opposing defilement for each pair (lines 31–51), which matches Pradeepika. Pradeepika adds similes (the gem clearing water, the gatekeeper of six doors, the charioteer, the tanned hide, refined gold) and the occurrence rules. No substantive disagreement was found.

**Internal tension across concepts (not a contradiction within C023):** Pradeepika C023 line 73 counts considering one's high lineage (උසස් කුලගෝත්‍ර) as a support for shame at wrong. Pradeepika C022 line 107 counts lineage-based pride (ජාති මානය) as conceit. The texts do not reconcile these.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Faith as defined here is specific to the Triple Gem and cannot become "belief" or "trust" in general. The "false resolve" examples (including killing animals for care of the sick, line 47) are sectarian judgements. Hell-realm fear (line 77), habits across saṃsāra (line 157), recollection of past lives (line 63) and path factors are doctrinal. The lineage and caste-based reasons for shame (line 73) must not be presented as admirable or used to stereotype any community. The kāya/citta pairs refer to mental factors, not the physical body (line 119). They must not be turned into claims about bodily relaxation.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source describes good action as something that must be actively assembled against a mind that drifts the other way. Three strands have a clear human texture. A person may hold back from harm because of who they take themselves to be, or because of whose eyes are on them, and these two reasons behave differently. Doing good becomes easier with practice, and without it people feel awkward, afraid or embarrassed. And people can talk against what they themselves do, openly or by implication. The cards are interpretation. The source presents these factors as simultaneous in a single mind-state, not as a sequence.

## Idea decisions

- **C023-I01 — Holding back for self-respect or for reputation.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika lines 67–85 and Margaya line 15.
- **C023-I02 — Why doing good feels awkward without practice.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika lines 149 and 157, with Margaya lines 43–47.
- **C023-I03 — Speaking against what you quietly do.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika lines 165–177 and Margaya line 51.
- Rejected candidate: *Giving to someone you dislike* (non-aversion, lines 93, 99–103). It is supported but thin. It could be a supporting reference for a future generosity card.
- Rejected candidate: *Devotion that does harm while feeling good* (lines 47–51). It is doctrinally bound to what counts as the proper object of faith, and it overlaps C006-I02.
- Rejected candidate: tranquillity making good deeds sweet (line 127). It is close to C023-I02 and weakly developed.
- Rejected candidate: balance as a charioteer (lines 109–115), and lightness and malleability. These are too abstract, or defined only as opposites of defilements.
