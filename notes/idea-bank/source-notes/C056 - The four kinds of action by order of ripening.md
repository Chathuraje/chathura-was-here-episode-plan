# C056 — The four kinds of action by order of ripening

## Central meaning

This tetrad ranks which action gives the next rebirth first:

1. **ගරුක (weighty).** Its result cannot be blocked by any other action. Unwholesome: fixed wrong view (නියත මිථ්‍යාදෘෂ්ටිය) and the five immediate-result acts (පඤ්චානන්තරිය), namely killing one's mother, father or an arahant, wounding a Buddha and splitting the Sangha. Wholesome: the fine-material and immaterial absorptions (Pradeepika lines 25–79; Margaya lines 12–18).
2. **ආසන්න (near-death).** An action done, gathered or recalled close to death (Pradeepika lines 83–105; Margaya line 22).
3. **ආචීර්ණ / යද්බහුල (habitual).** An action done repeatedly, or done once and recalled again and again (Pradeepika line 109; Margaya line 26).
4. **කටත්තා / කෘතත්වාත් (done once).** Any remaining action (Pradeepika line 113; Margaya line 30).

Pradeepika line 117 gives the order: weighty, then near-death, then habitual, then done-once. Margaya line 30 notes that these are the same generative actions described in C055.

## Essential distinctions

- **Remorse and repair do not stop a weighty result.** Pradeepika lines 53–59 say that after an immediate-result act, no amount of merit can prevent the next-birth result. The example is Ajatasattu, who killed his father, later heard the teaching, repented, asked forgiveness, became a foremost lay follower and funded the First Council. He is still said to suffer in hell. Margaya line 16 adds that someone with fixed wrong view is freed if they give up the view before death.
- **Near-death recollection.** In Pradeepika lines 91–97, King Dutugemunu is dying. He has the record of his merits read to him. When it reaches the meal he gave away while hiding in the forest after defeat, hungry and with almost nothing, this recollection brings him more joy than any of his great building works, and it becomes the object of his dying mind. Margaya line 22 says a person with many wrongs can reach a good birth by doing or recalling good at death. A person with much merit can fall by recalling wrongs. A good friend who prompts good deeds or reminds the dying person of them is very useful. Pradeepika line 99 gives an executioner who fed a monk shortly before death.
- **Near-death wrong view after long effort.** In Pradeepika lines 101–105, the lay follower Mahā Vācakāla practised meditation for thirty years without gaining even a glimmer. Disheartened, he concluded that the teaching does not lead anywhere, "because a pure teaching would have given fruit by now". He did not consider that his own understanding was immature. This view became his near-death object, and he is said to have been reborn as a huge crocodile.
- **Habit through repetition or recollection.** Pradeepika line 109 says a deed done once but recollected constantly becomes habitual. So does strong joy over a good deed or strong burning remorse (සන්තාපය හෙවත් තැවිල්ල) over a bad one. Margaya line 26 says that when an act is repeated, earlier acts become decisive support (උපනිඃශ්‍රය) for later ones, making them stronger. Acts done only monthly or yearly gain this strength only if they are large enough to be remembered.
- **Weak but near the door.** Pradeepika line 119 gives the simile of an old weak cow that entered the pen last and so leaves first when the gate opens.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Agreement.** Both sources give the same four kinds, the same ordering principle and the same membership of weighty actions.
- **Complementary detail.** Pradeepika gives technical ranking among the five immediate-result acts (lines 61–67), notes which impulsion (ජවන) moment carries them (lines 39, 53, 69) and gives narrative examples. Margaya gives the rule that only a weighty action can block another weighty action (line 18) and ranks habitual action second in strength, although near-death action ripens first (line 26).
- **Disagreement on fixed wrong view.** Pradeepika line 37 says the holder certainly goes to Avīci and gives no exception. Margaya line 16 says the holder is freed if the view is given up before death. This is a genuine difference in what the extracts state. Margaya's printed wording is "දෘෂ්ටිය කළ හොත්" ("if the view is made"), which is probably a text error for "හළ හොත්" ("if the view is abandoned"). The reading "given up" depends on that correction.
- **Disagreement on done-once action.** Margaya line 30 defines it broadly as all remaining action from this and past lives. Pradeepika line 113 defines it as action done once and forgotten, very weak, able to give rebirth only if repeated recollection has strengthened it. That last condition overlaps with Pradeepika's own definition of habitual action at line 109.
- **Wholesome weighty action.** Pradeepika line 79 adds that someone with all the absorptions who wishes for a sense-sphere birth becomes a powerful deity or an excellent human. Margaya line 14 only states the no-wish condition.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The concept's claims concern rebirth destination, hell durations and the crocodile rebirth, and none of these can be premises. The ranking of mother-killing over father-killing by the parents' virtue (Pradeepika line 67) and the story of the arahant flying through the air (line 91) are doctrinal or legendary narrative. Named historical figures (Dutugemunu, Ajatasattu, Mahā Vācakāla) appear in the sources as teaching stories, not as verified documentary subjects. Near-death recollection must not be presented as determining any real outcome after death.

## Human meaning paragraph

Taken as limited analogies, three human processes are visible. First, at the end of life, what a person recalls and how they feel about it may not follow the size of their achievements. A small gift made in hardship can outshine monuments, and other people can shape what is recalled. Second, an act gains weight through repetition, and also through being gone over again and again in memory, whether with joy or with remorse. Third, long effort that brings no visible result can turn into a judgement that the path itself is false, without the person asking whether their own practice fell short. The source also sets a limit on repair: some harms are not undone by later remorse and good works. That limit is used as supporting material for C055-I01.

## Idea decisions

- **C056-I01 — The small gift remembered above great works.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 91–97 and Margaya line 22. The rebirth outcome is excluded.
- **C056-I02 — Replaying one act until it weighs like habit.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika line 109 and Margaya line 26.
- **C056-I03 — Blaming the path after years without results.** Accepted for research. Based on Pradeepika lines 101–105. It must keep open the possibility that the path really was unsuitable.
- Merged into C055-I01 as supporting material rather than a separate card: *Remorse that repairs but cannot undo* (Ajatasattu, Pradeepika lines 55–59; Margaya line 16). It shares the mechanism and question of C055-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *A last-minute good deed after a life of harm* (executioner, Pradeepika line 99; Margaya line 22). Its force depends entirely on the rebirth claim, and as a human story it risks a moral shortcut. The deathbed recollection element is kept in C056-I01.
- Rejected candidate: ranking of the five gravest acts and the parents' relative virtue. This is technical doctrine with no human mechanism.
