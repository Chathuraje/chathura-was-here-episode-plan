## Central meaning

A path thought process (මාර්ග වීථිය) is the single mind-door process in which supramundane path consciousness arises once, taking nibbāna as object, followed immediately by two or three fruition moments. There are four paths (stream-entry, once-return, non-return, arahantship), and a person passes through each only once. Before the path moment come sense-sphere wholesome impulsions called preparation, access and conformity (පරිකර්ම, උපචාර, අනුලෝම), then change-of-lineage (ගෝත්‍රභූ) for the first path, or purification (වෝදාන) for the later three. A person of dull wisdom has a preparation moment and two fruition moments; a person of sharp wisdom has no preparation moment and three fruition moments (seven impulsions in total either way). After each path come reviewing processes (ප්‍රත්‍යවේක්ෂා): of the path, the fruit, the defilements abandoned, the defilements remaining, and nibbāna. The arahant has no remaining defilements to review, giving 19 reviewings in all. Fruition attainment (ඵල සමාපත්ති) is a later, repeatable entry into one's own fruit for as long as one has resolved beforehand.

## Essential distinctions

- **Conformity versus change-of-lineage** (Pradeepika lines 85–91; Book 4 lines 48–56). Conformity knowledge can dispel the darkness covering the truths but cannot take nibbāna as object. Change-of-lineage can take nibbāna as object but cannot dispel that darkness. The three-winds-and-clouds simile illustrates this.
- **Reviewing** (Margaya line 33; Pradeepika lines 112–134, 144–146; Book 4 lines 83–95). After the path, the person reviews what was attained, what was abandoned and what remains. Some learners, or persons of dull wisdom, do not review abandoned and remaining defilements.
- **Similes for the whole insight sequence** (Book 4 lines 107–139). A bat searches all five branches of a tree, finds no flower or fruit, lets go and flies to another tree. A householder wakes to find his house on fire, looks for a way out and escapes. A farmer leads home cattle he thinks are his, realises at dawn they belong to the king, drops them and flees. A man discovers his companion is a flesh-eating spirit and flees. A mother picks up a crying child she thinks is hers, realises it is not, sets it down and goes back upstairs before anyone calls her a child thief.
- **The canal-jump simile** (Pradeepika line 83; Book 4 lines 44–46). The jumper lands trembling and only then stands firm. Book 4 explains the trembling as not yet having gained repetition in an object met only once.
- **Fruition attainment** (Margaya line 37; Pradeepika lines 176–200; Book 4 lines 206–247). Entered in seclusion, with insight into arising and passing. It is kept up by not attending to conditioned signs, attending to the signless, and a prior time limit. One emerges by attending to conditioned signs again.
- **Two kinds of practitioner** (Pradeepika lines 27–49): the dry-insight practitioner (ශුෂ්ක විදර්ශක), who does not use absorption as a base, and the calm-vehicle practitioner (සමථයානික), whose path takes on the factors of the absorption used as a base.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

All three sources agree on the path-process structure, the dull/sharp distinction, the replacement of change-of-lineage by purification in the later paths and the 19 reviewings. Book 4 adds the six similes and the four explanatory frames. Pradeepika Book 1 adds the three gateways to liberation (lines 67–73), the arithmetic of 1,000 and 120,000 "methods" (lines 148–170), and the seven purifications leading up to the path (lines 51–59).

Genuine disagreement or tension:
- **Fruition-attainment process.** Margaya (lines 37–45) shows only conformity moments (four for dull, three for sharp) before the fruit, with no purification moment. Pradeepika Book 1 (lines 188–196) shows preparation, access, conformity and purification. Book 4 (line 224) also names purification.
- **Dry-insight practitioner and fruition attainment.** Pradeepika line 200 argues that since the dry-insight noble person enters fruition attainment, he must have developed absorption before the path. This sits uneasily with the definition at line 35 (reaching the path without absorption as a base). The two may be reconcilable (absorption developed but not used as base), but the text does not say so.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The path moment, nibbāna, the ending of defilements, the "seven more births at most" claim for stream-enterers (Book 4 line 81), rebirth in higher worlds (Book 4 lines 163–192) and the counts of methods are doctrinal claims. The sequence is measured in mind-moments, not in minutes or days, and cannot be presented as a psychological stage model. The similes are teaching devices for insight into impermanence, suffering and non-self. Any human use of them is a limited analogy. The fear in the similes is part of the insight sequence (knowledge of appearance as terror), not an endorsement of fear as a motive in daily life.

## Human meaning paragraph

The similes describe a recognisable human turn. A person holds something as theirs, or searches something for what they want. Then they see it clearly, as not theirs, as dangerous, or as empty of what they were looking for, and the grip releases, sometimes quickly and with alarm. The reviewing processes describe a separate step: after a real change, looking back at what was gained, what has actually gone and what is still there. Both readings go beyond the text. The sources describe a single supramundane event and its aftermath, not ordinary decisions.

## Idea decisions

- **C100-I01 — Realising that what you held was never yours.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the cattle, spirit-companion and child similes (Book 4 lines 125–139), with the burning-house simile as support.
- **C100-I02 — Taking stock of what is gone and what remains.** Accepted. Based on the five reviewings in all three sources, including the explicit review of remaining defilements and the note that some people skip it.
- **C100-I03 — Letting go only after searching every branch.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the bat simile (Book 4 lines 107–111). Its mechanism is disillusion reached by exhaustive checking, which is different from I01's sudden recognition.
- Rejected candidate: *Landing somewhere new before feeling steady* (canal-jump simile). It refers to the gap between two mind-moments, not to practice over time, and the human reading largely duplicates C023-I02 (Why doing good feels awkward without practice).
- Rejected candidate: *Setting a time to come back before withdrawing* (fruition attainment's prior time limit). The C102 preliminary duties support this more fully. See C102-I01.
- Rejected candidate: the three gateways to liberation and the "1,000 methods". These are doctrinal classification with no human mechanism.
