## Central meaning

Kasina meditation fixes the mind on a prepared disc (earth, water, fire, air, four colours, light, space) until an inner image forms. The steps are: purify conduct, cut off obstacles (පළිබෝධ), get a subject suited to one's temperament from a good spiritual friend (කල්‍යාණ මිත්‍ර), live in a suitable place, remove minor obstacles, then make and gaze at the disc while repeating its name. The mental image first becomes a "learning sign" (උද්ග්‍රහ නිමිත්ත), seen with eyes closed as clearly as with eyes open, then a bright, still "counterpart sign" (ප්‍රතිභාග නිමිත්ත), which supports access concentration and then the first absorption. Pradeepika adds the ten skills for absorption (අර්පණා කෞශල්‍ය), analysis of the first jhāna, extending the sign, the other nine kasinas, and the supernormal fruits of each kasina.

## Essential distinctions

- **Balancing faculties** (Pradeepika lines 253–269). Faith must be balanced with wisdom, effort with concentration. Strong faith with weak wisdom gives confidence in the wrong place; strong wisdom with weak faith leads to the side of the crafty (කෙරාටික), "hard to cure like an illness caused by medicine" (261). Strong concentration with weak effort gives idleness; strong effort with weak concentration gives restlessness (263). For concentration practice somewhat stronger faith is useful (265); mindfulness must always be strong (269).
- **Rousing, restraining, gladdening, leaving alone** (lines 273–283). A slack mind needs investigation, effort and joy, not tranquillity (like blowing on dry fuel); an agitated mind needs tranquillity, concentration and equanimity, not more energy (like damp fuel on a fire to put it out). A dull, joyless mind is stirred by reflecting on birth, ageing, sickness and death; a mind running evenly is left alone, like a charioteer who does nothing when the horses go evenly.
- **Overstriving** (line 298). Some, when the counterpart sign appears, tighten effort to reach absorption quickly; restlessness follows and they fail. Others, seeing the fault in excess effort, slide into idleness and also fail. Only balanced effort succeeds.
- **Suitability judged by effect** (lines 223–247). Dwelling, alms village, talk, people, food, weather and posture are "suitable" if the sign arises and holds there and the mind settles, "unsuitable" if not.
- **Archer simile** (line 394). A skilled archer notes how his feet, bow, string and arrow were placed when he hit the hair, and repeats it. So the meditator notes what food, company, lodging and posture were present when absorption came, so as to restore it when lost.
- **Obstacles** (lines 48–91) include a dwelling one is attached to, families, gain and honour, students, building work, travel, relatives (whom a monk must nurse when ill), sickness, study and powers.
- **Margaya's lay method** (lines 9–17). Beside the demanding monastic path, a layperson can set aside even half an hour a day. Impure conduct is not a bar: meditation itself gradually gives strength to keep precepts (15). Any subject is acceptable for ordinary practice; temperament matters for those seeking absorption now (17).
- **Distractions during practice** (Margaya lines 31–37). Stillness brings bodily pains that ease with time; visions, amplified small sounds and lights appear as concentration grows; they are not real and should be ignored, since pursuing them disrupts practice.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on disc making (reddish clay, free of the four colour-faults), seat height and distance, reflecting on the triple gem and radiating loving-kindness first, repeating "pathavi", the eyes-closed test for the learning sign, moving away to develop it, the bright counterpart sign and the arising of the first absorption. Margaya is a practical manual for laypeople today (cement in the clay, a tray ten to eleven inches, one hour daily for months, line 39). Pradeepika is a monastic commentary.

Genuine differences of emphasis: Margaya says conduct is the foundation but meditation still helps those whose conduct is impure (line 15); Pradeepika places purifying conduct first in the sequence (line 28) and says those with heavy action-, defilement- or result-obstructions cannot gain results (line 486). Margaya's lesser practice is a lay path with no absorption expected; Pradeepika addresses only the full path.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The supernormal powers from each kasina (lines 464–484), past-life predisposition to seeing the sign (207, 418), the nāma-kāya continuing into future lives (Margaya 11–13), the claim that one person in tens of thousands can follow the full path (Margaya 9), protective deities (Pradeepika 97), cognitive-process mechanics of absorption (300–304) and bright-sign phenomenology are doctrinal. Line 486 lists intersex and "paṇḍaka" persons among those unable to benefit; this must never inform any card. Margaya's warnings about visions and sounds must not be used to interpret anyone's perceptual experiences clinically.

## Human meaning paragraph

Settling attention, in these sources, is a matter of balance and of noticing conditions. Pushing harder at the moment success seems near can produce the agitation that prevents it, while backing off from that mistake can tip into slackness. Trust without discernment misplaces itself, and cleverness without trust becomes slippery and hard to correct. And because places, company, food and posture help or hinder differently for each person, the practical skill is to notice what was present when things went well and recreate it. Applying these to non-meditative work is interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C087-I01 — When pushing harder keeps the goal away.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on lines 263, 273–283 and 298.
- **C087-I02 — Trust without judgement, cleverness without trust.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on lines 261 and 265.
- **C087-I03 — Recreating the conditions of a past success.** Accepted. Based on the archer simile (394) and the effect-based suitability tests (223–247).
- Rejected: *Half an hour for the inner life* (Margaya 9–13). Duplicates C001-I03.
- Rejected: *Not chasing the visions that come with stillness* (Margaya 35–37). Too close to C016-I01 and risks clinical misreading.
- Rejected: *Practising before one's conduct is clean* (Margaya 15). A real tension (doing a good practice before feeling worthy of it), but a single sentence of support and close to C055-I01; recorded as a held question.
- Rejected: *Leaving practice to nurse a sick parent* (Pradeepika 81). Monastic rule with thin human elaboration; overlaps C001-I03.
- Rejected: stillness pain that eases (Margaya 33). Physiological claim, too thin.
- Rejected: kasina fruits, obstructions and jhāna analysis. Doctrinal and technical.
