# C042 — Charting the cognitive process

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–39). Frontmatter declares PDF 325–325 (printed 315–315). The inclusion note (line 12) says the extract was cut from the middle of PDF 324–325. It consists only of a table of abbreviations (lines 23–39). There are no further page markers.
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–40). Heading `# 5. වීථි සටහන්`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

The concept gives a notation for charting cognitive processes. Each mind-moment is drawn as three dots (arising, standing and dissolving) with a letter above or below it (Margaya line 3). Pradeepika gives the full abbreviation table, including the mind-door, absorption, path, fruit, death and rebirth moments (lines 25–39). Margaya charts the very-great-object eye-door process (lines 25–30). It then explains what each stage contributes (line 34), states that each moment knows nothing beyond its own function (line 36), and gives the mango simile (lines 38–40).

## Essential distinctions

- **Seeing is not yet complete knowing** (Margaya line 34). Eye-consciousness sees only colour (වර්ණ මාත්‍රය) and ceases, so knowledge of the object is not complete. Receiving, investigating and determining follow, and the impulsion moments "take the object well". "Seeing is completed by impulsion."
- **Registration is of little use** (Margaya line 34). Registration moments arise only for sense-sphere beings. Brahmās see perfectly without them.
- **Mango simile** (Margaya lines 38–40). A man sleeps under a mango tree, and a ripe mango falls near his ear. He wakes (adverting), opens his eyes and looks (eye-consciousness), and picks it up (receiving). He squeezes it to see if it is ripe (investigating) and smells it to be sure (determining). He eats it (impulsion), swallows what is left in his mouth with his saliva (registration) and sleeps again (life-continuum).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources complement each other. Pradeepika gives a longer table covering every process type. Margaya gives a shorter table only for the eye-door process, plus the explanation and simile. The abbreviations differ. Pradeepika uses `ති` for past life-continuum, `පං` for five-door adverting and `ප` for preparation (පරිකර්මය). Margaya uses `තී` and `ප` for five-door adverting. Pradeepika also assigns `වො` both to determining (වොට්ඨපනය, line 39) and to cleansing (වොදනය, line 34), which is ambiguous.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The simile illustrates events said to take place within a fraction of a moment. The man's slow, deliberate checking is the vehicle of the simile, not the teaching. The claims about Brahmās and the seventeen-moment lifespan are doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source's point, carefully put, is that contact with an object does not deliver knowledge of it all at once. The first registration is thin, only colour. What the thing is and what to do with it are settled through further stages before full engagement happens, and each stage "knows" only its own part. The idea card extends this, as a limited analogy, to the everyday gap between a first glance and actually knowing what one has seen. That extension goes beyond the text's time-scale.
