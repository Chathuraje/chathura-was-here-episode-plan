## Central meaning

Margaya (lines 3–5) says a cognitive process cannot be produced by a person's own effort or by any god's power. It arises only when an object strikes a sense-sensitivity, as a drum sounds when struck or a match flames when rubbed. No one directs the order of the minds. Each mind arises through the force of the one before. Margaya explains this with the five natural orders (නියාම): seed, season, kamma, dhamma and mind (lines 9–25).

Pradeepika explains in detail how the contact reaches the life-continuum at the heart-base, using the two-faced drum and fly simile (line 41) and the bird's shadow (line 45). It describes each stage and gives the blind man carrying a lame man simile for the eye-sensitivity and eye-consciousness (line 57). It then states how the ethical quality of the impulsion is set (lines 77–83):
- If determining settles on a pleasing object as "worth clinging to" through unwise attention (අයොනිසො මනසිකාර), one of the eight greed-rooted impulsions arises.
- If it settles on an unpleasant object as "worth being angry at", one of the two hatred-rooted impulsions arises.
- If it is confused, one of the two delusion-rooted impulsions arises.
- If it settles with wisdom, or with confidence before a supremely desirable object, a wholesome impulsion arises (functional, for an arahant).

Part 2 sets out four regularities (නියම) for absorption, result, registration and impulsion.

## Essential distinctions

- **Result versus response.** The seeing, receiving and investigating moments are *results* (විපාක). Whether they are wholesome-result or unwholesome-result depends on the object being desirable or undesirable (Pradeepika lines 55, 65–67, 190–229; Margaya C045 lines 28–32). The impulsion is where wholesome or unwholesome quality and "kamma" arise (Pradeepika lines 83–85).
- **Object quality does not fix the response.** Worldlings with wrong views can have displeasure-accompanied impulsions even toward a supremely desirable object such as the Buddha, because of distortions of perception, mind and view (විපල්ලාස) (Pradeepika line 254). Margaya C045 (lines 30–34), consulted for comparison, adds that whether an object is good or bad is hard to decide. The commentary uses the view of ordinary middle people as the standard, and impulsion arises according to a person's disposition.
- **Impulsion count.** Seven is normal. Five or six occur when the heart-base is weak: severe illness, near death, falling, drowning, great fear, fainting, birth (line 292). Doctrinal physiology.
- Five-door impulsions do not become full courses of action. Only the fourth, "meaning-establishing" mind-door process does, and its seven volitions give results in this life, the next life or later lives (lines 85–89). Doctrinal.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree that the process is conditioned and has no controller. Margaya frames this with the five natural orders. Pradeepika frames it with detailed conditions for eye-consciousness (intact sensitivity, object, the preceding adverting as proximity condition; line 59).

**Interpretive difference to note:** Pradeepika places unwise or wise attention in the *determining* moment (lines 77–83). Margaya (C041 line 7; C042 line 34) presents determining only as judging the object's nature "as investigated", and places the variable in the impulsion and the person's disposition (C045 line 34). This is recorded as a difference in emphasis, not resolved.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The following are doctrinal: the life-continuum's object from the previous life (line 29), the heart-base, the kamma-result account of why pleasant or unpleasant objects are met (line 192), the multi-life fruition of volitions (line 89), the absorption and path tables (lines 93–186), the arahant's functional minds, and the twin miracle and supernormal powers (lines 294–312). The seed and season orders are traditional nature observations (for example, chickpea tops turning north) and are not botanical findings.

## Human meaning paragraph

The passage separates two things that people often blur. What reaches the senses, pleasant or unpleasant, arrives without being chosen or commanded. The response, whether clinging, irritation, confusion or clear appreciation, depends on how the object is taken up: with careless or careful attention, and according to the person's settled disposition and views. As an interpretation, one person can meet the same thing with delight and another with resentment, and neither reaction is fixed by the thing itself. The source frames this ethically (the response is where action begins), not as a matter of taste.

## Idea decisions

- **C043-I01 — When careless attention decides what a sight becomes.** Accepted for research. It is based on Pradeepika lines 65–83 and 254, with Margaya C045 lines 30–34 as comparison. The card is deliberately narrowed to the quality of attention, because the broader idea that responses come after arrival already exists as C009-I02 and C034-I02.
- Rejected candidate: *No one is steering the moment of seeing* (no controller; natural orders). It is closely tied to a doctrinal not-self argument. Its usable human content is already carded as C040-I02 (*Responses that unfold without anyone deciding*, same mango and natural-order material from C040) and C034-I02.
- Rejected candidate: *Shock and fear shorten the mind's response* (fewer impulsions in drowning, falling, fear). This is a doctrinal physiological claim that cannot be carried as a human mechanism.
- Rejected: absorption, registration and supernormal-power regularities. These are technical or doctrinal.
