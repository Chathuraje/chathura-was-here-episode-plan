## Central meaning

The 27 sublime (මහග්ගත) and 40 supramundane mind-states are counted by absorption level (ධ්‍යාන). The absorption factors fall away in stages (Pradeepika line 35). The first absorption has applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, happiness and one-pointedness. The second drops applied thought. The third drops sustained thought. The fourth drops rapture. The fifth replaces happiness with equanimity. Each level has one fewer factor.

- **Supramundane states:** 36/35/34/33/33.
- **Sublime states:** 35/34/33/32/30.

Sublime states lack the abstinences. Supramundane states lack compassion and appreciative joy. The fifth absorption lacks compassion and appreciative joy as well.

## Essential distinctions and reasons given

- **No abstinences in sublime states.** Pradeepika (line 75): absorption is the fruit of meditative merit, whereas abstinences arise in moral-conduct merit. Margaya (line 15): these states arise with meditation devices (කසිණ) as object.
- **No compassion or appreciative joy in supramundane states.** Margaya (line 15) says their object is nibbāna, while compassion and appreciative joy take beings as object. Pradeepika states the absence without giving a reason (line 41).
- **No compassion or appreciative joy in the fifth absorption.** Pradeepika (line 97) says states with equanimity cannot take happy or suffering beings as object.
- Pradeepika (line 37) says moral conduct, concentration and wisdom are all strong in supramundane states. Margaya (line 17) says the largest number of simultaneous factors (36) is in first-absorption supramundane states. It also says sense-sphere wholesome states can have at most 34 at once, despite the nominal 38.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree. The two sources give different but compatible reasons for the absent abstinences (type of merit versus type of object). **Tension to hold:** Pradeepika line 97 says neutral-feeling states cannot take happy or suffering beings as object. Yet both sources include compassion and appreciative joy in neutral-feeling sense-sphere wholesome and functional states (C030 Pradeepika lines 63, 100; C030 Margaya lines 5, 9). Either the rule is meant only for absorption, or the sources are inconsistent. Neither text resolves this.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Absorption attainments, supramundane path and fruit, and nibbāna as object are doctrinal and meditative claims. The staged dropping of factors is listed, not explained. This source does not say how or why a meditator lets go of each factor.

## Human meaning paragraph

The passage describes, in outline, a deepening concentration. It becomes quieter as thinking, excitement and even pleasure fall away, until only balance and unification remain. But the source is a count table. It gives no account of the experience or the method. Turning it into a human mechanism would mean supplying content from elsewhere.

## Idea decisions

- No suitable idea.
- Rejected candidate: *Settling by letting go of even pleasant excitement*. The stages are only listed. The how and why belong to the meditation concepts C087–C088 (kasiṇa meditation, second and higher absorptions), where the reasons may be given.
- Rejected candidate: *Compassion cannot arise from pure neutrality*. It is contradicted by both sources' treatment of neutral-feeling sense-sphere states. Record it as a held question, not a card.
