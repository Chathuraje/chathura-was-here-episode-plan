## Central meaning

This is the method of association (සම්ප්‍රයෝග නය): for each of the 13 ethically variable factors (අඤ්ඤසමාන), which of the 89 (or 121) types of consciousness it appears in. The seven universals are in all consciousness. Of the six occasionals: විතක්ක in 55, විචාර in 66, අධිමොක්ඛ in 78, විරිය in 73, පීති in 51, ඡන්ද in 69 (Pradeepika lines 45–87; Margaya lines 5–15).

## Essential distinctions

- The ten sense consciousnesses (ද්විපඤ්ච විඤ්ඤාණ) are "weak" and have only the seven universals (Pradeepika line 59; Margaya line 21).
- Initial application is absent in second and higher jhāna because it has been removed by meditative development (Margaya lines 19, 21).
- Decision (අධිමොක්ඛ) and doubt (විචිකිච්ඡා) are mutually opposed, so decision is absent from the doubt-accompanied state (Pradeepika line 69; Margaya line 23).
- Rapture occurs only with joyful feeling (Pradeepika line 79; Margaya line 25).
- Wish-to-act (ඡන්ද) is absent from rootless states and from the two delusion-rooted states, because delusion brings agitation and indecision in which no wish arises (Pradeepika line 87; Margaya line 25).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree between sources. Pradeepika gives more reasoning (e.g. line 75 on why effort is absent in the sixteen rootless states, "by the fixed order of mind"); Margaya adds memorisation advice (line 27). No genuine disagreement found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The counts depend on the whole 89/121 classification including meditative absorptions and supramundane states, which are doctrinal. "Weak consciousness" is a technical property, not a claim about attention strength that can be measured.

## Human meaning paragraph

The only human-facing threads are that firm decision cannot coexist with doubt in the same moment, and that restless confusion leaves no room for a genuine wish to act. Both are stated as one-line reasons for a table entry, with no example or further explanation, so any card would be mostly my own psychology.
