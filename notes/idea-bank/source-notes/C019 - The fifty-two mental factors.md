## Central meaning

Mental factors (චෛතසික) are the second of the four ultimate realities. A mind-state (සිත) never arises without them, and they never arise without a mind-state (Pradeepika line 29; Margaya line 11). The mind is primary and the factors secondary. Whatever the mind does is done through the strength of its factors, and a mind is strong or weak as its factors are (Pradeepika line 29). Four marks of association define a factor: it arises together with the mind, ceases together with it, takes the same object, and depends on the same physical base (Pradeepika lines 33–47; Margaya lines 3–17). Margaya adds a division of labour: taking the object is the mind's only work, while tasting it, liking it or opposing it belong to the factors (line 15).

There are fifty-two factors in three groups: 13 “other-common” (අඤ්ඤසමාන), 14 unwholesome (අකුසල) and 25 beautiful (සොභන). The first group splits into 7 universal factors and 6 occasional factors (Pradeepika lines 55–65 and the lists at lines 75–166; Margaya line 19). Between 7 and 36 factors arise with any single mind-state (Pradeepika line 49; Margaya line 9).

## Essential distinctions

- **Ethically variable factors.** The 13 other-common factors become unwholesome when joined with an unwholesome mind and wholesome when joined with a wholesome mind, and are neutral in resultant and functional states (Pradeepika lines 61–63). Pradeepika's simile: they are like a crafty (කෙරාටික) friend skilled at fitting in, who kills and steals when keeping company with wrongdoers and gives and makes merit when keeping company with givers (line 63).
- **Fixed groups.** Unwholesome factors arise only in unwholesome states and obstruct the wholesome (line 67). Beautiful factors arise only in beautiful states (line 71).
- **Opposed factors.** Margaya notes that some factors are opposed like light and darkness and never arise together, so all 52 can never occur at once (line 7). Only the factors needed for a given mind-state's task arise with it.
- **Simultaneity.** Margaya uses a timber image: the sapwood does not decay first while the heartwood remains. All the factors of one mind-moment cease together (line 13). This is not a sequence.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the four marks, the count of 52, the three groups and the range of 7 to 36 factors. Pradeepika adds the crafty-friend simile and the strength claim. Margaya adds the division of labour between mind and factors, the opposed factors and the timber simile. No genuine disagreement was found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The physical base (හෘදයවස්තු, the heart-base) and sense-organ bases are doctrinal physiology and must not be presented as anatomy. The framework analyses momentary mind-states, not personality traits, so “wholesome” and “unwholesome” here describe a moment's quality, not a person. Most of this concept is a classification list with no human mechanism of its own. The individual factors are explained in C020 onward.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source treats capacities such as attention, effort, interest and decisiveness as parts of every mental act that have no moral character of their own. They take their character from the aim they are joined with in that moment. The same energy that drives a harmful act can drive a generous one. This is my interpretation in everyday terms. The source speaks of factors in a single mind-moment, not of skills or habits across a life, so any everyday reading is a limited analogy.

## Idea decisions

- **C019-I01 — Abilities that take the colour of their aim.** Held pending a merge decision. The interpretation passed review as a limited analogy, but parallel batches produced C075-I01 (When focus and effort serve a harmful aim) and C011-I01 (Knowledge that serves harm as easily as good) with the same central mechanism. C019 is where the variable-quality principle is first defined, so the coordinator should decide which card absorbs the other. Based on the ethically variable factors and the crafty-friend simile (Pradeepika lines 61–63), supported by C020 (Pradeepika line 111) and C021 (Pradeepika lines 25, 73–75).
- Rejected candidate: *Choosing your company changes what you do.* The crafty-friend simile is only an illustration of how factors take on the character of the mind-state. Turning it into a teaching about peer influence would change its meaning.
- Rejected candidate: *Some states of mind cannot coexist* (light and darkness, Margaya line 7). The source states it without explaining which factors or why. That detail belongs to later concepts.
- Rejected: the four marks of association and the group lists. They are technical classification with no human mechanism.
