# C074 — The collection of unwholesome states

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–101). Heading `# 1. අකුශල සංග්‍ර‍හය`, with a lesson marker `**2 වන පාඩම**` at line 67. No page locators.

## Central meaning

The unwholesome collection groups the fourteen unwholesome mental factors under nine overlapping headings: the four taints (ආශ්‍රව), floods (ඕඝ), yokes (යෝග), ties (ග්‍රන්ථ), four clingings (උපාදාන), six hindrances (නීවරණ), seven latent tendencies (අනුසය), ten fetters (සංයෝජන) and ten defilements (ක්ලේශ). Each heading names the same few factors (mainly greed, wrong view, delusion, hatred, conceit, doubt, restlessness) by a different image of what they do: intoxicate and leak out, sweep away, yoke, knot, grip, obstruct, lie dormant, bind, burn and stain. Pradeepika's closing chart (lines 721–734, PDF 196 / printed 185) shows which factor appears under which heading.

## Essential distinctions

- **Three stages of a defilement** (Pradeepika lines 486–498, PDF 181–182 / printed 170–171; Margaya lines 71–79). A latent stage in which the tendency is not active but can arise when a suitable object appears. An "arisen" stage (පරියුට්ඨාන) in which it occupies the mind. A "transgression" stage (ව්‍යතික්‍රම) in which it moves body and speech. Margaya (line 79) adds that an arisen tendency sometimes subsides there and sometimes goes on into action. It also says a forest ascetic with deep meditation can fall from it on meeting certain objects, because the tendency lay unnoticed (line 75).
- **Latent tendencies inside good acts.** Pradeepika says latent sensual desire rises unnoticed even while a person gives or keeps precepts, so that merit is dedicated to future pleasures (line 502). Latent conceit makes a person, after watching others' merit-making, want to arrange their own to be grander (line 518). Conceit is based on wealth, family, virtue or knowledge and takes the forms "better", "equal" and "inferior".
- **Restlessness and remorse** (උද්ධච්ච කුක්කුච්ච). Restlessness is a mind that cannot settle, like wind-rippled water, a flag in wind or scattered ash (line 356). Remorse is sorrow over wrongs done and good left undone, with regret as its function (line 358). Both are said to arise from thinking about past misfortune (Pradeepika line 360) or the misfortune of relatives (Margaya line 62). Calm opposes both. Aids to removal include much learning, questioning, good discipline, association with elders, good friends and suitable conversation (lines 370–380).
- **Sloth and torpor** (ථීනමිද්ධ): a shrinking, unworkable mind, like a feather curling in fire (line 316). Aids include moderate eating, changing posture, perceiving light, the open air, good friends (lines 328–340). Margaya gives laziness and bodily inactivity as causes (line 62).
- **Hatred's nine grounds** (Pradeepika lines 276–289): "he harmed me, is harming me, will harm me", the same for a loved one, and "he helped, helps, will help my enemy".
- **Envy and stinginess** appear only in the Abhidhamma list of fetters. Envy is not tolerating others' prosperity. Stinginess concerns dwelling, family, gain, praise and teaching (lines 644–648).
- **Cleverness in harm.** Pradeepika (line 434, PDF 179 / printed 168) says skilled worldly people who show ingenuity in killing and similar acts are displaying delusion wearing the face of wisdom, not wisdom.
- **Craving versus clinging.** Pradeepika (line 230) says desire to obtain an object is craving, while greed for an object already obtained is clinging. The snake seizing a frog is the image of clinging (line 177).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the nine headings, their members and their reduction to underlying factors. Pradeepika gives longer canonical definitions, similes, removal aids and the stage of the path at which each is cut off. Margaya is more compact and adds that an arisen tendency may subside without becoming action (line 79), and that latent tendencies are neither strictly ultimate realities nor outside them.

Differences to record, not resolve:
- **Fetter counts.** Margaya (line 90) reduces the fetters to nine factors by combining both lists. Pradeepika reduces the Suttanta list to seven (line 599) and the Abhidhamma list to eight (line 650). Its chart and line 683 give nine for the union. This is a difference in counting method, not in content.
- **Cause of restlessness and remorse.** Pradeepika says past misfortunes (line 360). Margaya says relatives' misfortunes and similar (line 62). The details complement each other.
- **Cause of sloth and torpor.** Pradeepika line 322 reads "කය නැමෙන බවය" (body bending), while line 324 reads "කය නොතැමෙන බවය" (body not bending) and Margaya line 62 reads "කය නො සොල්වන බව" (not moving the body). See suspected text errors in the batch report.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Most of this concept is doctrinal. That includes path-stage eliminations, rebirth in the four hells or the Brahma worlds, the "1,500 defilements" count (Margaya line 101), the description of rebirth-linked cognitive processes (Pradeepika lines 673–677) and the claim that the life-continuum mind is pure. The wrong views about rituals (සීලබ්බත පරාමාස) describe other religious practices ("cow conduct", "dog conduct"). They must not become an idea, because doing so would stereotype other traditions. Pradeepika's remark that doubt arises from reasoning through modern sciences (lines 522–526) is a doctrinal and apologetic position and must not be treated as a finding. The removal aids are monastic practice and should not be turned into self-help advice.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe unwholesome tendencies as present even when nothing shows. They wait until an object or situation calls them up, then take over thought, and only sometimes go on into speech and action. They can surface inside acts that look entirely good, such as giving, where a wish to outdo others or to gain future pleasure can slip in unnoticed. Separately, the mind can be shaken by replaying past misfortunes and by regret over what was done and left undone, and this unsettledness is treated as blocking calm rather than as a moral failing in itself. The reading below is interpretation: these are ordinary human processes of trigger, escalation, comparison and brooding. The source's framing in terms of rebirth and liberation is not carried over.

## Idea decisions

- **C074-I01 — A dormant urge that waits for its trigger.** Accepted. Based on the three stages of a defilement in both sources.
- **C074-I02 — Brooding on what was done and left undone.** Accepted. Based on the definitions, causes and opposites of restlessness and remorse in both sources.
- **C074-I03 — When giving turns into outdoing others.** Accepted as a limited application. Based on Pradeepika's example of latent conceit (line 518), with envy (line 644) as context.
- Merged as supporting material: *cleverness in harm is not wisdom* (Pradeepika line 434). This supports C075-I01, which covers the same mechanism of capacities serving harm. It is not a separate card.
- Rejected candidate: *wanting to get versus holding what you have* (line 230). It rests on a single sentence, and it overlaps C005-I02 (love that is also holding on).
- Rejected candidate: sloth and its remedies (eating less, changing posture, light). This would become generic advice, and the remedies are monastic.
- Rejected candidate: doubt as a hindrance. The source's doubt concerns the Buddha, rebirth and the teaching. Its distinction from ordinary doubt about unknown matters (line 404) is interesting, but the passage is apologetic (lines 522–526).
- Rejected candidate: clinging to rituals as the path. This would stereotype other religious communities.
- Rejected candidate: hatred's nine grounds. The list is vivid, but a grudge card would largely duplicate expected hatred-rooted concepts from Chapter 1. It is flagged for the coordinator as a possible supporting source rather than a new card.
