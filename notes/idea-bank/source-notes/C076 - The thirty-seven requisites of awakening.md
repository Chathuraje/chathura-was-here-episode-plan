## Central meaning

"Bodhi" is the wisdom of the four supramundane path moments that penetrates the four noble truths (Pradeepika line 18; Margaya line 5). The states that support it are the requisites of awakening (බෝධිපාක්ෂික ධර්ම): four foundations of mindfulness, four right efforts, four bases of success, five faculties, five powers, seven awakening factors and eight path factors, 37 in all. By underlying nature they reduce to 14 mental factors; energy appears in nine places, mindfulness in eight, wisdom in five, concentration in four, faith in two, and nine others once (Pradeepika lines 1443–1490; Margaya lines 109–111). All 37 arise together only in the path moment, directed at nibbāna. Before that, in mundane practice, they arise separately in different moments (Pradeepika line 37, line 1467; Margaya line 113).

Most of the Pradeepika text is practical meditation instruction under the four foundations of mindfulness. It covers the 32 body parts and how to learn and attend to them (lines 171–362), nine kinds of feeling (364–431), sixteen kinds of mind (433–491), and contemplation of hindrances, aggregates, sense bases, awakening factors and truths (495–889). Each hindrance and awakening factor is given the conditions that produce or remove it. The later sections define right effort, with narratives about losing attainments (891–1097), the bases of success (1099–1186), faculties, powers, awakening factors and path factors.

## Essential distinctions

- **Balancing, not maximising.** The meditator should attend now to the sign of concentration, now to exertion, now to equanimity. Only concentration brings laziness, only exertion brings restlessness, only equanimity keeps the mind from settling. The goldsmith simile says constant blowing burns the gold, constant sprinkling cools it and constant watching leaves it unrefined (lines 276–292, PDF 245–246 / printed 234–235). Six conditions: restrain the mind when it needs restraint, exert when exertion is needed, gladden it when it needs gladdening, and look on without interfering when it runs evenly (lines 294–308). When the mind is slack, rouse it with investigation, energy and joy. When it is over-excited, calm it with tranquillity, concentration and equanimity. When it runs evenly, do nothing, like a charioteer with even horses (lines 775–783, PDF 282–283 / printed 271–272; lines 1300–1304). Mindfulness is needed everywhere, like salt in every curry (lines 673, 1306).
- **Balancing the faculties** (lines 645–671, PDF 273–275 / printed 262–264). Strong faith with weak wisdom makes a person trust the unworthy. Strong wisdom with weak faith makes a person crafty (කෛරාටික), incurable like an illness caused by medicine, and leads them to think that merely intending good is merit, so they do not give. Strong concentration with weak energy leads to laziness, strong energy with weak concentration to restlessness.
- **Conditions for energy** (lines 693–729). They include reflecting on the dignity of alms food: donors are not relatives or servants and give from their own need, so a lazy recipient does not honour it (line 705). The story of the elder Mahāmitta follows (lines 707–711). His supporter sends him fine rice, milk, ghee and honey while she plans to eat sour gruel of broken rice and boiled leaves. He decides he cannot eat it with a defiled mind, returns to his cave and strives.
- **Occupation that keeps defilements from arising** (lines 990–1014, PDF 303–305 / printed 292–294). Duties, study, ascetic practice, meditative mastery, insight and building work keep defilements from getting an opening. When the duty lapses, study stops or the building is finished, they arise through careless attention and heedlessness. The same point is made by stories of a novice who loses his powers on hearing a woman's song (lines 948–972) and a former monk turned labourer who warns younger monks (lines 978–986).
- **Four ministers' sons** (lines 1168–1182). They reach office through service, courage, birth or skill, like practitioners who lead with desire, energy, mind or investigation.
- **Hindrances and their removal** (lines 507–577). For each hindrance the text gives its cause (careless attention to a sign), its remover (careful attention) and six supports. For sloth these are noticing how much food brings drowsiness, changing posture, perceiving light, open air, good friends and suitable talk (line 555).
- **Equanimity** (lines 797–819). Reflecting that a robe has no owner and will wear out to a doormat. Avoiding people possessive of pupils or requisites, such as a teacher who searches "like a startled deer" when a pupil is out of sight and will not lend him out (line 809).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- **Agreement.** Both sources give the seven groups and 37 members, the reduction to 14 factors with the same place-counts, the definition of bodhi as path wisdom, and the claim that all arise together only at the path moment (Margaya lines 5, 109–113; Pradeepika lines 18–37, 1443–1467).
- **Complementary.** Margaya is a compact definitional summary. Pradeepika supplies the practice instructions, similes and narratives. Margaya adds a gloss not found in Pradeepika: each awakening factor removes a specific trouble, such as shrinking, shaking, burning or unrest (lines 86–100). Its account of right effort is also more personal: not repeating wrongs one has done, not starting wrongs learned from seeing others or from books, and securing virtues a worldling can lose (lines 39–45). Pradeepika treats right effort through technical types of "arisen" defilement (lines 912–1063).
- **Possible disagreements, recorded not resolved.** (1) At the fruition moment Pradeepika says 33 are present, without the four right efforts (line 37). Margaya says all are "generally" found in all supramundane minds (line 113). (2) In mundane practice Pradeepika says only one can be had at a time (line 1467). Margaya says not all arise at once, "as suitable" (line 113). (3) Margaya defines the success (iddhi) of the bases of success as jhāna, higher knowledge, path and fruit (line 57). Pradeepika says the four bases arise in supramundane minds taking nibbāna (line 1186). (4) Naming differs for the same 14 factors: Margaya lists විතර්කය and තත්‍රමජ්ඣත්තතාවය (line 109), Pradeepika lists සම්මා සඞ්කප්ප and උපෙක්ඛා (line 1445).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The goal (path knowledge, nibbāna, arahantship) and its stages are doctrinal. So are the rebirth claims (past-life habits shaping which meditation object appears, lines 328–332; the fear of hell and animal births as a spur to energy, line 695; the crafty person reborn in hell, line 657) and the supernormal powers in the narratives (lines 948–986). Contemplating the body as repulsive (lines 183–362) and seeing all beings as heaps of parts (line 320) are specialised monastic practices. They must not become premises about how people should view bodies. "Faith" in the balancing passage is faith in the Buddha, teaching and community. Widening it to trust in general is interpretation. The instructions to avoid lazy, restless or unwise people are monastic advice and are not grounds for judging real people.

## Human meaning paragraph

Much of this concept is technical meditation instruction. Two practical teachings run through it and can be carried, with care, into ordinary experience. The first is that good qualities work in pairs and need adjusting over time. Pushing, calming and leaving alone each have their moment, and any one done constantly spoils the work. Trust without judgement and judgement without trust each fail in their own way. The second is that a person's effort can be moved by what others have given up for them: someone who knows a supporter goes without so that they can eat well may feel they must be worth it. The everyday readings below are interpretation. The source's aim is liberation, and its own examples are monks and meditators.

## Idea decisions

- **C076-I01 — Wanting to be worth what others give up.** Accepted for research. Based on the reflection on alms food and the Mahāmitta story (lines 705–711).
- **C076-I02 — Knowing when to push and when to leave alone.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the goldsmith simile, the six conditions, the charioteer simile and the pairing of factors against slackness and agitation. It overlaps C013-I03 (*Answering each inner obstacle with its opposite*) but adds timing and non-intervention. The two are linked, not merged.
- **C076-I03 — When trust outruns judgement, or the reverse.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the balancing of faith and wisdom (lines 655–659).
- Rejected candidate: *old urges returning when a routine or project ends* (lines 990–1014). This is the same mechanism as existing C013-I02 (*Calm that lasts only while it is kept up*). C076 is recorded as possible supporting material for that card (a building project finishing, study stopping) and does not get a duplicate card.
- Rejected candidate: *leaning on different strengths to reach the same goal* (four ministers' sons, lines 1168–1182). It duplicates C075-I02 (*Which inner drive leads a hard undertaking*).
- Rejected candidate: *possessive care of a protégé* (teacher searching like a startled deer, line 809). It is vivid, but close to C005-I02 (*Love that is also holding on*), and the source uses it only as an example of a person to avoid. Kept as a held question.
- Rejected candidate: *pain that has no single place* (the sick elder, line 395). The narrative ends in arahantship and final passing. The mechanism, attending to feeling rather than a body location, is too tied to insight practice.
- Rejected candidate: *noticing a neutral feeling only by inference* (hunter tracking a deer over rock, line 423). It is a perceptual nicety with little human tension, and it partly overlaps C040-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *one pleasant sight branching into pride, envy and hoarding* (line 603). It overlaps C026-I01, C028-I02 and C074-I03.
- Rejected candidate: *letting go of the marker once the path is worn* (well simile, line 260). The image is attractive but it is a single simile about dropping a meditation label and is too thin for a card.
- Rejected candidate: *choosing company that steadies or scatters you* (lines 621–623, 675–677, 723–725, 755–759, 787–789, 809–815). It is repeated but given as monastic advice without mechanism, and it risks labelling people.
