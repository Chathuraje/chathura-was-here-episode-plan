## Central meaning

"Power" (සෘද්ධිය / ඉද්ධි) means accomplishing an intended result, including by skilful means (Book 4 line 24). There are ten kinds: resolve, transformation, mind-made body, the pervasion of knowledge, the pervasion of concentration, the "noble" power, powers born of kamma, powers of the meritorious, powers from spells and lore, and success through right application. The concept then details the three attained by higher knowledge: multiplying oneself, appearing and vanishing, passing through walls, and so on. Each is produced by entering the base absorption, preparing, re-entering and resolving.

## Essential distinctions

- **Noble power** (ආර්ය සෘද්ධිය; Book 4 lines 85–87, printed as "ආයි"; Margaya line 13). One can dwell perceiving the repulsive as not repulsive (by extending loving-kindness or reflecting on elements), the attractive as repulsive (by reflecting on unattractiveness or impermanence), or stay even toward both with six-factor equanimity. The sources attribute this to arahants only.
- **Success through right application** (Book 4 lines 121–127; Margaya line 21). Accomplishment through methodical practice, including abandoning defilements. Book 4 extends it to arithmetic, music, medicine, learning the Vedas or Tipitaka, and even ploughing and sowing.
- **The four bases of power** (Book 4 lines 157–179): desire-to-act, effort, mind and investigation, each joined with concentration. Neither desire nor concentration alone can produce the power (line 179).
- **Preconditions and difficulty** (Pradeepika.md lines 25–46). Fourteen ways of taming the mind. Each stage is "very difficult", and quick entry into absorption is the hardest.
- **Stories.** Most examples are miracle narratives: Bakkula in the fish, Sañjīva unburnt, Uttara and the hot oil, Samavati and the king's arrow, Cūḷapanthaka's thousand copies, Moggallana and Nandopananda, the descent from Tavatimsa. Cūḷapanthaka's story (lines 225–263) also contains a non-miraculous core. He could not memorise a verse in four months, was sent away as incapable, was told that failing to recite does not make one incapable, and came to insight while rubbing a clean cloth and seeing it soiled by his own hand's sweat.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

- The three sources agree that only resolve, transformation and mind-made powers come from higher knowledge (Margaya line 9; Book 4 line 129).
- **Sañjīva.** Margaya line 11 says he was a disciple of Kakusandha Buddha and that "people" burned him, thinking him dead. Book 4 line 69 says a group of cowherds did so and attributes his safety to successive attainments. The details are complementary.
- **Base absorption.** Pradeepika.md (Book 1) line 46 names the fifth absorption (fivefold count). Book 4 line 221 names the fourth (fourfold count). This is the same state under different counting.
- **Preparation.** Book 1 lists eight kasinas, from earth to white (line 25). Book 4 also uses the space and water kasinas for specific feats (lines 347, 353). The detail is complementary.
- Margaya adds a claim that Book 4 does not state in these words: a person destined for arahantship in this life cannot die before attaining it (line 9).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Every supernormal feat, the kamma-born and merit-born powers, the protective power of concentration or loving-kindness against fire, oil and arrows, and cosmology (Meru, Brahma worlds, nāgas) are doctrinal claims. They must never become idea premises. Stories about named historical or canonical figures are not evidence that the events occurred. The "noble power" is described as an arahant's attainment. Any everyday version is a limited analogy of deliberate reframing, not that attainment.

## Human meaning paragraph

Two threads survive translation as limited analogies. First, the text describes a trained ability to change how disgust and attraction land: meeting what is repulsive with goodwill or a matter-of-fact view, and meeting what is alluring with a sober view of its impermanence, or staying even toward both. The ability is deliberate and practised, not a denial of the senses. Second, in Cūḷapanthaka's story, a learner judged incapable by one standard (memorising a verse) comes to understanding through a simple, repeated physical task and a direct observation that the stain came from his own hand. Any application to caregivers, teachers or learners is my extension.

## Idea decisions

- **C090-I01 — Seeing the repulsive kindly and the lovely soberly.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the noble power.
- **C090-I02 — Unable to memorise, yet able to understand.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the non-miraculous core of Cūḷapanthaka's story.
- Rejected: *the four bases of power* (desire, effort, mind, investigation). Substantially duplicates C075-I02 (Which inner drive leads a hard undertaking).
- Rejected: *success through right application in crafts and farming*. The source gives a definition and a list, with no mechanism beyond "methodical practice succeeds".
- Rejected: *quick entry into absorption protecting against a blow*. The mechanism is doctrinal. The skill aspect is covered by C088-I03, which lists C090 as a supporting concept.
- Rejected: *Devadatta's transformation to win over Ajatasattu* (line 455). Abilities serving a harmful aim duplicates C019-I01.
- Rejected: *wishing to become "one" again out of fewness of wishes, to avoid being recognised* (line 283). A single clause, not developed.
- Rejected: all miracle and protection stories (doctrinal).
