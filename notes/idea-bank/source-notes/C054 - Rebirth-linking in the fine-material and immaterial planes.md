# C054 — Rebirth-linking in the fine-material and immaterial planes

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–67 of 67). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 5, PDF 462–463 (printed 452–453). The extract starts mid-page at PDF 462 (line 21). Page marker at line 41 (PDF 463 / printed 453).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–41 of 41). Heading `# 4. රූපාවචර - අරූපාවචර ප්‍ර‍තිසන්ධි`, sub-headings `## රූපාවචර භූමින්හි ආයුඃ ප්‍ර‍මාණය` (line 15) and `## අරූප භූමීන්හි ආයුඃ ප්‍ර‍මාණය` (line 29). No page locators.

## Central meaning

Rebirth in a fine-material plane happens through the resultant of the jhāna attained, and the grade of attainment decides the level within that group. Someone who has only the first jhāna, unlost at death, is reborn in Brahmapārisajja; medium development leads to Brahmapurohita; refined development, practised with five kinds of mastery, to Mahābrahmā (Pradeepika line 25). There are six fine-material rebirth-linkings (five jhāna resultants plus the non-percipient plane, which has matter only) and four immaterial ones (Pradeepika lines 51–67; Margaya lines 5–13, 27). The same resultant serves as rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death consciousness.

## Essential distinctions

- **Fourfold and fivefold jhāna schemes** (Margaya line 3). The Sutta Piṭaka counts four jhānas; the Abhidhamma gives both. Mind-states are classified by five, planes by four; the second and third jhāna of the fivefold scheme are the second of the fourfold. This explains why the second-jhāna planes receive two resultants (Margaya line 7; Pradeepika lines 56–57).
- **Non-percipient plane** (Margaya line 11). Only matter at rebirth, during life and at death.
- **Why lifespans are so long** (Margaya line 17). Rebirth there comes from very powerful wholesome kamma.
- **Timing within an aeon** (Margaya line 19). The first-jhāna planes are destroyed every aeon, so only those born at the start of the aeon live the full span there.
- **Aeons** (Margaya lines 33–41). Human lifespan falls to ten years, when disease, war or famine destroys most people. The few survivors take up good conduct, and lifespan rises generation by generation to an incalculable length. Then people turn to misconduct and decline again. One such cycle is an intermediate aeon (අන්තඃකල්පය). Four asaṅkhya aeons (formation, formed, dissolution, dissolved) make a great aeon.
- **A special case** (Pradeepika line 25). Non-returners in a female existence who attained the eight attainments are said to be reborn in Brahmapārisajja first and then rise through the planes.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the classification. Margaya supplies the fourfold and fivefold explanation, lifespans, the reason for long lifespans and the aeon structure. Pradeepika supplies the grades of jhāna and summary tables.

Genuine disagreements with Pradeepika in C052:
- **Size of an asaṅkhya aeon.** Margaya line 39 says 20 intermediate aeons (80 in a great aeon). Pradeepika C052 lines 142–146 says 64.
- **Lifespans.** Margaya line 21 gives Ābhassara 8 great aeons and line 23 gives Subhakiṇha 64. Pradeepika C052 lines 109 and 117 give "7 great aeons + 1 asaṅkhya aeon" and "63 great aeons + 1 asaṅkhya aeon". These could be a textual slip in Pradeepika or a different reckoning; the batch report records them as suspected errors. The other fine-material and immaterial lifespans agree.
- **Destruction order.** Both say seven destructions by fire, the eighth by water, and the sixty-fourth by wind (Margaya line 41; Pradeepika C052 lines 216–220).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

All of it is rebirth doctrine and cosmology. That meditative attainment determines a Brahma rebirth, that lifespans run to thousands of aeons, and that human lifespan rises and falls with morality are doctrinal claims. The special case for women non-returners reflects a doctrinal view of gender and must not be generalised.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept describes where meditative attainments are said to lead after death. The only passage touching human social life is Margaya's account of the lifespan cycle (line 33): after mass destruction a few survivors turn to good conduct, which carries forward across generations, and later generations turn away from it. That is the same process Pradeepika describes in more detail in C052 (line 142), where it is developed as C052-I03.

## Idea decisions

- **No card accepted.** Technical rebirth classification.
- Merged support: the lifespan-cycle passage (Margaya line 33) is recorded as supporting evidence for **C052-I03 — Why lessons learned in disaster fade in plenty**, not as a separate card.
- Rejected candidate: *How deeply a skill is practised decides where it holds* (grades of jhāna, Pradeepika line 25). The source's point is rebirth placement, not a human process.
- Rejected candidate: *Arriving late to something that is already ending* (Margaya line 19). An interesting image, but it concerns cosmic timing and a secular reading would be invented.
