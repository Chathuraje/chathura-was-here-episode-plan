# C049 — Classification of persons by rebirth and attainment

## Central meaning

Persons are classified by their rebirth-linking consciousness: rootless in a woeful state (දුගති අහේතුක), rootless in a happy state (සුගති අහේතුක), two-rooted (ද්විහේතුක) and three-rooted (ත්‍රිහේතුක). Three-rooted persons are worldlings or one of eight noble persons (path and fruit of stream-entry, once-return, non-return and arahantship), divided into trainees (ශෛක්ෂ) and the one beyond training (අශෛක්ෂ). The concept then counts which of the eighty process (වීථි) consciousnesses each person can have: 37, 41, 41, 54, 50, 50, 48 and 44 for the main types (Margaya lines 23–34; Pradeepika lines 65–103 and tables at 110–128). The nine sublime resultant consciousnesses are door-freed and excluded (Pradeepika lines 59–63; Margaya line 21).

## Essential distinctions

- Rootless and two-rooted persons cannot attain absorption, path or fruit in that life (Pradeepika lines 45, 73; Margaya line 15).
- Stream-entry permanently removes the four view-linked greed states and the doubt state (Pradeepika line 85); non-return removes the two hatred-rooted states (line 97); the arahant has no wholesome or unwholesome impulsions, only functional ones (line 101; Margaya line 17).
- Margaya counts four kinds of worldling and twelve persons in total, and treats the non-percipient being (අසංඥ) as rootless (lines 3–7). Pradeepika gives three kinds by rebirth, with the rootless type split in two (lines 27–43), and does not mention the non-percipient being here.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree between sources. Pradeepika adds the reasons and a set of tables; Margaya adds that a person at the path moment has only that single path consciousness (lines 27–33, 48). Internal inconsistencies in Pradeepika are listed below and in the batch report.

- Line 103 gives the worldling's count in words as සුපනස (54) but in numerals as (45). Lines 77–81 give 54. C050's table (Concept 50 Pradeepika line 67) shows 45 for a three-rooted person without absorption and 54 with it, so the numeral may be a slip or an unmarked distinction.
- Line 103 says trainees get "fifty" (පනසකි) consciousnesses, then totals them as සපනස් (56).
- The transcriber's note at line 117 records that the printed table omits "unwholesome 12" in the second row, so its column sums to 29 while the brace says 41.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Almost all of this concept is rebirth mechanics and attainment doctrine. It also contains a hazardous claim: that people born blind or deaf, with limited intelligence, or living by begging have a "low" human birth produced by weak merit (Pradeepika line 43), and that such persons cannot attain higher states in this life. This must never be used as a premise, framing or illustration for real people with disabilities or in poverty. The idea of fixed spiritual capacity set at birth is doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept supports no direct human mechanism. It is a catalogue of which mind-states are possible for which kind of being. The one process-like element, that certain states stop arising permanently after an insight, is an attainment claim that cannot be observed or verified from outside, and presenting it as a story of personal change would misrepresent the teaching.

## Idea decisions

- No suitable idea.
- Rejected candidate: *Some reactions stop returning after a deep shift* (stream-entry removing view-linked greed and doubt; non-return removing hatred). It rests on attainment claims, would invite labelling real people as spiritually advanced, and the source gives no human process.
- Rejected candidate: *Capacity set by starting conditions*. Rests on rebirth and on the disability framing above; ethically unusable.
