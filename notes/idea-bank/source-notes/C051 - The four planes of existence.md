## Central meaning

The chapter on processes "freed from the cognitive series" opens with four fourfold sets: planes, rebirth-linking, kamma and death (Pradeepika line 29). This concept covers the first set. A plane (භූමි) is where beings arise and live, or where mental and material phenomena arise (Pradeepika line 33; Margaya line 3). There are four: the states of loss (අපාය), the fortunate sense-sphere planes (කාම සුගති), the fine-material planes (රූපාවචර) and the immaterial planes (අරූපාවචර). The first two together make the eleven sense-sphere planes. This concept details the four states of loss (hell, animal birth, ghost realm, asura) and the seven fortunate sense-sphere planes (human world and six heavens), with lifespans, the kinds of person found in each and the number of mind-states available to them.

## Essential distinctions

- **Hells.** Eight great hells, each 15,000 yojanas below the last, each linked to kinds of wrongdoing (killing, mistreating parents and teachers, deceiving others, wrong view, the gravest acts), with lifespans computed in hell-years (Pradeepika lines 55–83). Margaya describes them as square iron chambers full of flame with sixteen minor (ඔසුපත්) hells around each (lines 7–9).
- **Animals, ghosts and asuras have no fixed place.** Their own aggregates are their "plane". An animal born in a palace or in filth has the same rebirth type (Pradeepika line 103; Margaya lines 13, 17, 29).
- **Four kinds of ghost.** One kind, පරදත්තූපජීවික, lives on what others give. They are said to receive food by rejoicing in merit given by others and to look to relatives for merit (Margaya line 21; Pradeepika line 91 names the four).
- **Asura is an ambiguous name.** Some asuras belong to the states of loss (කාලකඤ්ජික ghosts). Others, such as Vepacitti's host, are deva-like enemies of the gods (Pradeepika lines 95–101; Margaya lines 29–31).
- **Why the human plane is called fortunate.** It lacks the refined pleasures of the heavens, but it gives the opportunity to keep the higher precepts, and Buddhas and great disciples arise there. The word මනුෂ්‍ය is glossed as "having an elevated mind" (Pradeepika line 142).
- **Heaven names.** නිම්මාණරති gods delight in splendour they create themselves. පරනිම්මිත වසවත්ති gods bring under their control the splendour others create (Pradeepika lines 158, 162).
- **Persons and mind-states.** Detailed counts of the mind-states available to each type of person, and the sub-types of stream-enterers, once-returners and non-returners by the number and place of remaining rebirths (Pradeepika lines 166–238).
- **Lifespans** of the six heavens in divine and human years (Pradeepika lines 242–258).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the fourfold scheme, the eight great hells, the four ghost types and the ambiguity of "asura". Margaya adds the physical description of hells and the ghosts' dependence on relatives' merit. Pradeepika adds lifespans, hell-by-hell wrongdoings, the explanation of why the human plane is fortunate and the full person-by-person counts. Minor naming differences: Pradeepika's `තාපය / මහා තාපය` (line 57) versus Margaya's `තාපනය / මහා තාපනය` (line 7). Pradeepika gives 136 hells as 8 great hells plus 128 minor ones (line 81), which matches Margaya's 128 minor hells (line 9). No genuine disagreement found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Almost all of this concept is cosmology, rebirth mechanics and lifespan arithmetic. None of it may become an idea premise. The link between specific wrongdoing and specific hells is a doctrinal claim about rebirth, not an observation about human behaviour. The ghosts' dependence on merit from relatives is doctrinal. The counts of mind-states per person are technical classification.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept offers no human process that the sources themselves describe. It sets out where, according to the teaching, beings can be born and for how long. Two passages touch human life indirectly. The human plane is valued not for its comfort but for the opportunity it gives for restraint and understanding. And one kind of ghost is said to depend on what living relatives give. Neither passage explains a mechanism in people; any card built on them would rest on inference well beyond the text.

## Idea decisions

- **No card accepted.** The concept is a cosmological classification.
- Rejected candidate: *Caring for the dead through what the living give.* Sri Lankan merit-sharing for dead relatives is a real practice, but the source only states the doctrine from the ghost's side (Margaya line 21). It says nothing about the living's motives or experience. Held as a question for a concept that treats merit-sharing directly.
- Rejected candidate: *Less comfort, more room to grow* (Pradeepika line 142). It is a doctrinal ranking of planes and would turn into generic advice. Its human side is better supported by the prosperity-and-carelessness passage in C052 and C054, so it is recorded as supporting context for C052-I03.
- Rejected candidate: *Making splendour versus controlling others' splendour* (Pradeepika lines 158, 162). These are etymologies of heaven names, with no described process.
- Rejected candidate: *Same condition in a palace or a rubbish heap* (Pradeepika line 103). It is a rebirth-type claim, and a secular reading would be generic.
