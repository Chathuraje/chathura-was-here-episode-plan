# C050 — Mind-states available in each plane of existence

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–80). Frontmatter: Book 1, chapter 4, PDF 407–410 (printed 397–400). The extract begins mid-page on PDF 407 / printed 397 (line 21). Headings `### භූමි භෙදය` (line 23) and `### භූමි පුද්ගල භෙදය` (line 61). Page markers at lines 35 (408/398), 47 (409/399), 59 (410/400).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–21). Heading `# 13. භූමිවල සිත් ලැබෙන සැටි`. No page locators. Line 21 marks the end of chapter 4.

## Central meaning

The eighty process consciousnesses are distributed across three kinds of plane. All eighty occur in the eleven sense-sphere planes, as suits each person. The fine-material (රූප) planes have sixty-four: they lack the two hatred-rooted states, the eight great resultants and the six smell, taste and body consciousnesses. The immaterial (අරූප) planes have forty-two: no five-door processes at all, no hatred, and no first path. The non-percipient plane has none (Pradeepika lines 29–55; Margaya lines 3–15).

## Essential distinctions

- **Why hatred is absent in the higher planes.** Margaya (line 17): hatred is a hindrance (නීවරණ) that obstructs absorption. Greed also obstructs absorption, but not every greed becomes a hindrance, so greed-rooted states remain in those planes. "There is no hatred that is not a hindrance." Pradeepika (lines 39, 45) gives a related reason: birth there comes through meditation of detachment from sense pleasures, so senses that serve only sense pleasure, and hatred, do not arise.
- **Why the first path is absent in the immaterial planes.** Pradeepika (line 45): stream-entry needs hearing the teaching from another (පරතොසොස, as printed), and there is no sense base there.
- **Disability.** Pradeepika (line 31): where a being lacks a sense door from birth, such as blindness or deafness, processes of that door do not arise. The same passage repeats the "low birth" framing from C049.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree (80, 64, 42, none). The reasons differ in emphasis: Margaya argues from hindrances to absorption, Pradeepika from the meditative cause of birth and the absence of doors. These are compatible, not contradictory. Pradeepika adds the tree, mountain, river and ocean deities and the rebirth route out of the non-percipient plane (lines 31, 53–55).

Possible text issues: Pradeepika line 33 reads "පටිසජවනයන් වූ දෙස මූල සිත්", probably a misprint or OCR error for පටිඝ ජවන. The table at lines 61–80 is headed "වීථී සිත්" (process consciousnesses), but the brahma rows appear to include the door-freed sublime resultants (for example, worldling fine-material 43 rather than 38 by the process-only count; worldling immaterial 27 rather than 23). This is my arithmetic check, not a confirmed error.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Planes of existence, brahmas, deities, the non-percipient being and rebirth routes are cosmology. The claim that higher beings lack smell, taste and touch is doctrinal. The disability framing must not be used.

## Human meaning paragraph

The only part with a possible human reading is Margaya's reason for which states survive in the planes reached through deep concentration. Some wanting is compatible with a settled, absorbed mind; every form of hatred or resentment is an obstacle to it. Read outside the cosmology, this would suggest that ill-will and deep settledness exclude each other in a way that mild wanting and settledness do not. That is an interpretation of a one-sentence doctrinal rationale about meditative absorption and rebirth planes, so it is held rather than accepted.

## Idea decisions

- **C050-I01 — Why resentment unsettles more than wanting does.** Held. The source rationale is one sentence in a cosmological argument, and the concept of absorption is not ordinary concentration.
- Rejected candidate: anything from plane counts, deities or the non-percipient rebirth route. Cosmology only.
