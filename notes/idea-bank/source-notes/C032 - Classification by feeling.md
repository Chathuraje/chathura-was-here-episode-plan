## Central meaning

Chapter 3 opens by treating mind and the 52 factors as 53 phenomena, to be sorted in six ways (lines 29–35). Feeling (වේදනා) is defined as experiencing the "taste" of an object. It is present in every mind-state (lines 41–43; Margaya line 5).

- **By experience** (lines 50–83; Margaya line 5), feeling is threefold. Pleasant feeling (63 states) arises when an object is desirable, dear and liked. Painful feeling (3 states) arises when an object is undesirable and disliked. Neutral feeling (55 states) is neither pleasant nor painful. Pradeepika links it to a moderately desirable object (line 75). Margaya says there is no fourth way of experiencing an object.
- **As faculties** (lines 85–107; Margaya lines 5–13), feeling is fivefold. Bodily pleasure (1 state: body-consciousness) is separated from mental joy (62 states). Bodily pain (1 state: body-consciousness) is separated from mental distress (2 states: the aversion-rooted states only). Neutral feeling covers the remaining 55.

Pradeepika then adds teaching not found in Margaya (lines 109–123, PDF 243–244 / printed 233–234):

- All worldly feelings are called suffering because they arise from conditions.
- **Suffering as pain** (දුක්ඛ දුක්ඛ): pain that is painful by nature.
- **Suffering of conditioned existence** (සංඛාර දුක්ඛ): all pleasant and neutral feeling, oppressed by arising and passing away.
- **Suffering of change** (විපරිනාම දුක්ඛ): pleasant feelings that are not painful by nature but cause pain by changing. The examples are bodily pleasure and the long bliss of the highest formless realm at its end.
- **Hidden suffering** (පටිච්ඡන්න): aches in the hands, feet, head, teeth or ears, and mental burning from lust or hatred. These can be known only by asking, and their cause is not evident.
- **Evident suffering** (අපටිච්ඡන්න): external harm such as violence and bondage. It can be known without asking, and its cause is plain.
- **Indirect and direct suffering** (පරියාය / නිප්පරියාය).

## Essential distinctions

- Pleasant feeling is different from wholesome. The four joyful greed-rooted states count among the pleasant ones (line 58; line 135).
- Bodily pain is a resultant body-consciousness. Mental distress occurs only in the two aversion-rooted states (lines 99–105; Margaya lines 7, 11).
- Hidden and evident suffering are distinguished by whether another person can know the suffering without asking, and whether its cause is visible (lines 119–121).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the three- and fivefold schemes and on the counts (63/3/55; 1/62/1/2/55). Pradeepika adds the object-quality descriptions and the whole suffering analysis. Margaya adds that there is no fourth mode of experience. No disagreement was found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Lifespans in the highest formless realm (line 117), the pains of the four lower realms (line 111), and "three-plane" classification are cosmological and doctrinal. "All feeling is suffering" is a doctrinal thesis about conditioned existence, not a claim about mood. Calling mental distress "aversion-rooted" is an ethical classification. Applying it to people in pain could morally blame sufferers.

## Human meaning paragraph

People meet objects as pleasant, painful or neither. The source separates what is felt in the body from what is felt in the mind. It notes that pleasure can become a source of pain simply by changing or ending. It also distinguishes suffering others can see from suffering that stays hidden unless someone asks. The everyday applications below are interpretation. The source frames these points as an analysis of conditioned existence.

## Idea decisions

- **C032-I01 — When pleasure turns into pain by ending.** Accepted. Based on suffering of change (line 117).
- **C032-I02 — Suffering nobody sees unless they ask.** Accepted. Based on hidden versus evident suffering (lines 119–121).
- **C032-I03 — Pain in the body versus distress in the mind.** Held. The body–mind distinction is well supported. But the implied lesson (bodily pain need not become distress) is not stated in this source. Linking distress to aversion risks moral blame of people in pain. It needs a source that explains the relationship, possibly C007 (the two aversion-rooted states) or later concepts.
- Rejected candidate: *Everything felt is a kind of suffering*. It is a doctrinal thesis. As an idea it would become a slogan.
- Rejected candidate: *Neutral feeling comes from moderately liked things*. It is a one-line description, and only Pradeepika gives it.
