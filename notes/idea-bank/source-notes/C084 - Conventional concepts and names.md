# C084 — Conventional concepts and names

## Central meaning

A concept (ප්‍රඥප්ති) is a convention distinct from mind and matter (Pradeepika line 25). It appears to the mind as really existing, but on examination is found not to exist in the ultimate sense (Margaya line 7). The test is division. Cloth pulled apart gives threads, and no cloth remains (Margaya line 7). There are two kinds:
- **Meaning-concepts** (අර්ථ ප්‍රඥප්ති): what comes to mind on hearing a word, such as "table". Sight can also give rise to them (Margaya line 9). Types include shape, collection, being, direction and time (Margaya lines 11–19). Pradeepika lists collection, shape, being, direction, time, space and meditation-sign concepts (lines 27–41).
- **Name-concepts** (නාම ප්‍රඥප්ති): the names themselves. A name is not the sound. The three sounds "මේ-ස-ය" are ultimate realities. The name "table" is what arises in the hearer's mind from their sequence, and it in turn brings up the meaning-concept (Margaya line 21).

Name-concepts are sixfold (Margaya lines 23–35; Pradeepika lines 47–71):
1. names for what exists ultimately (mind, feeling);
2. names for what does not (table, mountain, human, child);
3. names that use an existing quality to point to something non-existent ("the wise one", "the faithful one", "the envious one", "the greedy one", "the white man"; Pradeepika: a person who gained the six higher knowledges);
4. names that use something non-existent to point to something existing ("a woman's wisdom", "the lute's sound", "a child's mind"; Pradeepika: "a woman's voice");
5. existing pointing to existing ("eye-consciousness");
6. non-existing pointing to non-existing ("girl child", "king's son").

## Essential distinctions

- A concept is not a lie. Margaya says ordinary people experience concepts as the things of the world, and that understanding concepts helps separate them clearly from the ultimate (line 5).
- Sound is ultimate, and a name is a concept. Margaya warns that this "may be confusing for some" (line 21).
- In type 3, the quality is real (wisdom, faith, envy, greed, the colour white are ultimate phenomena) but the person it is attached to is a concept (Margaya line 29).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the definition and on the six kinds of name-concept. They differ in several places:
- **Classification.** Pradeepika classes earth, mountains, rivers and oceans as *collection* concepts and chariots as *shape* concepts (lines 29–31). Margaya classes earth, mountains and trees as *shape* concepts and tables, chairs and chariots as *collection* concepts (lines 13–15). This is a genuine difference and is not reconciled here.
- **Coverage of the lists.** Pradeepika adds space and sign concepts. Margaya adds explanations of what each concept depends on, for example direction and time dependent on the movement of sun and moon (lines 17–19).
- **Examples.** The examples for types 3, 4 and 6 differ, but illustrate the same principle.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

"Does not exist in the ultimate sense" is a doctrinal and analytical claim, not a denial that people, rivers or days matter, and not a statement of physics. The six higher knowledges (Pradeepika line 59) and meditation signs (line 41) are doctrinal and not used. Margaya's "white man" example (line 29) concerns colour as an ultimate phenomenon. It must not be used for any idea about race.

## Human meaning paragraph

As interpretation beyond the text: the source notices that everyday names like "the greedy one" or "the wise one" are built by taking a real, momentary quality and fastening it to a person, who is only a conventional unit. This offers a way to look at how labels form. A passing state becomes a standing name, and the name can then outlast the state it was built on. The source itself makes a classificatory point, not a social or ethical one.

## Idea decisions

- **C084-I01 — When a passing quality becomes a person's name.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on type 3 name-concepts (Margaya line 29; Pradeepika lines 57–59), with the momentariness of qualities as context from other concepts.
- Rejected (merged into existing): *cloth is only threads*. This is the same mechanism as C001-I01. It is recommended as an added supporting reference to C001-I01, not a new card.
- Rejected candidate: *a name is not its sounds* (Margaya line 21). This is close to C066-I02 (Hearing the words without the act of meaning) and C036-I01, and the source gives a structural point rather than a lived tension.
- Rejected candidate: *days and directions as names for the sun's movement* (Margaya lines 17–19). There is too little explanation, it risks becoming astronomy trivia, and its mechanism is covered by C001-I01.
