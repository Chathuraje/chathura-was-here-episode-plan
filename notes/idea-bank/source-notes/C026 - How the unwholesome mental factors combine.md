## Central meaning

The 14 unwholesome factors appear only in the 12 unwholesome consciousnesses, in five groups: the delusion quartet (මෝහ, අහිරික, අනොත්තප්ප, උද්ධච්ච) in all 12; the greed triad (ලෝභ, දිට්ඨි, මාන) in the 8 greed-rooted; the hatred quartet (දෝස, ඉස්සා, මච්ඡරිය, කුක්කුච්ච) in the 2 hatred-rooted; sloth and torpor (ථීන, මිද්ධ) in the 5 prompted unwholesome; doubt in the one doubt-accompanied state.

## Essential distinctions

- **Why the delusion quartet is universal to unwholesome states** (Margaya line 15). If there is understanding that sees the fault of unwholesome action, or shame (හිරි) that is disgusted by it and fear (ඔත්තප්ප) of it, or the mind is established in wholesome things, unwholesome consciousness does not arise. Every unwholesome consciousness needs delusion that conceals the danger, shamelessness, fearlessness of wrong, and restlessness. Pradeepika (lines 29) states the fact without this reasoning.
- **View and conceit never together** (Pradeepika line 35; Margaya line 17). Both arise with greed, but grasping things as permanent/self and grasping "I am superior" are different operations, so conceit occurs only, and only sometimes, in view-dissociated states.
- **Stinginess arises with aversion, not greed** (Margaya line 19). Stinginess comes about because of attachment to one's own things, but it occurs as *not tolerating one's things being shared with others*, so it arises with aversion. Pradeepika (line 39) places it in the hatred quartet and adds that envy, stinginess and remorse arise separately, only sometimes, and never without aversion.
- **Sloth and torpor** make the mind unwieldy and occur only sometimes, only in prompted states (Pradeepika line 43; Margaya lines 9, 21).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on all counts. Margaya supplies the explanations (lines 15–21); Pradeepika supplies the formal groupings, the fixed/variable distinction and the summary table. No disagreement found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claim that these four factors are present in *every* unwholesome consciousness is a doctrinal classification about mind-moments; it cannot be observed. The view "the five aggregates are a permanent self" (Margaya line 17) is a technical doctrine of wrong view and does not map onto everyday opinions.

## Human meaning paragraph

Two mechanisms emerge. First, the texts claim that harmful action happens only when, at that moment, the harm is out of view and neither shame nor caution is active, together with a restless, unsettled mind. General moral knowledge may still be present at other times (compare C006-I01). Second, holding tight to what is one's own can turn into resentment when others might share it; stinginess is thus described as closer to irritation than to enjoyment of possessions. Both readings are interpretation beyond the text's moment-level classification.

## Idea decisions

- **C026-I01 — Stinginess as resenting that others share.** Accepted. Based on Margaya line 19 and Pradeepika line 39.
- **C026-I02 — When shame and caution fall silent together.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Margaya line 15, with Pradeepika line 29. It is in tension with C006-I01 and should be read alongside it. It also borders C023-I01 (shame and fear of wrong as reasons for restraint); Batch F suggested merging negative-side cards into C023-I01, but this card's mechanism (momentary co-absence with a concealed harm and restlessness) differs, so it is kept separate pending review.
- Rejected candidate: *Feeling superior and holding a view cannot coincide* (Margaya line 17). Technical; the "view" in question is a specific doctrinal self-view.
- Rejected candidate: sloth and torpor in prompted states. Overlaps C006-I03 (needing a push) and adds only a classification.
