# C004 — The fifty-four sense-sphere mind-states

## Central meaning

The 54 sense-sphere consciousnesses are 12 unwholesome, 18 rootless and 24 beautiful (Pradeepika lines 25–33; Margaya line 3). The 12 unwholesome states are divided by root: 8 greed-rooted, 2 hatred-rooted, 2 delusion-rooted (Pradeepika line 47; Margaya line 9). Pradeepika makes delusion (මෝහ) the root present in every unwholesome state: it is like darkness that hides the nature of the object, so the deluded mind does not know true from false or fault from blamelessness (line 39). When a deluded mind meets a pleasing or neutral object, attachment (greed) appears; when it meets a disliked object, anger (hatred) appears; when delusion is so strong the object cannot be settled, only indecision or restlessness appears (lines 41–45). Margaya begins the detailed eightfold greed classification (lines 13–30), which C006 develops.

## Essential distinctions

- **Delusion is not lack of worldly learning.** Pradeepika (lines 55–59) says delusion is what is called ignorance, foolishness and not-knowing, and then observes that many intelligent, highly educated people regarded as advanced still kill animals for bodily pleasure, fish as a sport, gamble, drink, set traps, hunt, and that scientists make atomic weapons that can destroy populated countries in a moment.
- **Not knowing it is wrong versus knowing it is wrong.** Those without belief in kamma, striving only for pleasure in this world, do wrong "without knowing, without knowing it is a fault" (line 67). By contrast, learned Buddhists do wrong "knowing it is wrong" (line 69), and their acts still have results. Pradeepika attributes restraint to habits of wholesome action built over saṃsāra (line 69).
- **Greed** (Margaya line 13) is the attitude that a thing is good, beautiful, sweet; it is named by words including wanting, lust, affection (ආදරය) and love (ප්‍රේමය). Greed never occurs with displeasure, only with joyful or neutral feeling (Margaya line 15).
- **Ten unwholesome courses of action** (Pradeepika line 51) and their results (line 53).
- **Greed arises first in every new life** as attachment to the new existence (Pradeepika line 73). Doctrinal rebirth mechanics.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the 54 and on the three roots. Pradeepika explains why delusion underlies all unwholesome states and why greed is listed first; Margaya defines greed and feeling more precisely and starts the eightfold scheme. No disagreement found. Pradeepika's examples (lines 57–59) and its statement that such people will certainly go to hell (line 63) are its own moral and doctrinal position; Margaya has no parallel passage.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth in hell (line 63), results across lives and habits carried through saṃsāra (line 69), and greed as the first response to a new existence (line 73) are doctrinal. Pradeepika's list of wrongdoers (meat-eating, fishing, drinking, gambling, weapons science) is a moral judgement within the teaching. It must not be used to label any real person, community or occupation, especially people who fish, hunt or raise animals for a living. Margaya's inclusion of love and affection under greed is a technical usage and must not be carried as "love is greed" (see C005-I02 for the handled version).

## Human meaning paragraph

Pradeepika separates two things everyday speech often merges: being knowledgeable and seeing harm. Its interpretation is that a person can be highly skilled and learned while a harm they cause does not register as harm, because the "darkness" in question is about the nature of the act, not about information. It then sets a second case beside the first: people who do see the fault and act anyway. The human reading (interpretation beyond the text) is that the question "did they know?" has at least two meanings, and that expertise answers neither.

## Idea decisions

- **C004-I01 — Great learning that does not reveal the harm.** Accepted for research, with strong limits on the source's examples. Based on Pradeepika lines 39, 55–59 and 67.
- Merged into existing idea (no new card): *Knowing it is wrong and doing it anyway* (Pradeepika line 69). Same mechanism as C006-I01 *Knowing the cost and still wanting it*. Recorded here as supporting material for C006-I01; C004-I01 cites it only as a contrast.
- Rejected candidate: *Too confused to want or refuse* (line 45, delusion-rooted indecision). The two delusion-rooted states are the subject of a later concept; creating a card here would pre-empt that concept's fuller source.
- Rejected candidate: *Clinging first to simply being alive* (line 73). Rebirth mechanics; a secular version would substantially alter the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: *Wanting cannot feel unpleasant* (Margaya line 15). A technical feeling rule without a described human process; overlaps C006's rejected neutral-feeling candidate.
