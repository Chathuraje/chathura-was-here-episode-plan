# C017 — Expanded method for counting supramundane mind-states

Note: the folder name contains two spaces after "Concept 17 -".

## Central meaning

In the brief method there are eight supramundane states (four paths, four fruits). A "dry-insight" practitioner who develops insight without absorption gets path and fruit states accompanied by the first-absorption factors. One who first attains absorption and uses it as the basis for insight gets path and fruit states accompanied by the factors of that absorption, first to fifth. Multiplying eight by five gives forty supramundane states, and the full count of consciousness rises from 89 to 121 (Pradeepika line 25, lines 27–41; Margaya lines 3–17). Margaya then gives the 121 by plane, by kind and by feeling (lines 19–23).

## Essential distinctions

- Dry insight (ශුෂ්ක විදර්ශක) versus absorption-based insight (Pradeepika line 25; Margaya line 3).
- First- to fourth-absorption supramundane states are joyful (32); fifth-absorption ones are neutral (8) (Margaya line 13).
- 67 absorption states in total: 11 each for the first four absorptions, 23 for the fifth (Margaya line 15).
- For prompting, exalted and supramundane states are counted as prompted (Margaya line 23).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the forty-fold expansion and its basis. Pradeepika gives the rationale in terms of the practitioner's route; Margaya gives the totals and the cross-classifications. See the batch report for a suspected arithmetic error in Margaya line 21.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Path and fruit attainments and absorption levels are claims about meditative realisation. The concept is a counting method.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept is a technical counting scheme. It does not describe a human process that can be explored in ordinary life without importing claims about enlightenment.
