## Central meaning

The eight great resultant states (මහා විපාක) mirror the eight great wholesome states of C011 and arise as their results. They serve as rebirth-linking consciousness in the human and divine sense-worlds and also arise during life as results, for example when enjoying a pleasant object (Pradeepika lines 34–50; Margaya lines 16–18). The eight great functional states (මහා ක්‍රියා) mirror the wholesome states but occur only in arahants, who have no further rebirth, so their good acts produce no result (Pradeepika lines 67–69; Margaya line 22). Pradeepika then lists which of the 54 sense-sphere states each type of person has (lines 73–107) and gives the full breakdown chart (lines 115–122). Margaya gives the parallel counts (lines 35–47) and urges memorising them (line 49).

## Essential distinctions

- **Quality of the deed and quality of the rebirth.** Pradeepika line 42–48 divides wholesome deeds into strong or weak, three-rooted or two-rooted, and links each to a type of rebirth-linking: with or without knowledge, or rootless. It adds that those without the right conditions who meditate may fail and even become mentally disturbed (line 44).
- **Weak, knowledge-dissociated good** (line 48): good done without knowledge, "seeing how others do good, out of තරහ (pique or rivalry) and to gain the praise of the world". The source ties such weak good to rebirth as poor, disabled, dull and helpless humans.
- **Functional states in arahants** (lines 67–69): they do good acts but without karmic force. They act with mindful understanding, or "as if from habit"; Buddhas have only the knowledge-associated kind.
- **Resultant states are shared** by all persons including the Buddha (line 52).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both agree on the structure of eight resultant and eight functional states, on their mirroring of the wholesome states and on arahants alone having functional states. Pradeepika adds the fourfold strength division, the person-by-person counts and charts, and the motive sentence at line 48. Margaya adds that rootless wholesome-resultant states (sense-consciousness of pleasant objects) are also results of sense-sphere good (lines 16–18) and that a very weak good deed brings rebirth through the neutral investigating state (line 18), which agrees with Pradeepika line 48. There is a minor difference in presentation, not in doctrine: Pradeepika notes that some teachers allow the Buddha the smile-producing state and knowledge-dissociated functional states (line 77).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

- Almost all of this concept is rebirth mechanics and the classification of realised persons.
- The claim that poor, disabled or less intelligent people are born so because of weak past good (line 48) must never be used as a premise, framing or explanation for any real person or community.
- The claim that meditators lacking the right rebirth conditions may become mentally disturbed (line 44) is doctrinal and is not a health claim.

## Human meaning paragraph

Within a mostly doctrinal passage, one sentence describes a recognisable human process: doing good without understanding, prompted by seeing what others do, out of pique or rivalry, and to be praised. The source is interested in what such good produces in future lives; that part is excluded. What remains usable, as interpretation, is the observation that visible good deeds can be started by comparison and the wish for public approval.

## Idea decisions

- **C012-I01 — Doing good to match or outshine others.** Held, with the rebirth consequence explicitly excluded. It rests on a single sentence (Pradeepika line 48) and shares its mechanism with C074-I03 (When giving turns into outdoing others). Recommended: merge into C074-I03, with C012 as a supporting reference for the praise-seeking motive. It differs from C011-I03 (motive at the outset versus interruption during the act).
- Rejected candidate: *Doing good with nothing left to gain* (arahants' functional states). A claim about realised persons only; no ordinary-person mechanism.
- Rejected candidate: weak good leading to hardship. Doctrinal and ethically harmful if applied to people.
