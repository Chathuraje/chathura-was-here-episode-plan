# C053 — The four kinds of rebirth-linking

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–56 of 56). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 5, PDF 460–462 (printed 450–452). The extract starts mid-page at PDF 460 (line 21). Page markers at lines 38 (PDF 461 / printed 451) and 54 (PDF 462 / printed 452).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–37 of 37). Heading `# 3. ප්‍ර‍තිසන්ධි චතුෂ්කය`, sub-heading `## කාම සත්ත්වයන් ගේ ආයුෂ` at line 21. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Rebirth-linking (ප්‍රතිසන්ධිය) is the joining of the last mind-moment of one existence to the first of the next, with no gap (Pradeepika line 32). There are four kinds, by plane: states of loss, fortunate sense-sphere, fine-material and immaterial. This concept covers the first two. Rebirth in the states of loss happens through a single resultant mind-state, the unwholesome-resultant investigating consciousness with neutral feeling. The same mind-state then functions as life-continuum (භවඞ්ග) and as death consciousness (ච්‍යුති) for that whole existence (Pradeepika lines 36–42; Margaya line 5). Fortunate sense-sphere rebirth happens through nine mind-states: one weak wholesome-resultant investigating consciousness and the eight great resultants (Margaya line 7).

## Essential distinctions

- **The near-death object** (Margaya line 5). At the approach of death, an unwholesome act, a sign of that act, or a sign of the destination appears to the dying being. The first mind of the new existence takes that object.
- **One mind-state across a life** (Margaya line 5, bold). For one being in one existence, rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death consciousness are the same type of mind-state.
- **Nāgas and supaṇṇas** (Pradeepika line 44). Their rebirth-linking is of the lowest kind, but during life they may enjoy deva-like pleasures because other wholesome kamma ripens.
- **Weak-resultant rebirth in fortunate planes** (Pradeepika line 48; Margaya lines 9–15). The texts say that people born blind, deaf, mute, without smell, with intellectual disability or mental illness, with variant sex characteristics or intersex, or with speech impediments, and "slow-witted beings who mostly serve others", are reborn through a very weak wholesome result. Some vinipātika asuras are also named: spirits living in trees, caves and cemeteries, eating discarded food, sometimes troubling infants, the sick and the weak, and accepting offerings in kattadi (exorcist) rituals (Margaya line 15).
- **Two- and three-rooted resultants** produce human and deva rebirths. The more powerful devas are reborn through three-rooted resultants; asuras, gandhabbas and lesser devas through two-rooted ones, so they cannot attain jhāna or the paths in that life (Pradeepika lines 52–56).
- **Sense-sphere lifespans** (Margaya lines 23–37). No fixed span for beings in the states of loss, humans or vinipātika asuras; heaven lifespans each four times the previous.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the classification. Margaya adds the near-death object, the explicit "one mind-state per existence" statement, the detailed list of persons with weak-resultant rebirth and the description of vinipātika asuras. Pradeepika adds nāgas and supaṇṇas and the powerful and lesser devas. Pradeepika says vinipātika asuras have rootless rebirth-linking (line 48). Margaya says not all do, and some are two- or three-rooted and can even attain the paths (line 15). This is a genuine difference in emphasis: Margaya qualifies what Pradeepika states generally. Margaya's heaven lifespans (lines 25–37) agree with Pradeepika C051 (lines 248–258).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Everything about rebirth-linking is doctrinal. The claim that disability, intellectual difference, mental illness, intersex and variant sex characteristics, and servitude result from weak merit is doctrinal and, if carried into documentary thinking, stigmatising and harmful. It must never be used to frame real people. The Margaya list uses derogatory Sinhala terms (for example කෙළතොලුවෝ, පිස්සෝ). The spirit beliefs and kattadi offerings are cultural and doctrinal material, not evidence of spirits.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept describes how, according to the teaching, one existence joins the next. It does not describe a human process that can be observed in life. The only passage near human experience is the statement that a deed, a sign of it, or a sign of what lies ahead comes to mind as death approaches. In this concept that statement serves the rebirth mechanism and is given in one sentence without explanation.

## Idea decisions

- **No card accepted.** Technical rebirth classification.
- Deferred candidate: *What comes to mind as death approaches* (Margaya line 5). Facing death is an acceptable human theme, but here the passage is one sentence in service of rebirth mechanics. The near-death objects are treated in full in C061, whose card C061-I01 (Old deeds feeling new again near death) already covers this ground. No duplicate card here.
- Rejected candidate: anything built on the weak-resultant list. Ethically unsafe; it would attach moral causation to disability and sex variation.
- Rejected candidate: *Offering food to what troubles the weak* (kattadi offerings, Margaya line 15). This is a cultural practice mentioned in passing, with no mechanism described in the source.
