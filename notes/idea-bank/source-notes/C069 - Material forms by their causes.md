# C069 — Material forms by their causes

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–107). Frontmatter: Book 2, ෂට්ඨම පරිච්ඡේදය, PDF 105–107 (printed 94–96). No internal heading. Page markers at lines 44 (PDF 106 / printed 95) and 74 (PDF 107 / printed 96).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–23). Heading `# 7. කර්මාදියෙන් හට ගන්නා රූප`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

This concept sorts the 28 material forms by how many of the four causes (kamma, mind, temperature, nutriment) can produce them. Nine arise from all four (eight inseparables and space). Three (lightness ලහුතා, softness මුදුතා, workability කම්මඤ්ඤතා) arise from mind, temperature and nutriment. Sound arises from mind and temperature. Nine arise only from kamma (sense-faculties, sex, heart-base, life-faculty). Two intimations arise only from mind. Four characteristics (growth, continuity, decay, impermanence) arise from none, being only marks of other matter. It then lists kamma-born (18), mind-born (15), temperature-born (13) and nutriment-born (12) matter.

## Essential distinctions

- **Lightness, softness and workability depend on good conditions.** Margaya line 11: these three arise from good mind, good temperature and good food. When temperature is bad, when the mind is bad, or with unsuitable food, the clusters lack these three, and the body is heavy, stiff and not fit for action. Pradeepika lines 26–30 give only the list (arising from mind, temperature and nutriment) without the explanation.
- **Speech vs wind noise.** Margaya line 9: speaking is mind-born sound; sound from wind is temperature-born.
- **Space between clusters.** Margaya line 13: space is strictly unproduced, but because it separates clusters produced by the causes, it is counted as arising from all four.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The lists agree. Margaya adds the explanatory sentences on lightness and on space. There is one genuine difference: Pradeepika line 58 says the four characteristic forms cannot be said to arise from any cause, while Margaya line 15 agrees but notes that the commentaries (අටුවා) present growth and continuity as arising from causes. Pradeepika does not mention that commentarial view.

Suspected text error: Margaya line 11 reads "සෘතුව නරක් වූ කල්හි හටගන්නා චිත්තජ රූපයන්හි ද, සිත නරක් වූ කල්හි හට ගන්නා චිත්තජ රූපවල ද". The first "චිත්තජ" should almost certainly be "සෘතුජ" (temperature-born), matching the pattern of mind / temperature / food.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The causal framework (kamma producing sense organs and sex, 17-moment matter, space as a produced form) is doctrinal physiology. The claim that a "bad mind" makes bodily matter heavy is a historical account, not a medical or psychological finding. It may support only a limited analogy about ordinary experience.

## Human meaning paragraph

The one passage with human content says that the body's lightness, pliancy and readiness for work are not fixed. They appear when the mind, the climate and the food are good, and they are missing when any of these is bad, leaving the body heavy and stiff. The source places mind alongside weather and food as an equal condition. The everyday reading, which is interpretation, is that how ready the body feels to act depends partly on one's state of mind, and that people may not easily tell which of these conditions is at work.

## Idea decisions

- **C069-I01 — When mind, food and weather make the body heavy.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Margaya line 11, with Pradeepika lines 26–30 confirming the three conditions.
- Rejected candidate: "Growth and ageing as marks, not things" (characteristic forms). Doctrinal ontology, and it overlaps C001-I01.
- Rejected candidate: speech vs wind sound. Too thin.
