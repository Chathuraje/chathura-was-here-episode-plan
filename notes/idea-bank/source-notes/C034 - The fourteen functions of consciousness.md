## Central meaning

Every mind-state and mental factor has a function (කෘත්‍ය); none is without one. There are fourteen: rebirth-linking (පටිසන්ධි), life-continuum (භවාඞ්ග), adverting (ආවජ්ජන), seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, receiving (සම්පටිච්ඡන), investigating (සන්තීරණ), determining (වොට්ඨපන), impulsion (ජවන), registration (තදාරම්මණ) and death (චුති). Some of the 89 mind-states perform one function, others two to five, but only one at a time (Margaya lines 45, 69). The classification is taught as a prerequisite for the chapter on cognitive processes (චිත්ත වීථි) (Margaya lines 5, 39; Pradeepika line 201).

## Essential distinctions

- **Sensing is bare.** Eye-consciousness only knows colour; it cannot know whether the object is desirable (Pradeepika line 91). Hearing, smelling and tasting consciousness likewise do not register sweet or harsh qualities (lines 103, 115, 125). Deciding “this is such a tree, such an animal, such a substance” takes a mass of mind-moments, several cognitive processes (line 95).
- **An ordered build-up within a process.** Adverting turns the mind toward a new object “like opening a door to enter a house” (line 79), then sense-consciousness, receiving (line 147), investigating before firm recognition (line 155), determining how the object is to be dealt with (line 167), then impulsion. The source itself states this order; it is not an imposed sequence.
- **Impulsion is where action and moral weight lie.** Impulsion runs “as if speeding” and performs bodily, verbal and mental action (Pradeepika line 173). These seven “karma mind-states” are stronger than the other process mind-states (lines 175–179). Margaya line 41: the main part of the mind is impulsion; knowing the object well, doing actions, and merit and demerit happen there.
- **Sense-consciousness, receiving and investigating are results (විපාක).** Whether a desirable or undesirable object meets the eye is framed as the result of past wholesome or unwholesome action (lines 93, 105, 117, 127, 137, 149, 157). This is doctrinal.
- **Registration after impulsion.** A runner cannot stop at once and takes about two more steps; likewise two registration moments follow impulsion, but only in strong processes (line 185).
- **Body-contact alone carries pain or pleasure** at the sensing stage, because its object is the coarse great elements; the other four senses carry neutral feeling (line 139).
- **Life-continuum** is the unnoticed background stream when no new object is taken (Pradeepika line 65; Margaya line 41), called the “natural mind” (ප්‍රකෘති සිත) and pure because unmixed with defilement (line 73).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the fourteen functions, the mind-states that perform each, and the counts (19 rebirth-linking/life-continuum/death, 55 impulsion, 11 registration, 68 single-function states). Pradeepika adds the objects of each function, the rebirth-linking typology tied to strength of past action (lines 41–57), the timing of kamma results for the first, seventh and middle impulsion moments (line 179), the runner simile and two tables of functions and function-places (lines 205–245). Margaya adds the plain claim that life-continuum states are the most frequent yet unfelt and that impulsion is the main and morally decisive part (line 41), and advises memorising rather than fully understanding at this stage (line 39). Margaya uses Sanskritised names (ආඝ්‍රාණ, ආස්වාදන, ව්‍යවස්ථාපන, තදාලම්බන); Pradeepika uses Pali forms. No substantive disagreement found.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth-linking, death consciousness, objects taken from the previous life, and the timing of kamma results are doctrinal. Pradeepika line 47 links being born blind or deaf to weak wholesome action; this must never be carried into ideas or used about real people. The process is said to take place at extraordinary speed (billions of mind-moments in a finger-snap, line 37), so any everyday “first glance, then recognition, then reaction” reading is a limited analogy at a different timescale, not a description of the doctrine and not neuroscience. The claim that the sensed object's pleasantness is a karmic result must not become a premise.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe experience as built by stages that do different work. Something first catches the mind; the sense registers it without knowing what it is or whether it is good; it is received, examined and settled; only then does the part that acts take over, and that is where the source locates doing and moral weight. As interpretation beyond the text: what first reaches a person is not yet a judgement, recognition takes more than a glance, and the response is a separate event from the impression. The fine detail of the doctrine (timing, karmic results, rebirth) does not transfer.

## Idea decisions

- **C034-I01 — Seeing before knowing what you see.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on bare sensing and multi-process recognition (lines 91, 95, 155, 167).
- **C034-I02 — What arrives uninvited and what you do with it.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on the contrast between result mind-states (sensing, receiving) and impulsion as the site of action and merit/demerit (Pradeepika lines 173–179; Margaya line 41). The karmic-result framing is excluded.
- Rejected candidate: *A strong impression keeps running after it ends* (registration, runner simile, line 185). Registration is a sub-second resultant moment; stretching it into lingering thoughts would alter the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: *The unnoticed background mind* (life-continuum). The concept is doctrinal (object from the previous life, purity) and has no human mechanism the source explains.
- Rejected candidate: anything built on rebirth-linking, death consciousness or the typology of births.
