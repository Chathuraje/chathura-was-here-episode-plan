## Central meaning

Objects that strike the five doors are of four kinds: very great (අතිමහන්ත), great (මහන්ත), slight (පරිත්ත) and very slight (අතිපරිත්ත). Mind-door objects are clear (විභූත) or unclear (අවිභූත) (Margaya line 3). The fuller the object, the more process minds arise; a very slight object only makes the life-continuum waver and no process arises (Margaya line 9). The sequence of process minds occurs without any maker or doer, by the natural order of mind (චිත්ත නියාමය) (Pradeepika lines 27–31). The mango simile illustrates the full eye-door process (lines 33–37).

## Essential distinctions

- **Two criteria for "great" versus "slight".** Margaya line 5 defines prominence by size, good light or nearness. Margaya line 7 defines the four kinds technically by how many mind-moments of the object's life have passed before it strikes the door (1; 2–3; 4–9; 10–15). The sources present both without reconciling them; both are kept.
- **No doer.** When asked how the ordered minds come about, the answer is: "without a maker or agent, by its own nature, from the time the object strikes the sensitivity, by the law of mind" (Pradeepika line 27). This law is one of five natural orders: seasons, seeds, dhamma, kamma and mind (lines 29–31). Examples: creepers winding round a nearby tree; each tree bearing its own kind of flower and fruit.
- **Mango simile** (Pradeepika lines 33–37): a man sleeping under a mango tree, head covered, wakes at the sound of a falling ripe mango, opens his eyes and sees it, takes it, squeezes it, smells it to decide it is ripe, eats it, swallows the lingering taste with saliva, and sleeps again. Mapped to: life-continuum, object striking, adverting, eye-consciousness, receiving, investigating, determining, impulsion, registration, return to life-continuum.
- **Root mind** (Margaya line 17): the life-continuum carries the near-death object into the next life; processes arise at its "tip". Doctrinal rebirth mechanics.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Pradeepika supplies the no-doer principle, the five natural orders and the simile; Margaya supplies the six kinds of object, the two criteria, process counts (15 per door, 75 in total, line 13) and the root-mind account. No explicit disagreement, but the prominence criterion (line 5) and the timing criterion (line 7) are different kinds of explanation.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The mind-moment counts and the 75 processes are doctrinal. The dhamma-order examples (earthquakes at a Bodhisatta's birth, marks of a great man) and kamma-order are doctrinal claims. The life-continuum as a root mind carried from the last life is rebirth doctrine. The simile's stages are momentary mind-states, not deliberate acts; the man's squeezing and smelling are imagery, not a claim that people consciously check.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source makes two claims that can be followed in experience. First, not everything that reaches the senses gets processed to the same depth: a faint or distant thing may only stir the mind and leave nothing, while a vivid, near or well-lit thing sets off a full sequence ending in enjoyment and registration. Second, that sequence runs in its own order without anyone inside deciding each step, like a sleeper roused by a falling fruit who wakes, checks, eats and falls asleep again. The everyday reading (interpretation, limited analogy) concerns how much of what we notice and respond to is set by the strength of what arrives, and how much of the response unfolds without a deliberate chooser.

## Idea decisions

- **C040-I01 — Not everything that reaches us gets noticed.** Accepted as limited analogy. Based on Margaya lines 5–9.
- **C040-I02 — Responses that unfold without anyone deciding.** Accepted as limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 27–37; absorbs the candidate from C038 (Margaya C038 line 3, every act is a chain).
- Rejected candidate: *The mind returning to rest after each disturbance* (return to life-continuum). Too closely tied to the rebirth-linked "root mind" account (Margaya line 17).
- Rejected: the five natural orders as an idea. Mostly doctrinal examples; only used as context in C040-I02.
