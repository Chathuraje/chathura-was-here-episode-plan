# C059 — Results of sense-sphere wholesome action

## Central meaning

Sense-sphere wholesome action (කාමාවචර කුසල කර්ම) is grouped by door (body, speech, mind), as giving, virtue and meditation (දාන, සීල, භාවනා), and as ten bases of merit: giving, virtue, meditation, respect (අපචායන), service (වෙය්‍යාවච්ච), sharing merit (පත්තිදාන), rejoicing in others' merit (පත්තානුමෝදනා), listening to the teaching, teaching, and straightening one's view (Pradeepika lines 23–31, 33–283). Its results depend on whether the act is joined with understanding (three-rooted ත්‍රිහේතුක or two-rooted ද්විහේතුක) and whether it is superior (උත්කෘෂ්ට) or inferior (ඕමක). Superiority depends on the purity of the intentions before and after the act (Pradeepika lines 387–425; Margaya lines 5–15). The chapter also covers fine-material and immaterial wholesome action, the sixteen ways circumstances block or enable results, and where results arise.

## Essential distinctions

- **Before, during and after.** Giving involves three intentions: while gathering the gift (පූර්ව), while letting it go (මුඤ්චන), and when recalling it later (අපර) (Pradeepika lines 45–49). Weak before-and-after intentions weaken the result. Margaya (line 7) makes it concrete: a good act is inferior if done to look superior or put others down, if unwholesome thoughts arise just before or after, or if the person later thinks "that was foolish". It is superior if free of these and followed by repeated gladness that "I did something good".
- **Motive grades.** Giving and virtue are inferior when done for fame and praise, with self-exaltation and contempt for others, or for future wealth; middling when done for merit or one's own liberation; excellent when done for the welfare of all (lines 53–59, 153–157).
- **Kinds of giver.** Slave of giving (keeps the sweet, gives the unsweet), companion in giving (gives what one eats oneself), master of giving (eats the unsweet, gives the sweet) (lines 63–69). Unworthy giving is done carelessly, through others, without respect; worthy giving is prepared, respectful, by one's own hand, regular (lines 77–79).
- **Knowledge versus habit.** Small children raised among courteous elders who practise respect have wholesome minds even without knowing any benefit; doing it mindfully and reflecting on benefits makes it knowledge-associated (line 225). Service done from habit, by copying others or without knowing benefits is knowledge-dissociated (line 235).
- **Rejoicing needs detail.** Rejoicing in another's merit is complete only if the person rejoicing is told how the good act was done; merely saying "rejoice" cannot evoke the same wholesome intention (line 249).
- **Teaching carefully.** Teachers who departed from the texts said so, naming it as their own or their teacher's opinion, to avoid misleading listeners (line 271).
- **Sixteen conditions.** Birth, body, time and skill (ගති, උපධි, කාල, ප්‍රයෝග) can block or enable the results of both good and bad action (lines 303–361).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both sources agree on the three-rooted/two-rooted and superior/inferior grid and its rebirth results (Margaya lines 9–15; Pradeepika lines 381–407). Margaya supplies the concrete "that was foolish" regret and the self-exaltation example (line 7). Pradeepika supplies the donor types, grades of motive, the ten bases in detail and the sixteen conditions. Margaya says joyful or neutral feeling and prompted or unprompted mode do not govern the kind of result, and records a minority teachers' view to the contrary that most teachers do not accept (lines 11, 17). Pradeepika does not discuss this.

A possible internal tension in Pradeepika: line 411 lists giving to immoral people among the marks of inferior three-rooted action, while line 85 says a virtuous donor giving to a wicked recipient still gains great fruit through the donor's own purity. These may be reconcilable (line 411 combines several marks), but they are recorded here rather than resolved. Margaya's file also includes fine-material results (lines 19–35), which overlap with Pradeepika lines 437–485.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The result structure (rebirth in good destinations, three-rooted rebirth, lifespans in aeons, Brahma worlds, Pure Abodes) is doctrinal. The ranking of gifts by the recipient's spiritual attainment (lines 95–121) is doctrinal and should not become a hierarchy of worthy people. Merit transfer to dead relatives (lines 239–245) is a doctrinal and ritual practice. The sixteen conditions use examples of slaves, disability as blocked merit, and a man who prospers by flattering patrons and stealing skilfully (lines 327, 335, 345, 361). These reflect a historical and discriminatory framing and must not be used as premises. The prohibited gifts (line 81), including "gift of a woman", are historical.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source does not treat a good act as a single moment. What a person feels while preparing it, why they do it, and how they later look back on it all shape what the act becomes. Later regret can sour it; repeated gladness can strengthen it. The quality of a gift is also measured against what the giver keeps: giving away only what one would not want differs from giving the better share. Finally, some goodness is learned by copying before it is understood. Children who grow up among courteous elders already act respectfully without knowing why, and adults may serve out of habit or example. These readings are interpretation. They concern everyday giving and respect, not the doctrinal results.

## Idea decisions

- **C059-I01 — How later regret changes a past kindness.** Accepted. Based on before-and-after intentions and the "that was foolish" regret.
- **C059-I02 — Giving away only what you would not keep.** Accepted. Based on the three kinds of giver.
- *Kindness learned by copying before understanding* (lines 225, 235). Not issued as a card. It shares its mechanism with existing C011-I02 (Doing good by habit without asking why), which uses a parallel children-and-habit passage from C011. Lines 225 and 235 here should be added to C011-I02 as supporting evidence rather than duplicated. No ID was assigned.
- Rejected candidate: *Sharing gladness in a good deed you only heard about* (line 249). The human reading (vicarious joy needs the story) is plausible, but the passage is embedded in the doctrine of merit transfer, and the inferential bridge is long. Recorded as a held question in the batch report.
- Rejected candidate: *Doing good to be seen as better than others* (lines 53, 153; Margaya line 7). Shares a mechanism with C005-I01 (Same act, different reason) and C001-I02 (Learning for a title instead of for change). Also close to C011-I03, C012-I01 and C074-I03. Folded into C059-I01 as context.
- Rejected candidate: *Luck, timing and skill deciding whether good is rewarded* (lines 303–361). Doctrinal, and the examples are discriminatory.
- Rejected candidate: *Marking your own opinion when you depart from tradition* (line 271). A good research thread, but a single sentence of support.
