## Central meaning

Meditation subjects (කර්මස්ථාන) are the objects and exercises taken up in mental development. There are two kinds: calm (සමථ), which gathers the mind to one point and suppresses the hindrances, and insight (විදර්ශනා), which sees mind and matter as impermanent, unsatisfactory and not-self (Book 4 lines 33–41; Margaya lines 7–11). There are forty calm subjects: ten kasiṇas, ten foul objects, ten recollections, four immeasurables, one perception (repulsiveness of food), one analysis (four elements) and four formless objects (Book 4 lines 251–269; Margaya line 15). Development runs through preparatory, access and absorption stages, with preparatory, learning and counterpart signs (Margaya lines 68–82; Book 1 lines 47–65).

## Essential distinctions

- **Access versus absorption** (Book 4 lines 53–65). Access concentration holds the mind by suppressing hindrances while the absorption factors are still weak; absorption holds it through strong factors. Ten subjects (six recollections, recollection of peace, of death, food, four elements) reach only access (line 61). Each recollection passage explains why: the qualities recalled are deep and many (e.g. lines 490, 572, 612, 638, 652, 746).
- **Practice versus understanding** (lines 105–167). Painful/pleasant practice and slow/quick understanding are separate axes with separate conditions (suitability, preliminary duties, craving, defilements; skill, ignorance, faculties; plus prior-life practice).
- **Declining, stable, progressing, penetrating** concentration (lines 203–211).
- **Foul objects** suit specific kinds of lust (lines 307–330); only the first absorption is possible because strong applied thought is needed to hold a repulsive object (line 332); the marks of foulness are also present in the living body, hidden by ornament (line 334).
- **Recollections of virtue and generosity** are of one's own conduct. Virtue should be unbroken and not grasped as a bargain for heavenly rebirth or as purity by rule (line 604); the generosity practitioner must already be given to giving and gives that day (line 620). The six recollections succeed fully only for noble disciples, whose qualities are established (line 656); ordinary people may still recollect peace (line 746).
- **Food** (lines 766–863): four nutriments are defined, but only physical food is the object; ten aspects of repulsiveness; the son's-flesh simile of eating without taste-craving (lines 781, 857–861).
- **Four elements** (lines 865–1265): the butcher simile, in which "cow" gives way to "meat" once the carcass is divided, just as "being" gives way to "elements" (lines 873–879); four methods and thirteen aspects; the body as a machine run without a self (line 1099); benefits including enduring fear in wild places (line 1265).
- **Five benefits** of concentration (lines 1267–1296): pleasant abiding, basis for insight, supernormal knowledge, special rebirth, cessation attainment.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The three sources agree on the forty subjects, the two kinds of development and the three stages. Margaya gives the practical construction of kasiṇas (lines 19–33), which Book 4's extract omits; Book 4 gives the recollection, food and element methods in depth, which Margaya only lists. Book 1 supplies the cognitive-process view and the keen/dull distinction, complementing Book 4's practice/understanding conditions. Minor differences: the kasiṇa order (Book 4 line 255 puts light before space; Margaya line 19 the reverse) and the recollection order (Book 4 line 259 and line 360 put peace last; Margaya line 45 puts it seventh). Margaya line 9 says calm yields absorption, knowledges and good rebirth; Book 4 lines 1267–1296 give five benefits. No substantive conflict was found. Book 1 line 61 ("access comes together with absorption") refers to the absorption process, not to the ten access-only subjects of Book 4 line 61, so it is not a contradiction.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth benefits after each recollection, deities as witnesses, supernormal powers, the cessation attainment, prior-life practice as a condition, the paramāṇu measures and kalāpa counts, the world-destruction and snake-mouth similes, and the claim that the path ends ageing and death are doctrinal. Corpse contemplation and disgust toward food and the body are monastic training methods with clear potential for harm if generalised; the foul-object matching is gendered (line 283). None of these can be an idea premise.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe deliberate practices of attention. Some are hard to sustain and some are easy, and ease of effort is not the same as speed of understanding. A person can steady the mind by honestly going over what they have kept and given, provided the record is real and not held as a bargain. A craving can be loosened by following its object past the moment of enjoyment to what it cost and what it becomes. The cards below are interpretations beyond texts concerned with monastic meditation.

## Idea decisions

- **C085-I01 — A hard road does not mean slow understanding.** Accepted as a limited analogy (Book 4 lines 105–167; Book 1 lines 67, 85–95). Prior-life conditions excluded.
- **C085-I02 — Drawing steadiness from conduct you can honestly recall.** Accepted (Book 4 lines 582–656; Margaya lines 50–51).
- **C085-I03 — Following an appetite through to its aftermath.** Held (Book 4 lines 766–863). Built on disgust; risk of harmful framing of food.
- Rejected: *Imagining respected people present before a lapse* (Sangha recollection, line 580; Dhamma, line 496). Close to C023-I01 (restraint for self-respect or reputation); too brief in the source to stand alone.
- Rejected: *A cow becomes meat once it is cut up* (butcher simile, lines 873–879) and *attractive shapes hide the elements* (lines 1134–1138). Duplicates C001-I01 and the rejected candidate in C064.
- Rejected: *Seeing only elements makes fear bearable* (line 1265). One sentence, and depends on the doctrinal not-self analysis.
- Rejected: *Matching the foul object to the specific attraction* (lines 307–330). Doctrinal corpse practice, gendered, and overlaps C013-I03.
- Rejected: *Concentration that slides back when old perceptions return* (lines 203–211). Overlaps C013-I02.
- Rejected: *Book learning alone cannot see ultimate truths* (Margaya line 3; Book 4 line 486). Overlaps C073-I03 and C001-I02.
- Rejected: *Holding an image until it appears with eyes closed* (signs, Margaya line 82; Book 4 line 297). Technical, and overlaps C013-I01.
