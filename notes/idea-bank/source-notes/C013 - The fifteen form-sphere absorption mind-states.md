# C013 — The fifteen form-sphere absorption mind-states

## Central meaning

Form-sphere consciousness (රූපාවචර සිත්) is the absorption (ධ්‍යාන) consciousness developed through concentration practice, typically on a kasiṇa object. It is fivefold by absorption level and threefold by kind: wholesome (කුසල්), resultant (විපාක, arising as rebirth in the Brahma worlds) and functional (ක්‍රියා, in arahants), making fifteen. The five absorptions are defined by progressively fewer factors: applied thought (විතක්ක), sustained examination (විචාර), rapture (පීති), happiness (සුඛ), one-pointedness (එකග්ගතා), ending with equanimity and one-pointedness.

## Essential distinctions

- **Two meanings of ධ්‍යාන** (Pradeepika lines 48–54). It means close, sustained looking at an object (ආරම්මණ උපනිජ්ඣාන), illustrated by a person who wants to examine a thing carefully, keeps it near, turns from other objects and looks long, until every detail becomes clear (lines 48–52). It also means "burning" the opposing hindrances (නීවරණ) (line 54).
- **Four kinds of abandoning** (lines 56–66). Momentary displacement by wholesome states (තදඞ්ග), like a lamp that dispels darkness only while lit. Suppression by absorption (විෂ්කම්භණ), like moss parted from pond water that closes again when drawing stops; if absorption is lost, lust and the rest return. Cutting off by the path (සමුච්ඡේද), like lightning destroying a tree to its roots. Calming by fruition (පටිප්පස්සද්ධි), like pouring water on burnt rubbish to cool it.
- **Factor-to-hindrance pairing** (lines 79–91). One-pointedness counters sensual desire; rapture counters ill-will because anger does not arise while one is glad; applied thought counters sluggishness, like a strong man rousing a faint man and helping carry his load; happiness counters restlessness and remorse; sustained examination removes doubt by turning the object over thoroughly.
- **Progressive refinement** (lines 99–119). Each absorption is practised in five masteries (adverting, entering, resolving duration, emerging at will, reviewing, line 99). A factor that has done its work becomes unnecessary and coarse and is dropped (line 103: applied thought is no longer needed once sluggishness no longer arises).
- **Untrained mind** (line 27): restless, jumping between sense objects, arising and ceasing rapidly; set firmly on a good object it can do much.
- Margaya (lines 9–13) adds that ordinary one-pointedness is weak and cannot hold one object long, whereas absorption can hold it for hours or a whole day, and that applied thought sends the mind back again and again so it does not shrink back.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the fifteen-fold structure, the five factor sets, rebirth in the Brahma worlds as the resultant, arahants' states being functional, and feeling (four joyful, fifth equanimous; Pradeepika line 167, Margaya line 37). On prompting, Pradeepika (line 29, line 167) states all are prompted (සසංඛාරික); Margaya (line 39) says the question has no real use and they are counted as prompted only for convenience. This is a difference of emphasis, not a contradiction. Pradeepika alone gives the hindrance pairing, the four kinds of abandoning, qualifications for attainment (lines 121–151), the kasiṇa procedure (lines 153–165) and the supernormal knowledges (lines 191–201). Margaya alone states the duration claim and that such states arise only in few people with past merit (line 3).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth as a Brahma, the qualifications based on rebirth-consciousness and obstructions from past kamma (lines 125–141), the kasiṇa light growing to fill all directions (line 159), and the five supernormal knowledges (lines 191–201, including reading minds from the colour of blood in the heart-base, line 197) are doctrinal claims and cannot become idea premises. The hindrance pairing is a doctrinal analysis of meditative states, not a tested psychological model. Margaya's duration claim is not a measurable finding.

## Human meaning paragraph

A scattered mind picks up one attractive thing after another and cannot see any of them well. When attention is held on one object and brought back each time it slips, the object becomes clearer in its detail. Certain states push out others while they last: gladness leaves little room for resentment, and an effort to engage can lift heaviness. The sources are explicit that this displacement is not removal. Like a lamp or parted moss, the relief lasts only while the state is kept up. The everyday readings in the cards are interpretation beyond a text concerned with meditative absorption.

## Idea decisions

- **C013-I01 — Looking long enough for something to become clear.** Accepted. Based on the "close looking" meaning of absorption (Pradeepika lines 48–52), the untrained mind (line 27) and Margaya lines 9–13.
- **C013-I02 — Calm that lasts only while it is kept up.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the lamp and moss similes (Pradeepika lines 58–62).
- **C013-I03 — Answering each inner obstacle with its opposite.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the factor-to-hindrance pairing (lines 79–93).
- Rejected candidate: *Effort without the promised result turning to cynicism* (line 129: people who practise all their lives without attainment may become weary or unhinged and conclude the practice is empty). The cause the source gives is the type of rebirth-consciousness, so a secular rendering would alter the teaching. Recorded as a held question in the batch report.
- Rejected candidate: *Dropping a support once its job is done* (lines 103–115). It describes meditative stages and overlaps with the refinement pattern carried in C014-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Mastery includes being able to stop* (five masteries, line 99). Too thin in the source, which lists the masteries without explanation.
- Rejected: supernormal knowledges and Brahma rebirth (doctrinal).
