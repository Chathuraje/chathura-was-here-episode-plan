## Central meaning

The mind-continuum runs unbroken from existence to existence until final nirvana. Within one life, the rebirth-linking consciousness (ප්‍රතිසන්ධි), life-continuum (භවඞ්ග) and death consciousness (චුති) all take one and the same object, which is the object grasped by the near-death impulsions (මරණාසන්න ජවන) of the previous life. That object is one of three: the action itself (කම්ම), a sign of the action (කම්ම නිමිත්ත: something used in or connected with it), or a sign of the destination (ගති නිමිත්ත). Immediately after death consciousness, a new rebirth-linking consciousness arises. Nothing, mental or physical, passes from the old life to the new, yet the new life belongs to the same continuum and is spoken of as the same person (Pradeepika line 85; Margaya lines 15–17).

## Essential distinctions

- **Action as object** (Pradeepika line 33). A past harmful action (killing, stealing, sexual misconduct) that comes forward to give its result appears "as if it were being done anew", and five impulsions like those at the time of the act arise. It may also appear on and off for some days before death. A wholesome act (giving, virtue, meditation) likewise brings impulsions like those at the time of doing it (line 35).
- **Sign of action** (Pradeepika lines 45–55; Margaya line 3). The animal killed or the club used; for wholesome acts, the Buddha image, temple, Bodhi tree, flowers, lamps, incense, offerings. These can be past or present, and can reach any of the six doors: a Buddha image or offerings seen, sermons, merit-book reading or pirith heard, incense smelled, an offering tasted, requisites touched (line 51).
- **Memory and dream** (Margaya line 7). Deeds done in this life and remembered appear as recollection; forgotten deeds and past-life deeds, and the signs, appear "like seeing a dream". At that moment old deeds long buried "become new as if done that very moment".
- **Leaning toward what appears** (Margaya line 9). The dying person's mind bends toward whatever has appeared, because ignorance and craving are unabandoned.
- **Not brought by others** (Pradeepika line 29). These objects are not brought by mother, father, relatives, friends, teachers, or a powerful god or Brahma; they appear through the power of the rebirth-producing action.
- **Arahants** (Pradeepika line 73; Margaya line 7): no such objects appear before final passing.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Both agree on the three objects, the unbroken continuum, and that nothing transfers. Margaya adds the memory/dream contrast and the "old deeds become new" image; Pradeepika adds the six-door detail, the detail that the object can recur for days before death, and the fine-material and immaterial cases (lines 39, 57–59). Pradeepika line 71 records that some teachers hold destination signs reach only the mind door, a disagreement within the tradition. **Tension:** Pradeepika line 29 says relatives and teachers do not bring these objects, while line 51 lists sermons, pirith and offerings present at the deathbed as possible signs; the text does not reconcile these beyond attributing the appearance to karma. **Numerical difference:** Pradeepika line 87 says about sixteen life-continuum moments follow rebirth-linking, then a process with five greed impulsions of attachment to the new existence; Margaya's C062 file (line 7) gives fifteen or sixteen and seven greed impulsions.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Rebirth itself, destination signs (hell fires, celestial mansions, the mother's womb), the claim that even a fly struck by a hammer sees one of the three objects before dying (Margaya line 7), the causal role of the last object in the next birth, and the infant and old-age account of life-continuum moments (Pradeepika line 91) are doctrinal. None may become an idea premise.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source describes, doctrinally, a moment near death when a significant past act, or an object tied to it, returns to mind with the freshness of the moment it was done, sometimes recurring over days, and the mind leans toward it. The everyday reading below is interpretation and a limited analogy: people facing the end of life sometimes report that particular past actions, kind or harmful, come back vividly, including ones they had put out of mind, and that objects connected to those acts carry that weight. The source does not describe regret, reconciliation or life review as such; those are later research questions.

## Idea decisions

- **C061-I01 — Old deeds feeling new again near death.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika line 33 and Margaya line 7.
- Rejected candidate: *Being the same person with nothing carried over* (Margaya lines 15–17). The claim concerns rebirth continuity; carrying it into one life would substantially alter the teaching, and the parts-and-whole mechanism is already covered by C001-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *The world receding again in old age* (Pradeepika line 91). A doctrinal-physiological account; no human process beyond it.
- Deathbed sensations (Pradeepika line 51) are developed under C062-I01, where Margaya gives the fuller statement, with C061 as supporting concept.
