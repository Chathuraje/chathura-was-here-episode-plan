## Central meaning

This is a counting method (සංග්‍රහ නය). For each of the twelve unwholesome mind-states (eight greed-rooted, two aversion-rooted, two delusion-rooted), it lists which of the 52 mental factors are present. Fourteen factors are present in every unwholesome state: the seven universals, applied thought, sustained thought, energy, and the "delusion four" (delusion, shamelessness, fearlessness of wrongdoing, restlessness) (Pradeepika line 27; Margaya line 5). The other factors vary with the type of state.

## Essential distinctions

- **Greed-rooted states** (Pradeepika line 29; Margaya lines 7–23). Greed is always present. Wrong view is present only in the four view-associated states. Conceit (මානය) is possible only in the four view-dissociated states, and only occasionally. Joy (ප්‍රීතිය) is absent when the feeling is neutral. Sloth and torpor (ථීන, මිද්ධ) occur only occasionally, and only in prompted states.
- **Aversion-rooted states** (Pradeepika line 31; Margaya line 25). Aversion is always present and joy never is. Envy (ඉස්සා), stinginess (මච්ඡරිය) and remorse (කුක්කුච්ච) are occasional. Margaya adds that when they occur, only one of the three is present at a time.
- **Delusion-rooted states** (Pradeepika line 33, lines 85–91; Margaya lines 29–31). In the doubt-associated state, doubt (විචිකිච්ඡා) is present, *and therefore* (එම නිසා) decision (අධිමොක්ඛ) is absent. Joy is absent because the feeling is neutral. Neither delusion-rooted state has desire-to-act (ඡන්දය). The restlessness-associated state keeps decision, but still has no desire-to-act.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree (19, 19, 18, 18, 20, 21, 21, 20, 20, 22, 15, 15). The two sources number the states differently. Pradeepika groups them as five spontaneous and five prompted states (line 35). Margaya pairs each spontaneous state with its prompted form. This is a difference in arrangement, not in content. Complementary details: Margaya says envy, stinginess and remorse arise one at a time (line 25). Pradeepika gives the causal link between doubt and the absence of decision (line 33). Pradeepika marks conceit as occasional (line 29). Margaya line 13 lists it without that qualifier. This is a minor difference of emphasis.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The counts are a classification of momentary mind-states, not observable personality traits. Placement tells us which factors can co-occur. It does not explain why, except for the doubt–decision link. Conceit appearing only without wrong view is a placement rule with no reason given, so it is not carried into an idea.

## Human meaning paragraph

The table implies some structural facts about unwholesome moments. Where there is doubt, there is no settling on a decision and no wish to act. Confusion and restlessness lack the plain wish to do something. Envy, stinginess and remorse sit with displeasure and aversion, not with greed or joy, and they do not appear together. Everyday readings of these facts are interpretation. For example, envy can feel like wanting, yet here it is classed as a kind of displeasure.

## Idea decisions

- **C028-I01 — Doubt that leaves no room to decide.** Held. Pradeepika states the causal link directly (line 33). However, the C008 source note records that this doubt is specifically religious and doctrinal, and that Pradeepika (C008) excludes ordinary uncertainty. A practical-choice reading would alter the teaching. Possible merge into C021-I01 (deciding as the opposite of doubt).
- **C028-I02 — When envy is displeasure rather than wanting.** Merged into C007-I02 (Unable to bear another person's good fortune), which already defines envy as not bearing another's prosperity and places it with aversion. C028 adds only the placement detail: occasional, and one at a time with stinginess and remorse. See also C026-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Pride and fixed belief do not share a moment* (conceit only in view-dissociated states). This is a placement rule with no stated reason.
- Rejected candidate: *Needing a push brings heaviness* (sloth and torpor in prompted states). This is already covered by C006-I03, which uses the same Pradeepika point from C006.
