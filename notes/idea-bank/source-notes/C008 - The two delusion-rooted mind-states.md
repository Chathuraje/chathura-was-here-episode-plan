# C008 — The two delusion-rooted mind-states

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–70). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 1, PDF 51–54 (printed 40–43). Extract starts mid-page on PDF 51 / printed 40 (line 21). Page markers at line 32 (PDF 52 / printed 41), line 50 (PDF 53 / printed 42) and line 66 (PDF 54 / printed 43).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–24). Heading `# 9. මෝහමූල සිත් දෙක`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Delusion (මෝහ) darkens the mind so that it cannot know a matter correctly (Margaya line 3). It is present in all unwholesome states but strong in these two. The first is joined with doubt (විචිකිච්ඡා); the second with restlessness or agitation (උද්ධච්ච). Both have neutral feeling (උපෙක්ඛා) and both are unprompted.

## Essential distinctions

- **Why the feeling is neutral.** When delusion is strong, a pleasant object cannot be taken as pleasant nor an unpleasant one as unpleasant, so neither joy nor displeasure arises (Pradeepika line 30). Margaya (line 7) adds that the mind, unsure of the object and shaken by restlessness, cannot properly taste the object.
- **Which doubt.** Pradeepika (line 36) explicitly excludes ordinary doubt that arises from being unable to investigate an unknown matter. The unwholesome doubt is the eightfold doubt about the Buddha, the teaching, the community, the value of moral conduct, past and future births, and dependent origination (lines 38–58). Margaya (line 5) gives a shorter version: doubt about the Triple Gem, results of merit and demerit, rebirth.
- **Effect of doubt.** It obstructs wholesome action and is a hindrance (Pradeepika line 34). The doubting mind bends to sense pleasures rather than to the wholesome, and wholesome action done with doubt is not strong (Margaya line 16).
- **Restlessness.** Normally one-pointedness (එකග්ගතා) keeps the mind on its object until the mind ceases; sustained, this becomes concentration. Agitation weakens it, unsettles the other co-arising factors, and turns them off course. When strong, the person cannot think steadily about anything (Pradeepika lines 62–64). Restlessness is present in every unwholesome state (line 62).
- **Occasions of restlessness** (Margaya line 20): sudden matters, doing many tasks at once, working quickly.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the two types, neutral feeling and unprompted status. They give different reasons for unprompted status. Pradeepika (line 70) simply says they arise without urging. Margaya (line 9) says doubt and restlessness arise by nature in beings and are not produced by another's urging. They also differ on emphasis: Pradeepika says restlessness is in every unwholesome state; Margaya says delusion is. These are compatible (both can be true in the wider system) and are recorded, not merged. Margaya alone adds the weakness of these states' results, the occasions for restlessness, and the causes and remedy of doubt (line 18): criticising matters beyond ordinary knowledge and hearing shallow criticism increase doubt; the remedy is to develop faith.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The objects of the unwholesome doubt are religious and doctrinal. Pradeepika itself separates this doubt from ordinary uncertainty, so turning it into "doubt in general" would alter the teaching. Margaya's advice that doubt grows from criticism and is cured by faith must not become a claim that questioning is harmful. Claims about lower realms and result strength (Margaya lines 16, 20) are doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources describe two ways a mind fails to settle. In one, it cannot decide and so cannot commit. In the other, it is shaken so that it cannot stay with anything long enough to take it in, and even pleasant or unpleasant things register flatly. Margaya links the second to sudden demands, many tasks at once and hurry. The everyday reading is interpretation: agitation does not only feel uncomfortable, it may also reduce how fully a person registers and responds to what is in front of them.

## Idea decisions

- **C008-I01 — When hurry leaves nothing fully taken in.** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. Based on Pradeepika lines 30, 62–64 and Margaya lines 7 and 20.
- **C008-I02 — Acting on something you only half believe.** Held. Margaya line 16 supports the claim that an act done with doubt is weak, but the doubt concerned is specifically religious, and Pradeepika line 36 excludes ordinary uncertainty. A secular card would substantially change the teaching.
- Rejected candidate: *Criticism that breeds doubt* (Margaya line 18). Doctrinal and ethically risky; it could frame inquiry as a fault.
- Rejected candidate: the eight objects of doubt as separate ideas. Doctrinal content only.
