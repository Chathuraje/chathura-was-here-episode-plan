## Central meaning

Everything taught in the Abhidhamma reduces to four ultimate realities (පරමාර්ථ ධර්ම): consciousness (චිත්ත), mental factors (චෛතසික), matter (රූප) and nibbāna (නිර්වාණ). They are described as the last, indivisible constituents found when the world with its beings is analysed finely. They are not lumps of substance but forces or activities (Pradeepika line 34: ක්‍රියා මාත්‍ර වූ ශක්ති විශෙෂයකි; Margaya line 3: බල වේගයෝ), and so they have no shape such as long, short, round or square.

## Essential distinctions

- **Consciousness** is the capacity of knowing an object (Pradeepika line 36; Margaya line 9). Seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching and thinking are all consciousness. Margaya adds that no consciousness arises without an object, that each sense impact gives rise to a separate consciousness like separate drum sounds from separate strikes, and that each consciousness ceases immediately.
- **Mental factors** arise and cease together with consciousness, take the same object and share the same base (Pradeepika line 38; Margaya line 13). Margaya counts 52. No consciousness arises wholly without mental factors, but not every consciousness has all of them.
- **Matter** is 28 kinds, built on the four great elements (Pradeepika line 40; Margaya line 17). The word is glossed as that which is disturbed or altered by opposing conditions such as cold and heat. Large aggregations of it appear as trees, rocks and bodies, but Pradeepika calls these the altered appearance of a mass of activities. Matter is coarser than mind.
- **Nibbāna** is unconditioned, beyond birth, ageing and death, the peaceful happiness free of craving (Pradeepika lines 42–46; Margaya line 21). The other three are conditioned (සංඛත) and impermanent.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the four realities, their order and their basic definitions. Complementary detail: Margaya supplies the drum analogy, the count of 52 mental factors and the four shared marks of mental factors. Pradeepika explicitly classes nibbāna as a mental (නාම) reality, subtler than consciousness. An apparent tension: Pradeepika (line 34) says ultimate realities are not visible to the eye, while Margaya (line 17) says that one of the 28 kinds of matter, the colour element, is visible. This may be a difference of emphasis (Pradeepika speaks of the realities as grasped by insight) rather than a real conflict, but it is recorded, not resolved.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claim that all things reduce to these four is a doctrinal ontology, not physics. Pradeepika's statement that trees, rocks and bodies are "the altered appearance of a mass of activities" must not be equated with modern physics of energy or fields. Nibbāna is a soteriological goal and must not be rendered as calm, relief or contentment. The number of mental factors and kinds of matter are classification schemes.

## Human meaning paragraph

The concept is a map of what the teaching counts as real. It does not describe a human process with conditions, interpretation and response. The only human-level observations are that knowing always has an object, that experience comes as separate impacts rather than one continuous substance, and that what looks solid may be a pattern of activity. The first is too general to be a mechanism. The second is developed more fully in C003 and is used there. The third repeats C001-I01.

## Idea decisions

- **No suitable idea.** The concept is a technical classification of ultimate realities.
- Rejected candidate: *Solid things as patterns of activity* (Pradeepika line 40, Margaya line 3). Rejected because it duplicates the mechanism of C001-I01 and would invite false equivalence with physics.
- Rejected candidate: *Each impact gives its own moment of knowing* (Margaya line 9, drum analogy). Not given a separate card. It is cited as supporting material for C003-I01, where Pradeepika develops the same point into a human-level observation.
- Rejected candidate: *Knowing always needs something to know* (Margaya line 9). Too general to name a specific tension.
- Rejected candidate: anything built on nibbāna. Doctrinal goal; would be flattened into generic peace or letting go.
