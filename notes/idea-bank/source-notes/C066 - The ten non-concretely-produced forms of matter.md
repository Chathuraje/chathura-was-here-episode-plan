## Central meaning

Ten material phenomena are "not concretely produced" (අනිෂ්පන්න): they do not arise directly from karma, mind, temperature or nutriment, but are modes or marks of other matter (Margaya line 27). They are:

1. **Space as delimitation** (පරිච්ඡේද රූප / ආකාශ ධාතු), the gap that keeps material groups distinct.
2. **Two intimations** (විඤ්ඤත්ති): bodily and verbal, the special mode of mind-born matter by which a person's intention is shown to others.
3. **Three mutable qualities** (ලහුතා, මුදුතා, කම්මඤ්ඤතා): lightness, softness and workability of the body.
4. **Four characteristics** (ලක්ෂණ රූප): initial growth (උපචය), continuity (සන්තති), decay (ජරතා) and breaking up (අනිච්චතා).

## Essential distinctions

- **Movement vs. intimation.** The moving of limbs is not the intimation. The intimation is the special mode of mind-born air-element matter that moves them in the intended way (Pradeepika line 44; Margaya line 11).
- **Thinking about walking vs. walking.** Many mind-processes about how, where and in which direction to walk produce no walking. A further force, generated by the mind in line with the intention, must arise in the limbs, provided they are not weakened by illness, injury, or damage to or disconnection from the brain. Stopping happens when a new intention replaces that force with one for standing or sitting (Pradeepika lines 46–50). Margaya's image: like a match struck in a dark room lighting the whole room, the mind with effort fills the needed limbs with "wind" (line 11).
- **Sound vs. verbal intimation.** Radio and gramophone can reproduce sound groups like those of speech, "but there is no verbal intimation in them" (Pradeepika line 54). The intimation lasts one mind-moment and ends with the mind that produced it (lines 56–60). Syllables made at different places in rapid succession are heard as a word, and the listener grasps the meaning only if they have heard those sounds before (line 62).
- **Lightness, softness, workability** arise together. Heaviness, stiffness and unfitness come from unsuitable climate, mind or food, overwork, mental distress, lack of sleep, poison, or eating too little or too much. They lift with suitable conditions. The three arise together, and whichever counters the current problem is felt most (Pradeepika lines 80–106; Margaya line 19).
- **Growth hides decay.** In childhood growth is obvious and decay is not. After growth ends, decay becomes evident. Grey hair, lost teeth and wrinkles are not decay itself but signs that decay has occurred (Pradeepika line 137).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the list, on intimation as conveying intention, on the conditions for lightness and so on, on the four characteristics, and on lifespans (Pradeepika lines 141–149; Margaya line 29: intimation one mind-moment, growth, continuity and impermanence one small moment each, decay 49 small moments, the other 22 phenomena 17 mind-moments). Both count five "mutable" phenomena when intimations are included (Pradeepika line 76; Margaya line 21). Pradeepika adds the walking and stopping account, the radio and gramophone remark, the listener's familiarity, and the visible signs of ageing. Margaya adds the match-in-the-dark image and names a "good mind" (සිත හොඳ වීම) among conditions for lightness.

Genuine difference: on unbounded space (අජටාකාශ), Margaya (line 5) calls it the world-cavity in which all things and persons exist. Pradeepika (line 32) says the sky we see is not empty, being full of air, and that unbounded space is only where there is no air, light, heat or cold. The two are not reconciled here.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The mind-moment lifespans and the timing of growth (for example that all sense-decads are complete by day 77 of gestation, Pradeepika line 125) are doctrinal or historical embryology. The "mind-born air element" is a historical physiological account and must not be equated with nerves or muscles, even though Pradeepika mentions the brain. Rebirth-linked distinctions (womb-born vs. spontaneously arising beings, line 127) are doctrinal.

## Human meaning paragraph

Three human processes are supported. First, intending an action and the body actually starting it are different events. Deliberation alone produces nothing until a further mind-generated force moves the limbs, and a body's condition can block that force. Second, communication is an act: meaning travels on movement and sound, but a replay of the same sound lacks the momentary act of intending, and a listener understands only through prior familiarity. Third, wearing down is present from the start but hidden by growth, and what people call ageing is the late appearance of signs. My extensions to everyday life are marked in each card.

## Idea decisions

- **C066-I01 — Deciding to move is not yet moving.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Pradeepika lines 46–50; Margaya line 11. Related to C006-I03, which concerns being prompted to act on a desire, not the step from intention to bodily movement.
- **C066-I02 — Hearing the words without the act of meaning.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Pradeepika lines 36, 54–62; Margaya line 15.
- **C066-I03 — Ageing noticed only after growing stops.** Accepted. Pradeepika line 137; Margaya line 25. Supporting: C064 (fire element ripens and then wears down). Related to C039-I01 (steady replacement hiding change).
- Rejected candidate: *When the body feels heavy and unready* (lightness, softness, workability). Supported, but the account is historical physiology, and a card would drift into generic health advice. The same mechanism is already carded as C069-I01 (When mind, food and weather make the body heavy), so Pradeepika lines 80–106 and Margaya line 19 here could be added to that card as supporting sources.
- Rejected candidate: *The space that keeps things separate* (delimiting space). A metaphysical point with no human process in the text.
