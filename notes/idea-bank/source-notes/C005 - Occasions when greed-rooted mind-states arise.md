# C005 — Occasions when greed-rooted mind-states arise

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–44). Frontmatter declares Book 1, chapter 1, PDF 45–46 (printed 34–35). Page marker at line 44 (PDF 46 / printed 35). Content: the heading `##### ලෝභමූල සිත් අට` and a list of the eight states with Sinhala glosses. Nothing more.
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–23). Heading `# 6. ලෝභමූල සිත් ඇති වන අවස්ථා`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Margaya lists everyday occasions where greed-rooted states mostly arise. These include seven of the ten unwholesome courses of action; drinking, gambling and bribery; entertainment, adornment and fine food taken for enjoyment; earning, protecting and spending wealth; courtship and married affection; and raising, pampering and marrying off children. It says that for ordinary people living apart from the teaching, most of the day's mind-states are greed-rooted. It then draws a key line. The same outward activity (earning, dressing, eating, feeding animals) does not produce unwholesome states when it is done out of faith, loving-kindness or compassion, or with wisdom about its usefulness.

## Essential distinctions

- **The purpose decides, not the activity** (Margaya lines 9–21):
  - Food prepared or eaten for taste or to beautify the body → greed-rooted. Food prepared to relieve hunger and sustain life → not unwholesome.
  - Clothing worn to look beautiful, to attract others or to appear superior → greed-rooted. Clothing worn to cover the body, protect from cold, heat or insects → not unwholesome.
  - Wearing a good or valuable garment **to avoid the contempt** of people who would look down on poor clothing → explicitly *not* unwholesome (line 19). This is a subtle and important nuance.
  - Feeding animals or small children in order to enjoy watching them eat → greed-rooted. Feeding animals as a kindness → wholesome.
  - Catching beautiful animals, or raising them in order to look at their beauty → greed-rooted.
- **Family affection** (line 7): seeing, talking to and providing for a spouse and children, and raising, pampering and arranging marriages for children, are said to be *mostly* (බොහෝ සෙයින්) greed-rooted. The qualifier matters.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

Pradeepika's extract gives only the classification list. Margaya gives the occasions. There is no conflict. One possible text issue: in Pradeepika item 7 (line 40), the gloss reads “මෙහෙයීමක් ඇති සිත” (a state *with* prompting), but the Pali label is අසංඛාරික (unprompted), and item 8 is the prompted one. This looks like a transcription or print error. It does not affect the ideas and is recorded for human review rather than corrected.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The claim that most of an ordinary person's daily mind-states are greed-rooted is a doctrinal assessment, not an observation we can verify. Calling family love “greed-rooted” uses a technical term (attachment or clinging). It does not mean selfishness in everyday English, and it must not be rendered as a moral accusation against parents or spouses.

## Human meaning paragraph

The source proposes that an ordinary act has no fixed moral quality. Eating, dressing, earning or feeding an animal can be driven by enjoyment, display or clinging, or by need, protection, kindness or practical wisdom. One nuance stands out: dressing well so as not to be humiliated is treated differently from dressing well to be admired. The same page also says that much of warm family life involves attachment, while elsewhere (C006) such attachment is described as weak in result. The everyday reading is interpretation. What a person is trying to get from an act matters more than the act itself, and this cannot be seen from outside.

## Idea decisions

- **C005-I01 — Same act, different reason.** Accepted. Based on the food and clothing contrasts, including the avoiding-contempt nuance.
- **C005-I02 — Love that is also holding on.** Accepted. Based on family affection being mostly attachment-rooted.
- **C005-I03 — Feeding others for the joy of watching them.** Accepted. Kept separate from I01 because the mechanism involves another living being: care that may serve the carer's own pleasure.
- Rejected candidate: a list of vices (drinking, gambling, bribery). It is a topic list with no mechanism in the source.
- Merge check with C006: none needed. C006 covers inner distinctions (view, prompting). C005 covers purpose in outward acts.
