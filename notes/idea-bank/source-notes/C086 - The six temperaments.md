## Central meaning

Meditation subjects (කමටහන්, forty in all) are many so that each person can take the one that suits them. The choice is made by temperament (චරිතය), meaning a person's predominant nature. There are six: lust (රාග), hatred (ද්වේෂ), delusion (මෝහ), faith (ශ්‍රද්ධා), intelligence (බුද්ධි) and discursive thinking (විතර්ක). Each has typical signs, dominant defilements or virtues, suitable surroundings (සප්පාය) and suitable meditation subjects. The second half of both sources classifies the forty subjects by which absorptions (ධ්‍යාන) they can yield, by their objects, by the kind of sign (නිමිත්ත) they produce, and by how a teacher assigns them.

## Essential distinctions

- **Every person has all of these.** Margaya line 7: because lust, hatred and the rest exist in everyone, temperament is very hard to understand. Temperament is a matter of which is predominant, not of kind.
- **Paired resemblances** (Pradeepika lines 37, 77, 103; Margaya lines 15, 17, 19). Faith resembles lust: faith is "smooth" on the wholesome side as lust is on the unwholesome; faith searches for virtues as lust searches for sense objects; faith does not let go of what is beneficial as lust does not let go of what is harmful. Intelligence resembles hatred: both lack clinging affection; intelligence looks for faults that exist, hatred for faults that do not; intelligence pushes away formations as hatred pushes away beings. Delusion resembles discursive thinking: both are unsettled and do not sink into the object.
- **Signs of temperament** include gait, sitting, sleeping, waking, food preference and how one looks at a pleasing or displeasing sight (Pradeepika lines 39–41, 79–83, 107–111). The lustful person gazes long at a pleasing form, clings to small virtues and ignores real faults; the hating person is upset by small faults and ignores real virtues; the deluded person judges a sight only by others' opinions, blaming when they blame and praising when they praise (line 111).
- **Suitability runs against the temperament.** The lustful person is suited by a dirty, broken, bat-infested lodging, rough cloth, an uneven road and disrespectful villagers (line 73). The hating person is suited by a clean, decorated, comfortable lodging and a donor who serves fine food respectfully (line 101). The deluded person needs open, unconfined space (line 127); the thinking type needs a sheltered cave-like dwelling (line 191). Margaya explains why some subjects are unsuitable: loving-kindness may stir lust in a lustful person toward the persons imagined; contemplation of foulness may stir aversion in a hating person (line 25); a large kasina disc lets a thinker's mind run to its parts, while a small disc confuses a deluded mind (line 35).
- **The teacher's method** (Pradeepika line 278). A teacher who can read minds assigns a subject directly. One who cannot asks the student which states are strong in them, which subject feels easy, and toward which the mind inclines.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The two sources agree on the six temperaments, the resemblance pairs, the subject assignments (lust: ten foulness plus body-parts; hatred: four divine abidings plus four colour kasinas; delusion and thinking: breathing; faith: six recollections; intelligence: death, peace, food repulsiveness, four elements; all: remaining kasinas and four formless) and the kasina size rule. Pradeepika adds gait and sleep signs, lists of defilements, suitable lodgings, the teacher's method, and the six inclinations of bodhisattas (lines 260–276). Margaya adds a plain explanation of why subjects are unsuitable, and a positive list of lustful traits such as keeping work, body, clothes and home beautifully (line 9).

Genuine small differences: Pradeepika says the hating type likes sour food (line 83); Margaya says harsh or pungent food (කටුක, line 11). Margaya's hating type is fearful and changes quickly (line 11); Pradeepika does not say this. Absorption counts differ by method, not in substance: Pradeepika uses the fourfold jhāna scheme (lines 212–228), Margaya the fivefold (lines 45–51).

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The causes of temperament are doctrinal: past-life acts, arriving from hell, the nāga realm or animal birth, drinking in a past life, and the balance of the four elements (Pradeepika lines 37, 81, 105). The typology is a monastic assignment tool, not a personality science. Reading temperament from gait, sleep posture or food preference must never be applied to documentary subjects, and nobody should be labelled "a hating type" or "deluded". The claim that a teacher can read minds is doctrinal. Absorption counts, sign types and plane restrictions (lines 195–256) are technical and yield no human idea.

## Human meaning paragraph

The sources suggest that people differ in which inner pull dominates, while sharing all of them. Some good dispositions have the same shape as harmful ones and differ in direction: warm devotion clings as craving does, and discernment detaches and finds fault as hostility does. Some people take their judgements of praise and blame from the people around them. And the same surroundings or practice can steady one person and unsettle another, so help has to be matched to the person, often by asking what feels easy. Everything beyond the sources' own examples is interpretation.

## Idea decisions

- **C086-I01 — When a good trait resembles a harmful one.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the resemblance pairs in both sources.
- **C086-I02 — Praising and blaming because others do.** Accepted. Based on Pradeepika line 111 and Margaya lines 13 and 17.
- **C086-I03 — What steadies one person unsettles another.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on suitability lists and Margaya's explanation of unsuitable subjects. Related to C013-I03, but that card matches a counter-state to a momentary obstacle; this one concerns fitting surroundings to a person's lasting leaning, including the counter-intuitive pairing of comfort with the hostile type and austerity with the lustful type.
- Rejected: *Planning at night what the day will scatter* (thinking type, Pradeepika lines 185–187). Too thin, and close to C020-I03 and C074-I02.
- Rejected: *Keeping everything beautiful* (lustful type, Margaya line 9). The source treats care for beauty as a sign of lust, which would moralise ordinary craft and neatness.
- Rejected: temperament signs from gait and sleep. Diagnostic of persons; unsafe.
- Rejected: the six bodhisatta inclinations (lines 260–276) and absorption classifications. Doctrinal and technical.
