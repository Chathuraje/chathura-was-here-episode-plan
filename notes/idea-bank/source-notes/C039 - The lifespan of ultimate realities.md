# C039 — The lifespan of ultimate realities

## Sources and coverage

- `Abhidhammattha Pradeepika.md`: read in full (lines 1–33). Frontmatter: Book 1, chapter 4, PDF 315–316 (printed 305–306). Page marker at line 27 (PDF 316 / printed 306). Extract begins mid-page on PDF 315 (line 21).
- `Abhidharma Margaya.md`: read in full (lines 1–13). Heading `# 2. පරමාර්ථ ධර්මයන්ගේ ආයුෂ`. No page locators.

## Central meaning

Every mind-state and every unit of matter has three phases: arising (උත්පාද), standing (ඨිති) and dissolution (භඞ්ග). For a mind-state the three are equal and together form one mind-moment (චිත්තක්ෂණය), less than a hundred-thousandth of an eye-blink (Margaya line 3). Most kinds of matter last 17 mind-moments, i.e. 51 sub-moments: one arising, 49 standing, one dissolving (Pradeepika line 31; Margaya line 5). Six minor kinds of matter last less.

## Essential distinctions

- **Process minds and process-free minds** (Pradeepika line 23): process minds take objects from the six doors in this life; rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death minds take a kamma-related object from the previous life. Doctrinal.
- **Why things look lasting although their matter is momentary** (Margaya lines 7–11): a heap of sand seen from afar looks like one thing but is many grains; everything seen is a mass of material groups (රූප කලාප). Even a mustard seed contains countless groups. Objects appear unchanged for two reasons: not all groups break at once, and new groups arise in the same places as those that break. At any moment groups at every stage of their 51 sub-moments are present, so when some break, all the others remain.
- **A process takes as object a set of matter of equal age within the thing** (Margaya line 13), not all of it at once.

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the three phases and the 17-moment / 51-sub-moment span of matter with a long standing phase. Pradeepika names the six shorter-lived kinds of matter; Margaya only calls them "minor". Margaya alone provides the sand-heap and staggered-renewal explanation. No disagreement.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

The durations and counts are doctrinal and must not be compared with atomic physics, cell turnover or neural timing. The rebirth-linked objects of process-free minds are doctrinal. The sand-heap and renewal account is offered by the author to make a doctrinal claim acceptable; its use here is only as a limited analogy.

## Human meaning paragraph

The author anticipates a reader's objection: things around us clearly last for months and years. His answer is that apparent permanence can be produced by constant, staggered replacement. Because parts break and are renewed at different times and in the same places, nothing looks different. The everyday reading (interpretation, limited analogy) is that continuity people experience in a thing may be made of ongoing renewal that goes unnoticed precisely because it never happens all at once.

## Idea decisions

- **C039-I01 — When steady replacement looks like staying the same.** Accepted as limited analogy. Based on Margaya lines 7–11.
- Rejected candidate: *Mind as faster than matter* (equal-phase mind-moments versus long-standing matter). Pure doctrinal timing, no human mechanism.
- Overlap note: C001-I01 treats a whole as parts arranged. C039-I01 differs: it concerns how gradual renewal hides change over time, not what a whole is.
