## Central meaning

A door (ද්වාර) is a place of entry and exit. Besides the visible openings of the body (ඡිද්දද්වාර), there are six “doors” in the doctrinal sense (මණ්ඩද්වාර): the sensitive matter of eye, ear, nose, tongue and body, and the mind door. Objects enter through them and mind-states arise. Mind-states are then classified by door: those arising through doors (ද්වාරික, 70) and those free of doors (ද්වාරවිමුක්ත, 19), which take an object from the previous life and perform rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death functions. Further groupings: one-door (36), five-door (3), six-door (31), sometimes six-door and sometimes door-free (10), always door-free (9).

## Essential distinctions

- **Entry and exit.** Pradeepika (line 27): contact of the object with the door is “entry”; the arising of the mind-state that grasps it is entry; its ceasing is the object's “exit”. Without a door there is no entry of that kind of object, and the corresponding mind-states do not arise.
- **Two senses of “eye”.** Wisdom-eye (five kinds) and fleshly eye; the fleshly eye is the compound eyeball (සසම්භාර) and the subtle sensitive matter (ප්‍රසාද) on which it depends (lines 45–51). The same compound/sensitive split is given for ear, nose, tongue and body (lines 57–97); hair, nails and dry skin lack touch-sensitivity (line 97).
- **Mind door.** Pradeepika (line 101) identifies it precisely as the arrest-life-continuum moment (භවඞ්ගුපච්ඡේද) before mind-door adverting, supported by heart-base matter, and not the corresponding moment in a five-door process. Margaya (line 19) says more loosely that the life-continuum, the “natural mind”, is the mind door, and that it helps know both the five sense objects and many things outside the five senses.
- **Process detail.** A visible object lasts 17 mind-moments; a five-door process can contain at most 14 door-bound mind-states, a mind-door process at most 10; weak objects produce fewer (Pradeepika lines 115–119, 172).
- **Fine-material, immaterial and supramundane states arise only through the mind door** (Pradeepika line 190; Margaya line 33).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the six doors, the counts (46 per sense door, 67 for the mind door, 19 door-free, 36/3/31/10/9). Margaya explains why the eye-door list sums to 49 before removing the three investigating states counted twice (line 25). Complementary: Margaya gives a concrete image of the eye's sensitive spot, a place the size of a louse's head in the centre of the dark of the eye where images of objects fall (line 9); Pradeepika gives the kamma-born nine-fold material composition (line 49) and the 17-moment process.

Genuine differences, recorded without resolving:
1. **Meaning of “exit”.** Margaya (lines 5, 9) takes exit as the mind going out toward the object; Pradeepika (line 27) takes exit as the ceasing of the mind-state.
2. **What the mind door is.** Margaya (line 19) says the life-continuum mind; Pradeepika (line 101) restricts it to the arrest-life-continuum moment in a mind-door process only.
3. **Why the 19 are door-free.** Margaya (line 37) says because they themselves are the mind door; Pradeepika (lines 107, 176, 219–223) says because they take an object from the previous life, not from a door in this life.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Door-free states, objects from a previous life and heart-base matter are doctrinal. The physical accounts of sensitive matter (the louse-head-sized spot, nine kamma-born material qualities, 17-moment lifespan of matter) are historical doctrinal physiology and must not be presented as anatomy or neuroscience. The statement that no door means no entry must not be applied to people with sensory impairments as a claim about their experience; C034 Pradeepika line 47 separately links congenital blindness to weak past action, which strengthens this caution.

## Human meaning paragraph

The text treats each sense as a doorway through which a particular kind of object can enter and give rise to a mind-state, with a sixth doorway for what the mind takes directly. The most human-sounding element is Margaya's image of two-way traffic: objects come in while the mind goes out to meet them. But the source uses this chiefly to explain the word “door”, gives it no further development, and Pradeepika reads “exit” differently. Everything else is technical classification or doctrinal physiology.

## Idea decisions

No suitable direct human idea. Candidates considered and rejected:
- *Taking something in is also reaching out to it* (Margaya lines 5, 9). An etymological gloss, contradicted in detail by Pradeepika line 27; too thin to carry a mechanism without invention. Recorded as a held question in the batch report.
- *What has no doorway cannot enter experience* (Pradeepika line 27). Risks becoming a claim about sensory impairment; the source offers no human process beyond the classification.
- *Some things reach us only through the mind* (Margaya line 19; Pradeepika line 101). The mind-door objects listed (subtle matter, mind-states, nibbāna, concepts) are technical; memory or imagination is not discussed, so a card would be substitution rather than interpretation.
- Door-free mind-states, supramundane states via the mind door, and the counts: technical classification, no human idea.
- The perception-in-stages material in lines 115–119 is already used as context for C034-I01 and is not duplicated.
