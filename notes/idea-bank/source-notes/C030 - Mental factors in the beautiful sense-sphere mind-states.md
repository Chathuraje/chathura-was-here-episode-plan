## Central meaning

There are 24 beautiful sense-sphere states: eight wholesome, eight resultant and eight functional. The wholesome ones can have up to 38 factors: the 13 ethically variable factors and all 25 beautiful factors. No unwholesome factor is ever present (Pradeepika line 25). Pradeepika explains in practical terms when the optional factors appear (line 25):

- Joy is present when merit is done with a glad mind. It is absent when merit is done in an even, neutral way (මධ්‍යස්ථව).
- The three abstinences (right speech, right action, right livelihood) are present only in a moral-conduct act of restraint (සීලමය), and only one at a time.
- Compassion (කරුණා) arises when suffering beings are the object. Appreciative joy (මුදිතා) arises when happy beings are the object.
- Wisdom (පඤ්ඤින්ද්‍රිය) is present when the good act is done with understanding.
- In acts such as offering flowers, worship or listening to a sermon, neither the abstinences nor compassion and appreciative joy are present.

Of the five occasional factors (the three abstinences, compassion and appreciative joy), only one arises in a single mind-state (line 39). Margaya C031 line 17 confirms the result: the practical maximum is 34, not 38.

## Essential distinctions

- **Functional states** (arahants only). These have no abstinences, because abstinences are strictly wholesome (lines 29, 72). Compassion and appreciative joy remain possible.
- **Resultant states.** These have no abstinences, because they are results. They have no compassion or appreciative joy, because they cannot take happy or suffering beings as object (line 33; Margaya line 3).
- **Joy and wisdom** are independent variables that form the four pairs (දුක) (line 27).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The counts agree: wholesome 38/37/37/36, functional 35/34/34/33, resultant 33/32/32/31. Pradeepika supplies the situational examples. Margaya gives compact rules. Both sources include compassion and appreciative joy in neutral-feeling (upekkhā) sense-sphere wholesome and functional states (Pradeepika tables lines 63, 100; Margaya lines 5, 9). This bears on a tension recorded in C031. There, Pradeepika says neutral-feeling states cannot take happy or suffering beings as object.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Merit (පින්කම්), arahant-only functional states and "results of wholesome action" are doctrinal. The presence of joy or wisdom is a classification of a mind-moment. It is not a measure of how good a person is.

## Human meaning paragraph

A good act can be done gladly or with an even, unexcited mind. It can be done with understanding or without it. Which kind of goodwill arises depends on what is attended to: a person's suffering, or their happiness. Acts of restraint involve a specific refraining that ordinary devotional acts do not. Everyday readings of these points are interpretation. The source does not rank the glad form above the even form in this passage.

## Idea decisions

- **C030-I01 — Doing good without feeling glad about it.** Accepted. It is based on joy being present or absent in otherwise identical wholesome states (line 25, lines 47–55; Margaya line 3). This takes up the held question "doing good when it brings no joy" from the C011 source note. The card notes C011 Margaya's general ranking of joyful above neutral states.
- **C030-I02 — Kindness that changes with what we notice.** Accepted. It is based on compassion toward suffering and appreciative joy toward happiness, one at a time, and their absence in routine devotional acts (lines 25, 39).
- Rejected candidate: *Doing good without understanding why* (the wisdom-dissociated pair). It is already covered by C011-I02 (Doing good by habit without asking why).
- Rejected candidate: *Restraint exists only where there is something to refrain from*. The source's reason why arahants' functional states lack abstinence is that abstinences are "strictly wholesome", not the absence of temptation. The reading would alter the text.
