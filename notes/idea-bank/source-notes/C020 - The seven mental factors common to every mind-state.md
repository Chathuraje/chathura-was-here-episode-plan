# C020 — The seven mental factors common to every mind-state

## Central meaning

Seven factors arise in every mind-state without exception, whether wholesome, unwholesome, resultant or functional: contact (ඵස්ස), feeling (වේදනා), perception or recognition (සඤ්ඤා), volition (චේතනා), one-pointedness (ඒකග්ගතා), mental life faculty (ජීවිතින්ද්‍රිය) and attention (මනසිකාර). No mind-state arises with fewer (Pradeepika lines 27–29, 111; Margaya lines 3–5).

- **Contact.** The object “striking” the mind, which makes the other factors' work possible. It is listed first although all arise together (Pradeepika lines 33–41). Margaya: like someone groping in the dark who knows only what the hand touches (line 9).
- **Feeling.** Experiencing the “taste” of the object as pleasant, painful or neutral (Pradeepika line 47; Margaya line 13). Pleasant feeling brings the wish to experience it again, so craving grows. Painful feeling brings aversion (Pradeepika line 53).
- **Perception.** Recognising the object's marks. Each perception leaves a mark (ලකුණ) that a later mind uses to recognise the object again, and repeated marking over time makes recognition firm (Pradeepika line 61; Margaya line 17). Perception recognises only by marks such as white, red or blue, but does not know why the thing is white, red or blue (Pradeepika line 63).
- **Volition.** The urge or effort that makes every act happen. It is compared to a general who arms himself and deploys troops (Pradeepika line 73; Margaya line 23). Volition is called kamma. Pradeepika compares it to a debt that lasts until its result is paid (lines 75–79).
- **One-pointedness.** One object has many aspects. Seen from a hundred places, it looks a hundred ways. A single mind-state can take only one aspect at once, and one-pointedness fixes it on one (Margaya lines 27–29; Pradeepika line 83). In unwholesome states, one-pointedness co-occurs with restlessness (උද්ධච්ච). Pradeepika's image is a person riding a cart on a road full of potholes: shaken about, yet still travelling in the cart and not letting go of the object (line 89).
- **Mental life faculty.** Sustains co-arising mental states and the mind-stream. Pradeepika warns that it is mistakenly taken as an undying self, although it arises and ceases with each mind-state (line 97). Margaya says it keeps the mind-stream going, even through death, until final nirvāṇa (line 33).
- **Attention.** Pradeepika: turns the mind to one chosen object among many and keeps others out. It is wise (යෝනිසෝ) or unwise (අයෝනිසෝ) depending on whether it joins with understanding. Attention alone has no power to choose good or bad objects (lines 105–109). Margaya: it keeps returning the momentary mind-stream to objects, and it is hard to understand (line 37).

## Essential distinctions

- Recognition (perception) is separate from understanding. It knows marks, not causes (Pradeepika line 63).
- Contact, feeling and the others arise together. Listing contact first is not a claim of sequence (Pradeepika lines 33–37).
- One-pointedness in every mind is not the same as developed concentration (සමාධි). Concentration is the name mostly used for strong, developed one-pointedness (Margaya line 29; Pradeepika line 85).
- Attention is neutral. Its wise or unwise quality depends on what it joins with (Pradeepika line 109).

## Agreement, complementary detail and disagreement

The sources agree on the seven factors and their basic functions. They complement each other on one-pointedness: Margaya gives the hundred-viewpoints image, Pradeepika the pothole cart. There is a difference of emphasis on the mental life faculty. Pradeepika stresses that it is momentary and not a lasting self. Margaya stresses continuity through death until final nirvāṇa. These are not strictly contradictory, but they should not be merged into one claim. On attention, Pradeepika emphasises selecting one object and excluding others, while Margaya emphasises repeatedly returning the mind to objects. These are complementary.

## What cannot safely be carried into everyday language

Kamma as a debt paid in this or later lives, rebirth-linked objects at the life-continuum (Pradeepika lines 39, 75–79), the continuity of the life faculty through death (Margaya line 33) and the co-arising timing of factors within a mind-moment are doctrinal. The physical-contact analogy is explicitly *not* literal (Margaya line 9). None of this should be presented as neuroscience of perception or attention.

## Human meaning paragraph

Every moment of experience, as the sources describe it, includes a meeting with something, a pleasant, painful or neutral tone, recognition by familiar marks, an urge to act, a settling on one aspect, and a turning toward that object. In everyday terms (my interpretation), we recognise things by signs built up through repetition, which is fast but can stop short of understanding. We can only take one side of a thing at a time, so what we see depends on where we stand. And a mind can be agitated and still stay stuck on the same thing. These are limited analogies drawn from an analysis of momentary mind-states.

## Idea decisions

- **C020-I01 — Knowing the sign without knowing the reason.** Accepted. Perception builds recognition through repeated marks but does not know causes (Pradeepika lines 61–63; Margaya line 17).
- **C020-I02 — Seeing only one side of something at once.** Accepted. One-pointedness takes one aspect of a many-sided object (Margaya lines 27–29; Pradeepika line 83).
- **C020-I03 — Restless yet still gripping the same thing.** Accepted as a limited analogy. Based on the co-occurrence of restlessness and one-pointedness in unwholesome states (Pradeepika line 89). The link to worry or rumination is my extension and is labelled as such on the card.
- Rejected candidate: *Wanting again what once felt good* (Pradeepika line 53). This is a real mechanism, but here it is a single sentence without explanation. It is likely to be treated fully in later concepts (for example dependent origination), where a card would be better sourced. Flagged for the coordinator.
- Rejected candidate: *Wise and unwise attention* (Pradeepika line 109). Only named here, with no explanation. Its neutrality point is used as support for C019-I01.
- Rejected candidate: *Intention as a debt that must be repaid* (Pradeepika lines 75–79). This is a doctrinal claim about kamma and rebirth and cannot be a premise.
- Rejected candidate: *Mistaking what keeps us going for a lasting self* (Pradeepika line 97). The passage is short and turns on a doctrinal no-self claim. It may fit better with later concepts on self-view.
