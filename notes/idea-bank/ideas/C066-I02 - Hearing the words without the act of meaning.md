# C066-I02 — Hearing the words without the act of meaning

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Speaking to someone is a brief act of meaning carried on sound. A machine can reproduce the sound but not the act, and even live speech is understood only by a listener who already knows those sounds.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes "intimation" as the way a person who wants to make an intention known moves the body or speaks, and the way others come to know that intention. It then draws a sharp line. A radio or gramophone can reproduce sound just like the speech, "but there is no verbal intimation in it". The act of meaning ends with the moment of mind that produced it, while the sound lingers a little longer. The source also notes that syllables come so fast that they are heard as words, and that a listener grasps the meaning only if they have heard such sounds before. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that people who keep recordings, such as a voice message saved after a death or an old family cassette, may hear exactly the words while knowing that no one is meaning them now.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: families who keep and replay recorded voices and people who keep sound archives. The research would ask what listeners feel is present or missing when a voice plays, and what they must already know to understand it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we replay someone's voice, what do we receive, and what do we supply ourselves?

## Limits and alternatives

The source makes a technical point about a category of matter, not about grief or memory. Those applications are my extension. Many listeners experience a recording as truly carrying the speaker's intention, and a recording is real evidence of a past act of meaning. Understanding also depends on context, tone and relationship, not only on familiarity with sounds.
