# C099-I02 — Doing it once is not yet mastering it

**Status:** Merged into C088-I03 (*Mastering a stage at will before moving on*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards say that reaching a state once is different from being able to enter, hold, leave and review it at will. C099 adds the absorption-process detail. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C088-I03 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Reaching a state or skill for the first time is a single event. Being able to enter it, hold it, leave it and examine it whenever one chooses is a separate achievement, and the next step depends on that second one.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources give two versions of one mental process. The first time a meditator reaches absorption, the absorbed moment occurs only once, and the mind falls back. Later, entered deliberately, it repeats for as long as the person wishes. Between these lies practice in five abilities: turning attention to each part of the state at will, entering it quickly, keeping it for a chosen time, emerging at a chosen time, and reviewing it afterwards. The meditator must train these before seeking a deeper state, and the first state so practised supports everything higher. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that a first success can feel like arrival, yet is not yet something one can call on. Control includes leaving and looking back, not only getting in. The tension is between moving on after a breakthrough and making it reliable.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: learners in fields where a first success is memorable but repeatability is what counts, such as a first successful catch, pot or performance, and how they and their teachers judge when someone is ready for the next stage. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What changes between the day something first works and the day we can make it happen when we choose?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes meditative absorption, not crafts or sports. Its moment-by-moment counts are doctrinal, not a timing model. Some learners move ahead productively before mastering earlier stages. A first success that cannot be repeated may reflect luck, conditions or a teacher's help rather than a lack of practice.
