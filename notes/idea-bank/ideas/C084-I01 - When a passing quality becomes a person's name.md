# C084-I01 — When a passing quality becomes a person's name

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Words such as "the greedy one" or "the wise one" take a quality that really occurs and fasten it to a person as a lasting name. The label can then stay long after the quality has come and gone.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source sorts names by what they point to. One group uses something real to point to something conventional. Wisdom, faith, envy and greed count as real qualities, but "the wise one" or "the greedy one" points to a person, whom the source treats as a conventional unit. Its point is classification, not ethics. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that this shows how labels form: a real episode of envy or generosity is observed, turned into a description of who someone is, and passed on as if it named a fixed thing. Elsewhere this corpus describes such qualities as arising and passing moment by moment, so the tension lies between a quality that comes and goes and a name that stays.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: nicknames and reputations in schools, workplaces or extended families that grew from one remembered act or trait, how their bearers experienced them, and whether anyone shed one. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When does describing what someone did become deciding who they are?

## Limits and alternatives

The source makes no claim about labelling or stigma; that application is interpretation. It treats these names as valid conventions, and many labels are accurate, affectionate or useful. A stable trait may genuinely justify a lasting description, and people sometimes embrace their labels. The source's list includes a colour-based example ("the white man"); this card does not extend to race or appearance.
