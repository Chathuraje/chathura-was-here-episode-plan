# C013-I02 — Calm that lasts only while it is kept up

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Holding a troubling urge or emotion at bay through a good activity or a settled state is not the same as being rid of it. When the activity stops, what it held back can return.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source separates kinds of "abandoning". In the first, a wholesome state keeps its opposite from rising only while it lasts, like a lamp in a dark night: the dark returns the moment the lamp is put out. In the second, absorption pushes defilements such as lust further away, like moss on a pond parted by a pot or stick. While the absorption is kept, the moss stays apart; if it is lost, the moss spreads back. Both are contrasted with a third kind, like lightning destroying a tree to the root. My interpretation is that people often take calm or restraint maintained by a routine for a finished change, and are surprised or ashamed when the old pull returns after the routine lapses. The source treats the return not as a failure of character but as how suppression works.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who kept a strong urge or temper quiet through a demanding practice, such as sport, prayer, caregiving or steady work, and noticed what happened when that practice was interrupted by illness, migration or retirement. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When something we thought we had overcome comes back, what had actually been holding it away?

## Limits and alternatives

The similes describe meditative states and doctrinal defilements, not addiction or mood science. The source's "complete uprooting" belongs to the supramundane path (C016) and must not be offered as an achievable psychological outcome. A returning urge may reflect new stress or loss of support rather than the end of a practice, and some routines produce durable change the lamp image does not capture.
