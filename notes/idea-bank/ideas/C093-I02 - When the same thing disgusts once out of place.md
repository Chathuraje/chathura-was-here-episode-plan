# C093-I02 — When the same thing disgusts once out of place

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Something admired in one setting, such as hair on a head, can bring immediate disgust in another, such as in a bowl of rice. The reaction can follow what the thing is believed to be, or where it came from, even when it cannot be seen.

## Human mechanism and tension

To show that hair is repulsive, the source points to how people already act. Seeing anything hair-coloured in rice or porridge, they push it away. Eating at night, if they touch a plant fibre, they throw it out, thinking it is hair. City people who value cleanliness refuse vegetables grown on village sewage, because of where they grew. The source's point is doctrinal: the body really is foul, and meditators use this to weaken lust. My interpretation is not the source's claim. Its own examples suggest disgust depends heavily on category, origin and location. A fibre becomes disgusting only when taken for hair. The tension is that disgust feels like direct perception of the thing, while it may be tied to a belief about what the thing is and where it belongs.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: foods, materials or places that people accept or refuse depending on origin, such as recycled water, second-hand clothing or objects from a funeral house. The research would ask how learning where something came from changes a reaction. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How much of what repels us is the thing itself, and how much is what we think it is?

## Limits and alternatives

This reverses the source's use of the examples, which it treats as evidence of real foulness. The card must not be used to shame bodies or people. Alternative explanation: disgust at hair in food or sewage-grown vegetables may reasonably track real contamination risk. Cultural purity rules, not individual belief, may also drive the reaction.
