# C011-I01 — Knowledge that serves harm as easily as good

**Status:** Merged into C075-I01 (*When focus and effort serve a harmful aim*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. This card applies the same mechanism to learning and skill. See also C004-I01, where learning fails to reveal harm. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C075-I01 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Being learned or skilled does not decide whether knowledge will help or harm. A different kind of understanding, about what harms and benefits oneself and others, has to steer it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source separates two things ordinary speech calls "knowledge". Society regards the educated, worldly-skilled person as wise. Yet that cleverness can readily serve killing animals, making weapons and making poisons, harming oneself and others. The understanding that belongs to good action is different: it knows wholesome from unwholesome, moves away from harm, does good for self and others, and aims to rise in character, not only intellect. The mechanism, as I read it (interpretation), is that learning is a capacity without a direction of its own. The tension is that skill is admired on its own terms, while what it serves can go unasked, sometimes by the skilled person too.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people whose expertise can be turned either way, such as someone who knows farm chemicals or a craftsperson whose skill could make tools or weapons, and how they decide what work to accept. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What tells us what our knowledge should be used for, when the knowledge itself cannot?

## Limits and alternatives

In the source, the understanding that counts includes belief in karma and its results, so rendering it as general ethical understanding is a limited analogy. The examples must not be used to judge people in those occupations; making medicines or chemicals is often protective work. Harmful use of skill may come from economic pressure, orders or limited choice rather than lack of moral understanding.
