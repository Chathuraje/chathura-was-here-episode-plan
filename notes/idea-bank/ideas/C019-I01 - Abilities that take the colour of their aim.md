# C019-I01 — Abilities that take the colour of their aim

**Status:** Merged into C075-I01 (*When focus and effort serve a harmful aim*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards say that capacities such as focus and effort have no moral direction of their own. C019 extends this to interest and decisiveness. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C075-I01 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Focus, effort, interest and decisiveness are not good or bad in themselves. They become harmful or beneficial according to the purpose they serve.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes thirteen mental factors, including attention, concentration, effort, determination and the wish to act, that appear in both harmful and beneficial states of mind. Joined with a harmful mind they are wholly harmful; joined with a beneficial mind, wholly beneficial. Its image is a friend good at fitting in: with people who kill and steal he does the same, and with people who give he gives too. The source also says attention alone cannot choose good or bad objects. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people often admire a capacity itself, such as discipline or sharp focus, as if it guaranteed good outcomes. The source suggests the capacity serves whatever it is joined to. This goes beyond the text, which analyses single mind-moments, not lasting skills.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who used the same strength, such as organising ability, persuasion, stamina or technical skill, first for one purpose and later for a very different one. The inquiry would ask what, in their own account, changed: the ability or the aim. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we praise someone's drive or focus, what are we actually praising?

## Limits and alternatives

The source classifies momentary mind-states within a doctrinal ethics. It does not describe character, talent or learned skill, so this is an analogy. Skills are not fully neutral in practice: some are shaped by the setting in which they were learned, and practising a capacity may itself change a person's values. Another explanation for a change in how someone uses an ability is circumstance, such as opportunity, pressure or need, rather than a change of purpose.
