# C014-I02 — Moving beyond something while still hanging from it

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A step that goes beyond an earlier stage can still depend on that stage for its footing. Surpassing something and being free of it are not the same.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source's simile: in a great desert stands a pavilion whose floor is covered in filth. A man exhausted by the sun runs in, finds nowhere to stand, and hangs from a corner of the roof. A second man holds on to the first man's feet. A third hangs from the other corner, and a fourth holds the third man's feet. None touches the floor. The source maps this onto its states: the second attainment takes the first attainment itself as its object, and the fourth takes the third, even though what is taken had been given up as coarse. Each goes beyond what it rests on, yet cannot exist without it. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that people describe progress as leaving something behind, while their new position is held up by the very thing they claim to have passed. If the first man lets go, the second falls.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: a new craft style, school or movement defined by departing from an older one while still relying on its methods, people or memory; or a younger generation whose break from a family trade depends on what that trade built. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What in our past are we still holding on to in order to stand where we are now?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The desert means saṃsāra and the pavilion the formless worlds; these are doctrinal. The dependence in the source is about which mental state serves as object, not about social or historical influence. Many changes do become independent of their origins, and continuity may be chosen rather than hidden.
