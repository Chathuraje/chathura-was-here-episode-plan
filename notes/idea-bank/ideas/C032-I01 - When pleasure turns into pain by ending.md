# C032-I01 — When pleasure turns into pain by ending

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A pleasure that is not painful in itself can still become a source of pain, because it changes or comes to an end.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source distinguishes kinds of suffering. One is pain that is painful by its own nature. Another it calls the suffering of change. This covers pleasant feelings, including bodily pleasures, which "are not suffering by nature" but become a cause of suffering by turning over (පෙරලීමෙන්). Its extreme example is doctrinal: a bliss enjoyed for an immense time in the highest formless realm, at the moment it ends. The mechanism does not deny that the pleasure was real. It locates the pain in the ending. My interpretation is that the more fully something good has been enjoyed, the more its passing can hurt. People therefore face a real tension between enjoying fully and protecting themselves from loss. The source does not tell people to avoid pleasure. In this passage it only analyses where the pain comes from.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: transitions where a good period ends. Examples include the end of a festival season, a boom harvest or tourist season followed by a slump, retirement from valued work, or children leaving a busy home. The research would ask how people hold the memory of the good time alongside the ache of its ending. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Does knowing that a good time will end change how we live it?

## Limits and alternatives

This belongs to a doctrinal thesis that all conditioned feeling is suffering. The realm example is cosmology and must not be used as a premise. The source does not claim that people consciously anticipate loss. Pain at an ending may also come from practical hardship, such as lost income, from changed identity, or from grief for people rather than pleasure. Many people find that an ending deepens their appreciation rather than spoiling it. That is an equally valid finding.
