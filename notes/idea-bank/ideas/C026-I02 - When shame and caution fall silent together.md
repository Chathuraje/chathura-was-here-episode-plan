# C026-I02 — When shame and caution fall silent together

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Harmful acts may require that, at that moment, the harm is out of view and both the inner recoil of shame and the fear of consequences have gone quiet, along with a restless mind.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source explains why four factors accompany every unwholesome mind-state. If understanding that sees the fault, shame that is repelled by wrong, dread of wrong, or a mind settled on something good is present, the state does not arise. So every such state needs a confusion that hides the danger, an absence of shame, an absence of fear, and a shaking, restless mind. A related passage in another concept says that whoever does wrong does it through not seeing, as if bewildered. My interpretation beyond the text is that a person can hold values steadily in general yet, in a particular moment, not have them active. This sits in useful tension with C006-I01, where a person acts while knowing the cost: the two may differ between knowing in general and seeing in the moment.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people reflecting on a past act they regret, such as a harsh word, a dishonest shortcut or a violent moment, describing what they were aware of at the time, whether they felt hurried or unsettled, and when shame or caution returned. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Where do shame and caution go in the moment we do what we would normally never do?

## Limits and alternatives

This rests on a doctrinal claim about every unwholesome mind-moment, which cannot be observed. It must not be used to excuse harm as mere confusion or to diagnose anyone. People may act with full awareness and deliberate calm; social pressure, intoxication, fear, group dynamics or planning over time may explain the act better than momentary blindness.
