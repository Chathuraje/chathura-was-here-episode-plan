# C098-I01 — Unfairness that comes from fear, not favour

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A person can go wrong in what they do, or in what they leave undone, because they are afraid. Fear bends a decision just as wanting, hostility or confusion can.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source names four "wrong courses": doing what should not be done and not doing what should be done, when moved by desire, hatred, delusion or fear. Two features matter. Fear stands on the same level as favouritism and hostility, so the wrong is not only siding with a friend or against an enemy but also choosing what keeps oneself safe. And failing to act counts as a wrong course, not just acting badly. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that fear-driven unfairness is hard to see, because it looks like caution, neutrality or silence. Someone who acts from favour visibly takes a side; someone who keeps quiet from fear may seem to take none. The tension is between protecting oneself and doing what a situation requires. The source does not resolve it.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: roles in which people must judge, report, allocate or speak for others, and in which there may be a cost to doing so. The inquiry would ask how people recognise the moment when self-protection begins to decide the outcome, and what makes some of them act anyway. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we stay silent or step back, how do we tell prudence from fear deciding for us?

## Limits and alternatives

The card rests on a one-line definition with no examples. Its placement of these biases at stream-entry is doctrinal. Not acting can be wise: the danger may be real, or acting could harm others more. Silence may also come from uncertainty, loyalty or exhaustion rather than fear, and an observer cannot tell which.
