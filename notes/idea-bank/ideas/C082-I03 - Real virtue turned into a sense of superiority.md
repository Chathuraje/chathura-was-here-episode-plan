# C082-I03 — Real virtue turned into a sense of superiority

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Faith, good conduct, learning, generosity and understanding are genuine strengths. They can also become the very ground on which pride, contempt for others and a fixed self-image grow.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source gives these five qualities as strong supports for good acts. It then shows the same qualities supporting conceit: "I have faith; others do not have faith like mine", "I am virtuous", "I am learned", "I am a giver", "I am wise". It traces a chain from there. Raising oneself up brings craving, seeing others as lower brings aversion, and holding both brings confusion. Taking "I" as the one who does the good and owns its fruits is called a wrong view. A related passage shows a past gift savoured as "I made great merit" instead of being renewed. The quality itself stays good. The pride arises with it as its support. As I interpret it, beyond the text, the more someone excels at what a community values, the more material they have for comparison. Unlike C012-I01 and C074-I03, where comparison drives the act, here a strength already earned becomes the platform for looking down.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people recognised for devotion, service, scholarship or generosity in any faith or civic setting. The research could explore how they speak about those who lack these qualities, and how those around them experience it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How does someone hold a real strength without it becoming a measure against others?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's account of self-view and its claim that only worldly good can support such states are doctrinal. Nothing here shows that devout, learned or generous people are proud. Confidence, self-respect and frustration at genuine wrongdoing are not conceit. Observers may read pride into someone's role or manner. No subject should be morally labelled.
