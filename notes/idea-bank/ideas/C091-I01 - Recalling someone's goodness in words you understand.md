# C091-I01 — Recalling someone's goodness in words you understand

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Calling to mind the real qualities of someone deeply respected can lift and steady the mind, but only when the person grasps what they are recalling. Repeating words of praise one does not understand does not do the same work.

## Human mechanism and tension

The lay manual says not to try to recall every quality. Choose one or a few that your own mind clearly grasps, and reflect on them in any way you like. Reciting qualities memorised from books without understanding them is not this practice. The commentary describes what happens when recollection works: the mind stops being pushed by craving and anger, becomes upright, and a strong gladness arises that leads to calm and steadiness. The tension, in my interpretation beyond the text, lies between inherited forms of honouring someone, such as set verses and speeches, and the personal understanding that gives them their effect. A form can be said perfectly while nothing is recalled.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: remembrance practices for teachers, parents, elders or public figures, such as anniversaries, alms-givings in someone's memory or school founders' days. The inquiry would ask participants which specific quality they actually call to mind, and whether reciting the official words or remembering in their own terms changes how they feel. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What changes when we remember a person's goodness in our own words rather than in the words we were given?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's object is the Buddha, whose listed qualities include doctrinal claims such as omniscience; extending the practice to other people is interpretation. The described freedom from craving and anger is doctrinal, not measured. Recited formulas may carry real meaning through rhythm and community without word-by-word understanding. Gladness in remembrance may come from nostalgia or belonging instead. Admiration can also idealise a person.
