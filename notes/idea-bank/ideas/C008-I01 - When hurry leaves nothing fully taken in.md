# C008-I01 — When hurry leaves nothing fully taken in

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

An agitated mind, pulled by sudden demands or many tasks at once, may be unable to stay with anything long enough to take it in, so that even good or bad things register flatly.

## Human mechanism and tension

One source explains that a mind normally holds its object steadily, and that sustained steadiness becomes concentration. Agitation weakens that hold. It unsettles the rest of the mind and turns it off course, so the person cannot think steadily about any matter. The other source names when this kind of agitation tends to arise: when matters arise suddenly, when doing many tasks at once, and when working quickly. Both sources add that in this state the mind cannot properly "taste" its object. A pleasant thing is not received as pleasant or an unpleasant one as unpleasant, so the feeling stays neutral. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that hurry is often chosen to get more done or to live more fully, yet the source suggests it may flatten the very experience it is rushing toward.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: work or occasions with constant interruptions and parallel tasks, such as a busy market stall, a festival organiser's day, or a household during a wedding. The inquiry would ask what people later remember or felt about moments they were rushing through. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What do we miss experiencing when we are busy getting through it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes a doctrinal momentary mind-state, not a finding about multitasking or stress. It should not be equated with neuroscience. Flat feeling during busy periods may instead come from fatigue, practised routine, or a deliberate focus on the task. Some people report feeling most alive when busy. Memory of an event is not a reliable measure of how fully it was experienced.
