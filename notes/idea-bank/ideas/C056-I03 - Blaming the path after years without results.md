# C056-I03 — Blaming the path after years without results

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

When long, sincere effort brings no visible result, disappointment can harden into the conclusion that the path itself is false, without asking what else might have fallen short.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source tells of a devoted lay follower who gave generously and practised meditation for thirty years, hoping for a breakthrough, but gained "not even a glimmer". Disheartened, he reasoned that a genuine teaching would have produced results by now, so this one could not lead anywhere. The source notes that he never considered that his own understanding might be immature, and that this conviction filled his mind as he died. The mechanism is a turn from hope to contempt. Because the waiting was long, the missing result becomes evidence against the method rather than a question about fit, readiness or expectation. As I read it, the tension lies between two ways of bearing failure. Blaming the path protects self-respect. Questioning oneself risks self-blame. The source takes the second side, and a documentary inquiry should assume neither.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who spent many years on a discipline, craft, religious practice, exam or long venture without reaching what they hoped for. The research would ask how they now judge both the path and themselves. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

After years of effort with nothing to show, how does a person decide whether the path failed or they did?

## Limits and alternatives

The source assumes that the teaching is sound and the practitioner was at fault. That is a doctrinal position. Sometimes a method really is flawed, the promise was misleading or the conditions were unfair, and leaving is a clear-sighted choice. The source's account of his rebirth is excluded.
