# C075-I01 — When focus and effort serve a harmful aim

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Concentration, persistence and ingenuity are not good in themselves. The same capacities that carry a worthwhile task can make a harmful act more thorough and effective.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources call three capacities "mixed": directed thought, effort and one-pointed focus. In a wholesome mind they belong to a good path, in an unwholesome mind to a harmful one. One source says an act of killing contains its own sustained, focused attention. The other defines wrong effort as the exertion put into completing a harmful act, and wrong concentration as strong focus while doing it. The unwholesome collection adds that skilled people's ingenuity in harmful acts is delusion wearing the face of wisdom. The tension, in my interpretation, is that we praise focus, hard work and cleverness, while the source asks first what they serve. Admiration for competence can hide the direction of the work.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who look back on a time when their skill and dedication served something they now regret, such as a scam or illegal logging, describing how being good at it felt then, and whether they later redirected those skills. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we notice when our best abilities are working for something we would not choose?

## Limits and alternatives

The categories are doctrinal; "wrong" concentration is not a psychological measure. This card does not claim skilled people in harmful work are deluded or evil. Many act under coercion, poverty, deception or institutional pressure, or did not know the harm. Research must avoid labelling livelihoods or communities.
