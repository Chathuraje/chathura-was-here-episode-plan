# C003-I02 — When a tired body makes every act need pushing

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Whether an action arises readily or needs urging depends partly on the condition of body and mind and on practised skill. Ease and skill go with readiness; exhaustion and heaviness go with needing a push.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary defines two ways a state of mind can arise: keenly by its own nature, or only through one's own or another person's urging and encouragement. It then names conditions. The ready kind appears when mind and body are at ease and there is skill and good practice in the task. The urged kind appears when mind and body are exhausted and heavy. Elsewhere it adds that a mind resting on a tired, afflicted body is weakened. The source applies this to good and harmful states alike. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people often read a need for pushing as unwillingness or laziness. The source points instead to tiredness and practice. The same person may act eagerly when rested and skilled, and only with urging when worn down or new to the task.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations where rest, practice and readiness can be observed over time. Examples could be long harvest seasons, night-shift work, caregivers of sick relatives, or learners of a craft whose early hesitation becomes fluency. The research would ask people when a task "comes by itself" and when they need someone, or themselves, to push. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we need to be pushed, is it the will that is missing, or the strength and practice?

## Limits and alternatives

The source states these conditions briefly and does not claim they are the only causes. Alternative explanations for needing a push include fear, doubt, conflicting duties, lack of interest, illness or coercion. Seeing that someone needed urging does not show whether they were tired, unskilled or unwilling.
