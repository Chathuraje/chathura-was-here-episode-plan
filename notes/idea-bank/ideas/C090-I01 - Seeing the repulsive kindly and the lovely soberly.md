# C090-I01 — Seeing the repulsive kindly and the lovely soberly

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

How strongly something disgusts or attracts us is not entirely fixed. The source describes a practised ability to choose the frame through which the object is met.

## Human mechanism and tension

Among ten kinds of "power", the source places one that involves no feats. It is the ability to dwell unrepelled by something repulsive, repelled by something alluring, or even-minded toward both, as one chooses. It names the means. Goodwill can be extended toward the repulsive thing, or it can be viewed as mere physical elements. The alluring thing can be viewed as unattractive or impermanent. Or a person can meet both without joy from fond attachment and without aversion. The text treats this as mastery over one's own mind. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that people in some kinds of work and care may consciously shift the frame so that they are neither pushed away nor pulled in. The tension lies between an honest first reaction and a deliberate response.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who regularly handle what others avoid, such as those who wash the sick or the dead, clean drains or process fish and meat, or who work surrounded by what others covet. The inquiry would ask how they describe changing the way these things strike them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we stop flinching at something, or stop wanting it, what has actually changed in how we see it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source attributes this fully only to arahants, and that claim is doctrinal. Here it is used only as analogy. Reduced disgust may simply be habituation, exhaustion or emotional numbing, which differ from the goodwill the text describes. Suppressing disgust can also cause harm. Outsiders cannot tell which process is at work without the person's own account.
