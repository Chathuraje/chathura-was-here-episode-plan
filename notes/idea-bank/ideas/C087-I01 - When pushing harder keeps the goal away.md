# C087-I01 — When pushing harder keeps the goal away

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Extra effort at the moment a goal seems close can create the agitation that stops it being reached. Backing off to avoid that mistake can tip into slackness, which fails too.

## Human mechanism and tension

The commentary describes a meditator whose inner image has become steady. Some, seeing it, tighten their effort to reach full absorption quickly; restlessness arises from the excess effort and they cannot reach it. Others, having seen the harm of too much effort, fall into idleness and also fail. Only the one who develops the image with balanced effort succeeds. Elsewhere the text uses a fire image. To revive a dying fire, add dry fuel and blow; to calm a blaze, add damp fuel. And when the mind runs evenly, the practitioner should do nothing, like a charioteer who leaves well-paced horses alone. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the hardest moment is not the start but the approach to success, where eagerness is strongest and restraint feels like giving up.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: skills where a final step resists force, such as a potter centring clay, a musician before a performance, a batsman approaching a century or a student in a last exam week. Research would ask what changed when people stopped pushing, and how they avoided simply slackening. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do people tell, near the end of something difficult, whether they need more effort or less?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes a specific meditative transition in a doctrinal framework, not performance psychology. Failure near a goal may come from fatigue, fear of failure, outside pressure or simple lack of preparation rather than over-effort. In many tasks, pushing harder near the end is exactly what works.
