# C065-I01 — Feeling what you reached for

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

When we touch something, several qualities meet the skin at once, but we register only one. Which one depends on what we were expecting and on our own body, which is the measure against which hot and cold are felt.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source says hardness, heat and pressure strike the body together, yet one moment of touch-awareness takes up only one of them, usually the most prominent. When a person touches something expecting hardness or warmth, that expected quality is what is felt. Heat and cold are not absolute: a thing feels hot when it is warmer than one's own body and cold when it is cooler, and both are never felt at once. So touch, which feels like direct contact, is described as selective and measured against the toucher. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that hands that test fruit, cloth, clay or a child's forehead work through trained expectation and through their own temperature. That can make them accurate, and it can make them miss what they were not feeling for.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: work or care in which people judge by touch, such as checking ripeness, grading fabric, shaping clay, massage or nursing a sick relative. The research would ask what each person feels for and whether expectation has misled them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we reach out already knowing what we want to feel, what do our hands stop telling us?

## Limits and alternatives

This comes from a doctrinal analysis of matter, not experiment, and must not be presented as neuroscience. Missing a quality may come from fatigue, numbness, haste or lack of training rather than expectation. The selective role of wanting in sight is already carded in C009-I01. This card's distinct points are touch and the body as its own baseline.
