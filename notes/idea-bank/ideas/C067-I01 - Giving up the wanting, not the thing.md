# C067-I01 — Giving up the wanting, not the thing

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

What a person needs to let go of may not be an object or a body but the wanting and delight attached to it. Removing the thing and releasing the wanting are different acts.

## Human mechanism and tension

Within a technical list, the source says material things are not what must be abandoned. What must be abandoned is desire, passion, delight and craving concerning them. When the craving is gone, the thing is counted as abandoned, even though it is still there. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that people often deal with a pull by acting on the object: throwing it away, selling it or avoiding it. The source implies this does not by itself reach the wanting. A person could also keep the same house or the same body and change only how tightly they hold it. The text does not describe how the wanting is released, so that stays open.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people who decided whether to keep or part with something they were strongly attached to, such as family land, a vehicle, a collection, jewellery or a former way of dressing. The inquiry would ask whether parting with it changed the attachment, or the attachment changed while it stayed. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we get rid of something we crave, what happens to the craving?

## Limits and alternatives

The passage is two sentences in a doctrinal classification. It does not claim ordinary people can or should do this, and it gives no method. It must not become generic "let go" advice. People part with things for reasons unrelated to desire, such as debt or family duty. Continued ownership does not show attachment, and giving something away does not show freedom from it. Only a person's own account could suggest what changed.
