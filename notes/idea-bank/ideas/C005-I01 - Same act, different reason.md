# C005-I01 — Same act, different reason

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

An ordinary act such as dressing, eating or earning money has no fixed character. What it becomes depends on what the person is trying to get from it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source gives paired examples. Cooking or eating for taste, or to improve one's looks, arises from wanting. Cooking or eating to relieve hunger and stay alive does not. Clothing chosen to look beautiful, win others' attention or appear superior arises from wanting. Clothing chosen to cover the body or protect against cold, heat and insects does not. The source then adds a sharp nuance. Choosing a good, even valuable, garment *so as not to be looked down on* for poor clothing is also not treated as unwholesome. The difference between dressing to be admired and dressing to avoid humiliation is fine but real. From outside the two look identical. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people constantly judge others' choices by appearance, while the meaningful difference lies in a purpose only the person can report. Even the person may be unsure of their own mixed motives.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: occasions when people dress, cook or spend with care, such as preparing for a job interview, a wedding, a village festival, a first day at school or a visit to officials. The research would ask what they hope the effort will achieve or prevent. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we put care into how we look or what we serve, what are we really trying to secure?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's classification of wholesome and unwholesome is a doctrinal framework, not a psychological test. Motives are usually mixed, and people may give socially acceptable reasons. Enjoyment, cultural expression, creativity and respect for others are not captured by the source's binary and should not be flattened into “greed”. An observer cannot infer another's purpose from what they wear or eat.
