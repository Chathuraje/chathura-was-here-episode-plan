# C066-I03 — Ageing noticed only after growing stops

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Wearing down is present all along, but while a body is growing, growth hides it. What people later call ageing is the late appearance of signs of a process already under way.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source holds that all matter arises, decays and breaks up, and that neither arising nor decay can be seen directly. In childhood growth is obvious, so decay goes unnoticed. Once growth is complete, decay starts to show. The source then makes a careful distinction: grey hair, lost teeth and wrinkled skin are not decay itself, only signs that decay has happened. A related passage says the same ripening heat that matures and strengthens a young body later wears it down. The tension, as I read it beyond the text, lies between how change is lived and how it is noticed. People often feel that ageing suddenly arrives with a first grey hair or an old photograph. The source describes a continuous process that becomes visible only late.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: moments when people first notice signs of ageing in themselves or a parent, and how they describe when it "started". This could come up while looking through old photographs, when returning home after years away or in work that depends on physical strength. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When did we really begin to grow old, and why did we notice only later?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's micro-level timing of decay is doctrinal and must not be presented as biology. Ageing is not only decline: skill, experience and relationships may deepen, and the source's scheme does not address them. People may notice ageing late because of social cues, illness or life events rather than because growth masked it. Visible signs vary widely and do not reliably indicate health.
