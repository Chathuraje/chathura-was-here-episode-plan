# C007-I01 — When an obstacle to wanting becomes an enemy

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

When something stands between a person and what they want, the wanting can turn into hostility toward whatever stands in the way.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source gives a stark example. A person who strongly craves an animal's meat cannot have it while the animal lives. The living animal becomes the obstacle, a wish to end its life arises, and the source says that "from here onwards" the mind is no longer greed-rooted but hatred-rooted. Elsewhere it describes hatred as treating an object as hostile and wanting to remove or destroy it. So the push against the obstacle is a different state from the desire that started it, even though one grew out of the other. The tension, as I interpret it beyond the text, is that the person may still think of themselves as simply wanting something, while their attention has shifted to fighting what blocks it. The obstacle may be blameless: a living being, a rule, a delay, another person's need.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations where a goal is blocked and resentment grows toward the barrier rather than the goal. Examples might be a land or water dispute where a neighbour's claim stands in the way, frustration at an official process that delays a plan, or conflict within a family over who gets a shared resource. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

At what point does wanting something become fighting whatever is in the way?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes two separate mind-states in succession within its own doctrinal model. It is not a psychological theory of frustration and aggression, and it does not moralise meat eating as such. Hostility toward a barrier may instead come from a real injustice, fear of loss, or exhaustion, rather than from blocked desire. Behaviour cannot show which state a person was in.
