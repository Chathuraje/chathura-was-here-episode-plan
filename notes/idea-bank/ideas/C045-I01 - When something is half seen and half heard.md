# C045-I01 — When something is half seen and half heard

**Status:** Merged into C040-I01 (*Not everything that reaches us gets noticed*), which has the same mechanism: depth of processing set by the object's strength and conditions, with very faint objects only stirring the mind. This card keeps its ID and preserves two differences worth carrying into research on C040-I01. The first is Margaya's description of the middle zone as "seen and not seen, as in a dream". The second is the question of how reliable later recall of half-registered things is. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Contact with something does not guarantee that it is experienced. Depending on conditions, it can be fully taken in, registered faintly and dropped, or pass without being known at all.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes a gradient. When the sense, the object, light and attention are all strong, a sight or sound is "felt very well". When they are weaker, for example the object is small, far away or poorly lit, the mind registers the object but has no time to engage with it, and the result is weak knowing "like something seen in a dream, as if seen and not seen" or "as if heard and not heard". When they are weaker still, the object only disturbs the resting mind and nothing is known. The source calls these "empty" processes. Where no full response forms, it says, no ethically weighty action arises. My interpretation is that much of daily life passes in this half-registered zone. The tension is between how confidently people later say what they saw or heard and how faint the original contact may have been.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: moments where half-registered sights and sounds matter, such as a night-bus driver's glimpse of a figure at the roadside, a vendor half-hearing a call in a crowded market, or neighbours disagreeing about whether they heard something. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How much of what we pass through do we really see, and how sure can we be afterwards?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited analogy. The source's moment counts and its "no kamma" claim are doctrinal, and the description must not be equated with research on attention or subliminal perception. Faint recall may also come from memory reconstruction, fatigue, expectation or later suggestion, not from weak initial contact. Responsibility for what was half noticed remains an open question.
