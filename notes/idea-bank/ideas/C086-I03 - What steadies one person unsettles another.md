# C086-I03 — What steadies one person unsettles another

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

The same surroundings, comforts or practices can help one person settle and pull another off balance. What helps depends on which pull is already strong in that person.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources match surroundings and practices to a person's dominant leaning, and the matches often run against it. A person driven by desire is suited by a shabby lodging, rough cloth and people who show no respect. A person prone to anger is suited by a clean, beautiful, comfortable place and food served with care. A mind that easily becomes confused needs open space; a mind that runs from thought to thought needs a sheltered, enclosed one. One source explains the risk of a poor match: a kindness practice may stir desire in someone already full of desire, and dwelling on the unattractive may feed irritation in someone already irritable. A teacher who cannot see a student's mind asks which states are strong and what feels easy. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that well-meant help often assumes one remedy for everyone, and that the person's own report is a key guide.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: teachers, carers or rehabilitation workers who adjust a setting for different people, such as quiet corners versus open spaces or plain versus comfortable rooms, and how they find out what works for each. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we find out what kind of place or help actually steadies a particular person?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a monastic assignment scheme built on a doctrinal typology, not evidence about environments. Its claim that a desire-driven person benefits from squalor must not be used to justify poor conditions for anyone. People may respond differently because of health, culture, sensory needs or mood on the day rather than temperament.
