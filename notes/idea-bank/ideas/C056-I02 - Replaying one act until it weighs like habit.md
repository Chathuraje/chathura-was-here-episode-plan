# C056-I02 — Replaying one act until it weighs like habit

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

An act done once can come to carry the weight of a lifelong habit if it is gone over again and again in memory, whether with pride or with regret.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes habitual action in two ways. The first is plain repetition, such as a trade practised daily or acts done weekly, monthly or yearly, where each earlier act supports and strengthens the later ones. Occasional acts gain this weight only if they are large enough to be remembered. The second is less obvious. A deed done once, but recollected constantly, also counts as habitual. So does strong gladness over a good deed or burning remorse over a harmful one. In my interpretation, memory is treated as a form of doing. A single moment of courage or of harm can become a defining habit through return alone. Unlike ideas about regret as such, this applies equally to cherished good deeds.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people for whom one moment, such as an accident they caused, a word said in anger or one act of courage, has become a constant reference point in how they see themselves. The research would ask how and when they return to it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What does a single past act become when we keep going back to it?

## Limits and alternatives

The source's claim concerns the moral weight of action, not a psychological finding about rumination. Returning to a memory may be mourning, responsibility or learning rather than entrenchment. Trauma, blame imposed by others or unresolved consequences may keep a memory present regardless of choice.
