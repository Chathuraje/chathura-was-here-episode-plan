# C006-I01 — Knowing the cost and still wanting it

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Knowing that an action has consequences does not necessarily remove the desire to do it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source separates desire that comes with a belief justifying the act from desire that works without any such belief. In its third type of greed-rooted mind, the person does not deny that harmful actions bring harm or that good actions bring benefit. They act anyway because the desire is not restrained. The tension is between what a person understands and what they keep pursuing. Understanding is present, but it does not rule. My interpretation (not the source's wording) is that this gap is ordinary. It does not make someone permanently dishonest or weak, and it does not mean their understanding is fake. The source links the pattern partly to long habit and to wanting gain or avoiding trouble. Those two motives give later research something concrete to look for.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations where someone openly names the cost of a pursuit yet continues it. This could be spending a household cannot afford, a work ambition that strains family time, or holding on to a possession or position. These are proposed areas of inquiry, not cases documented by the source. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What keeps something desirable after we have seen clearly what it costs?

## Limits and alternatives

This is a limited application of one doctrinal distinction, not a translation of the full eightfold classification. The source's explanation involves habit across lifetimes, which is a doctrinal claim. An outwardly similar choice may come from necessity, social pressure, obligation or a lack of real alternatives rather than unrestrained desire. Behaviour alone cannot show that a person “knows the cost”. Only their own account, handled carefully, could suggest it.
