# C036-I02 — Senses stay in the present while thought travels

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting and touching can only meet what is happening now. Thinking can reach the past, the future and ideas that belong to no time at all.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source draws a firm line. Minds working through the eye, ear, nose, tongue or body take only a present object; a past or future sight or sound never reaches them. Minds working through the mind door take objects of every time, as well as concepts and things outside time. The source adds that a present sight reaches thought only after the sensing process has finished, so what the mind reflects on is always slightly behind what the senses met. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that a person's senses and thought can be anchored in different times: standing in a place while thinking of a memory or tomorrow. The source does not say the present is better. The tension is simply that the senses cannot follow thought into the past or future, and thought never quite catches the present as it happens.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: moments when people are physically present in a place charged with memory or expectation, such as returning to a childhood home, waiting at a hospital or arriving at a place about to change. The inquiry would ask what they notice with their senses and where their thoughts go. No person, event or outcome is established.

## Open human question

When we stand somewhere important, what do our senses hold on to while our thoughts are elsewhere?

## Limits and alternatives

The source classifies objects; it does not describe mind-wandering or advise living in the present. Its time units are doctrinal. Memory itself arguably shapes present perception (see C036-I01), so the separation may be less clean in experience. Absorption in thought may reflect grief, planning or creativity, not a lack of presence.
