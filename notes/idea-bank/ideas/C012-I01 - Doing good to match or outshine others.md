# C012-I01 — Doing good to match or outshine others

**Status:** Merged into C074-I03 (*When giving turns into outdoing others*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards describe a good deed pulled by comparison and the wish to outshine others. C012 adds that watching others can set off the deed in the first place. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C074-I03 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A good deed can be set off by watching others do good: by the sting of comparison and the wish to be praised, rather than by understanding why the deed matters.

## Human mechanism and tension

In a passage mostly about the results of good deeds, the source describes one kind of weak good. It is done without understanding, after seeing how others do good, out of pique or rivalry, and to win the world's praise. The source still classes these deeds as genuinely good, only weak. The mechanism, as I interpret it, is social: another person's visible generosity becomes a standard, and matching or exceeding it becomes the motive. The tension is that comparison and the wish for approval can still benefit those who receive, while shifting the giver's attention from the recipient to the audience. Unlike a generous act later interrupted by pride, here comparison is where the act begins.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: settings where giving is public, such as donation lists read aloud, named plaques, festival offerings or charity posts online. The research would ask givers what first moved them and how others' giving shaped their choices. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When someone else's generosity moves us to give, what exactly have we been moved by?

## Limits and alternatives

The source links weak good to future hardship; that rebirth claim is doctrinal, excluded, and must never be applied to real people. The idea rests on one sentence, and the word rendered "pique or rivalry" (තරහ) usually means anger, so its sense needs checking. Alternatively, others' generosity may inspire rather than provoke, and public recognition may follow community custom rather than vanity.
