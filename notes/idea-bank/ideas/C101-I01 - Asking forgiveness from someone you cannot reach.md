# C101-I01 — Asking forgiveness from someone you cannot reach

**Status:** Merged into C089-I03 (*Seeking forgiveness when the person is out of reach*) during consolidation on 2026-09-17. Both cards come from the same Book 4 passage (the porridge story and apologising to someone out of reach), which the extraction repeats in two concept folders. This card keeps its ID as an alias and still holds its own source evidence. Use C089-I03 as the main card. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A wrong done by words can be repaired by asking the wronged person's forgiveness. When that person cannot be faced, is far away or has died, the apology is still directed toward them in some form.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source tells of a young man in religious training who saw an elder sit on a log to drink hot gruel and mocked him as a hungry old man who had shamed them. The elder was easing a stomach pain. Later the elder told him he had spoken against a worthy person, and the young man asked forgiveness. The source then sets out graded ways to apologise: in person, through a messenger, before others, or, if the person cannot be found, by confessing remorse to a learned elder and bowing toward the direction the person went. If they have died, one goes to their deathbed or cremation place. My interpretation is that the mechanism is relational: remorse alone is not treated as enough, and the apology must be addressed to someone. The tension is between a wrong that needs the other person and circumstances that remove them.

## Open human question

What does an apology need in order to feel complete when the person it is owed to cannot answer?

## Limits and alternatives

In the source, the offence is doctrinally grave because of the spiritual standing of the person insulted. That claim cannot be carried forward. A symbolic apology may relieve the one who apologises without repairing anything for the one harmed. People may instead seek repair through changed behaviour.
