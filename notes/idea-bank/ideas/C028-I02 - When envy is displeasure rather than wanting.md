# C028-I02 — When envy is displeasure rather than wanting

**Status:** Merged into C007-I02 (Unable to bear another person's good fortune). That card already defines envy as not bearing another's prosperity and places it with aversion. C028 adds only placement detail, which is preserved here: envy, stinginess and remorse are occasional in the displeasure states and arise one at a time. See also C026-I01 on stinginess. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Envy may be less a desire for what someone else has than a displeasure at their having it. Stinginess, likewise, may be less a love of one's own things than a displeasure at sharing them.

## Human mechanism and tension

In the source's table, envy, stinginess and remorse never appear in the greed-rooted mind-states. They appear only in the two aversion-rooted states, which always carry displeasure (unhappy mental feeling) and never joy. They are occasional there, and only one of the three is present at a time. The classification suggests (this is my interpretation, not the source's explanation) that envy belongs with resisting and disliking, not with wanting. Everyday speech often treats envy as wanting another's success or possessions. The source's placement instead points to the unhappiness of seeing it. The difference matters. Wanting can be satisfied by getting. Displeasure at another's good may remain even when one has enough.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: situations of comparison between neighbours, siblings or colleagues. Examples include a new house built, a child's exam success, or a promotion. The research would explore whether the difficult feeling people describe is about wanting the same thing, or about the other person having it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When someone else's good fortune hurts, is it because we want it, or because they have it?

## Limits and alternatives

The card rests only on where envy is placed in a table. Neither source in C028 defines envy or explains the placement. Envy, admiration, ambition and a sense of injustice can look alike from outside. Resentment of another's gain may be a reasonable response to unfairness, not envy. People should not be labelled envious on the basis of observed behaviour.
