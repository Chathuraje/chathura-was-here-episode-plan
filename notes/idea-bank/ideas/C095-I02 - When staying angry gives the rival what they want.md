# C095-I02 — When staying angry gives the rival what they want

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Holding on to anger at someone who harmed us can bring on ourselves the very losses that person might wish on us. The injury continues, now carried out by our own mind.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source quotes a teaching on what an enemy wishes for an enemy: that they look ugly, live in discomfort, lose wealth, possessions, good name and friends. (A seventh wish concerns rebirth.) An angry person, it says, brings these on themselves. It then gives self-questions. The enemy hurt your body; why hurt your own mind, which they cannot reach? If they wanted to provoke you, why fulfil their wish? Your anger may or may not hurt them, but it hurts you now. Anger is like picking up hot coals to throw at someone, which burns your own hand first, or throwing dust into the wind. My interpretation is that the passage reframes resentment as continuing the harm rather than answering it. The tension is that anger feels like loyalty to oneself or to justice, while these reflections present it as doing the rival's work.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people living with a long grudge after a land dispute or inheritance quarrel. The research would ask what the grudge has cost them in ease, friendships or livelihood, and whether they ever saw it as serving the other side. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we keep a grievance alive, whose purposes does it end up serving?

## Limits and alternatives

The source offers these as meditative reflections, alongside doctrinal ones about hell and rebirth, not as findings about health. The card must not suggest victims should stop naming harm or seeking justice. Alternative explanations: anger can protect, mobilise action against real wrongs, or mark a boundary. Lost ease and lost friends can equally result from the original harm itself.
