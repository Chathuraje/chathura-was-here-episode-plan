# C058-I02 — When annoyance hardens into wishing someone harm

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Feeling irritated with someone, or admiring what they own, is different from settling on a wish that they be ruined or that their things become yours. The step between the two happens entirely inside the mind.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source treats some harmful actions as purely mental. For ill will, it separates mere anger, which it treats as minor, from the full act of wishing a being's destruction. That wish arises when someone appears as something unwanted and displeasure turns into "may they perish". For covetousness, it separates relish for another's property from the strong wish to make it one's own. It also says greed and hatred that stay only in the mind are strong, while those expressed through body or speech are weaker. My interpretation is that the text marks a threshold. A feeling passes; a wish takes a direction and can be held, returned to and fed. The tension is that nothing visible happens, so the person may not treat it as an act at all.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: long-running disputes over land, boundaries, inheritance or a lost job, in which people describe how their feelings toward a neighbour, relative or rival changed over years. The research could ask what turned passing resentment into a lasting wish, and what loosened it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What turns a passing flash of resentment into a wish we keep carrying?

## Limits and alternatives

The claim that unexpressed ill will is "strong" concerns karmic result in the doctrine, not a measured psychological effect, and it is not a claim that thoughts cause harm to others. The source's categories are not clinical accounts of anger. Lasting resentment may reflect a real, unresolved injustice rather than a personal failing. People's inner wishes cannot be read from behaviour and should only be explored through their own account.
