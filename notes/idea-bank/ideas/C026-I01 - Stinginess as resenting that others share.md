# C026-I01 — Stinginess as resenting that others share

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Refusing to share may be driven less by enjoying what one owns than by an irritation at the thought of others having it too.

## Human mechanism and tension

One source asks why stinginess is grouped with aversion rather than with greed, since it clearly grows out of attachment to one's own things. Its answer is that stinginess operates as *not tolerating* one's own things becoming common to others. The feeling at its centre is resistance, not pleasure. The other source places stinginess in the group that "corrupts the mind" alongside aversion, envy and remorse, and says it never arises without aversion, only sometimes, and not at the same moment as envy or remorse. The human tension, which is my interpretation beyond the text, is that holding on is usually explained in terms of love or need for things, while this account points to something sharper: a discomfort at others sharing, using or matching what is "mine". That might explain why stinginess can extend to things one does not need.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: shared resources where people describe tension over access, such as a family property, a water source, a well-used tool or vehicle, a trade skill or recipe, or a spot in a market, and what they felt when others began to use it. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When we refuse to share, is it the thing we are protecting or our sense that it is ours alone?

## Limits and alternatives

The classification concerns single mind-moments and does not describe a person's character. Not every refusal to share is stinginess: protecting a livelihood, caring for limited resources, fairness to those who contributed, past exploitation or cultural rules about property may be sound reasons. A rejected C022 candidate (guarding what one does not need) overlaps partly.
