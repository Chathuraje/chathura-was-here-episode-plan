# C027-I01 — Not harming is a different act from helping

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Holding back from a particular wrong is a separate act, tied to a specific occasion, and is not produced by doing good deeds such as giving or kind speech.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources say the factors of abstinence are absent from the good mind-state involved in giving, worship, preaching or listening to teaching. They arise only when someone actually refrains from a wrong. One source adds that abstinence happens one at a time: refraining from lying is not refraining from slander, and refraining from a spoken wrong is not refraining from a bodily one. The companion concept makes the same point from another side: speaking kindly, reading good books or praising others is meritorious but is not abstinence, because nothing is being refrained from. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people and communities often measure goodness by visible contributions, while the moment of declining a particular harm is invisible, specific and not covered by generosity elsewhere. Someone can be generous and still, at another moment, lie.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people known for generosity or public service, or ordinary people, describing a specific occasion when they declined something harmful, and whether they see that as part of the same "goodness" as their giving or as something separate. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Does being generous make it any easier to refuse the particular wrong in front of us?

## Limits and alternatives

The one-at-a-time rule concerns single mind-moments in a doctrinal classification; it does not say a person can only avoid one kind of wrong in a day, or that giving has no bearing on character. Habits of generosity and restraint may in practice reinforce each other. The card should not suggest that generous people are secretly hypocritical.
