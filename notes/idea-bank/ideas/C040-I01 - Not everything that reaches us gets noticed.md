# C040-I01 — Not everything that reaches us gets noticed

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Things that reach the senses are processed to very different depths. A vivid thing sets off a full response, while a faint one may only stir the mind and leave nothing behind.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source sorts sense objects into four grades. A very great object is very prominent because it is large, well lit or near; the lower grades are less and less evident. The fuller the object, the more mental steps arise. A very slight object only makes the resting mind waver; no process of knowing occurs at all. The source also gives a second, technical criterion based on how late in its short life the object strikes the sense. Both accounts agree on the outcome: depth of processing depends on the object's strength, not on a choice. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that much of what touches a person passes without being known, and what gets noticed is shaped by size, light and nearness rather than by importance. The tension is that something significant but faint, distant or poorly lit may never be registered, while something trivial and close fills the mind.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: places where slow or quiet changes go unnoticed beside louder ones, such as a gradually drying stream near a busy road, a quiet neighbour's needs in a crowded household, or small wear in a structure people pass daily. The inquiry would ask what made something finally noticeable. No person, event or outcome is established.

## Open human question

What in our surroundings reaches us every day without ever becoming something we notice?

## Limits and alternatives

The grades and counts are doctrinal, not attention science. The source does not discuss importance or neglect; that is my extension. Noticing also depends on training, expectation, care and emotional state, which this account does not address. Not noticing something is not a moral failure.
