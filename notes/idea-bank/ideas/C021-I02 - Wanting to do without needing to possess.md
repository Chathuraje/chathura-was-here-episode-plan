# C021-I02 — Wanting to do without needing to possess

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Wanting to do something is not the same as clinging to it. The same wish to act can feed possessiveness or work against it.

## Human mechanism and tension

The sources separate two things that ordinary speech often calls “desire”. One is the wish to do, to take up an object or to act. The other is clinging, which the sources call greed or craving. The wish to do looks a little like greed, one source admits, but it has no clinging in it. It is “a wanting free of attachment”. Its character comes from the state it joins. Alongside greed it supports greed. In a state opposed to greed it works against greed. One source adds that when confusion or doubt grips the mind, this wish does not arise at all. The tension, in my interpretation, is that people are often told to want less, as if all wanting were the problem. The source suggests the wish to act is needed; the question is whether clinging rides along.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people deeply motivated to make or do something, such as a craft, a garden, a community project or a skill, who describe their relationship to the result. The inquiry would ask how the wish to do and the need to keep or own show up differently. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Where does the wish to make something end, and the need to own it begin?

## Limits and alternatives

The distinction is doctrinal and concerns momentary mental factors, not a test for telling motivation from attachment in anyone else. Motives are usually mixed, and a person may sincerely describe their work as unattached while clinging to recognition or control. Another explanation for loose attachment to results is necessity or lack of power over them, rather than a different kind of wanting.
