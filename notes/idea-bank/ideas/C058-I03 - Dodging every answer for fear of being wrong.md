# C058-I03 — Dodging every answer for fear of being wrong

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A person who does not really know what is right may avoid ever saying anything definite. Each question is met with "not this, not that, not neither", and the fear behind the evasion can take several forms.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes four "eel-wriggling" positions, named after a fish that slips away when grabbed. In three of them, the person does not truly know whether an act is good or harmful. The first fears that giving a judgement would be a lie. The second fears that taking a position would bind them to it. The third fears being questioned by sharp, learned people and is ashamed of being exposed. The fourth simply lacks understanding and answers everything with layered denials. The source counts all of these as mistaken views, not as humility. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the tension is between honest uncertainty and self-protection. Refusing to answer can look careful, yet it can also shield a person from being tested, and it leaves others with nothing to rely on.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: roles where people are regularly asked for a clear judgement, such as officials, teachers, elders asked to settle a dispute, or experts facing public questions. The research could ask how they decide when to commit to an answer and when to hold back, and what they fear in each case. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

When does refusing to give an answer protect the truth, and when does it only protect ourselves?

## Limits and alternatives

The source classifies these positions as doctrinally wrong views within a specific religious debate. It does not condemn honest statements of uncertainty in general. Evasive answers may also come from real complexity, legal or professional constraints, politeness, or danger in speaking openly. Their motive cannot be inferred from the words alone.
