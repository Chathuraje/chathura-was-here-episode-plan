# C073-I02 — Getting what you want and wanting it again

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Obtaining what we want does not end the wanting. Because what we obtain does not last, the wish to obtain it again returns.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source explains one name for nibbāna, "desireless", by contrast with ordinary life. Things that arise also pass away, so however much a person obtains, the wish to obtain again keeps arising, and the source calls that recurring wish the root of suffering. A second passage says nothing that arises and ceases moment by moment can hold lasting happiness. The mechanism is a loop rather than a single act of greed: satisfaction is real but temporary, and its fading renews the want. The tension, as I interpret it, is that people expect one more achievement or purchase to settle the matter, while the end of each satisfaction quietly sets up the next pursuit. Unlike C006-I01 (wanting despite known costs), the point here is that success itself does not close the want.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: pursuits built on repeated attainment, such as a sales target, a competition circuit, a growing online following or collecting. The inquiry would ask what happens in the days after a goal is reached and how the next goal takes shape. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What happens to a satisfaction in the days after we finally get what we wanted?

## Limits and alternatives

That wanting is the root of all suffering is a doctrinal teaching, not a finding, and this card does not claim ambition is harmful. Repeated effort may come from necessity, duty, love of a craft or ordinary growth rather than a craving loop. Some satisfactions do last. Behaviour alone cannot show which is happening.
