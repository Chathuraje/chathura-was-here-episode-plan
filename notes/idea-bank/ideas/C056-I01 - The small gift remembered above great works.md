# C056-I01 — The small gift remembered above great works

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Looking back near the end of life, a person may find the deepest meaning not in their largest achievement but in a small act done when they had almost nothing to give.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source tells of a dying king who has the record of his good deeds read aloud. He had built great monuments. The memory that moves him most is a single meal he gave away years earlier, while hiding after a defeat, hungry and with little to spare. Of all his works, this one brings the greatest joy. Another passage says that recalling good near death brings gladness, recalling wrongs brings distress, and a friend who reminds the dying of their good deeds is valuable. The tension, in my interpretation, is between scale and meaning. What others measure and admire may not be what a person treasures when looking back. The cost of a gift to the giver may matter more than its size.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: older or dying people, and the families with them, describing which of their acts they return to most. The research would compare these with the achievements others celebrate in them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Looking back on a whole life, why might the smallest gift matter most?

## Limits and alternatives

In the source, recollection at death decides the next birth, and this card adopts none of that. The king's joy may reflect the drama of the moment, relief at survival or the storytelling rather than the gift itself. Many dying people do not review their lives, and prompting by others can pressure as well as comfort.
