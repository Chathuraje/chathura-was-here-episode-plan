# C023-I03 — Speaking against what you quietly do

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

A gap between what someone does and the goodness they display can take different shapes. They may simply deny it, openly lecture others, or build an upright image indirectly by talking about other people's faults.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source describes a quality of straightness that opposes hiding one's faults and displaying virtues one lacks. It distinguishes three kinds of crookedness, each named after a bent shape. In the first, a person does wrong in deed, speech and thought, yet says they do not, swinging one way in acting and the other in denying. In the second, a person does wrong while warning others to fear wrongdoing. In the third, the person never claims innocence directly. Instead they explain to others how bad wrongdoing is, and listeners conclude that the speaker must be upright. The tension, as I read it, is that the most persuasive self-presentation may involve no false statement at all, only well-placed moral talk.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: how communities come to trust or distrust people who speak publicly about honesty, discipline or morality. This could include how people judged a discovered gap between someone's words and conduct. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we come to believe that someone who speaks well about goodness must also live it?

## Limits and alternatives

This is an interpretation of a doctrinal classification tied to "doors" of action. It is not a method for detecting hypocrisy. People can sincerely teach standards they struggle to meet, and admitting that struggle is not crookedness. Speaking against a wrong one once committed may reflect change or repair. This card must never be used to label a documentary subject a hypocrite on the basis of outside observation.
