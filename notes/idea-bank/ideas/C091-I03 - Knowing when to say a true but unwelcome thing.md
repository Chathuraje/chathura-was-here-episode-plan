# C091-I03 — Knowing when to say a true but unwelcome thing

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Whether something should be said depends on whether it is true and useful. Whether the listener will like it does not decide that question. It decides only when and how carefully to say it.

## Human mechanism and tension

One quality the practitioner recalls is well-spokenness, explained by four cases. Words that are untrue and harmful are not spoken, whether pleasant or unpleasant to the listener. Words that are true and beneficial are spoken, and for both the pleasant and the unpleasant kind the speaker "knows the time" to say them. Pleasantness is removed as a test for speaking but kept as a matter of timing. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that this names a double failure: avoiding a true, useful thing because it will hurt, or saying it at once without asking whether the moment allows it to be heard. The tension is between honesty and care, and the source places their meeting point in timing.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people whose role involves delivering unwelcome truths, such as doctors giving a diagnosis, elders settling family disputes, or inspectors reporting unsafe work. The inquiry would ask how they judge when a person is ready to hear something, and what they have seen happen when the timing was wrong. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

How do we tell the difference between waiting for the right moment and avoiding a hard truth?

## Limits and alternatives

The source describes the Buddha's speech as an object of recollection, not a communication method, and gives no guidance on judging the time. Its list is compressed; the fuller traditional version also covers true but useless speech. "Beneficial" is judged within a religious framework. Waiting for the right time can become indefinite avoidance, some situations such as safety risks allow no delay, and listeners may experience careful timing as manipulation.
