# C085-I02 — Drawing steadiness from conduct you can honestly recall

**Status:** Accepted for research. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Calling to mind promises one has actually kept, or gifts one has actually given, can steady a person. This works only when there is something true to remember, and it can slide into holding one's goodness as a trophy.

## Human mechanism and tension

Among its forty practices the source describes going over one's own conduct: checking that no undertaking is broken at the start, middle or in patches, like cloth without torn edges or holes. A second practice goes over one's own giving. It is meant for someone already inclined to give, who gives that day before recalling it. Meanwhile, the source says, the mind is not overpowered by greed or anger and becomes upright and settled. The results it names include freedom from fear of self-blame, greater care about even small faults, and gladness. It adds two limits. These recollections succeed fully only for people whose qualities are actually established. Conduct should also not be held as a bargain ("this will make me a god") or as proof of purity. My interpretation is that honest memory of integrity steadies a person, while the same memory, inflated or used to rank oneself, becomes something else.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people facing pressure, accusation or temptation who describe what they remind themselves of about how they have lived, and whether that memory steadied or hardened them. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

What is the difference between remembering that we have lived well and needing to believe it?

## Limits and alternatives

The recollections sit inside monastic and lay vows and aim at meditative calm; the rebirth benefits are doctrinal. Steadiness under pressure may come from support, faith, temperament or simple necessity instead. Recalled "clean" conduct may be selective memory, and a person's calm cannot show whether their record is honest.
