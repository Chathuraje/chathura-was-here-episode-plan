# C098-I02 — Blame stops stinging before praise stops pulling

**Status:** Accepted for research as a limited analogy. This is a conceptual interpretation. No real story has been verified.

## Core idea

Being unshaken by loss, blame and pain may be a different achievement from being free of the pull of gain, praise and pleasure. The second may be the harder one.

## Human mechanism and tension

The source lists eight "worldly conditions" that keep life turning: gain and loss, fame and disrepute, praise and blame, pleasure and pain. In its chart of what each stage of the path removes, it splits the pairs. Reactions to loss, disrepute, blame and pain end at the third stage. Attachment to gain, fame, praise and pleasure ends only at the last. The same chart places the end of hostility at the third stage and the end of greed at the last. The source gives no explanation. My interpretation, beyond the text, is that the split follows those two roots: resisting what hurts belongs with hostility, and being drawn to what pleases belongs with wanting, which the text treats as lasting longer. The tension would be that a person can become hardened to criticism or hardship, and proud of it, while still quietly depending on approval and reward.

## Possible everyday expression

Hypothetical research direction: people whose work exposes them to both public criticism and public recognition, such as performers, officials, athletes or craftspeople, and how they describe their relationship to each over time. No person, event or outcome has been established.

## Open human question

Once criticism no longer wounds us, what still holds us through praise?

## Limits and alternatives

The chart records a doctrinal order of attainments, not an observed sequence in people's lives, and it gives no reason for the order. The link to hostility and greed is my inference from other rows. Indifference to blame can come from numbness, contempt or protective withdrawal rather than any freedom. Enjoying praise may be healthy, and some people find blame far harder to bear.
