## Held questions

1. **C067-I02 — Why the senses feel most like me.** The source ranks the sense-faculties as most intensely held as "mine" (Pradeepika line 120; Margaya lines 7, 21) but gives no explanation. Should a doctrinal ranking of attachment support research into how people relate to sensory change, given the risk of framing disability as loss of self?
2. **C068-I01 — Not every joy turns into laughter.** Explicit distinction (Pradeepika line 169) but only a list of states, with a ranking by attainment. Is the gap between gladness and laughter enough of a mechanism, or should this merge into C005-I01 as another "same outward act, different inner source" case?
3. **C068 posture passage** (Margaya lines 13–15; Pradeepika lines 117–119): a sleeping sitter falls because weak mind-states cannot hold posture. Rejected as historical physiology. Reviewer may confirm.

## Source differences worth recording

- C068: Margaya records two teachers' views absent from Pradeepika (no death-consciousness produces matter, line 11; external food only supports internal nutriment, line 27). Pradeepika alone attributes crying to hate-rooted states and discusses Buddhas' laughter (lines 161, 165).
- C069: Pradeepika line 58 says the four characteristic forms arise from no cause; Margaya line 15 notes the commentaries treat growth and continuity as caused.
- C070: the fourth shared feature of a cluster is worded differently (Pradeepika line 18 vs Margaya line 9).
