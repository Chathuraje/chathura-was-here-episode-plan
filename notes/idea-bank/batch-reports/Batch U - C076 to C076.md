## Held questions

1. **Possessive care of a protégé** (Pradeepika line 809, PDF 285–286 / printed 274–275). A teacher cuts his pupils' hair and sews their robes himself, searches "like a startled deer" when a pupil is out of sight, and refuses to lend him to another monk. This is a vivid image of care mixed with ownership, but the source uses it only as an example of a person to avoid. Should it support C005-I02 (*Love that is also holding on*) as supplementary material, or become a separate card about mentors?
2. **Routine that holds urges back** (lines 990–1014, PDF 303–305 / printed 292–294). The concrete examples (a building project finishing, study stopping, duties lapsing) could be added to C013-I02's source trace as supporting concept C076. The coordinator should decide. No duplicate card was made.
3. **Joy from recalling one's own past generosity** (line 735, PDF 280 / printed 269). Rapture is said to arise when a monk recalls giving fine food to fellow monks in a famine, or giving to the virtuous as a layman. Possibly related to C056-I01 or C059-I01. Not carded.
4. **"Faith" widened to trust** in C076-I03. Reviewers should confirm that the limited-analogy label is enough, since the source's faith is specifically in the Triple Gem.
5. **Sacrifice as pressure** in C076-I01. The source only presents the reflection as motivating. Research should not assume the effect is positive.

## Source differences (not errors)

- Fruition moment: 33 factors, without the right efforts (Pradeepika line 37), versus all found "generally" in all supramundane minds (Margaya line 113).
- Mundane practice: only one at a time (Pradeepika line 1467) versus not all at once, "as suitable" (Margaya line 113).
- Meaning of iddhi: jhāna, higher knowledge, path and fruit (Margaya line 57) versus bases arising in supramundane minds taking nibbāna (Pradeepika line 1186).
- Right effort: Margaya (lines 39–45) frames it personally (not repeating one's own past wrongs, not starting wrongs learned from others or books, securing virtues that can decline). Pradeepika (lines 912–1063) frames it through technical kinds of arisen and unarisen defilement.
- The same 14 factors are named differently: විතර්කය / තත්‍රමජ්ඣත්තතාවය (Margaya line 109) versus සම්මා සඞ්කප්ප / උපෙක්ඛා (Pradeepika line 1445).
