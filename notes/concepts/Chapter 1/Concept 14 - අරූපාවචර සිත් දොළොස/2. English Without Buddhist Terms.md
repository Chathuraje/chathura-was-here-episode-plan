### The Nature of Formless Consciousness

Formless states of consciousness refer to levels of awareness that belong to an existence entirely free from physical matter and the physical body, consisting solely of mental processes. Unlike levels of existence where a physical body is present, formless existence possesses no material form whatsoever, functioning only through consciousness and its mental factors. 

In total, there are twelve such formless states of consciousness: four wholesome states, four resulting states, and four purely functional states. When combined with the fifteen states of subtle material absorption, they constitute twenty-seven exalted meditative states. Structurally, all formless states belong to the fifth level of meditative absorption because they are constituted by the exact same two mental factors: neutral balance (equanimity) and unified concentration (one-pointedness). They do not divide into four distinct states due to any difference in their internal mental factors, but solely because of the progressive difference in the object upon which the mind is focused.

---

### The Causality and Experience Leading to Formless States

A practitioner who attains the fifth level of material absorption reflects upon the inherent defects and dangers of possessing a physical body. Maintaining the body requires arduous effort, and it remains perpetually vulnerable to aging, illness, physical hazards, and external harm. Furthermore, embodiment provides the ground for physical and verbal misconduct, which leads to severe suffering in states of deprivation after death. Recognizing the physical body as the root cause of cyclic suffering, the practitioner develops an aversion to material form and seeks a purely formless existence through meditation.

In this process, the practitioner also examines the luminous mental representation that was used to suppress mental defilements and attain the fifth material absorption. Although this mental light is refined and beneficial, it is still a representation of physical form. Because it shares the nature of material form, the practitioner perceives that it, too, can cause distress and fear, and therefore resolves to eliminate even this subtle visual object from awareness.

### The Four Progressive Stages of Wholesome Formless Consciousness

1. **Consciousness of Boundless Space**  
   The practitioner extends the mental light of the visualization across a chosen area and then deliberately removes attachment to that light, wishing to see the open expanse behind it. When this attachment is abandoned, the luminous form disappears from awareness like the opening of a window shutter, revealing the empty space left behind. This space is not an independently existing concrete reality, but an unbounded mental concept without a beginning or an end. By focusing continuously upon this conceptual opening with the reflection "infinite space, infinite space," the practitioner passes through preparatory thoughts, and the first formless wholesome consciousness arises once, unified in equanimity and concentration, taking boundless conceptual space as its object.

2. **Consciousness of Boundless Awareness**  
   After attaining the first formless absorption and mastering it through repeated review and proficiency, the practitioner observes that this first state is still too close to material absorption and was attained within the spatial arena of the removed light, and is therefore not yet fully refined. The practitioner abandons the object of space and redirects awareness toward the first formless consciousness itself. By contemplating "consciousness is infinite, consciousness is infinite," the second formless wholesome state arises, taking that first exalted consciousness as its direct object.

3. **Consciousness of Nothingness**  
   Having attained and mastered the second formless state, the practitioner recognizes that its object—the first formless consciousness—is still relatively coarse. Meditative consciousness is not permanent; when another state of mind occurs, that previous absorption consciousness ceases to exist. The practitioner therefore focuses upon the non-existence or utter absence of that first formless consciousness, contemplating "there is nothing, there is nothing." This gives rise to the third formless wholesome state, which takes the conceptual non-existence (the absence of the first consciousness) as its object.

4. **Consciousness of Neither-Perception-Nor-Non-Perception**  
   After mastering the third state, the practitioner realizes that it remains close to the second state. Letting go of the concept of absence, the practitioner takes the third formless consciousness itself as an object, contemplating it as "peaceful and refined." This produces the fourth formless state. In this state, perception and its accompanying mental constituents are so exceptionally subtle that it is difficult to determine whether consciousness is present or absent. Because perception is neither fully active nor completely non-existent, it is designated as the state of "neither-perception-nor-non-perception."

### The Analogy of the Pavilion in the Wasteland

The operational relationship between these four states and their respective objects is illustrated through an analogy:

* **The Meaning of the Elements:**
  * The vast wasteland represents the terrifying cycle of conditioned existence.
  * The ground covered in filth represents the realm of sensory and physical embodiment.
  * The pavilion in the center represents the four formless realms.
  * The four men represent practitioners of the four formless absorptions.

* **The Progression of the Four Men:**
  1. **The First Man:** Exhausted by the heat, he runs into the pavilion. Seeing the ground covered in filth, he avoids stepping on it by leaping up and hanging onto one corner of the pavilion’s roof frame.  
     *(This corresponds to the first formless consciousness, which avoids physical form by grasping the conceptual object of infinite space).*
  2. **The Second Man:** Running in and finding no place to stand, he hangs onto the feet of the first man without touching the ground.  
     *(This corresponds to the second formless consciousness, which transcends the concept of space by directly grasping the first formless consciousness).*
  3. **The Third Man:** Running in to seek relief and finding no clean place to stand, he leaps up and hangs onto the opposite corner of the pavilion’s roof frame, just like the first man.  
     *(This corresponds to the third formless consciousness, which transcends the preceding consciousness by grasping the conceptual absence or non-existence of the first consciousness).*
  4. **The Fourth Man:** Running in behind him and finding no open place, he hangs onto the feet of the third man without touching the ground.  
     *(This corresponds to the fourth formless consciousness, which transcends the concept of absence by directly grasping the refined third consciousness).*
