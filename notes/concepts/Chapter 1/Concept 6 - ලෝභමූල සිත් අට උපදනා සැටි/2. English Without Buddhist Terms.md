### 1. The Nature of Attachment-Rooted Consciousness and Its Arising

Among the twelve unwholesome states of consciousness, the primary group consists of the **eight attachment-rooted states of consciousness**. These states are defined by craving—the fundamental tendency of the mind to cling to and grasp its objects.

The process of their arising begins when consciousness encounters an object through the senses. Objects may be agreeable, disagreeable, or neutral. When an agreeable object is met, delight or joy arises, producing a pleasant feeling. Following this pleasant feeling, the mind naturally attaches to and grasps the object. A state of consciousness characterized by this attachment is an attachment-rooted state.

These states are classified into eight distinct types based on the combination of three structural factors:
1. **The Feeling Tone:** Whether the experience is accompanied by pleasure or by neutral indifference.
2. **The Cognitive View:** Whether the state is accompanied by an erroneous conviction (a mistaken belief) or is dissociated from it.
3. **The Mode of Initiation:** Whether the state arises spontaneously without prompting (unprompted) or is induced through prompting and effort (prompted).

---

### 2. The Three Determining Factors and Their Conditions

#### (a) The Factor of Feeling (Pleasant vs. Neutral)
* **Accompanied by Pleasure:** When experiencing an agreeable object, the mind enjoys it with delight. Beyond the presence of a pleasant object, this state is conditioned by: having taken birth through a resultant consciousness characterized by pleasure, possessing shallow or non-profound patterns of thought, and currently being free from calamities such as aging, illness, disease, and misfortune.
* **Accompanied by Neutrality:** When consciousness meets a neutral object—one that is neither agreeable nor disagreeable—neither pleasure nor displeasure is produced, and an attitude of neutral, indifferent feeling arises. This state is conditioned by: having taken birth through a resultant consciousness characterized by neutrality, having a disposition toward deep reflection, and being free from diseases, afflictions, and misfortune.

#### (b) The Factor of View (Accompanied by Error vs. Free from Error)
In unwholesome states of consciousness, "view" refers strictly to **erroneous conviction or false belief**, as correct understanding occurs exclusively in pure, wholesome states.
* **Accompanied by Erroneous Conviction:** The unwholesome act is performed together with a justifying false belief. Examples include believing that moral cause and effect do not exist, that there is no afterlife, that there are no benefits in generosity or moral restraint, that there is no wrong in taking another’s property if one desires it, that killing animals for meat or pleasure is blameless, that unrestrained sensual indulgence or intoxication carries no fault, or that lying, slander, harsh speech, and idle chatter are harmless. This condition is caused by: associating with those who hold false views, remaining distant from the truth, frequently entertaining improper thoughts, and reflecting superficially or unwisely.
* **Dissociated from Erroneous Conviction:** The individual acts unwholesomely without holding any justifying false belief. Such a person does not deny the results of actions; they know that bad deeds bring suffering and that good deeds like generosity, ethical conduct, and mental development bring benefits. However, because of habits formed across past existences, or for personal gain, or to avoid trouble and hardship, they are unable to restrain their craving and commit the act without adopting an erroneous doctrine.

#### (c) The Factor of Initiation (Unprompted vs. Prompted)
* **Unprompted (Spontaneous):** The state of consciousness arises immediately, keenly, and naturally from within, without requiring external instigation, encouragement, or deliberate self-urging. It is supported by: having taken birth through an unprompted resultant consciousness, bodily and mental health, abundant patience, thorough familiarity and habituation with the task, and obtaining suitable food and environmental conditions.
* **Prompted (Induced):** The state of consciousness arises through the instigation of others or by intentionally urging one's own hesitating mind. Such states are subject to sluggishness and mental heaviness; sluggishness and torpor of mind occur exclusively within prompted unwholesome states.

### 3. The Arising of the Eight Attachment-Rooted States in Human Experience

In concrete human experience, these eight states arise in the following ways:

1. **Pleasant, accompanied by erroneous conviction, unprompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising naturally with pleasure, conjoined with a false belief, without any prompting.
   * *Example:* Stealing another's property with delight and on one's own initiative under the belief that *"there is no wrong in taking what is surplus from the wealthy,"* or relishing delicious food with joy under the belief that *"these things are given to us by God."*
2. **Pleasant, accompanied by erroneous conviction, prompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with pleasure and conjoined with a false belief, but through external instigation or by actively overcoming one's own hesitation.
   * *Example:* Engaging in the same ideologically justified, joyful theft or indulgence when urged by another person or by forcing oneself to do it.
3. **Pleasant, dissociated from erroneous conviction, unprompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising naturally with pleasure, without any justifying false belief, and without any prompting.
   * *Example:* Stealing another's goods or enjoying delicious food with delight on one's own impulse, despite knowing that stealing is wrong.
4. **Pleasant, dissociated from erroneous conviction, prompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with pleasure, without a false belief, but through external prompting or deliberate self-effort.
   * *Example:* Committing an unrationalized theft or indulgence with pleasure, but only after being encouraged by others or after persuading oneself.
5. **Neutral, accompanied by erroneous conviction, unprompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with an indifferent feeling, conjoined with a false belief, without any prompting.
   * *Example:* Stealing or consuming intoxicants with an emotionally neutral state and without prompting, believing that taking from the rich is blameless or that the item is divinely provided.
6. **Neutral, accompanied by erroneous conviction, prompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with an indifferent feeling and a false belief, through external instigation or forced self-encouragement.
   * *Example:* Committing the same ideologically justified, emotionally neutral action when urged by others or by making an effort to push oneself.
7. **Neutral, dissociated from erroneous conviction, unprompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with an indifferent feeling, without any false belief, and without any prompting.
   * *Example:* Committing an act like theft with a completely neutral feeling, free from any justifying doctrine, entirely on one's own initiative.
8. **Neutral, dissociated from erroneous conviction, prompted:** 
   * *Meaning:* Arising with an indifferent feeling, without a false belief, through external prompting or by overcoming one's own hesitation.
   * *Example:* Committing an unrationalized, emotionally neutral transgression only when urged by someone else or through deliberate self-effort.

### 4. Relation to Destructive Actions and Causal Potency

Attachment-rooted consciousness can give rise to seven of the ten recognized unwholesome courses of action: **theft, illicit sexual misconduct, false speech, slanderous/divisive speech, frivolous/idle speech, covetousness (the intense desire to possess another's property), and holding false views**.
* Among these, **illicit sexual misconduct, covetousness, and false views** are generated exclusively by attachment-rooted states. The remaining four (theft, lying, divisive speech, and frivolous speech) can be caused by attachment, but may also arise through other unwholesome mental roots.

In terms of their causal potency and future fruition:
* **Full Courses of Action vs. Ordinary Attachment:** When attachment leads to full destructive actions (such as theft), the consciousness is powerful in producing results. In contrast, ordinary attachments—such as everyday affection for spouse, children, or personal wealth—are weak, and their causal fruition is indefinite.
* **Relative Strength Among the States:**
  * A state accompanied by an **erroneous conviction** is more potent than one dissociated from error.
  * An **unprompted** state is more potent than a **prompted** state.
  * In yielding future fruition, a state accompanied by a **neutral feeling** is more potent than one accompanied by a **pleasant feeling**.
