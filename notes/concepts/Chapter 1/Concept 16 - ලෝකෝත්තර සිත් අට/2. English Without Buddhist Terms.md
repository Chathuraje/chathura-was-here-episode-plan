### 1. The Nature of Transcendent Consciousness

In this philosophical system, the "world" is defined as the five aggregates of conditioned experience grasped through craving and distorted views, encompassing all sensory, subtle-form, and formless planes of existence. Ordinary moral actions and conventional meditative absorptions are categorized as mundane: they merely manage, suppress, or temporarily suspend mental impurities and maintain continuity within conditioned existence. 

In contrast, **transcendent consciousness** refers to an exceptional cognitive breakthrough that frees the individual from this conditioned continuum. Its defining function is not the temporary suppression of mental afflictions, but their **complete, root-level eradication**, ensuring that eradicated cognitive and emotional distortions never arise again within that conscious stream.

---

### 2. Classification and the Mechanics of Causation

Transcendent consciousness comprises **eight distinct states of awareness**:
* **Four transformative (causal/path) states**, which actively eradicate mental impurities.
* **Four resultant (fruition) states**, which directly experience the immediate peace of that liberation.
*(When differentiated according to the depth of meditative absorption, these expand to forty states).*

There are **no functional or neutral states** within transcendent consciousness. This is because a transformative path consciousness arises **only once** per stage of realization within an individual’s continuum. Its sole purpose is the total eradication of specific mental impurities; once those impurities are uprooted, there is nothing remaining at that level to destroy, and that specific transformative state cannot recur.

Unlike mundane actions that produce results after a temporal delay, transformative transcendent consciousness is immediate and timeless. The moment the transformative consciousness ceases, its corresponding resultant fruition consciousness arises immediately in the very next two to three mental moments. In these moments, consciousness directly experiences the unconditioned state of peace, reflecting the complete subsiding and cooling of the eradicated impurities.

---

### 3. The Operation of Transformative Awareness and the Analogy of the Lamp

A transformative state of consciousness occurs when the complete eightfold framework of liberation—comprising purified view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration—unifies within a single conscious instant focused directly upon unconditioned reality.

In that single instant, consciousness simultaneously executes four essential functions:
1. **Fully discerning the nature of suffering and conditioned existence.**
2. **Completely abandoning the origin of suffering (craving and clinging).**
3. **Directly realizing and experiencing the cessation of suffering (the unconditioned).**
4. **Cultivating and actualizing the transformative path.**

This simultaneous operation is explained through **the analogy of an oil lamp**:
When an oil lamp burns, it does not perform its actions in sequential steps; in a single moment, it simultaneously burns the wick, dispels the darkness, radiates light, and consumes the oil. Similarly, a transformative moment of consciousness simultaneously comprehends conditioned suffering (burning the wick), eradicates craving and delusion (dispelling darkness), actualizes the path of wisdom (radiating light), and realizes the unconditioned peace wherein all defilements are exhausted (consuming the oil).

---

### 4. The Four Sequential Stages of Liberation

The root-level eradication of mental impurities occurs in four progressive stages:

1. **The Initial Breakthrough (Entering the Stream of Liberation):**
   * This is the initial entry into the irreversible flow toward unconditioned peace.
   * **Impurities eradicated:** It completely eliminates all mental states rooted in distorted views and skeptical doubt, the false view of a substantial personal self, clinging to ineffective rituals, and all dispositions capable of causing rebirth in states of severe degradation. 
   * An individual at this stage who attains realization solely through discernment (without previous meditative absorptions) will experience at most seven further rebirths within the sensory realm (subdivided into those returning once, two to six times, or a maximum of seven times). Because receiving verbal instruction is an indispensable condition for this first breakthrough, it can only occur in realms where sensory faculties (such as hearing) exist, and never in purely formless planes.

2. **The Intermediate Stage of Attenuation:**
   * **Impurities weakened:** This stage does not eliminate an entirely new category of mental states, but significantly weakens, thins out, and attenuates gross sensory desires and gross aversion.
   * An individual at this stage returns to the sensory realm at most once more before attaining final liberation.

3. **The Stage of Non-Return:**
   * **Impurities eradicated:** It completely destroys all remaining subtle sensory desires, aversion, ill-will, remorse, and destructive forms of speech and intention.
   * Having extinguished all sensory craving and hostility, such an individual is never reborn into the sensory realm; upon death, they arise in refined planes of form, where they attain final liberation.

4. **The Final Realization of Absolute Liberation:**
   * **Impurities eradicated:** It uproots all remaining subtle attachments to higher, refined realms of existence, subtle conceit, mental restlessness, and fundamental existential ignorance without remainder.
   * Such an individual attains liberation with physical remnants intact during their natural lifespan, and enters complete, unconditioned cessation upon biological death. Having fully accomplished the task, they require no further spiritual training.

---

### 5. The Analogy of the Royal Treasure House

The sequential nature of these four realizations is illustrated by **the analogy of a royal treasure house**:

A person searching for rare treasures arrives at a locked, pitch-black royal storehouse. Unlocking the door, they light a lamp, enter, see the treasures, retrieve what they desire, extinguish the lamp, close the door, and exit (the first breakthrough and its fruition). They repeat this exact process a second and third time (the second and third stages). 

When they enter the fourth time and light their lamp, **the sun suddenly rises**, permanently illuminating the entire storehouse and the outside world. An artificial lamp is no longer necessary. The individual rests in undisturbed peace, freely viewing the treasures in full daylight until sunset (the sunrise represents the final realization of liberation, and the sunset represents the final cessation at biological death).

---

### 6. Preparation, Development, and the Balance of Calm and Insight

A transcendent breakthrough does not occur arbitrarily or without causes. It requires systematic preparatory development: establishing purified ethical conduct, cultivating mental stabilization (concentration), analytically distinguishing mental phenomena from physical phenomena, resolving doubts regarding causality, and systematically progressing through the successive stages of analytical insight regarding the arising and passing away of conditioned phenomena.

During preliminary training, either meditative calm (stillness) or analytical insight (discernment) may predominate. However, **at the precise moment of transcendent breakthrough, calm and insight operate in absolute, unified equilibrium**, like two equally strong oxen yoked to a single cart. Consequently, even an individual who practiced pure analytical discernment without prior meditative absorptions naturally and instantaneously experiences all the primary structural factors of meditative absorption during the transformative breakthrough moment itself. 

Thus, transcendent consciousness represents the definitive, irreversible culmination of mental development: the complete cessation of conditioned suffering and the direct realization of unconditioned peace.
