### 1. The True Meaning of "Rootless" Consciousness and Common Misunderstandings

In this analytical framework, the term "rootless" is often misunderstood as meaning something that arises spontaneously or without any cause. However, "roots" refers specifically to six primary causal dispositions: three unwholesome roots (attachment, aversion, and delusion) and three wholesome roots (non-attachment, non-aversion, and non-delusion/understanding).

When a mental state is accompanied by at least one of these six primary roots, it is designated as a **rooted state**. Conversely, mental states that arise completely devoid of all six root dispositions are designated as **rootless states of consciousness**. These states are not uncaused; rather, they operate as elementary, passive, or purely functional events without the presence of these six primary ethical drives. In total, there are eighteen such rootless states, divided into three functional categories:
1. **Rootless unwholesome resultant states (7)**
2. **Rootless wholesome resultant states (8)**
3. **Rootless functional states (3)**

### 2. The Ten Elementary Sensory Consciousnesses and the Mechanics of Perception

Ten of the rootless resultant states constitute the five pairs of elementary sensory consciousness (seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, and bodily sensing). Each sensory faculty possesses one resultant state arising from past unwholesome actions and one arising from past wholesome actions.

* **Visual Consciousness:** Arises dependent on the physical eye-sensitivity, which is produced by past action to receive light and color. Its function is the bare act of seeing an object. It requires four necessary conditions: an unimpaired eye-sensitivity, the presentation of a visible form, the presence of light, and mental attention.
* **Auditory Consciousness:** Arises dependent on the ear-sensitivity situated within the ear canal to register sound. It requires four conditions: an unimpaired ear-sensitivity, the presentation of a sound, an unobstructed ear passage (spatial openness), and mental attention.
* **Olfactory Consciousness:** Arises dependent on the nose-sensitivity situated within the nasal passage to register odors. It requires four conditions: an unimpaired nose-sensitivity, the presentation of an odor, the assistance of the air element entering the nostrils, and mental attention.
* **Gustatory Consciousness:** Arises dependent on the tongue-sensitivity to register flavors. It requires four conditions: an unimpaired tongue-sensitivity, the presentation of a flavor, the assistance of moisture (fluid element) on the tongue, and mental attention.
* **Bodily Consciousness:** Arises dependent on the body-sensitivity diffused throughout the physical frame to register tactile contact. It requires four conditions: an unimpaired body-sensitivity, the presentation of a tangible object (consisting of the fundamental material elements of solidity, temperature, or motion), the supporting solidity of the body itself, and mental attention.

#### The Difference in Affective Feeling (The Two Analogies)
The objects of sight, sound, smell, and taste are subtle, derived material qualities, and their corresponding physical faculties are equally delicate. The meeting of these objects and faculties is analogous to **one soft tuft of cotton wool striking another tuft of cotton wool**. Because the impact is gentle and the functional capacity of these sensory bases is minimal, the accompanying affective tone is strictly **neutral (indifferent feeling)**.

In contrast, tactile contact involves the fundamental material elements themselves colliding directly with the physical frame. This impact is analogous to **placing a lump of cotton wool on an anvil and striking it with an iron hammer**: the blow pierces straight through the soft cotton and strikes the hard anvil with full force. Because of this direct and forceful impact, bodily consciousness does not experience a neutral feeling. Instead, an unwholesome result produces immediate **physical pain (unpleasant feeling)**, while a wholesome result produces immediate **physical pleasure (pleasant feeling)**.

### 3. Objective Reality, Perceptual Distortions, and Active Responses

Whether an object is intrinsically "agreeable" (pleasant) or "disagreeable" (unpleasant) is established by the standard of a neutral, balanced observer:
* An intrinsically agreeable object conditions a **wholesome resultant consciousness**.
* An intrinsically disagreeable object conditions an **unwholesome resultant consciousness**.

However, due to cognitive and perceptual distortions, conscious beings frequently misapprehend reality, viewing what is repulsive as desirable, or what is sublime as disagreeable. For instance, excrement is an intrinsically disagreeable object. When an animal such as a pig encounters it, the initial visual consciousness is an unwholesome result (registering an unpleasant object). Yet, because of perceptual distortion, the pig subsequently responds with desire and craving.

The exact same disagreeable object triggers entirely different subsequent mental responses depending on the individual:
1. **An ordinary, undisciplined person:** Experiences the unwholesome visual resultant consciousness, and subsequently reacts with aversion-rooted active mental states (disgust and anger).
2. **A wise, reflective person:** Experiences the same unwholesome visual resultant consciousness, but reflects thoughtfully ("My own body is likewise composed of such impurities"), thereby generating wholesome active mental states accompanied by understanding.
3. **Fully enlightened beings:** Experience the same unwholesome visual resultant consciousness, which is immediately followed by pure, non-reactive functional active mental states.

This demonstrates a vital distinction: **passive resultant experience** (the involuntary sensory impact of past action) is entirely distinct from the **active mental response** (the subsequent ethical or emotional reaction generated by the subject).

### 4. The Processing States: Reception and Investigation

An external stimulus does not conclude its process at the sensory organ; it proceeds through an orderly sequence of consciousness:

* **Receptive Consciousness (2):** This state performs the task of receiving the sensory object immediately after it has been registered by the five senses. It is analogous to taking an object into one's hand before inspecting it. Arising dependent on the physical heart-base with a neutral feeling, it functions as an unwholesome result for disagreeable objects and as a wholesome result for agreeable objects.
* **Investigating Consciousness (3):** This state examines the received object, analogous to inspecting the object held in hand to determine its nature:
  1. *Unwholesome resultant investigating consciousness (with neutral feeling):* Examines disagreeable objects.
  2. *Wholesome resultant investigating consciousness (with neutral feeling):* Examines moderately agreeable objects.
  3. *Wholesome resultant investigating consciousness (with joyful feeling):* Examines exceptionally agreeable or sublime objects.

### 5. Extended Functions: Rebirth-Linking and Life Continuum

Beyond the temporary investigation of sensory data within a cognitive process, the two neutral investigating states perform fundamental structural roles in sustaining existence, known as the **"rootless rebirth-linking pair"**:

* **Woeful Rebirth:** If an unwholesome action (out of the eleven unwholesome actions, excluding restlessness) determines the transition of life at death, the initial consciousness that links the being into the four woeful realms of existence is the *unwholesome resultant neutral investigating consciousness*.
* **Impaired Human Rebirth:** If an exceptionally weak wholesome action determines the transition of life, the being is born into the human realm with congenital disabilities (such as congenital blindness, deafness, muteness, or physical deformity) through the *wholesome resultant neutral investigating consciousness*.

Furthermore, these same two investigating states function as the subconscious life-continuum that maintains individual existence between active cognitive processes, serve as the final death-consciousness of that life, and act as the retention states that linger briefly on prominent sensory objects at the close of an active cognitive process.

### 6. The Three Rootless Functional States

There are three rootless states that are neither moral causes (actions) nor results (effects), but perform purely operational duties within the mind:
1. **Five-Door Adverting Consciousness (Neutral):** Interrupts the passive subconscious flow and turns the stream of consciousness toward an incoming stimulus at one of the five physical sense doors.
2. **Mind-Door Adverting Consciousness (Neutral):** Directs the stream of consciousness toward an idea, memory, or mental object arising at the mental door.
3. **Smile-Producing Consciousness (Joyful):** A state unique to fully enlightened beings, causing them to smile at subtle realities of the world.

---

### 7. Why Are There No Rooted Unwholesome Resultant States?

Wholesome actions possess the strength to produce both elementary rootless results (basic agreeable sensations) and grand rooted results (which provide noble rebirth, high status, material wealth, and the capacity to enjoy and develop wholesome qualities).

In contrast, unwholesome actions produce **only the seven rootless unwholesome resultant states**; there is no category of "rooted unwholesome results."

The reason lies in the economy of causal reality: the sole purpose of an unwholesome result is to subject the being to the experience of suffering through the encounter with disagreeable objects. This experiential suffering is fully and completely accomplished by the seven rootless unwholesome states. If rooted unwholesome results existed, they would require the presence of primary unwholesome roots (attachment, aversion, or delusion) within a passive resultant state. However, primary roots are powerful active forces that belong to active volitional phases, whereas resultant states are inherently passive and weak. Because reality does not generate redundant mental phenomena where a function is already fully accomplished, the seven rootless states suffice to deliver the entirety of unwholesome retribution.
