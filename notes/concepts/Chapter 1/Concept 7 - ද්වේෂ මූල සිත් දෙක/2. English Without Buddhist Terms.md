### The Nature of Aversion, Resistance, and Mental Displeasure

Aversion is the oppositional and resistant quality of consciousness directed toward an object—whether a perceived form, sound, person, or event—that the mind encounters. It is the fundamental state of disliking or rejecting an object. Experiences such as anger, wrath, hatred, fear, disgust, and discontent are all manifestations of this single oppositional nature. Because consciousness collides with and strikes against the experienced object rather than accommodating it, this state is termed “resistance.”

Unlike consciousness rooted in desire, consciousness rooted in aversion does not vary in its feeling; it is invariably accompanied by painful mental feeling, or mental displeasure. Mental displeasure is the experiential undergoing of the undesirable and disagreeable quality of the object. Sorrow and grief are expressions of this exact feeling.

Consciousness rooted in aversion is divided into two types based solely on whether it arises with or without instigation:
1. **Unprompted resistant consciousness accompanied by mental displeasure:** This arises immediately and spontaneously from one's own disposition, without external encouragement, prompting, or deliberate prior instigation.
2. **Prompted resistant consciousness accompanied by mental displeasure:** This arises through external inducement, encouragement, or deliberate prior prompting.

---

### The Process and Causal Factors of Arising

When an unwelcome, disagreeable, or unpleasing object is encountered, a state of unpleasantness, impurity, and distress immediately arises in the mind, giving rise to the feeling of mental displeasure. Because this object is experienced as hostile, the mind forms a desire to destroy, crush, or torment it. This aggressive drive is known as resistance, wrath, or anger.

Four primary causes lead to the arising of this resistant consciousness:
1. Encountering an unwelcome or disagreeable object.
2. An ingrained habit of irritability and a quick-tempered disposition.
3. Ignorance or a lack of understanding.
4. Failing to reflect deeply or consider the broader consequences of the object.

Aversion frequently develops out of obstructed desire. When an individual seeks to fulfill a desire and encounters an obstacle, the mind shifts from wanting to an aggressive urge to eliminate the person or entity causing the obstruction. From that moment, resistant consciousness takes over, driving the individual to seek weapons or use physical force to destroy life, thereby carrying out the harmful action of killing.

---

### Internal and Somatic Effects, and Associated Mental States

When resistant consciousness arises, clear discernment is lost; an individual cannot clearly distinguish between right and wrong, or benefit and harm. Internally, the mind undergoes intense burning and agitation.

* **Physical and Behavioral Effects:** The inner distress, sorrow, and grief cause physical dryness in the body and alter the complexion. When this inner burning becomes unbearable, weeping begins; harsh and abusive words are spoken; individuals strike their own head and chest, or seize nearby objects to strike surrounding people or property. This state is the direct cause of both destroying the lives of others and taking one's own life.
* **Associated Mental States:** Three distinct negative mental states arise in conjunction with this resistant consciousness:
  * **Envy:** The inability to endure or tolerate the prosperity, success, and advantages of others.
  * **Stinginess (Possessiveness):** The begrudging attitude that one's own possessions and advantages should not be shared or attained by others.
  * **Remorse (Regret):** Anxious agitation and brooding over wrongs committed and good deeds left undone.

### Common Misconceptions and Everyday Triggers

A common misconception is the belief that when someone kills or harms a living being with laughter or for sport (such as hunting for amusement), aversion is absent. In such instances, aversion is still present, operating in a subtle form, whereas killing an attacking enemy involves fierce, overt hostility. Regardless of outward smiling, the mental state that destroys life remains rooted in aversion.

In ordinary human experience, resistant consciousness arises in numerous situations:
* Undergoing aging, physical disease, the misfortune or death of loved ones, encounters with the unpleasant, separation from the cherished, and the failure to obtain what is desired.
* Hearing others speak of one's faults, shortcomings, or past errors, or encountering disobedience from subordinates.
* Seeing others commit errors, or anticipating that others will commit errors.
* Seeing others handle one's cherished possessions, having one's requests refused, or having one's instructions and advice ignored or rejected.
* Responding with envy toward those who enjoy superior food, clothing, vehicles, dwellings, or social honor.

---

### Harmful Actions and Significance of Understanding

Resistant consciousness is the primary foundation for three major harmful courses of action: the intentional destruction of life, harsh speech, and malevolent ill-will (the wish for the destruction and ruin of others). Under the influence of this state, acts of theft, falsehood, slander, and frivolous talk are also carried out.

If these resistant states arise frequently at the terminal moment of death, they lead to rebirth in states of intense suffering and deprivation. Because resistant states arise frequently even in those striving for moral and spiritual perfection, it is essential to comprehend the destructive nature of aversion thoroughly and maintain continuous, mindful effort to avoid and restrain it.
